# Bibliography: Sources and Evidence

![Bibliography: Sources and Evidence — logo](stays-marketplace-architecture/posts/bibliography/assets/images/bibliography/logo.jpeg)

> **IN THIS SECTION, YOU WILL:** Find where the book's evidence comes from: each source once, with its year, its evidence type and the chapters that rely on it, findable by topic or by chapter.

> **KEY POINTS:**
>
> * **Sources are listed because they were cited.** Every entry was harvested from a page's **Sources and Evidence** section, and its **Cited in** line names those pages. Nothing is added for completeness, and a listing does not mean every page of a long document was read.
> * **Dates and evidence types tell you how far to trust a claim.** A company's engineering post records what it said it built in that year, a filing records reported numbers, partner documentation records an interface as it was read, a regulation records a requirement and trade press records an event. Weigh each claim by the kind and age of the source behind it.
> * **Keep documented facts, inferences and Corvane distinct.** The sources support the documented facts. Reference architectures for real companies are labeled inferences, the recommendations are my judgement, and Corvane, the travel group the book follows, is fictional: no source here describes it.

> **[FAILURE PATTERNS THIS SECTION ADDRESSES](#where-stay-platforms-go-wrong):**
>
> * **The Hotel-Shaped Home** — company engineering posts and partner documentation are the largest evidence types on this page, and each describes one company's hotel or homes platform at one date; reading the year, the evidence type and the citing context keeps a published hotel design from becoming the model for homes, or the reverse.

This page lists the external sources the chapters cite. The research was conducted in **October 2026**, and most entries were consulted then; where a chapter recorded an access date, a version or a "last updated" date, the entry keeps it. Some partner documentation is login-gated and some company pages blocked automated access during the research, so a few facts are known only through **search excerpts, archived copies or secondary summaries** (a trade-press article or a third-party digest of an engineering post); the entries say so where the citing chapter did. Ownership and brand names follow the sources as of 2026: the Priceline Group is now Booking Holdings, HomeAway is now Vrbo, Trip.com Group began as Ctrip and keeps it as a brand, and Agoda's Yield Control System (YCS) extranet is now the Agoda Partner Portal.

## How to Read This Register

**R** marks a consulted source; the numbers identify, they do not rank. The register holds **473 sources** harvested from the **Sources and Evidence** sections of 56 pages, deduplicated by normalized URL (scheme, `www.`, trailing slash, fragment, `.html` suffix and arXiv version ignored); where chapters cited the same legal text, filing or post under two URLs, the entry keeps both. Identifiers were assigned in topic order for this first edition and stay stable: **new sources append from the next free R-number and are never renumbered**, and a removed source's number is retired rather than reused, so a reference to an identifier keeps meaning the same document.

Each entry gives the identifier, the linked title, the author or publisher, the **publication year as the citing chapter gave it** ("n.d." where the source carries no date, with access or update dates kept where the chapter recorded them), one clause on what the source supports, the evidence type and the chapters that cite it. Company engineering evidence carries its year because old posts may describe systems that have since been replaced; a post more than about three years old is a record of that date, not of today. Where chapters cited one source with slightly different metadata, the entry keeps the wording most chapters used, and where they gave different years it keeps the most specific citation.

## Topic Index

Identifiers run in topic order, so each topic is one contiguous block. The first nine topics follow the book's parts: a source sits under the part whose chapters cite it most (the legal texts used by [Rules of the Stay: Short-Term Rental Regulation, DSA and Consumer Law](#stays-regulation) sit together under Part VII), and its **Cited in** line lists every page that uses it. The tenth collects the filings, results and company histories of the case companies, which chapters across the book cite, together with the sources only the case studies in Part X use. Engineering posts and research papers by a company sit under the part they inform, even when that company is one of the cases.

**Marketplace models, economics and history (R1–R25, 25 sources; [Part I — MARKETPLACE: The Business of a Night's Stay](#part-1)).** The OTA and hosted-stays business models, two-sided market economics, industry history from the GDS to the bedbanks, and the capability and domain-design methods used to divide the marketplace. The most widely cited is R1, *Productscape domain models for travel-accommodations and hosted-stays marketplaces*, cited in 14 pages.

**Supply, content and connectivity (R26–R108, 83 sources; [Part II — SUPPLY: Properties, Homes and the Partners Behind Them](#part-2)).** Property and listing models, partner and host onboarding, content and media, room mapping, connectivity to channel managers and property systems, iCal calendar sync, and extranets and host tools. The most widely cited is R28, *Reservations API overview*, cited in 9 pages.

**Availability, rates and pricing (R109–R158, 50 sources; [Part III — INVENTORY: Availability, Rates and Price](#part-3)).** Rates and availability (ARI) messages, availability caching and price delivery, overbooking and inventory integrity, dynamic pricing, promotions, loyalty and price-display rules. The most widely cited is R110, *About the Shopping API*, cited in 7 pages.

**Search, ranking and personalization (R159–R208, 50 sources; [Part IV — DISCOVERY: From Intent to Bookable Stay](#part-4)).** Search and retrieval, destinations, geo indexes and maps, learning to rank, personalization and recommendations, the property page, and the privacy rules that bound personalization. The most widely cited is R159, *150 successful machine learning models: 6 lessons learned at Booking.com*, cited in 10 pages.

**Booking, reservations and service (R209–R248, 40 sources; [Part V — BOOKING: Making and Keeping the Reservation](#part-5)).** Checkout orchestration, idempotency and sagas, reservation delivery and acknowledgement, changes and cancellations, package-travel rules, trip messaging and customer service. The most widely cited is R210, *Introducing AgentHub*, cited in 5 pages.

**Payments, payouts and tax (R249–R283, 35 sources; [Part VI — MONEY: Who Pays Whom, When and How Much](#part-6)).** Merchant, agency and facilitated payment models, payment services and strong customer authentication, payouts and settlement, taxes on stays, chargebacks and reconciliation. The most widely cited is R250, *Council Directive (EU) 2021/514 (DAC7)*, cited in 9 pages.

**Trust, safety and regulation (R284–R343, 60 sources; [Part VII — TRUST: Making Strangers Safe to Stay With](#part-7)).** Fraud and account takeover, identity and authorization, reviews and reputation, host and guest protection, and the short-term-rental, platform, competition and consumer law that becomes system requirements. The most widely cited is R286, *Regulation (EU) 2024/1028 on short-term rental data*, cited in 15 pages.

**Platform, data, ML and experimentation (R344–R400, 57 sources; [Part VIII — PLATFORM: The Distributed System Underneath](#part-8)).** Event streaming and change data capture, global scale and reliability, metrics and data platforms, experimentation, the ML platform and its regulation, and the demand channels guests arrive through. The most widely cited is R345, *How Agoda Scaled Its Price Cache with DragonflyDB*, cited in 10 pages.

**Architecture method, organization and AI (R401–R423, 23 sources; [Part IX — LEADERSHIP: The Chief Architect's Agenda](#part-9)).** Team design, architecture decision practice, migration patterns, long-term architecture method, and AI agents across the stay. The most widely cited is R402, *The Great Migration: from Monolith to Service-Oriented*, cited in 4 pages.

**Company records and case evidence (R424–R473, 50 sources; [Part X — PRACTICE: How Others Built It](#part-10)).** Filings, results, shareholder letters and company histories of Booking Holdings, Airbnb, Expedia Group and Trip.com Group, and the engineering and press sources the four case studies use alone. The most widely cited is R424, *Booking Holdings Form 10-K for fiscal 2025*, cited in 26 pages.
