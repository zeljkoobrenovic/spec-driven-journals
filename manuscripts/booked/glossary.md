# Glossary: The Language of Stays Marketplaces

![Glossary: The Language of Stays Marketplaces — logo](stays-marketplace-architecture/posts/glossary/assets/images/glossary/logo.jpeg)

> **IN THIS SECTION, YOU WILL:** Find the book's vocabulary in one place: read a plain-language definition grouped with the other terms of the same part, see where a term means something different on the hotel side and the homes side, follow the link to the chapter that owns it, or look a term or abbreviation up in the alphabetical index at the end.

> **KEY POINTS:**
>
> * **Look a term up, then read its neighbors.** Each section collects one part's terms, so the rows around a term carry the contrasts that cause real design errors: property versus unit versus listing, inventory versus availability, rate plan versus audience, offer versus reservation, agency versus merchant versus merchant of record, gross versus stayed reservations, a trust decision versus its enforcement.
> * **One term, one meaning, one owning chapter.** Every row links to the chapter that owns or best explains the term, and the chapters are written to follow these definitions. Where the accommodation marketplace and the hosted-stays marketplace use a word differently, the row says so; when a chapter and this page disagree, one of them needs correcting.
> * **Regulatory, standards and company terms date quickly.** Regulations, standards, product names and their status are given as of 2026, following the sources cited in the chapters. Check the current source before a definition here goes into a contract, a partner agreement, a policy or a design decision.

> **[FAILURE PATTERNS THIS SECTION ADDRESSES](#where-stay-platforms-go-wrong):**
>
> * **The Hotel-Shaped Home** — the same failure at the level of words: when "unit", "listing", "rate plan" and "availability" mean one thing on the hotel side and another on the homes side, one model gets forced onto both; naming the difference is the first defense.
> * **Price Drift** — when "price", "total", "fee" and "tax" are composed in different places, the numbers disagree because the words did first; one definition of the offer and the total display is where the repair starts.

## How to Use This Glossary

The sections follow the book's structure. The first collects the **evidence labels** every chapter uses and the **sixteen failure patterns**; the next nine follow **Parts I to IX**, from the business of a night's stay through supply, inventory, discovery, booking, money, trust and the platform underneath to the Chief Architect's leadership agenda; the last collects **Corvane's fictional names**. Part X's case studies apply these terms rather than adding new ones; the few generic terms they introduce sit with Part IX. The **alphabetical index** at the end lists every term and abbreviation with a link to the section that defines it.

Each section opens with the chapters that own its terms, then a `| Term | Definition | Chapter |` table. The definitions are short teaching descriptions in plain language, not legal text, standards or vendor documentation; the chapter in the last column gives the full treatment, the sources and the worked example. Rows that combine two or more terms with a slash define all of them, because the difference between them is the point. Where a term behaves differently on the **accommodation marketplace** (properties, partners, room types and rate plans) and the **hosted-stays marketplace** (homes, hosts and one calendar per unit), the definition says how. Corvane, named in a few rows and in the last section, is the fictional travel group this book follows ([Meet Corvane: Two Marketplaces, One Group](#meet-corvane)); its names and numbers are illustrations, not facts about any real company.

| **Rate Plan Sprawl** | Properties carry dozens of rate plans and derived rates nobody can explain, and every new product adds another combination to cache, map and test. | [Overbooked: Sixteen Ways Stay Platforms Go Wrong](#where-stay-platforms-go-wrong), [Rooms, Rates and Restrictions: The ARI Data Model](#rates-and-availability-model) |

| **The Same Hotel Twice** | One property appears as several products from different suppliers or channels, with mismatched rooms, content and prices. | [Overbooked: Sixteen Ways Stay Platforms Go Wrong](#where-stay-platforms-go-wrong), [Describing a Stay: Content, Media and Property Mapping](#content-and-media) |

| **Lost at the Front Desk** | Reservations are sent to the property or host without acknowledgement or reconciliation, and guests arrive to "we have no booking for you". | [Overbooked: Sixteen Ways Stay Platforms Go Wrong](#where-stay-platforms-go-wrong), [The Life of a Reservation: State Machines, Delivery and Operations](#reservation-lifecycle) |
| **The Hotel-Shaped Home** | One supply model is forced onto both marketplaces: homes squeezed into room types and rate plans, or hotels flattened into one-unit calendars. | [Overbooked: Sixteen Ways Stay Platforms Go Wrong](#where-stay-platforms-go-wrong), [Hotels and Homes: Modeling What Is Being Sold](#property-and-listing-model) |
| **Pay-to-Rank Creep** | Commission levels, programs and boosts accumulate in ranking until guests stop trusting the order and regulators ask for an explanation. | [Overbooked: Sixteen Ways Stay Platforms Go Wrong](#where-stay-platforms-go-wrong), [Ordering the Results: Ranking for Guests, Partners and the Marketplace](#stays-ranking) |

| **Ledgerless Money** | Money moves between guests, the marketplace, properties, hosts and tax authorities without one ledger that can say who is owed what. | [Overbooked: Sixteen Ways Stay Platforms Go Wrong](#where-stay-platforms-go-wrong), [Paying the Supply Side: Payouts, Commissions and Settlement](#payouts-and-settlement) |
| **The Tax Patchwork** | VAT, tourist taxes and platform reporting are added market by market as special cases, and nobody can say what was collected for whom. | [Overbooked: Sixteen Ways Stay Platforms Go Wrong](#where-stay-platforms-go-wrong), [The Tax Patchwork: VAT, Tourist Taxes and Platform Reporting](#taxes-on-stays) |
| **Trust After the Incident** | Verification, screening and protection arrive as reactions to incidents and headlines, each a separate tool with its own data. | [Overbooked: Sixteen Ways Stay Platforms Go Wrong](#where-stay-platforms-go-wrong), [Trust as a Platform: Trust and Safety for Stays](#stays-trust-and-safety) |

## Part I — Marketplace

The business vocabulary: which kind of accommodation marketplace this is, how it makes money and how it is decomposed. [Two Marketplaces for a Night's Stay: OTAs, Hosted Stays and How They Grew](#two-stay-marketplaces) owns the marketplace types, intermediaries and money roles; [Liquidity, Take Rates and Look-to-Book: The Economics of Stays Marketplaces](#stays-marketplace-economics) owns take rate, look-to-book and liquidity; [Mapping the Business: Capability Maps for Two Marketplaces](#stays-capability-map) and [Drawing the Lines: Bounded Contexts for Stays](#stays-bounded-contexts) own the two decompositions and the offer and reservation as aggregates.

| Term | Definition | Chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Accommodation marketplace (OTA-style marketplace)** | A platform where guests search, compare and book stays from many independently operated, mostly professional properties, and the reservation is passed to the property to fulfil. Supply is described as property, room type and rate plan, and the property's own systems usually assert availability and price, which the marketplace copies in. | [Two Marketplaces for a Night's Stay: OTAs, Hosted Stays and How They Grew](#two-stay-marketplaces) |
| **Online travel agency (OTA)** | The industry's historical name for the accommodation-marketplace type. It says "agency" even when the OTA acts as merchant of record, which is why the book names the money roles separately. | [Two Marketplaces for a Night's Stay: OTAs, Hosted Stays and How They Grew](#two-stay-marketplaces) |
| **Hosted-stays marketplace** | A platform where individuals and professional managers offer homes, apartments and private rooms, usually one unit with one calendar per listing. The platform holds the calendar, collects the guest's payment, and runs the verification, reviews and protection that make a stay with a stranger acceptable. | [Two Marketplaces for a Night's Stay: OTAs, Hosted Stays and How They Grew](#two-stay-marketplaces) |
| **Vacation-rental marketplace** | The older, narrower relative of hosted stays: a platform for whole holiday homes, historically run like classifieds where the owner paid to advertise and the guest paid the owner directly. Today it mostly names a supply segment (whole homes, weekly stays, changeover days, damage deposits) rather than a distinct business model. | [Two Marketplaces for a Night's Stay: OTAs, Hosted Stays and How They Grew](#two-stay-marketplaces) |
| **Metasearch** | A price-comparison engine that queries OTAs, hotel sites and other sellers for the same stay, shows prices side by side and sends the guest elsewhere to book. It holds the guest's attention but not the booking, and earns mainly per click or referral. | [Two Marketplaces for a Night's Stay: OTAs, Hosted Stays and How They Grew](#two-stay-marketplaces) |
| **Bedbank (wholesaler)** | A business-to-business intermediary that contracts hotel rates, usually net rates the reseller marks up, and resells them through APIs to travel agents, tour operators, OTAs and other distributors. It never meets the guest. | [Two Marketplaces for a Night's Stay: OTAs, Hosted Stays and How They Grew](#two-stay-marketplaces) |
| **Direct booking** | A guest booking with the property or host itself, without a marketplace: the hotel's own site and booking engine, call center or front desk, or a host's own site. It is the marketplace's competitor and, for many hotels, its benchmark for what commission is worth. | [Two Marketplaces for a Night's Stay: OTAs, Hosted Stays and How They Grew](#two-stay-marketplaces) |
| **Global distribution system (GDS)** | The network that connects travel agents to airlines, hotels and car rental companies (for example Sabre, Amadeus, Travelport). A GDS holds no hotel inventory of its own; it routes requests to the hotel's reservation system. | [Two Marketplaces for a Night's Stay: OTAs, Hosted Stays and How They Grew](#two-stay-marketplaces) |
| **Professional versus individual supply** | The axis the book prefers to building type: who operates the supply and with what systems. Professional operators (chains, independents, property managers) connect by API like hotels; individual hosts type calendars in by hand or sync by iCal, and need platform-supplied trust and protection. | [Two Marketplaces for a Night's Stay: OTAs, Hosted Stays and How They Grew](#two-stay-marketplaces) |
| **Room night** | One room or home occupied for one night, the unit in which accommodation volume is counted. A reservation of two rooms for three nights is six room nights; hosted-stays marketplaces usually count nights per listing, since one listing is one unit. | [Liquidity, Take Rates and Look-to-Book: The Economics of Stays Marketplaces](#stays-marketplace-economics) |
| **Take rate** | The marketplace's revenue from stays divided by the gross booking value of those stays over the same period. The book requires stating whether advertising is in the numerator, whether the denominator is gross or stayed, and that revenue is usually recognized at check-in, not at booking. | [Liquidity, Take Rates and Look-to-Book: The Economics of Stays Marketplaces](#stays-marketplace-economics) |
| **Gross booking value (GBV)** | The total value of bookings made, including the partner's or host's share. It can be measured gross (at booking) or stayed (after cancellations), and the definition must say whether taxes, cleaning fees and guest service fees are included. | [Liquidity, Take Rates and Look-to-Book: The Economics of Stays Marketplaces](#stays-marketplace-economics) |
| **Look-to-book ratio** | The number of shopping requests a marketplace serves per reservation made. Units must be stated: what counts as a request (a page versus each property lookup), whether the denominator is gross or stayed reservations, and which channel. It sizes the read path; metasearch and affiliate traffic sit an order of magnitude above a marketplace's own apps. | [Liquidity, Take Rates and Look-to-Book: The Economics of Stays Marketplaces](#stays-marketplace-economics) |
| **Booking window (lead time)** | The time between making a reservation and the first night of the stay, reported as a distribution (median and 90th percentile) rather than an average. It sets the availability horizon, cache refresh priorities, how long money is held, experiment length and data retention. | [Liquidity, Take Rates and Look-to-Book: The Economics of Stays Marketplaces](#stays-marketplace-economics) |
| **Local liquidity** | The likelihood that a guest searching a given cell finds and books a suitable stay, and that supply offered in that cell is booked. A cell is destination, dates, party, price band and lead time, so the same city can be liquid for hotels and thin for homes, or the reverse, depending on the party. | [Liquidity, Take Rates and Look-to-Book: The Economics of Stays Marketplaces](#stays-marketplace-economics) |
| **Cross-side network effects** | The effect where more guests make a marketplace more attractive to supply and more supply attracts guests. In stays they leak because hotels and many hosts sell on every channel and guests compare through metasearch. | [Liquidity, Take Rates and Look-to-Book: The Economics of Stays Marketplaces](#stays-marketplace-economics) |
| **Multi-homing** | Selling or searching on several platforms at once. Professional supply multi-homes through a channel manager by design; hosts multi-home less well, often by iCal alone, which brings double-booking risk. | [Liquidity, Take Rates and Look-to-Book: The Economics of Stays Marketplaces](#stays-marketplace-economics) |
| **Bookable nights / availability coverage** | Bookable nights are nights that are open, priced and not blocked by restrictions within the horizon. Availability coverage is bookable nights as a share of the nights a property or listing could sell. | [Liquidity, Take Rates and Look-to-Book: The Economics of Stays Marketplaces](#stays-marketplace-economics) |
| **Host acceptance rate** | The share of booking requests that hosts accept on request-to-book listings, where the host has a set time to respond. Each declined request is a search session that ended without a booking. | [Liquidity, Take Rates and Look-to-Book: The Economics of Stays Marketplaces](#stays-marketplace-economics) |
| **Contribution per stayed reservation** | Revenue per stayed reservation minus marketing and search costs. The book uses it, not revenue, to judge whether the cost of search and distribution is affordable. | [Liquidity, Take Rates and Look-to-Book: The Economics of Stays Marketplaces](#stays-marketplace-economics) |
| **Business capability** | Something the business is able to do, named without saying how, where or by whom (for example room and rate selection). It is not a system, a team or a product. | [Mapping the Business: Capability Maps for Two Marketplaces](#stays-capability-map) |
| **Capability map** | The structured set of an organization's capabilities, usually in two or three levels. It is useful when stable against org changes, free of implementation names and comparable across business units. | [Mapping the Business: Capability Maps for Two Marketplaces](#stays-capability-map) |
| **Reference model** | A generic capability map for a type of business, written so a real organization can compare itself against it. It is a starting hypothesis, not a description of anyone. | [Mapping the Business: Capability Maps for Two Marketplaces](#stays-capability-map) |
| **Overlap matrix** | A table that lists two capability maps side by side and classifies each pair as the same capability, a partial overlap, or one that only one side has. Matching must be by meaning, not by name. | [Mapping the Business: Capability Maps for Two Marketplaces](#stays-capability-map) |
| **Shared versus specific capability** | A shared capability is implemented once by the group and used by both marketplaces; a specific capability is implemented separately by each, because only one needs it or their needs differ too much. The book's rule scores differentiation, divergence and duplication per capability. | [Mapping the Business: Capability Maps for Two Marketplaces](#stays-capability-map) |
| **Shared contract** | A third grade between shared and specific: both marketplaces agree on the meaning and shape of a thing (an offer, a reservation, a cancellation outcome) but keep separate engines that produce it. | [Mapping the Business: Capability Maps for Two Marketplaces](#stays-capability-map) |
| **Shared engine** | One implementation used by both marketplaces, typically with per-brand configuration, chosen for capabilities that mean the same for hotels and homes, such as identity, tax calculation and notification delivery. | [Mapping the Business: Capability Maps for Two Marketplaces](#stays-capability-map) |
| **Brand-specific capability** | A capability each marketplace keeps for itself, because its meaning differs between hotels and homes (for example counted inventory versus a unit calendar) or it is core to one brand. | [Mapping the Business: Capability Maps for Two Marketplaces](#stays-capability-map) |
| **Core / supporting / generic (subdomain grades)** | Three grades of strategic value: core is why guests or suppliers choose you, supporting is necessary and specific but not a reason to choose you, generic is needed by everyone and can be bought. Scored per brand. | [Mapping the Business: Capability Maps for Two Marketplaces](#stays-capability-map), [Drawing the Lines: Bounded Contexts for Stays](#stays-bounded-contexts) |
| **Domain-driven design (DDD) domain / subdomain** | The domain is the area of business the software serves; a subdomain is a part of it with its own concerns, such as inventory, payments or trust. Subdomains are classed as core, supporting or generic. | [Drawing the Lines: Bounded Contexts for Stays](#stays-bounded-contexts) |
| **Bounded context** | The boundary inside which one model and its vocabulary apply consistently, with explicit relationships to other contexts. In the book's rule, one team owns one or more contexts, each context has exactly one owning team, and a context contains one or more services. | [Drawing the Lines: Bounded Contexts for Stays](#stays-bounded-contexts) |
| **Ubiquitous language** | The vocabulary used consistently inside one context, in conversation, documents and code. It is ubiquitous within the context, not across the company, so "night" or "booking" may legitimately differ between contexts. | [Drawing the Lines: Bounded Contexts for Stays](#stays-bounded-contexts) |
| **Context map** | A diagram of the contexts and the relationships between them: who is upstream, who adapts to whom, and where translation happens. | [Drawing the Lines: Bounded Contexts for Stays](#stays-bounded-contexts) |
| **Aggregate** | A cluster of domain objects treated as a single unit and the boundary of a transaction: invariants inside one aggregate hold immediately, and invariants across aggregates hold eventually through events and compensation. | [Drawing the Lines: Bounded Contexts for Stays](#stays-bounded-contexts) |
| **Domain event** | A record that something meaningful happened in a context, such as a reservation being confirmed, published so other contexts can react. | [Drawing the Lines: Bounded Contexts for Stays](#stays-bounded-contexts) |
| **Offer** | A priced, conditioned promise to sell a specific stay to a specific party, valid for a limited time: what is sold, price lines, conditions, payment arrangement, and provenance and validity. It is computed, immutable and short-lived; a changed input yields a new offer. | [Drawing the Lines: Bounded Contexts for Stays](#stays-bounded-contexts) |
| **Reservation aggregate** | The agreement that results from accepting an offer: identity, versions, frozen terms per version, state, parties and unit assignment. It excludes inventory decrement, payment capture, payout and commission invoice, which belong to other contexts and react to reservation events. | [Drawing the Lines: Bounded Contexts for Stays](#stays-bounded-contexts) |
| **Saga across aggregates** | A booking made as a sequence of steps across several aggregates (claim inventory, authorize payment, confirm with supplier or host, confirm reservation), with a compensating action for each step that fails. | [Drawing the Lines: Bounded Contexts for Stays](#stays-bounded-contexts) |
| **Anti-corruption layer (ACL)** | A translator that keeps an upstream model from leaking into the downstream one. Examples are the connectivity gateway in front of channel managers, the iCal import (which enters as a claim, not a reservation), and a translator in front of a monolith. | [Drawing the Lines: Bounded Contexts for Stays](#stays-bounded-contexts) |
| **Open host service / published language** | An open host service is one well-defined protocol offered by an upstream context to many consumers. A published language is a documented shared language for exchange, such as OpenTravel messages or the group's offer and reservation contracts. | [Drawing the Lines: Bounded Contexts for Stays](#stays-bounded-contexts) |
| **Customer-supplier / conformist** | Two upstream-downstream relationships. In customer-supplier the upstream team plans for the downstream's needs; in conformist the downstream adopts the upstream model as it is. | [Drawing the Lines: Bounded Contexts for Stays](#stays-bounded-contexts) |
| **Shared kernel** | A small part of the model co-owned by two teams, legitimate only if small, stable and co-owned by teams that talk weekly (identifier formats, money and date-range types). A shared supply model or a shared reservation table is the case to avoid. | [Drawing the Lines: Bounded Contexts for Stays](#stays-bounded-contexts) |

## Part II — Supply

Properties, homes and the partners behind them. [Hotels and Homes: Modeling What Is Being Sold](#property-and-listing-model) owns property, room type, unit and listing; [From Sign-Up to First Booking: Partner and Host Onboarding](#partner-onboarding) owns activation and verification; [Describing a Stay: Content, Media and Property Mapping](#content-and-media) owns mapping and content quality; [Plugging Into the Hotel Stack: Connectivity, Channel Managers and Standards](#connectivity-and-channel-managers) and [One Home, Many Calendars: iCal, APIs and Multichannel Hosts](#calendar-sync) own the two integration models; [Running the Supply Side: Extranets, Host Tools and Operations](#extranets-and-host-tools) owns the partner and host workspace.

| Term | Definition | Chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Property** | A place operated as one accommodation business at one address or site: a hotel, hostel, aparthotel, building of managed apartments or single house. It has an operator, a location, a jurisdiction and property-wide policies. | [Hotels and Homes: Modeling What Is Being Sold](#property-and-listing-model) |
| **Room type** | A category of units that a property treats as interchangeable for sale, with a unit count, a description and an occupancy. A room type promises a kind of room; the specific room is often assigned at check-in. | [Hotels and Homes: Modeling What Is Being Sold](#property-and-listing-model) |
| **Unit** | The smallest space sold to one party for exclusive use on a night: a hotel room, an apartment, a whole house, a private room in a home or a dormitory bed. It has its own occupancy limit and, where regulation requires one, its own registration number. | [Hotels and Homes: Modeling What Is Being Sold](#property-and-listing-model) |
| **Listing (stays)** | A published offer of one unit, or a small pool of identical units, on one hosted-stays marketplace, with host-authored content, one calendar, one price set and one cancellation policy. The accommodation marketplace has no listing; it sells room types. | [Hotels and Homes: Modeling What Is Being Sold](#property-and-listing-model) |
| **Multi-unit listing** | A listing that publishes a small pool of identical units with one calendar counting free units per night. It is a room type in all but name and still has to record which unit each guest received. | [Hotels and Homes: Modeling What Is Being Sold](#property-and-listing-model) |
| **Physical layer / commercial layer** | The physical layer describes places that exist whether or not anyone sells them (property, unit, spaces, beds); the commercial layer describes how a marketplace offers them (room types, rate plans, listings). Most modeling errors put an attribute on the wrong layer. | [Hotels and Homes: Modeling What Is Being Sold](#property-and-listing-model) |
| **Physical inventory / sellable inventory** | Physical inventory is a fact about the building (84 rooms). Sellable inventory is a commercial decision per room type and night, which may be lower (rooms held out) or higher (planned overbooking); storing both in one field corrupts both. | [Hotels and Homes: Modeling What Is Being Sold](#property-and-listing-model) |
| **Pool versus unit-level selling** | A room type sells a pool counted as a number per night; a listing sells one specific unit with a free-or-taken flag. The difference decides how inventory is counted, how a guest is made whole and what can be reported to a regulator. | [Hotels and Homes: Modeling What Is Being Sold](#property-and-listing-model) |
| **Partner / host** | The partner is the party behind a property on an accommodation marketplace; the host is the party behind a listing on a hosted-stays marketplace. Onboarding models the party, the supply unit, the authority to sell and the payout account as four separate records. | [From Sign-Up to First Booking: Partner and Host Onboarding](#partner-onboarding) |
| **Activation** | The decision, made by evaluating readiness checks, that a property or listing is bookable. It has a state and can go backwards, for example when a registration number lapses or a content change fails a check. | [From Sign-Up to First Booking: Partner and Host Onboarding](#partner-onboarding) |
| **Readiness check** | A named, machine-evaluable condition that must hold for a supply unit to be bookable, such as a signed contract or a valid registration number. Each check has an owner, a severity (blocking or advisory), a scope and a version. | [From Sign-Up to First Booking: Partner and Host Onboarding](#partner-onboarding) |
| **Know your business (KYB) for partners** | Verification of the legal party behind supply: the entity exists and is active in a trade register, the signatory is authorized, and beneficial owners are identified and screened. It is the core check on the accommodation side and for professional managers on both. | [From Sign-Up to First Booking: Partner and Host Onboarding](#partner-onboarding) |
| **Know your customer (KYC)** | Identity verification of a natural person: document, likeness, address and sanctions screening. On hosted-stays marketplaces it applies to nearly every host. | [From Sign-Up to First Booking: Partner and Host Onboarding](#partner-onboarding) |
| **Right to sell** | The authority to let a unit for short stays here and now, separate from identity. For hotels it is mostly the hotel license; for homes it is increasingly a registration number issued by a public authority. | [From Sign-Up to First Booking: Partner and Host Onboarding](#partner-onboarding) |
| **Registration number (short-term rental)** | A public-authority identifier for a rental unit. The EU Short-Term Rental Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2024/1028, applying from 20 May 2026) requires platforms to obtain it before a listing is offered, display it and randomly check it. | [From Sign-Up to First Booking: Partner and Host Onboarding](#partner-onboarding) |
| **Trader declaration** | A recorded statement of whether a host acts in a professional capacity. | **Turnover task** | A unit of on-property work derived from reservations (cleaning, linen, inspection, key or code handover) in the window between one check-out and the next check-in. | Term | Definition | Chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Availability, rates and inventory (ARI)** | The data a supplier sends a marketplace about what can be sold: how many units of a room type are free on each night, at what prices, under which restrictions. In this book most of what looks like ARI data is really ARI rules. On hosted-stays marketplaces the equivalent is one calendar per unit with a nightly price and day rules. | [Rooms, Rates and Restrictions: The ARI Data Model](#rates-and-availability-model) |
| **Inventory / availability** | Inventory is the number of sellable units of a room type on a night. Availability is what a guest can actually book for given dates, occupancy and conditions after restrictions are applied. | [Rooms, Rates and Restrictions: The ARI Data Model](#rates-and-availability-model) |
| **Rate plan / product** | A rate plan is a named commercial offer: a pricing logic plus conditions such as cancellation, meal plan and payment terms. A product is a room type combined with a rate plan, the thing ARI prices and restricts. Every rate plan of a room type sells from the same inventory pool. | [Rooms, Rates and Restrictions: The ARI Data Model](#rates-and-availability-model) |
| **Pricing type** | The rule for what one stored price means: standard (price at maximum occupancy), derived occupancy (base plus offsets), occupancy-based (one price per guest count) or length-of-stay. | **Length-of-stay (LOS) pricing** | A price stored per arrival date and stay length, so the price of a stay is no longer the sum of its nightly prices. It multiplies the key space of a cache and forces tiering. | [Rooms, Rates and Restrictions: The ARI Data Model](#rates-and-availability-model) |
| **Derived rate** | A rate plan whose price, and often its restrictions and open or closed status, follows a parent plan through a rule such as a percentage offset. | **Best available rate (BAR)** | The base public rate sold to unqualified guests, which a hotel revenue management system sets and from which most other rates are fixed offsets. | **Restriction** | A rule that removes availability for some stays without changing inventory, for example minimum or maximum stay, closed to arrival (CTA), closed to departure (CTD), stop-sell or advance purchase. | **Minimum stay (MinLOS / MaxLOS)** | The fewest or most nights a stay may have. A stay-through rule applies to every night the stay includes; an on-arrival rule applies only to stays arriving that day. | [Rooms, Rates and Restrictions: The ARI Data Model](#rates-and-availability-model) |
| **Gap night** | A short gap, often one or two nights, between two bookings that a minimum-stay rule can leave unsellable. Host pricing tools discount such gaps. Hosts also use restrictions as operational controls (for example a cleaner's schedule), while hotels use them as revenue controls. | [Rooms, Rates and Restrictions: The ARI Data Model](#rates-and-availability-model) |
| **Inclusion flag / fee basis** | Metadata stored with each amount saying whether it includes VAT, city tax or mandatory fees, and how each fee is charged (per stay, per night, per person per night or percentage). Without them each surface guesses and prices drift. | [Rooms, Rates and Restrictions: The ARI Data Model](#rates-and-availability-model) |
| **Stay evaluator** | The single versioned component that turns stored facts (inventory, prices, restrictions), rules, fees and policies into an answer for one stay: bookable or not, and at what price. | [Rooms, Rates and Restrictions: The ARI Data Model](#rates-and-availability-model) |
| **Availability engine** | The read model that answers "what can I book, and for how much" at search scale, from precomputed offers with an explicit staleness budget, falling back to on-demand computation. On hosted-stays marketplaces the hard part shifts from combinatorics to confidence that a free night is really free. | [Is It Still Available? Availability Search, Caching and Price Accuracy](#availability-engine) |
| **Availability query / offer** | A query names candidates, a stay and a party, plus audience and display currency. The answer is an offer: a priced, conditioned promise to sell a specific product for a specific stay to a specific party, valid for a limited time. | [Is It Still Available? Availability Search, Caching and Price Accuracy](#availability-engine) |
| **Offer computation / offer kernel** | Offer computation turns ARI into offers: per-night eligibility, stay-level restrictions, occupancy fit, discounts, fees, taxes and currency. Availability Search, Caching and Price Accuracy](#availability-engine) |
| **Card price** | The price a property shows on a search result card, usually the cheapest or best qualifying room-rate for the stay. | [Is It Still Available? Availability Search, Caching and Price Accuracy](#availability-engine) |
| **Price accuracy** | The probability that a price shown at an earlier step (search card, property page, checkout, statement) equals the price charged at the final step for the same stay, party and conditions, measured on a sample. Metasearch engines grade partners on it. | [Is It Still Available? Availability Search, Caching and Price Accuracy](#availability-engine) |
| **Cache staleness** | How far a served value is from the system of record. Age-based staleness is time since computation; divergence-based staleness is the probability the value differs from what the source would answer now. Age is an engineering metric and divergence is a business metric. | [Is It Still Available? Availability Search, Caching and Price Accuracy](#availability-engine) |
| **Staleness budget** | A written limit, per tier (search card, property page, metasearch feed), on how stale a served offer may be and what the price check does when the limit is breached. | [Is It Still Available? Availability Search, Caching and Price Accuracy](#availability-engine) |
| **Price check** | A re-computation of the selected offer from the system of record, with the offer kernel, when the guest moves to pay. It is the point where an offer becomes binding. | [Is It Still Available? Availability Search, Caching and Price Accuracy](#availability-engine) |
| **Cell (precomputed grid)** | The unit an availability grid stores and the key it is stored under, for example property, arrival date, stay length and party. Each key dimension multiplies the grid; dimensions left out must be computed at read time. | [Is It Still Available? Availability Search, Caching and Price Accuracy](#availability-engine) |
| **Occupancy-based pricing (OBP)** | A pricing type with a separate price for each number of guests. | [Rooms, Rates and Restrictions: The ARI Data Model](#rates-and-availability-model), [Is It Still Available? Availability Search, Caching and Price Accuracy](#availability-engine) |
| **Physical, sellable and available inventory** | Physical inventory is the units that exist on a night. Sellable inventory is the count a supplier offers a channel after closures, and can exceed physical inventory when a hotel overbooks on purpose. Availability is what a specific search can book after restrictions. | [Hotels and Homes: Modeling What Is Being Sold](#property-and-listing-model), [Never Sell the Same Night Twice: Overbooking, Allotments and Concurrency](#inventory-integrity) |
| **Allotment** | A block of rooms a hotel commits to one channel for a period, with unsold rooms returned on a release date before arrival. It is a scheduled obligation, not just a number, and is rare outside wholesale, tour operating and groups. | [Never Sell the Same Night Twice: Overbooking, Allotments and Concurrency](#inventory-integrity) |
| **Free sale** | A channel may sell up to the shared count without asking the hotel before each booking, each booking decrementing the count. Some wholesale contracts use the term differently, so the contract governs. | [Never Sell the Same Night Twice: Overbooking, Allotments and Concurrency](#inventory-integrity) |

Overbooking has an owner and a reason; the other two are defects. | [Never Sell the Same Night Twice: Overbooking, Allotments and Concurrency](#inventory-integrity) |

A home has no corridor of identical rooms to walk a guest into. | [Never Sell the Same Night Twice: Overbooking, Allotments and Concurrency](#inventory-integrity) |
| **Auto-replenishment** | A marketplace default that puts a canceled room back on sale, which can resell a room the hotel already resold or closed. | [Never Sell the Same Night Twice: Overbooking, Allotments and Concurrency](#inventory-integrity) |
| **Unit cardinality** | The number of identical units behind a listing or room type. | **Comp set (competitive set)** | The competing properties or listings a price is judged against. | **Holdout** | A standing random group of listings priced by the previous model version so the current model is always compared with something. | [Setting the Price of a Night: Revenue Management and Smart Pricing](#dynamic-pricing) |
| **Promotion / discount** | A promotion is a time- or condition-bound reduction of a published price, created by a party that agrees to fund it. | **Funder (partner-, marketplace- and co-funded)** | The party that bears a discount. Every discount line carries its funder to the ledger. | [Deals, Members and Programs: Promotions and Loyalty](#promotions-and-loyalty) |
| **Combination class** | The attribute of a promotion that says what it may stack with. Discounts do not commute once caps and fixed amounts are involved, so composition order is a contract. | [Deals, Members and Programs: Promotions and Loyalty](#promotions-and-loyalty) |
| **Closed user group (CUG) / conditional rate** | A CUG is an audience that must identify itself to see a price, such as signed-in members, app users or a corporate account. | **Loyalty program / loyalty currency** | A loyalty program recognizes repeat guests with tiers, benefits or a currency. | **Partner program** | A commercial arrangement in which a property or host gives something (higher commission, discounts, quality commitments) for something (a badge, a ranking boost, marketing spend). | **Rate parity (wide / narrow)** | A contractual commitment to offer a platform rates and conditions at least as good as elsewhere. | **Redemption / incrementality** | A redemption is a booking that used a discount. Incrementality is the share of redemptions that would not have happened without it. | [Deals, Members and Programs: Promotions and Loyalty](#promotions-and-loyalty) |

| **Allocation problem (party)** | The multi-room search task of finding combinations of room types whose occupancies cover the party, each with an open rate plan for the whole stay, and pricing each combination. | **Federated query** | A cross-brand search in which the API sends the same stay query to both marketplaces' engines and shows the second brand's results in a separate, labeled section rather than merging them into one ranked list, because the rankers' scores are not calibrated against each other. | [Searching Dates, Not Documents: Search Architecture for Stays](#stays-search-architecture) |
| **Place / destination** | A place is a named geographic entity with a stable identifier, geometry, multilingual names and relationships to other places. A destination is a place that guests search for and the marketplace sells; all destinations are places, but not all places are destinations. | [Where Exactly? Destinations, Geo and Map Search](#destinations-and-maps) |
| **Destination graph** | The versioned dataset of places, their geometries, names and relationships, read by search, landing pages, pricing, tax and regulation. Edges include contains, part of, served by, alias of and supersedes. | [Where Exactly? Destinations, Geo and Map Search](#destinations-and-maps) |
| **Place resolution** | Turning a guest's input (a typed string, an autocomplete pick or a deep link) into a place identifier, resolving ambiguity on the way. | [Where Exactly? Destinations, Geo and Map Search](#destinations-and-maps) |
| **Search area** | The geographic region the marketplace actually searches for a resolved place. It often differs from the place's official boundary, for example by adding resort or airport areas or leaving out low-demand districts. | [Where Exactly? Destinations, Geo and Map Search](#destinations-and-maps) |
| **Viewport search** | A query whose geographic constraint is the rectangle of map the guest is looking at, with the guest's dates, party size and filters attached. Every pin carries a price that depends on those dates. | [Where Exactly? Destinations, Geo and Map Search](#destinations-and-maps) |
| **S2 cells / H3 cells** | Hierarchical grids for spatial indexing. S2 cells nest exactly, so the book recommends them for retrieval terms and search areas; H3 hexagons smooth well, so the book recommends them for analytics, features and clusters. | [Where Exactly? Destinations, Geo and Map Search](#destinations-and-maps) |
| **Geocoding / reverse geocoding** | Geocoding converts an address to coordinates; reverse geocoding converts coordinates to an address or place. For hosted stays, city, region and country should be derived from the geocoded point and the destination graph, never typed by the host. | [Where Exactly? Destinations, Geo and Map Search](#destinations-and-maps) |
| **Approximate location** | The location shown to guests before booking, made of a public point derived from the exact point plus a display radius. Pins, clusters, distance labels and the property-page circle are all computed from it. Hotels publish their address; homes usually do not. | [Where Exactly? Destinations, Geo and Map Search](#destinations-and-maps) |
| **Public point** | The coordinate derived from a home's exact point and used for everything visible before booking. The search index for hosted stays holds only the public point. | [Where Exactly? Destinations, Geo and Map Search](#destinations-and-maps) |
| **Ranking** | In EU consumer law (Omnibus Directive (EU) 2019/2161), the relative prominence given to products as presented, organized or communicated by the trader, by any technological means. Badges, carousels, map pins and landing-page pins count, not only the sort order. | [Ordering the Results: Ranking for Guests, Partners and the Marketplace](#stays-ranking) |
| **Learning to rank (stays)** | Training a model to order priced offers by the estimated probability of an honored stay, with logged features and outcomes. The book recommends a gradient-boosted ranker with a LambdaMART-style objective and a multi-task target. | [Ordering the Results: Ranking for Guests, Partners and the Marketplace](#stays-ranking) |
| **Net booking** | A booking minus its cancellation, used as a ranking label. It rewards bookings that survive, but the label arrives weeks or months later. | [Ordering the Results: Ranking for Guests, Partners and the Marketplace](#stays-ranking) |
| **Relevance model** | The model that estimates, for one search and one candidate offer, how likely the guest is to complete a stay with it. It is kept apart from commercial rules. | [Ordering the Results: Ranking for Guests, Partners and the Marketplace](#stays-ranking) |
| **Ranking policy** | The explicit, versioned rules applied to the relevance model's scores: commercial adjustments, reliability and trust demotions, diversity and exploration. | **Program-driven visibility** | Exposure a partner gets by joining a program, usually in exchange for a higher commission or a funded discount. Its effect on position must be bounded and disclosed. | [Ordering the Results: Ranking for Guests, Partners and the Marketplace](#stays-ranking) |
| **Ranking transparency** | The ability to describe the main ranking parameters, their relative importance and the effect of payment, both in general and for one position. In the book's example it is a multiplier applied for a fixed period. | [Ordering the Results: Ranking for Guests, Partners and the Marketplace](#stays-ranking) |
| **Trip intent** | The marketplace's current estimate of a trip a guest is planning: where, when, who, what kind of stay and roughly what budget. | **Wishlist** | A guest-owned, named list of specific properties or homes, optionally bound to dates and a party and shareable with co-travelers. It is a commitment the marketplace re-evaluates against live supply, not a bookmark. | [Remembering the Traveler: Personalization, Recommendations and Saved Intent](#personalization-and-recommendations) |
| **Price alert (watch)** | A standing request to notify the guest when the bookable price for a defined stay changes in a way they care about. In practice a watch has a target, a condition, a frequency cap and an expiry. | [Remembering the Traveler: Personalization, Recommendations and Saved Intent](#personalization-and-recommendations) |
| **Listing embedding / guest embedding** | A listing embedding is a learned vector that places substitutable properties or homes close together, learned from session behavior (in the style of word2vec) and from content. A guest embedding does the same for a guest's history. | [Remembering the Traveler: Personalization, Recommendations and Saved Intent](#personalization-and-recommendations) |
| **Continuous cold start** | The problem, named by Booking.com researchers in 2015, that frequent visitors look new because they book rarely, their interests shift and they switch personas, so history is thin or misleading. | [Remembering the Traveler: Personalization, Recommendations and Saved Intent](#personalization-and-recommendations) |
| **Recommendation surface** | Any place where the marketplace proposes stays or destinations without a complete query, each with its own context and failure cost. Under the Digital Services Act, recommender systems must explain their main parameters. | [Remembering the Traveler: Personalization, Recommendations and Saved Intent](#personalization-and-recommendations) |
| **Profiling (GDPR)** | Automated evaluation of personal aspects such as preferences (GDPR Art. 4(4)). Guests may object to profiling for direct marketing (Art. 21), so the book keeps a non-profiling path for every recommender. | [Remembering the Traveler: Personalization, Recommendations and Saved Intent](#personalization-and-recommendations) |
| **Proximity features** | Features computed per coarse location bucket (about 1,000 users, derived from IP address and consent-aware) instead of per user, so cold-start traffic can be personalized without identity. | [Remembering the Traveler: Personalization, Recommendations and Saved Intent](#personalization-and-recommendations) |
| **Property detail page / listing page** | The page for one property (OTA) or home (hosted stays), usually opened from search with dates and party already set. | [The Moment of Decision: Property Pages, Room Selection and Total Price](#property-page-and-room-selection) |
| **Property detail assembly** | How the property page is built from many sources, such as content, offers, reviews and trust evidence, by fragments owned by different teams around one quote. | [The Moment of Decision: Property Pages, Room Selection and Total Price](#property-page-and-room-selection) |
| **Quote / price breakdown** | A quote is a priced offer with an identity: quote ID, itemized price breakdown, the inputs it was computed from, and a validity window. It makes "the price you saw" a fact that later steps can check. | [The Moment of Decision: Property Pages, Room Selection and Total Price](#property-page-and-room-selection) |
| **Total price display** | Composing and showing the price so that the total, including all mandatory charges, holds from search to checkout, with the headline price equal or close to the total. | [The Moment of Decision: Property Pages, Room Selection and Total Price](#property-page-and-room-selection) |
| **Headline price** | The most prominent price number on a surface. Regulation in several markets requires it to equal, or be close to, the total price. | [The Moment of Decision: Property Pages, Room Selection and Total Price](#property-page-and-room-selection) |
| **Drip pricing** | Showing a lower price first and adding unavoidable charges later in the purchase flow. The European Commission's 2021 UCPD guidance treats it as unfair; the UK bans it under the DMCC Act 2024 (consumer provisions from April 2025); the US FTC fees rule took effect 12 May 2025. | [The Moment of Decision: Property Pages, Room Selection and Total Price](#property-page-and-room-selection) |
| **Display policy** | A versioned, data-driven component that takes a quote and a context (market, surface, device, guest state) and decides which lines enter the headline, per-stay or per-night framing, wording, rounding and what to show when the quote is not exact. | [The Moment of Decision: Property Pages, Room Selection and Total Price](#property-page-and-room-selection) |
| **Payable at property** | A price line the guest pays at the property, such as city tax or a resort fee, rather than at booking. Display policy in the EU and UK still includes it in the headline total. | [The Moment of Decision: Property Pages, Room Selection and Total Price](#property-page-and-room-selection) |
| **Rate comparison** | How room and rate offers are enumerated, normalized into comparable fields (cancellation, payment timing, meal plan, occupancy), pruned and grouped so the rooms table helps the guest decide. | [The Moment of Decision: Property Pages, Room Selection and Total Price](#property-page-and-room-selection) |
| **Roomrate** | Booking.com's connectivity term for the sellable product: a unique combination of room type, rate plan and conditions. | [The Moment of Decision: Property Pages, Room Selection and Total Price](#property-page-and-room-selection) |
| **Dominated offer** | An offer for which another offer for the same room has equal or better conditions on every normalized field and a lower or equal total. Pruning removes it from the rooms table. | [The Moment of Decision: Property Pages, Room Selection and Total Price](#property-page-and-room-selection) |
| **Reference price ("was" price)** | A strikethrough comparison price, which is a claim that needs evidence. The book computes it from the platform's own sales history and stores the evidence. The Omnibus Directive's 30-day prior-price rule may not apply to accommodation. | [The Moment of Decision: Property Pages, Room Selection and Total Price](#property-page-and-room-selection) |
| **Trader status** | Whether a host or partner is a professional trader or a private peer. It determines which consumer rights apply and is required in the EU for professional hosts. | [The Moment of Decision: Property Pages, Room Selection and Total Price](#property-page-and-room-selection) |

Making and keeping the reservation. | **Facilitated payments** | The hosted-stays form of collection: the platform's payment entity collects the guest's payment as the host's limited payment collection agent, holds it until a trigger (usually check-in), deducts fees and pays out the rest. Economically close to the merchant model; legally framed as collecting on behalf of the host. | [Who Takes the Money? Merchant, Agency and Facilitator Models](#merchant-and-agency-models) |
| **Limited payment collection agent** | The legal role in which a hosted-stays platform's payment entity accepts the guest's funds on the host's behalf. Once the guest pays, the guest's debt to the host is extinguished and the platform owes the host, so the platform's ledger must say so. | [Who Takes the Money? Merchant, Agency and Facilitator Models](#merchant-and-agency-models) |
| **Merchant of record (MoR)** | The party the guest's payment is made to, who appears on the card statement and carries the card-scheme obligations for that payment: refunds, chargebacks and strong customer authentication. It answers "who did the guest pay?" and is a separate question from who supplies the stay. | [Who Takes the Money? Merchant, Agency and Facilitator Models](#merchant-and-agency-models), [Paying for a Future Night: Payment Orchestration for Stays](#stay-payments) |
| **Supplier of the stay** | The party that sells the accommodation itself, for VAT and revenue purposes. A marketplace can be merchant of record for the payment while the hotel or host remains the supplier, or it can resell the stay in its own name. | [Who Takes the Money? Merchant, Agency and Facilitator Models](#merchant-and-agency-models), [The Tax Patchwork: VAT, Tourist Taxes and Platform Reporting](#taxes-on-stays) |
| **Payment-model normalization** | Resolving, in one place, the payment arrangement of each booking (collector, collection schedule, supplier settlement method, commission direction, liability matrix) at quote time, freezing it on the reservation and versioning it on change, so no other system re-derives it. | [Who Takes the Money? Merchant, Agency and Facilitator Models](#merchant-and-agency-models) |
| **Payment arrangement record** | The per-offer, per-reservation-version record that makes the liability matrix concrete: model, collector, guarantee, supplier settlement, commission direction and who carries chargeback, fraud and currency risk. Checkout, ledger, issuing, invoicing and payouts all read it. | [Who Takes the Money? Merchant, Agency and Facilitator Models](#merchant-and-agency-models) |
| **Liability matrix** | A table that lists, for agency, merchant and facilitated models, who holds the money, who bears chargebacks, fraud and currency risk, who pays the supplier and who needs a license. Filled in before design work on any new payment option. | [Who Takes the Money? Merchant, Agency and Facilitator Models](#merchant-and-agency-models) |
| **Safeguarding** | The duty of a licensed payment or e-money institution to keep client money separate and protected, for example in bankruptcy-remote accounts. For architects it is a ledger requirement: the platform must show at any moment which funds belong to customers. | [Who Takes the Money? Merchant, Agency and Facilitator Models](#merchant-and-agency-models), [Closing the Books: Refunds, Chargebacks and Reconciliation](#refunds-and-reconciliation) |
| **Electronic money institution (EMI)** | A licensed institution that may issue electronic money and provide payment services, with safeguarding duties. A marketplace holds one, or uses a licensed partner, to collect and hold guest funds under the merchant or facilitated model. | [Who Takes the Money? Merchant, Agency and Facilitator Models](#merchant-and-agency-models) |
| **Payment Services Directive 2 (PSD2) commercial-agent exclusion** | Directive (EU) 2015/2366 excludes payments through a commercial agent acting for only the payer or only the payee. Collecting platforms rely on it by appointing themselves the host's agent, but regulators decide case by case, so large collectors hold licenses anyway. | [Who Takes the Money? Merchant, Agency and Facilitator Models](#merchant-and-agency-models) |
| **Principal / agent (accounting)** | Under IFRS 15 and ASC 606, an agent presents only its fee or margin as revenue and a principal that controls the service presents the full price. A merchant-model marketplace usually stays an agent, so the model changes the balance sheet, not the revenue line. | [Who Takes the Money? Merchant, Agency and Facilitator Models](#merchant-and-agency-models) |
| **Payment timing** | When the guest's money moves relative to booking and stay: pay now, pay at property, pay later online, pay in parts or third-party installments. It is a property of the offer, fixed on the reservation, and is a separate axis from the payment model. | [Paying for a Future Night: Payment Orchestration for Stays](#stay-payments) |
| **Deferred charge** | Any charge made after checkout without the guest present, under an agreement accepted at checkout: pay-later collections, balances, no-show and late-cancellation fees and post-stay damage charges. It can fail with nobody to fix it, so it needs retries and a deadline. | [Paying for a Future Night: Payment Orchestration for Stays](#stay-payments) |
| **Payment plan** | A versioned list of money obligations per booking (charges, holds, virtual cards, refunds), each with type, amount, currency, due date or trigger, instrument and state. Checkout creates it, modifications version it and one orchestration layer executes it. | [Paying for a Future Night: Payment Orchestration for Stays](#stay-payments) |
| **Virtual card (VCC)** | A single-use card number the marketplace issues per reservation, loads with the amount owed to the property and sends with the reservation; the hotel charges it at its own terminal. Parameters are activation, amount and expiry. | [Paying for a Future Night: Payment Orchestration for Stays](#stay-payments), [Paying the Supply Side: Payouts, Commissions and Settlement](#payouts-and-settlement) |
| **Payment orchestration (stays)** | The layer that turns a booking's payment obligations into payment service provider calls, in the right order, at the right time and idempotently, and reports the outcome. In stays the hard part is the right time. | [Paying for a Future Night: Payment Orchestration for Stays](#stay-payments) |
| **Customer-initiated transaction (CIT) / merchant-initiated transaction (MIT)** | A CIT involves the cardholder at the time. A MIT is started later by the merchant under a prior agreement and is generally outside SCA if the card was authenticated when saved. Later charges quote the network transaction identifier from the first CIT. | [Paying for a Future Night: Payment Orchestration for Stays](#stay-payments) |
| **Strong Customer Authentication (SCA)** | The PSD2 requirement (Directive (EU) 2015/2366, Art. 97) to authenticate with two of knowledge, possession and inherence, dynamically linked to amount and payee. Online card payments usually meet it with 3-D Secure. Most exemptions fit stays poorly. | [Paying for a Future Night: Payment Orchestration for Stays](#stay-payments) |
| **3-D Secure (3DS)** | The card issuer's authentication challenge inside the checkout flow, the usual way to apply SCA to online card payments. | [Paying for a Future Night: Payment Orchestration for Stays](#stay-payments) |
| **Network transaction identifier / scheme transaction reference** | A scheme-level reference returned by the first authenticated payment that proves the credential was set up for later charges. Each merchant-initiated charge quotes it; a checkout that fails to obtain it pushes later charges back into SCA. | [Paying for a Future Night: Payment Orchestration for Stays](#stay-payments) |
| **Payment service provider (PSP) / acquirer** | A PSP processes payments for the merchant through one API; an acquirer is the bank or processor that settles card transactions to the merchant. Orchestration may route across several of each. | [Paying for a Future Night: Payment Orchestration for Stays](#stay-payments) |
| **Presentment currency / payout currency** | The presentment currency is what the guest sees and pays; the payout currency is what the supplier receives. A third, listing or rate currency, is the one the host or hotel priced in. Each conversion has a rate, a time and an owner. | [Paying for a Future Night: Payment Orchestration for Stays](#stay-payments) |
| **Chargeback** | A reversal of a card payment initiated by the cardholder's issuer through the card network after a dispute. The merchant of record bears it. For future stays the dispute window can start on the stay date, so it can arrive after the host is paid. | [Paying for a Future Night: Payment Orchestration for Stays](#stay-payments), [Closing the Books: Refunds, Chargebacks and Reconciliation](#refunds-and-reconciliation) |
| **Payable / receivable** | A payable is an amount the marketplace owes a partner. A receivable is an amount a partner owes the marketplace: agency commission, cancellation fees, recoveries. The payable belongs to the booking, not to the payout. | [Paying the Supply Side: Payouts, Commissions and Settlement](#payouts-and-settlement) |
| **Settlement** | The process of determining, netting and discharging payables and receivables per partner, for a period or per booking. | [Paying the Supply Side: Payouts, Commissions and Settlement](#payouts-and-settlement) |
| **Settlement ledger** | The double-entry record of every payable and receivable per partner, at line level, with the event that caused each. It is a sub-ledger feeding the general ledger in summary and decides who is owed what. | [Paying the Supply Side: Payouts, Commissions and Settlement](#payouts-and-settlement) |
| **Payout** | Any transfer that discharges what the marketplace owes the supply side (hotels call it remittance). A payout settles many payables minus receivables at one moment; "paid" is a ledger state, not a booking property. | [Paying the Supply Side: Payouts, Commissions and Settlement](#payouts-and-settlement) |
| **Commission invoice** | A legal tax document issued to an agency partner for commission earned on stays in a period, with VAT treatment and a due date. It creates a receivable. | [Paying the Supply Side: Payouts, Commissions and Settlement](#payouts-and-settlement) |
| **Partner statement** | A periodic, line-level account of everything that changed a partner's balance (bookings, fees, commission, adjustments, payouts, card loads, invoices, payments received), usable for reconciling against the partner's own books. | [Paying the Supply Side: Payouts, Commissions and Settlement](#payouts-and-settlement) |
| **Balance states (pending, available, held, paid)** | Stages of a payable: pending (not yet releasable), available (releasable, unpaid), held (stopped by a risk, compliance or dispute decision) and paid. A release rule moves a payable from pending to available, usually tied to check-in or check-out. | [Paying the Supply Side: Payouts, Commissions and Settlement](#payouts-and-settlement) |
| **Debit balance** | A negative partner balance: receivables exceed payables, for example after a cancellation or claim following payout. It is recovered by deduction from future payables or collected. | [Paying the Supply Side: Payouts, Commissions and Settlement](#payouts-and-settlement) |
| **Netting** | Paying the difference between payables and receivables instead of paying the payables in full and invoicing the receivables separately. | [Paying the Supply Side: Payouts, Commissions and Settlement](#payouts-and-settlement) |
| **Beneficiary** | Anyone the marketplace pays, which may differ from the account managing the listing, as with co-hosts, property managers and split payouts. | [Paying the Supply Side: Payouts, Commissions and Settlement](#payouts-and-settlement) |
| **Payout rail** | The mechanism that moves payout money: bank credit transfer (SEPA, domestic or wire), card push, wallet, PSP balance or virtual card. | [Paying the Supply Side: Payouts, Commissions and Settlement](#payouts-and-settlement) |
| **Verification of the payee (VoP)** | A name check of the payee against the receiving account, which the EU Instant Payments Regulation requires payment service providers to offer for euro transfers. Used as a defense against changed bank details. | [Paying the Supply Side: Payouts, Commissions and Settlement](#payouts-and-settlement) |
| **Value-added tax (VAT)** | The EU consumption tax charged on the stay and, separately, on the marketplace's commission or service fee. Who must charge VAT on the stay depends on who supplies it: the property or host today, and under ViDA the platform as deemed supplier when the host does not charge VAT. | [The Tax Patchwork: VAT, Tourist Taxes and Platform Reporting](#taxes-on-stays) |
| **Tourist tax** | A levy set by municipalities, regions or states, usually owed by the guest and collected by the property or host. It comes as a fixed amount per person per night, a percentage of price, tiered by classification or per room. Platforms display it or collect and remit under agreement or law. | [The Tax Patchwork: VAT, Tourist Taxes and Platform Reporting](#taxes-on-stays) |
| **Deemed supplier** | Under ViDA (Council Directive (EU) 2025/516, Art. 28a of the VAT Directive), a platform facilitating short-term rentals of up to 30 nights is treated as supplying the stay itself unless the host gives a VAT number and declares they will charge VAT. Applies from 1 July 2028 to 1 January 2030, by member state. | [The Tax Patchwork: VAT, Tourist Taxes and Platform Reporting](#taxes-on-stays) |
| **VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA)** | The EU reform package whose Directive (EU) 2025/516 adds the deemed-supplier rule for short-term accommodation platforms. Member states may apply it from 1 July 2028 and must by 1 January 2030. | [The Tax Patchwork: VAT, Tourist Taxes and Platform Reporting](#taxes-on-stays) |
| **Tax engine** | A bounded context beside pricing that decides tax per booking. It calculates at quote time from the seller's tax profile, the jurisdictions and effective-dated rules, freezes the tax determination at booking and feeds the ledger and reporting. | [The Tax Patchwork: VAT, Tourist Taxes and Platform Reporting](#taxes-on-stays) |
| **Tax determination** | The frozen record on a reservation of the tax inputs, rule versions, outputs and engine version, plus the liability decision of who collects. Later rate changes apply as deltas, never by silent recalculation. | [The Tax Patchwork: VAT, Tourist Taxes and Platform Reporting](#taxes-on-stays) |
| **Taxpayer / collector / remitter / reporter** | Four separate tax roles: who owes the tax, who takes it from the guest, who pays it to the authority and who reports to it. A hotel on an agency booking plays all four; on hosted stays they are often split between host and platform. | [The Tax Patchwork: VAT, Tourist Taxes and Platform Reporting](#taxes-on-stays) |
| **Place of supply (accommodation)** | The country where a service is taxed for VAT. In the EU, accommodation is taxed where the property is. The platform's own fee is taxed where a business customer is established, or where the property is for a consumer. | [The Tax Patchwork: VAT, Tourist Taxes and Platform Reporting](#taxes-on-stays) |
| **Tour operators' margin scheme (TOMS)** | The EU special VAT scheme for travel agents (Art. 306 of the VAT Directive) that deal with customers in their own name. A merchant-model OTA that resells rooms in its own name may owe VAT on its margin under it. | [The Tax Patchwork: VAT, Tourist Taxes and Platform Reporting](#taxes-on-stays), [Who Takes the Money? Merchant, Agency and Facilitator Models](#merchant-and-agency-models) |
| **DAC7** | Council Directive (EU) 2021/514, requiring digital platforms to report sellers' rental activity, including immovable property, from 1 January 2023, with reports due by 31 January of the following year. Agency bookings count as consideration. | [The Tax Patchwork: VAT, Tourist Taxes and Platform Reporting](#taxes-on-stays) |
| **Reportable seller** | Under DAC7, an active seller not excluded, who is resident in a member state or rented out property located in one. A host living outside the EU with an apartment in Seville is reportable. | [The Tax Patchwork: VAT, Tourist Taxes and Platform Reporting](#taxes-on-stays) |
| **Tax identification number (TIN)** | The seller's tax identifier and its issuing state, collected for DAC7 reporting together with name, address, VAT number where available and date of birth. | [The Tax Patchwork: VAT, Tourist Taxes and Platform Reporting](#taxes-on-stays) |
| **Property listing (DAC7)** | Under DAC7, all property units at the same street address, owned by the same owner and offered on a platform by the same seller. It is the unit for which the platform reports address, consideration and rentals per quarter. | [The Tax Patchwork: VAT, Tourist Taxes and Platform Reporting](#taxes-on-stays) |
| **One-Stop Shop (OSS)** | An EU scheme, in place since 2021, that lets a business declare cross-border business-to-consumer services in one return instead of registering in every member state. | [The Tax Patchwork: VAT, Tourist Taxes and Platform Reporting](#taxes-on-stays) |
| **Income-tax withholding (platform)** | A duty, as in Italy's 2017 short-term-rental law, for a platform that processes payments to withhold and remit host income tax. It makes the seller's tax classification an input to payouts. | [The Tax Patchwork: VAT, Tourist Taxes and Platform Reporting](#taxes-on-stays) |
| **Refund funding** | The decision about whose money pays for a refund: amounts not yet paid out, a partner already paid, the platform or a third party such as an insurer. A refund is two events: money back to the guest, and its funding. | [Closing the Books: Refunds, Chargebacks and Reconciliation](#refunds-and-reconciliation) |
| **Recovery** | An amount the marketplace claims back from a property or host after paying them, such as a cancellation fee or a refund the partner is liable for. In the settlement ledger it is a partner receivable. | [Closing the Books: Refunds, Chargebacks and Reconciliation](#refunds-and-reconciliation), [Paying the Supply Side: Payouts, Commissions and Settlement](#payouts-and-settlement) |
| **Representment** | The merchant's contesting of a chargeback with evidence such as the checkout policy, guest-host messages, check-in confirmation and the authentication result. | [Closing the Books: Refunds, Chargebacks and Reconciliation](#refunds-and-reconciliation) |
| **Foreign-exchange (FX) result** | The gain or loss when an amount recorded in one currency is settled in another at a different rate, arising on payouts, card settlements, refunds and chargebacks, often months after booking. | [Closing the Books: Refunds, Chargebacks and Reconciliation](#refunds-and-reconciliation) |
| **Reconciliation** | A control that compares two or more independent records of the same money, matches them line by line and turns each difference into an exception with an owner. It proves completeness, accuracy and existence. | [Closing the Books: Refunds, Chargebacks and Reconciliation](#refunds-and-reconciliation) |
| **Three-way reconciliation (stays)** | Matching three kinds of record: the business record (reservation, payment arrangement version, payment plan: what should have happened), the ledger (what was recorded) and external evidence from PSPs, banks, issuers, rails and partners (what actually moved). Differs from the usual platform-processor-bank sense. | [Closing the Books: Refunds, Chargebacks and Reconciliation](#refunds-and-reconciliation) |
| **Exception (reconciliation break)** | A difference found by the matcher, held in a queue with an owning team, a service level and an age. An exception without an owner is a design defect. | [Closing the Books: Refunds, Chargebacks and Reconciliation](#refunds-and-reconciliation) |
| **Finance adjustment** | A ledger entry posted outside an automated business flow to correct an error, recognize a loss, apply a goodwill decision or settle an exception. It has a type, reason, evidence, maker and checker. | [Closing the Books: Refunds, Chargebacks and Reconciliation](#refunds-and-reconciliation) |
| **Ledger adjustment rate** | Adjustments per thousand reservations, by type and originating system. The book's best single indicator of where automated money flows are wrong. | [Closing the Books: Refunds, Chargebacks and Reconciliation](#refunds-and-reconciliation) |
| **Suspense account** | A holding account for money that has arrived or left but cannot yet be attributed, such as an unmatched bank credit. A queue, not a place to keep money. | [Closing the Books: Refunds, Chargebacks and Reconciliation](#refunds-and-reconciliation) |
| **Unclaimed funds** | Money owed to someone who cannot be paid, such as a returned payout, an uncashed refund or a virtual card the hotel never charged. It remains a liability to its owner, not income. | [Closing the Books: Refunds, Chargebacks and Reconciliation](#refunds-and-reconciliation) |

| **Trust policy** | A written, versioned rule about what is allowed on the platform, with the evidence standard for acting on it and the consequences. | **Booking risk score** | A calibrated estimate of how likely a reservation is to end in a fraud loss, computed at checkout, recomputed as evidence arrives and read at each later point of no return. | **Points of no return** | The moments in a booking after which a loss can no longer be avoided cheaply, such as virtual card activation, payout release or check-in. | **Risk spine / decision points** | The shared part of a fraud architecture (signal collection, entity graph, features, scoring, outcomes) and the many places that call it (login, checkout, message send, bank-detail change, payout release), each applying its own policy. | [Stolen Cards, Hijacked Extranets and Fake Homes: Fraud in Travel](#travel-fraud) |

| **Session risk** | A score on a partner or guest session, recomputed on each meaningful action from weak signals such as a new device, an unfamiliar network or reading all upcoming reservations in minutes. | [Stolen Cards, Hijacked Extranets and Fake Homes: Fraud in Travel](#travel-fraud) |

First-party fraud is committed by the account holder, for example a guest who stays and then disputes the charge. | [Stolen Cards, Hijacked Extranets and Fake Homes: Fraud in Travel](#travel-fraud) |
| **Payment request (structured)** | A platform object (amount, reason, link to the platform's own payment page) that is the only legitimate way for a property or host to ask a guest for money, so any free-text payment request is anomalous by definition. | [Stolen Cards, Hijacked Extranets and Fake Homes: Fraud in Travel](#travel-fraud) |
| **Insult rate** | The share of genuine guests wrongly declined or blocked by fraud controls, estimated from appeals, retries and a holdout, and tracked beside the fraud loss rate. | [Stolen Cards, Hijacked Extranets and Fake Homes: Fraud in Travel](#travel-fraud) |
| **Identity level (stays)** | The marketplace's statement of how sure it is that an identity belongs to a real, specific, reachable person, on a ladder (from IL0, a session, through IL1 reachable and IL2 paying to IL3 documented). It is not a badge or a risk score; policy requires a level only for actions that need it. | [Who Is Booking, Who Is Hosting: Identity, Accounts and Access](#identity-accounts-and-access) |
| **Partner role model** | The scopes, roles and policies, layered on delegation grants, that decide what partner staff, management companies, co-hosts and software may do on supply objects. One policy engine evaluates it, including conditions such as identity level or recent strong authentication. | [Who Is Booking, Who Is Hosting: Identity, Accounts and Access](#identity-accounts-and-access) |
| **Traveler profile** | The per-brand record of a traveler's preferences, companions, loyalty membership, payment method references and consents. It is kept separate from the identity and the evidence used to verify it. | [Who Is Booking, Who Is Hosting: Identity, Accounts and Access](#identity-accounts-and-access) |
| **Passkey** | A public-key credential under the W3C Web Authentication (WebAuthn) standard, usually synchronized across a person's devices. It is bound to the site's domain, so a lookalike phishing page cannot use it. | [Who Is Booking, Who Is Hosting: Identity, Accounts and Access](#identity-accounts-and-access) |
| **Phishing-resistant authentication** | Authentication that cannot be relayed by a fake site, such as a passkey or hardware key. NIST SP 800-63B-4 (2025) says passwords are not phishing-resistant and restricts SMS one-time codes. | [Who Is Booking, Who Is Hosting: Identity, Accounts and Access](#identity-accounts-and-access), [Stolen Cards, Hijacked Extranets and Fake Homes: Fraud in Travel](#travel-fraud) |
| **Step-up authentication** | A fresh, stronger authentication required at the moment of a sensitive action, such as changing bank details, exporting data or adding an administrator, rather than only at login. | [Who Is Booking, Who Is Hosting: Identity, Accounts and Access](#identity-accounts-and-access) |
| **Delegation grant** | A grant linking a delegate party to a target (property, listing, portfolio or legal entity) with a role, scopes and a validity period, so staff, co-hosts, PMS vendors and AI assistants act on someone else's behalf with an audit trail. | [Who Is Booking, Who Is Hosting: Identity, Accounts and Access](#identity-accounts-and-access) |
| **Scope (authorization)** | A fine-grained permission on a supply object, such as calendar write, rates write, guest contact read or payout configure. | **Verified review** | A review whose author is a party to a reservation that the reservation record shows as stayed, checked against stated eligibility rules. | **Double-blind review** | A two-way review in which each side's review stays hidden until both have submitted or the review window closes. It reduces retaliation and reciprocity at the cost of a publication delay. | [Trustworthy Reviews: Collection, Integrity and Quality Operations](#reviews-and-reputation) |
| **One-way / two-way reviews** | In one-way reviews guests review properties, the usual model for hotels on OTAs. | **Quality program** | A computed credential (badge, tier) or remediation track derived from reputation and operational records, with thresholds, a period and consequences. It turns review data into visibility and money, which creates the incentive to fake it. | [Trustworthy Reviews: Collection, Integrity and Quality Operations](#reviews-and-reputation) |
| **Review-trading ring** | A group of accounts that exchange cheap fake stays and reviews to buy ratings, quality badges and guest reputations. | **Eligibility evidence** | The reservation state at invitation, arrival evidence, stay dates and eligibility policy version stored with each review, proving "verified" under the rules then in force. | [Trustworthy Reviews: Collection, Integrity and Quality Operations](#reviews-and-reputation) |

| **Damage protection** | The marketplace's promise to reimburse a host for damage a guest caused and did not pay for, within limits. | **Liability insurance** | An insurance policy that pays third parties, such as an injured guest or a neighbor, when the host is legally liable. Claims are often handled by a claims administrator. | [Damage, Parties and Emergencies: Protection and Incident Response](#protection-and-incidents) |
| **Security deposit / post-stay charge** | Money taken from the guest before or during a stay and released afterward, as a card pre-authorization, a collected payment or a charge the guest agreed to at checkout. | [Damage, Parties and Emergencies: Protection and Incident Response](#protection-and-incidents), [Paying for a Future Night: Payment Orchestration for Stays](#stay-payments) |

| **Reservation screening** | A risk decision made before a proposed reservation is confirmed, about physical and behavioral risk such as parties, damage and unsafe use. | **Incident workflow** | The intake, triage, response and closure of reported or detected events during a stay (party, injury, intrusion, lockout) as records linked to the reservation, deduplicated by listing and time window. | [Damage, Parties and Emergencies: Protection and Incident Response](#protection-and-incidents) |
| **Active-stay roster** | The list of who is in a destination zone now and who arrives in the next 72 hours, used for outreach during a declared event. | **Bordereau** | The periodic report of claims and premiums sent to an insurance partner under the program contract. | [Damage, Parties and Emergencies: Protection and Incident Response](#protection-and-incidents) |
| **Reserve (claims)** | An estimate of what open claims will cost, booked so finance sees the protection liability before claims are paid. | [Damage, Parties and Emergencies: Protection and Incident Response](#protection-and-incidents) |

| **EU Short-Term Rental Regulation** | Regulation (EU) 2024/1028, applying from 20 May 2026. | **Unit (EU STR)** | In the EU regulation, furnished accommodation rented short term. It excludes hotels and similar accommodation (NACE group 55.1), hostels and campsites, so most homes are in scope and most hotels are not. | [Rules of the Stay: Short-Term Rental Regulation, DSA and Consumer Law](#stays-regulation) |
| **Single digital entry point (SDEP)** | The national interface through which a Member State's authorities receive platform reports and orders and publish registration lists under Regulation (EU) 2024/1028. | [Rules of the Stay: Short-Term Rental Regulation, DSA and Consumer Law](#stays-regulation) |
| **Market policy registry** | The versioned, effective-dated store of jurisdiction rules (registration areas, number formats, night caps, reporting duties) that other systems evaluate. A rule records its legal source, scope, parameters and owner. | [Rules of the Stay: Short-Term Rental Regulation, DSA and Consumer Law](#stays-regulation) |
| **Compliance by design (stays)** | Enforcing rules at a few fixed points (publish, book, scheduled sweep) with one evidence trail, so decisions carry rule version and reason code. In the DSA it is also the duty in Article 31 for marketplaces. | [Rules of the Stay: Short-Term Rental Regulation, DSA and Consumer Law](#stays-regulation) |
| **Obligation register** | The legal team's register of obligations, each with its source, effective period, authority, scope, implementing controls, control owners and the evidence that proves they ran. | [Rules of the Stay: Short-Term Rental Regulation, DSA and Consumer Law](#stays-regulation) |
| **Jurisdiction resolver / eligibility evaluator** | The resolver maps a unit's location to the country, region, municipality and registration areas that apply. The evaluator answers whether a unit may be offered, booked or kept live under current rules and returns a reason code. | [Rules of the Stay: Short-Term Rental Regulation, DSA and Consumer Law](#stays-regulation) |
| **Rule primitive** | One of about ten reusable rule types (registration required, number format, validating register, night cap, minimum stay, zone prohibition, license expiry and others) parameterized per jurisdiction, so adding a city means adding data. | [Rules of the Stay: Short-Term Rental Regulation, DSA and Consumer Law](#stays-regulation) |
| **Night cap / primary residence rule** | A limit on nights per year a unit may be rented, often applying to a host's primary residence, for example Paris reducing its cap from 120 to 90 nights from 1 January 2025. | [Rules of the Stay: Short-Term Rental Regulation, DSA and Consumer Law](#stays-regulation) |
| **Dry run (policy)** | A preview that lists the units a rule change would affect before it goes live, so a regulatory lead can approve a change by its impact rather than by a code diff. | [Rules of the Stay: Short-Term Rental Regulation, DSA and Consumer Law](#stays-regulation) |
| **Regulator gateway** | The integration with authorities, run as a product with service levels: it sends reports, order confirmations and statements of reasons and takes in orders, list updates and validity lookups. | [Rules of the Stay: Short-Term Rental Regulation, DSA and Consumer Law](#stays-regulation) |
| **Platform-to-Business (P2B) Regulation** | Regulation (EU) 2019/1150, applying from 12 July 2020, protecting business users such as properties and hosts. Terms must state the main ranking parameters and the effect of any payment on ranking (Article 5), give notice of terms changes, and give a statement of reasons for restricting or terminating a partner. | [Rules of the Stay: Short-Term Rental Regulation, DSA and Consumer Law](#stays-regulation), [Ordering the Results: Ranking for Guests, Partners and the Marketplace](#stays-ranking) |
| **Digital Markets Act (DMA)** | Regulation (EU) 2022/1925, applying only to designated gatekeepers. Article 5(3) bans parity clauses; Booking.com was designated a gatekeeper on 13 May 2024. | [Rules of the Stay: Short-Term Rental Regulation, DSA and Consumer Law](#stays-regulation) |
| **Trader traceability (DSA Art. 30)** | The duty for marketplaces where consumers contract with traders to collect and verify trader identity data. The Omnibus Directive adds a displayed trader or non-trader status. | [Rules of the Stay: Short-Term Rental Regulation, DSA and Consumer Law](#stays-regulation) |
| **Very large online platform (VLOP)** | A DSA designation for platforms above 45 million average monthly EU recipients, with extra duties. | [Rules of the Stay: Short-Term Rental Regulation, DSA and Consumer Law](#stays-regulation) |

## Part VIII — Platform

The distributed system underneath. [Streams of Nights: Event-Driven Architecture for Stays](#event-driven-stays) owns reservation events and ARI streams; [Peaks, Regions and Languages: Global Scale and Reliability](#global-scale-and-reliability) owns journey objectives, degradation and cost; [Knowing the Marketplace: Data Platform, Metrics and Partner Insight](#marketplace-intelligence) owns governed metrics and partner insight; [Testing Everything: Experimentation Platforms for Stays](#stays-experimentation) owns delayed outcomes and interference; [Models in Production: ML Platforms and Use Cases for Stays](#stays-ml-platform) owns the ML platform and model governance; [Where Guests Come From: Web, Apps, Metasearch and Partner Distribution](#demand-channels) owns metasearch and partner distribution.

| **Reservation event** | The published record that one reservation reached a new version, carrying that version's full contract: stay, unit or room, party, price lines, policy, payment arrangement and status. Losing one is an incident, not noise. | [Streams of Nights: Event-Driven Architecture for Stays](#event-driven-stays) |
| **Availability, rates and inventory (ARI) stream** | The ordered sequence of applied changes to availability, rate and inventory facts, keyed by property or listing, from which every price and availability read model is derived. | **Event notification / event-carried state transfer** | Two patterns Fowler separates: a notification says only that something changed and the receiver calls back for details; event-carried state transfer includes the data so receivers keep their own copy. | **Cost per booking** | Run cost for a period (cloud, data centers, licenses, CDN, maps, third-party APIs) divided by reservations made and also by reservations stayed, split by journey. | **Unit economics (FinOps)** | Linking what an organization spends on technology to the value that spending creates, for example cost per transaction. | [Peaks, Regions and Languages: Global Scale and Reliability](#global-scale-and-reliability) |

| **Metric layer (semantic layer)** | The component that holds governed metric definitions and serves them to every consumer: dashboards, notebooks, experimentation, feature pipelines and APIs. | **Data product (stays)** | A dataset or stream a named team publishes for others, with a schema contract, a freshness and quality objective, documentation, access rules and a support channel. | **Model registry / approved uses** | The record of every model version with its data, features, code, consumers, owner and limits. | **Post-stay tail** | The period after checkout during which a reservation still needs its system of record: no-show reporting, commission invoicing, refunds after complaints, chargebacks and tax records. | [Changing the Engine in Flight: Modernizing a Live Stays Marketplace](#modernizing-a-live-marketplace) |

| **Actor chain** | The record on a reservation of who acted: traveler, agent platform, agent session and authority ID. | [AI Across the Stay: Agents, Assistants and Automation](#ai-in-travel-stays) |

| **Stressor / attractor / residue** | A stressor is any change in the environment, likely or not. The attractor is the state the business falls into under it. The residue is what remains of the architecture, and the structural change, usually a separation, that lets the next version survive. | [Built for the Next Disruption: Evolving a Stays Platform Over a Decade](#evolving-the-platform) |
| **Naive architecture / residual architecture** | The naive architecture satisfies today's requirements and assumes today's world stays put. | **Stressor analysis (stays)** | Testing a stays platform against a list of environmental changes, tracing each through the guest's promise and recording the attractor, the residue and its status: integrated, candidate, gap or survived by composition. | [Built for the Next Disruption: Evolving a Stays Platform Over a Decade](#evolving-the-platform) |
| **Combined stressor** | Several stressors hitting one guest at once. If existing residues handle it with a coordinating case and no new structure, the decomposition is sound. | [Built for the Next Disruption: Evolving a Stays Platform Over a Decade](#evolving-the-platform) |
| **Fitness function (stays)** | An outcome measure of one part of the guest's promise, taken the same way across engines and years, with an owner, a threshold, a cadence and a decision that a breach triggers. It protects the decade, where a journey SLO protects the month. | [Built for the Next Disruption: Evolving a Stays Platform Over a Decade](#evolving-the-platform) |
| **Optionality** | Paying now to keep a hard-to-reverse decision open, justified where the stressor list holds a plausible reason to reverse it. | [Built for the Next Disruption: Evolving a Stays Platform Over a Decade](#evolving-the-platform) |
| **Guaranteed allotment / on-request contract** | Two agency hotel contract types. Under guaranteed allotment the OTA holds a room counter and confirms the guest at once, then notifies the hotel. Under on-request the hotel's answer decides confirmation. | [Agoda and Trip.com: Asian OTAs at Scale](#case-agoda-trip-com) |
