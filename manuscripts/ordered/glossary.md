# Glossary: Plain-Language Definitions

![Glossary: Plain-Language Definitions — logo](commerce-platform-architecture/posts/glossary/assets/images/glossary/logo.jpeg)

> **IN THIS SECTION, YOU WILL:** Look up any term the book uses, read a one- or two-sentence definition beside the terms it is confused with, and follow the link to the chapter that owns it.

> **KEY POINTS:**
>
> * **Look a term up, then read its neighbors.** Each section collects one part's terms, so the rows around a term carry the contrasts that cause real design errors: product versus variant versus SKU, on hand versus committed versus available-to-promise, authorization versus capture, order versus shipment versus invoice, system of record versus copy.
> * **One term, one meaning, one owning chapter.** Every row links to the chapter that owns or best explains the term, and the definitions follow how those chapters define them. When a chapter and this page disagree, one of them needs correcting.
> * **Rules, standards and vendor terms date quickly.** Laws, protocols, products and their status are given as of November 2026, following the sources cited in the chapters. Check the current source before a definition here goes into a contract, a vendor evaluation or a design decision.

The words that cause the most trouble in commerce are ordinary ones. **"Order" means three different numbers** to the platform, the ERP and the warehouse report at Kestrel & Pine ([Counting the Same Thing Everywhere: Data Platforms, Definitions and Analytics](#commerce-data-and-analytics)), and **"in stock" can mean on hand, available or "the last figure the connector received an hour ago"** ([Is It in Stock? Inventory, Availability and the Promise You Can Keep](#inventory-and-availability)). This glossary pins each term to one definition and one owning chapter, so that the chapters, their readers and the AI tools that read them use the words the same way.

## How to Use This Glossary

The sections follow the book's structure. The first collects the **six evidence labels**, the **three take-away tiers**, the **four scale stages** and the **sixteen failure patterns** from the Preface; the next eleven follow **Parts I to XI**, from the business of commerce through catalog, order, fulfillment, channels, integration, platform and trust to the journeys, the decisions that change with scale and the public names the case studies rely on. Then come two reference tables: the **standards, protocols and regulations** with their scope (EU, a country, the US or global), and **Kestrel & Pine's fictional names**. The **alphabetical index** at the end lists every term, alias and abbreviation with a link to the section that defines it.

Each section opens with the chapters that own its terms, then a table with 391 rows in all. The definitions are short teaching descriptions for a reader new to the field, not legal text, standards or vendor documentation; the chapter in the last column gives the full treatment, the sources and the worked example. Rows that combine two or more terms with a slash define all of them, because the difference between them is the point. Acronyms are expanded in the term or the definition. Kestrel & Pine, named in some rows and in its own section, is the fictional outdoor brand this book follows ([Meet Kestrel & Pine](#meet-kestrel-and-pine)); its names and numbers are illustrations, not facts about any real company.

## Part I — COMMERCE: What a Commerce Platform Is

The business vocabulary and the first architecture words: what an order is and who promises what, the business models and the numbers that judge them, the capability map, ownership of facts, and the kinds of platform on the market. [Follow the Order: What Happens Between “Buy” and “Delivered”](#journey-of-an-order) owns the promise and the handoff; [Who Sells What to Whom: Business Models and the Economics That Shape Architecture](#commerce-business-models) the models and economics; [The Map of a Commerce Platform: Capabilities and Layers](#commerce-capability-map) the capability and the layer; [Who Owns the Truth: Boundaries, Bounded Contexts and Systems of Record](#boundaries-and-systems-of-record) the system of record and the bounded context; [Suites, SaaS, Open Source and Composable: The Platform Landscape in 2026](#commerce-platform-landscape) the platform shapes.

| Term | Definition | Chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Promise** | A statement the merchant makes to the customer that some system will later have to make true: "in stock", "€199", "ordered before 22:00, delivered tomorrow". Promises are made early, in seconds, and kept late, over days. | [Follow the Order: What Happens Between “Buy” and “Delivered”](#journey-of-an-order) |
| **Handoff** | The moment responsibility for an order passes from one system to another, for example from the platform to the warehouse. Most failures happen at handoffs. | [Follow the Order: What Happens Between “Buy” and “Delivered”](#journey-of-an-order) |
| **Order** | The record of what was bought, by whom, at what price and tax, to be delivered where and how, and paid how. It is not a cart, not a shipment and not an invoice. | [Follow the Order: What Happens Between “Buy” and “Delivered”](#journey-of-an-order), [The Order Record: Lifecycle, State and the Order Management System](#order-management) |
| **B2C / D2C** | **B2C** (business to consumer) is selling to private consumers. **D2C** (direct to consumer) is a brand selling through channels it operates itself, its own web shop and stores, rather than through a retailer. | [Who Sells What to Whom: Business Models and the Economics That Shape Architecture](#commerce-business-models), [Meet Kestrel & Pine](#meet-kestrel-and-pine) |
| **Wholesale / B2B e-commerce** | **Wholesale** is a brand selling to retailers and distributors who resell. **B2B** (business to business) e-commerce is business buyers ordering electronically, through a web portal or by structured messages between systems. | [Who Sells What to Whom: Business Models and the Economics That Shape Architecture](#commerce-business-models), [Selling to Businesses: Accounts, Contract Prices, Quotes and Approvals](#b2b-commerce) |
| **B2B2C** | Business to business to consumer: a brand sells through a partner's consumer channel while keeping control of its product, content and price. | [Who Sells What to Whom: Business Models and the Economics That Shape Architecture](#commerce-business-models) |
| **Marketplace selling / marketplace operating** | Selling on someone else's marketplace as one seller among many, as against running the marketplace and taking a fee from sellers. The two have opposite architectures. | [Who Sells What to Whom: Business Models and the Economics That Shape Architecture](#commerce-business-models), [Selling on Someone Else's Platform: Marketplaces and Channel Integration](#marketplaces-and-channel-integration) |
| **Omnichannel** | Stores and digital channels sharing stock, orders and customers, so a shopper can buy in one channel and collect, return or get service in another. Also the name of the third scale stage. | [Who Sells What to Whom: Business Models and the Economics That Shape Architecture](#commerce-business-models), [Stores Are Systems Too: Point of Sale and Omnichannel Fulfillment](#stores-and-omnichannel) |
| **Channel** | Any place where a customer can buy and a promise is made: a storefront, a store till, a marketplace listing, the B2B portal, an EDI link, a click-and-collect option. | [Is It in Stock? Inventory, Availability and the Promise You Can Keep](#inventory-and-availability), [What Changes at Each Scale: From First Store to Group](#commerce-scaling-stages) |
| **Seller of record** | The party that legally sells to the customer: the one whose name is on the invoice, who owes the VAT and who answers for the consumer's rights. | [Who Sells What to Whom: Business Models and the Economics That Shape Architecture](#commerce-business-models), [Case: Amazon, From Monolith to Services to the Everything Store](#case-amazon) |
| **GMV (gross merchandise value)** | The value of orders passing through a platform, including what the platform does not keep; several vendors price on it. Definitions differ on refunds and fees (Shopify's filing calls it gross merchandise volume and counts it net of refunds), so always read the source's definition. | [Who Sells What to Whom: Business Models and the Economics That Shape Architecture](#commerce-business-models), [Suites, SaaS, Open Source and Composable: The Platform Landscape in 2026](#commerce-platform-landscape), [Case: Shopify, the Modular Monolith That Serves Millions of Merchants](#case-shopify) |
| **AOV (average order value)** | Revenue divided by the number of orders. Kestrel & Pine's online AOV in 2026 is €92. | [Who Sells What to Whom: Business Models and the Economics That Shape Architecture](#commerce-business-models) |
| **Conversion rate / cart abandonment** | **Conversion rate** is purchases divided by visits. **Cart abandonment** is the share of carts started and not bought; extra costs at checkout are the top reason shoppers give. | [Who Sells What to Whom: Business Models and the Economics That Shape Architecture](#commerce-business-models), [From Cart to Confirmation: Checkout Orchestration](#cart-and-checkout) |
| **Gross margin** | Revenue minus the cost of the goods sold, as a share of revenue; markdowns, returns and fees eat into it. Kestrel & Pine's is 54%. | [Who Sells What to Whom: Business Models and the Economics That Shape Architecture](#commerce-business-models) |
| **Return rate** | The share of items, or of orders, that come back; always say which. Kestrel & Pine's online item return rate is 24% and its order return rate 19%. | [Who Sells What to Whom: Business Models and the Economics That Shape Architecture](#commerce-business-models), [The Way Back: Returns, Exchanges, Refunds and Reverse Logistics](#returns-and-exchanges) |
| **EDI-type sales** | Orders received as structured messages between companies' systems rather than through a web page; for business customers in the EU they are the majority of electronic sales. | [Who Sells What to Whom: Business Models and the Economics That Shape Architecture](#commerce-business-models), [Documents Between Companies: EDI, Punchout and E-Invoicing](#edi-and-e-invoicing) |
| **Capability** | A job the business needs done, named without saying who does it: "calculate the price a given customer pays for a given product today". | [The Map of a Commerce Platform: Capabilities and Layers](#commerce-capability-map) |
| **System / vendor product** | A **system** is a piece of software that does one or more capabilities; a **vendor product** is a system someone sells, usually claiming several capabilities at once. | [The Map of a Commerce Platform: Capabilities and Layers](#commerce-capability-map) |
| **Layer** | A group of capabilities that share a purpose and a rhythm: the ones a shopper touches, the ones that describe what is for sale, the ones that move money and orders, the ones that move goods, partners and back office, and the foundations. | [The Map of a Commerce Platform: Capabilities and Layers](#commerce-capability-map) |
| **Business fact** | Something the business needs to be true about the world: this variant has this GTIN, this customer pays this price, there are 14 units of this size in Duiven. | [The Map of a Commerce Platform: Capabilities and Layers](#commerce-capability-map), [Who Owns the Truth: Boundaries, Bounded Contexts and Systems of Record](#boundaries-and-systems-of-record) |
| **System of record** | The one system whose value of a fact is the truth when copies disagree; every other holder keeps a copy. For master data it is often called the master. | [Who Owns the Truth: Boundaries, Bounded Contexts and Systems of Record](#boundaries-and-systems-of-record), [One Customer, One Product: Identity and Master Data Across Systems](#identity-and-master-data) |
| **Copy / writer** | A **copy** is a business fact held in any system other than its system of record. A **writer** is any system, app, script, job or person that can change a fact in a given system; a fact with two writers drifts. | [Who Owns the Truth: Boundaries, Bounded Contexts and Systems of Record](#boundaries-and-systems-of-record) |
| **Bounded context** | Eric Evans's term from *Domain-Driven Design* (2003): a boundary inside which a model, and the words in it, have one agreed meaning. "Order" and "stock" mean different things in different contexts. | [Who Owns the Truth: Boundaries, Bounded Contexts and Systems of Record](#boundaries-and-systems-of-record) |
| **Commerce platform** | The software that shows products, holds a cart, takes an order and the payment, and hands the order to whoever fulfills it. | [Suites, SaaS, Open Source and Composable: The Platform Landscape in 2026](#commerce-platform-landscape) |
| **SaaS (software as a service)** | Software the vendor runs for many customers at once and rents by the month; you configure it and the vendor upgrades everyone on the same day. | [Suites, SaaS, Open Source and Composable: The Platform Landscape in 2026](#commerce-platform-landscape), [Build, Buy or Assemble: Choosing and Changing Platforms](#build-buy-or-assemble) |
| **Suite (enterprise suite)** | One vendor's set of commerce, order management and content modules designed to work together, usually implemented by a partner. | [Suites, SaaS, Open Source and Composable: The Platform Landscape in 2026](#commerce-platform-landscape), [Build, Buy or Assemble: Choosing and Changing Platforms](#build-buy-or-assemble) |
| **Open source platform** | Commerce software whose code is published under a license that lets you run and change it; you or your agency host, extend and upgrade it, and forks (such as Mage-OS from Magento) can appear. | [Suites, SaaS, Open Source and Composable: The Platform Landscape in 2026](#commerce-platform-landscape) |
| **Composable** | Buying commerce as separate services, from one or several vendors, and assembling them yourself rather than taking one suite; every join between them is then yours. | [Suites, SaaS, Open Source and Composable: The Platform Landscape in 2026](#commerce-platform-landscape), [Build, Buy or Assemble: Choosing and Changing Platforms](#build-buy-or-assemble), [Case: commercetools, Shopware and the Composable Movement](#case-composable-movement) |
| **MACH** | The acronym a vendor alliance coined in June 2020 for Microservices, API-first, Cloud-native SaaS and Headless; founders were commercetools, Contentstack, EPAM and Valtech. It names a style, not a product. | [Suites, SaaS, Open Source and Composable: The Platform Landscape in 2026](#commerce-platform-landscape), [Case: commercetools, Shopware and the Composable Movement](#case-composable-movement) |
| **ERP-centered commerce** | A storefront attached to the ERP that already owns stock, orders and finance, with the web shop treated as a front end. | [Suites, SaaS, Open Source and Composable: The Platform Landscape in 2026](#commerce-platform-landscape) |
| **Specialist layer / specialist group** | A **specialist layer** is a product for one capability (OMS, PIM, WMS, returns, search, channel, iPaaS, payments) added beside a platform. A **specialist group** is several specialist products under one owner, kept as separate brands but pre-integrated. | [Suites, SaaS, Open Source and Composable: The Platform Landscape in 2026](#commerce-platform-landscape) |

| Term | Definition | Chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Product / variant** | A **product** is the sellable concept a shopper browses ("Ridgewalker shell jacket, women's"). A **variant** is one buyable combination of option values (size M, color moss) and must stay stable once it has been sold. | [What Are We Selling? | **GTIN / EAN-13** | The **Global Trade Item Number** under the barcode, issued through GS1 so that every company's systems mean the same item by it; never reused for a different item. In European retail it is the 13-digit **EAN-13**. | [What Are We Selling? Products, Variants and the Catalog](#product-information), [Documents Between Companies: EDI, Punchout and E-Invoicing](#edi-and-e-invoicing) |

| **Taxonomy** | A fixed tree of categories that a channel understands; each marketplace has its own, and products must be mapped to it. | [What Are We Selling? Products, Variants and the Catalog](#product-information) |
| **Bundle (kit) / configurable product** | A **bundle** or kit is a product sold as a set of other products (a tent with its footprint). A **configurable product** is the parent that groups purchasable child variants. | [What Are We Selling? Products, Variants and the Catalog](#product-information) |
| **PIM (product information management)** | The editorial system where product data is created, enriched, translated and approved before it is published to channels; at Kestrel & Pine the system of record for product content from Q3 2027. | [What Are We Selling? Products, Variants and the Catalog](#product-information) |
| **Feed / structured data** | A **feed** is a file or API stream of product records in a channel's format. **Structured data** is product information embedded in the web page itself (schema.org) so that search engines and agents can read it. | [What Are We Selling? | **Price list / price dimensions** | A set of prices that applies to one combination of **market**, **customer group**, **channel** and **validity window**, with a stated **tax basis**. | **Base price / contract price** | A **base price** is the list price for the general public. A **contract price** is agreed with one named account or group and outranks the list. | [What Does It Cost? Price Lists, Contract Prices, Currencies and Tax](#pricing-architecture), [Selling to Businesses: Accounts, Contract Prices, Quotes and Approvals](#b2b-commerce) |
| **Tax-inclusive / tax-exclusive** | A **tax-inclusive** price is what the consumer pays, with VAT inside it (€199 includes 21% Dutch VAT); a **tax-exclusive** price has tax added later, as wholesale prices do. | [What Does It Cost? | **Pricing engine / price history** | The **pricing engine** is the code that applies the price-selection rule to a request at a given moment. Price Lists, Contract Prices, Currencies and Tax](#pricing-architecture) |
| **Channel price rule** | A derived price for a channel that cannot call the pricing engine, such as base price plus an uplift within a floor and a ceiling for a marketplace. | [What Does It Cost?
