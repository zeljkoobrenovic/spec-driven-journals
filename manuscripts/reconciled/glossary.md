# 50. Glossary

![Glossary — logo](finance-systems-architecture/posts/glossary/assets/images/glossary/logo.jpeg)

> **IN THIS SECTION, YOU WILL:** Find the book's vocabulary in one place: look a term up in the alphabetical index, read a plain-language definition next to the terms it is most often confused with, and follow the link to the chapter that explains it in depth.

> **KEY POINTS:**
>
> * **Look a term up, then read its neighbors.** Each section groups the terms that one part of the book uses, so the rows around a term carry the contrasts that matter: clearing account versus suspense account versus dead letter, system of record versus golden copy, principal versus merchant of record, matching versus reconciliation.
> * **One term, one meaning, one owning chapter.** Every row links to the chapter that owns or best explains the term. The chapters are written to follow these definitions; when a chapter and this page disagree, one of them needs correcting, and the worked example in the chapter is the better test of which.
> * **Vendor and regulatory terms date quickly.** Product names, editions and regulatory status are given as of 2026, following the vendor and regulator documentation cited in the chapters. Check the current source before a definition here goes into a contract, a control or a design decision.

> **[FAILURE PATTERNS THIS SECTION ADDRESSES](#where-finance-architecture-goes-wrong):**
>
> * **The Doppelgänger Record** — one word mastered with different meanings in different systems ("revenue" in a provider report, in the billing engine and in the ledger) is this pattern in its semantic form; an owned definition is the first step out of it.
> * **Spreadsheet Glue** — when nobody has agreed what the words mean, the translations between systems' vocabularies end up in mapping spreadsheets that the close depends on.

## How to Use This Glossary

Look up a term or abbreviation in the alphabetical index below; each entry links to the section that defines it. The sections follow the book's structure: **accounting and finance** and **enterprise architecture** for Part I, **integration and data** for the flows that Parts I and III follow across platform boundaries, **platforms and vendors** and **payments** for Part II, **controls, resilience and regulation** for the obligations that run through every part, and **strategy and governance** for Part IV. The definitions are short teaching descriptions in plain language, not accounting standards, legal text or vendor documentation; the chapter in the last column gives the full treatment, the sources and the Northmere worked example. Northmere, the group named in some rows, is the fictional enterprise this book follows ([Meet Northmere: Five Brands, One Set of Books](#meet-northmere)). Rows that combine two terms with a slash define both, because the difference between them is the point.

## Accounting and Finance

The accounting vocabulary an architect needs to read a posting design, a close calendar or an audit finding. [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) teaches the model with worked journal entries; [Order to Cash, Procure to Pay, Record to Report](#finance-processes) owns the end-to-end processes; [Global by Design: Entities, Currencies, Jurisdictions and Brands](#global-scale) owns entities, currencies and intercompany; [OneStream: Consolidating Actuals, Planning the Future](#onestream) owns consolidation. These are teaching definitions, not accounting standards: where a definition and IFRS, US GAAP or a regulator differ, the standard wins.

| Term | Definition | Chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Double-entry bookkeeping | Recording every transaction as equal debits and credits, so that assets always equal liabilities plus equity. The balance is an invariant the ledger enforces, not a report that checks it afterwards. | [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) |
| Journal entry | A dated, balanced set of debit and credit lines posted to accounts in one legal entity and period. Once posted it is never edited; it is corrected by another entry. | [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) |
| General ledger (GL) | The complete set of accounts and balances of a legal entity, from which its financial statements are produced. | [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) |
| Book of record | The legally authoritative accounting record of a legal entity: its balanced entries and balances by account, dimension, ledger and period. It is the special case of a system of record for accounting balances; each entity has one per accounting basis. | [Anatomy of a Finance Estate: Layers, Planes and Boundaries](#finance-landscape) |
| Subledger | A detailed ledger for one kind of item (receivables, payables, partner balances, assets) whose total is carried in the general ledger as a single control account. | [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) |
| Control account | The general-ledger account whose balance must equal the sum of the open items in a subledger, such as `Partner payables`. | [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) |
| Subledger-to-GL reconciliation | The periodic proof that each subledger's total equals its control account in the general ledger. One of the most important key controls in a finance estate; Northmere runs it daily. | [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) |
| Summarization key | The attributes on a summarized ledger line (date, entity, account combination, batch) that let the subledger list every line behind it. A summary without one is where traceability is lost. | [Anatomy of a Finance Estate: Layers, Planes and Boundaries](#finance-landscape) |
| Drill-back key | The identifier on a posted entry that leads from the ledger back to the source transaction or subledger lines that produced it. | [Never Update, Only Append: Designing Custom Ledgers](#designing-financial-ledgers) |
| Chart of accounts | The list of accounts a legal entity posts to, with their structure and hierarchy. A group chart is mapped to local and operational charts by rule. | [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) |
| Natural account | The part of the account combination that says *what* an amount is: cash at bank, trade receivables, commission revenue. Kept few and stable. | [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) |
| Dimension (segment) | An attribute on a ledger line that says *where, for whom and why*: entity, brand, business model, market, cost center, intercompany counterparty. Analytical variety belongs in dimensions, not in new accounts. | [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) |
| Cost of revenue / R&D / G&A (technology cost) | The income-statement lines a technology cost can land in. No standard prescribes the split; common practice puts customer-serving production in cost of revenue, development and test in research and development, and corporate systems in general and administrative expense, so the environment tag becomes a gross-margin control. | [FinOps Meets the Ledger: Technology Spend as Financial Data](#finops) |
| Trial balance | A list of every account's balance at a date, with total debits equal to total credits. A balanced trial balance proves the double-entry invariant held, not that the books are complete or right. | [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) |
| Accrual | An entry that recognizes revenue earned or an expense incurred before it is billed or paid: accrued revenue (an unbilled receivable) or an accrued expense. | [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) |
| Reversing entry | An entry that exactly negates an earlier one, linked to its original. Corrections in a book of record are reversals plus new entries, never edits. | [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) |
| Commitment discount (prepaid cloud commitment) | A discount bought by committing to a level of cloud spend or capacity for one or three years, such as an AWS Savings Plan, a reserved instance or a Google Cloud committed use discount. An upfront payment is usually a prepaid expense amortized over the term, not a software asset; unused commitment is still expense in the period. | [FinOps Meets the Ledger: Technology Spend as Financial Data](#finops) |
| Revenue recognition | Deciding when and how much revenue to record. Under ASC 606 and IFRS 15, revenue is recognized as the entity satisfies its performance obligations to a customer. | [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) |
| Principal (revenue) | An entity that controls a good or service before it is transferred to the customer. It reports revenue **gross**, with the supplier's share as a cost. Collecting the cash does not make you the principal; control is the test. | [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) |
| Agent (revenue) | An entity that arranges for another party to provide the good or service. It reports revenue **net**: its commission or margin. Contrast principal, and contrast merchant of record, which is a payments role, not an accounting one. | [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) |
| Agency model / merchant model | Stayloft's two ways of selling stays. In the agency model the property collects the traveler's money and Northmere invoices its commission; in the merchant model Stayloft collects at booking, holds the funds and pays the property net of commission. Holding the cash changes payments and safeguarding, not necessarily the revenue answer: both are commonly agent. | [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) |
| Deferred revenue / contract liability | Consideration received or due before the entity has performed, recognized as revenue as the obligation is satisfied, such as a subscription billed in advance. | [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) |
| Funds held for customers | Cash collected from customers for services not yet delivered that will mostly be paid on to suppliers or refunded, not earned. Travel filings often call it deferred merchant bookings; Northmere's account is `Traveler funds held`. It is not deferred revenue. | [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) |
| Clearing account | A balance-sheet account where money waits between two known events, such as a provider's settlement and the bank credit. It should return to zero when the second event happens, and every item in it has an identity and an expected zero date. | [Payments-to-Ledger: Draining the Suspense Swamp](#payments-to-ledger) |
| Suspense account | An account holding amounts already posted whose destination is not yet known. An accountant must clear it. Contrast clearing account (destination known, waiting) and dead letter (a technical failure that never reached the books). | [Payments-to-Ledger: Draining the Suspense Swamp](#payments-to-ledger) |

| Book-to-Settle / Commission-to-Cash | Northmere's two travel variants of order-to-cash. In Book-to-Settle (merchant model) Northmere holds the customer's money from booking until it pays the partner out, net of commission; in Commission-to-Cash (agency model) the partner collects from the customer and Northmere invoices and collects its commission. | [Order to Cash, Procure to Pay, Record to Report](#finance-processes) |
| Hand-off (process) | The point where a financial document leaves one system, owner or identifier and continues under another. ## Payments

The vocabulary of card and alternative payments, payment providers and the reconciliation of money with records. [Payments for Finance Architects: Eleven Reasons the Numbers Disagree](#payments-fundamentals) owns the lifecycle and roles; [Adyen: One Platform from Checkout to Settlement](#adyen) and [Stripe: Every Movement Is a Balance Transaction](#stripe) own their account structures; [Payments-to-Ledger: Draining the Suspense Swamp](#payments-to-ledger) owns clearing design and the three-way match.

| Term | Definition | Chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Authorization | The first money step of the payment lifecycle (authorization, capture, clearing, settlement, payout, then possibly refunds and chargebacks): the issuer approves an amount and holds it on the cardholder's funds. No money moves, and authorizations expire. | [Payments for Finance Architects: Eleven Reasons the Numbers Disagree](#payments-fundamentals) |
| Capture | The merchant's instruction to collect all or part of an authorized amount. | [Payments for Finance Architects: Eleven Reasons the Numbers Disagree](#payments-fundamentals) |
| Clearing (payments) | The exchange of transaction details between acquirer and issuer through the scheme that fixes the obligation, including interchange and scheme fees. Not the same as a clearing account. | [Payments for Finance Architects: Eleven Reasons the Numbers Disagree](#payments-fundamentals) |
| Settlement | The scheme nets positions between issuers and acquirers, and funds move from issuer to acquirer. | [Payments for Finance Architects: Eleven Reasons the Numbers Disagree](#payments-fundamentals) |
| Payout | The acquirer or PSP pays the merchant in batches, net of fees, refunds, chargebacks and reserve movements. | [Payments for Finance Architects: Eleven Reasons the Numbers Disagree](#payments-fundamentals) |
| Refund / void | A void cancels an authorization before capture and moves no money; a refund returns captured money and can settle in a later batch or fail weeks later. | [Payments for Finance Architects: Eleven Reasons the Numbers Disagree](#payments-fundamentals) |
| Dispute / chargeback | A dispute is the cardholder's challenge through their issuer; a chargeback is the forced reversal that follows, debited from the merchant through the acquirer. Chargebacks can arrive months after the sale; the merchant contests one by representment, submitting evidence. | [Payments for Finance Architects: Eleven Reasons the Numbers Disagree](#payments-fundamentals) |
| Issuer | The bank that issued the card: it approves payments, bills the cardholder, receives interchange and raises chargebacks. | [Payments for Finance Architects: Eleven Reasons the Numbers Disagree](#payments-fundamentals) |
| Acquirer | The licensed institution that contracts the merchant, receives funds from the scheme and pays the merchant. It carries merchant credit risk. | [Payments for Finance Architects: Eleven Reasons the Numbers Disagree](#payments-fundamentals) |
| Card scheme | A card network such as Visa or Mastercard that sets rules, routes authorization and clearing, and charges scheme fees. | [Payments for Finance Architects: Eleven Reasons the Numbers Disagree](#payments-fundamentals) |
| Payment service provider (PSP) | A provider that packages acquiring (its own or partners'), gateway, risk and reporting into one service for merchants, such as Adyen or Stripe. | [Payments for Finance Architects: Eleven Reasons the Numbers Disagree](#payments-fundamentals) |
| Payment orchestrator | A layer over several PSPs for routing, retries, normalized data and sometimes a card vault. It never holds funds unless it also acquires. | [Payments for Finance Architects: Eleven Reasons the Numbers Disagree](#payments-fundamentals) |
| Payment facilitator (PayFac) | A master merchant that onboards sub-merchants under its own acquiring contract and carries their losses and know-your-customer duties. | [Payments for Finance Architects: Eleven Reasons the Numbers Disagree](#payments-fundamentals) |
| Merchant of record (MoR) | The entity whose name is on the cardholder's statement and that carries the payment side of the sale: refunds and chargebacks. | Interchange++ (IC++) / blended pricing | Interchange is the fee the acquirer pays the issuer per card transaction, set by the scheme. | Alternative payment method (APM) | Any non-card payment method, such as a wallet, bank transfer or local scheme. Each needs its own cash-recognition and aging rules. | [Payments for Finance Architects: Eleven Reasons the Numbers Disagree](#payments-fundamentals) |
| PSP token / network token | A PSP token stands in for a card inside one provider's vault and works only there; a network token is issued by the card scheme and updated when the card is reissued. | Rolling reserve | A percentage of a merchant's funds held back by the provider for a rolling period to cover risk. Reserved funds belong to the merchant but cannot be used. | [Payments for Finance Architects: Eleven Reasons the Numbers Disagree](#payments-fundamentals) |
| Presentment currency / settlement currency | The currency the customer is charged in and the currency the merchant is paid in. Foreign exchange can occur at several moments between them. | [Payments for Finance Architects: Eleven Reasons the Numbers Disagree](#payments-fundamentals) |
| Company account / merchant account (Adyen) | Adyen's company account represents the business and holds merchant accounts; a merchant account is where payments are processed and paid out, and where reconciliation starts. | [Adyen: One Platform from Checkout to Settlement](#adyen) |
| Balance Platform (Adyen) | The structure behind Adyen for Platforms: account holders (with legal entities and KYC data), balance accounts holding funds, transfer instruments for payouts, and split instructions that divide each payment. | [Adyen: One Platform from Checkout to Settlement](#adyen) |
| Payable batch (Adyen) | The unit Adyen pays out in: if a batch's payable balance is positive when it closes, Adyen instructs a net payout. | [Adyen: One Platform from Checkout to Settlement](#adyen) |
| Settlement details report (Adyen) | Adyen's transaction-level report of every credit and debit in a payable batch, with fees; the primary input for settlement matching. | [Adyen: One Platform from Checkout to Settlement](#adyen) |

| Webhook | A provider's HTTP notification of an event. Treat it as early, not final: verify signatures, expect duplicates, tolerate disorder and confirm against reports. | [Stripe: Every Movement Is a Balance Transaction](#stripe) |
