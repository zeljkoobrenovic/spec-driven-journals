# 50. Glossary

![Glossary — logo](enterprise-applications-and-financial-systems/posts/glossary/assets/images/glossary/logo.jpeg)

> **IN THIS SECTION, YOU WILL:** Find the book's vocabulary in one place: look a term up in the alphabetical index, read a plain-language definition next to the terms it is most often confused with, and follow the link to the chapter that explains it in depth.

> **KEY POINTS:**
>
> * **Look a term up, then read its neighbors.** Each section groups the terms that one part of the book uses, so the rows around a term carry the contrasts that matter: clearing account versus suspense account versus dead letter, system of record versus golden copy, principal versus merchant of record, matching versus reconciliation.
> * **One term, one meaning, one owning chapter.** Every row links to the chapter that owns or best explains the term. The chapters are written to follow these definitions; when a chapter and this page disagree, one of them needs correcting, and the worked example in the chapter is the better test of which.
> * **Vendor and regulatory terms date quickly.** Product names, editions and regulatory status are given as of 2026, following the vendor and regulator documentation cited in the chapters. Check the current source before a definition here goes into a contract, a control or a design decision.

> **[FAILURE PATTERNS THIS SECTION ADDRESSES](#where-finance-architecture-goes-wrong):**
>
> * **The Doppelgänger Record** — one word mastered with different meanings in different systems ("revenue" in a provider report, in the billing engine and in the ledger) is this pattern in its semantic form; an owned definition is the first step out of it.
> * **Spreadsheet Glue** — when nobody has agreed what the words mean, the translations between systems' vocabularies end up in mapping spreadsheets that the close depends on.

## How to Use This Glossary

Look up a term or abbreviation in the alphabetical index below; each entry links to the section that defines it. The sections follow the book's structure: **accounting and finance** and **enterprise architecture** for Part I, **integration and data** for the flows that Parts I and III follow across platform boundaries, **platforms and vendors** and **payments** for Part II, **controls, resilience and regulation** for the obligations that run through every part, and **strategy and governance** for Part IV. The definitions are short teaching descriptions in plain language, not accounting standards, legal text or vendor documentation; the chapter in the last column gives the full treatment, the sources and the Northmere worked example. Northmere, the group named in some rows, is the fictional enterprise this book follows ([Meet Northmere: The Running Example](#meet-northmere)). Rows that combine two terms with a slash define both, because the difference between them is the point.

## Accounting and Finance

The accounting vocabulary an architect needs to read a posting design, a close calendar or an audit finding. [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) teaches the model with worked journal entries; [The End-to-End Finance Processes](#finance-processes) owns the end-to-end processes; [Many Entities, Currencies, Jurisdictions and Brands](#global-scale) owns entities, currencies and intercompany; [OneStream: Consolidation, Planning and Performance Management](#onestream) owns consolidation. These are teaching definitions, not accounting standards: where a definition and IFRS, US GAAP or a regulator differ, the standard wins.

| Term | Definition | Chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Double-entry bookkeeping | Recording every transaction as equal debits and credits, so that assets always equal liabilities plus equity. The balance is an invariant the ledger enforces, not a report that checks it afterwards. | [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) |
| Journal entry | A dated, balanced set of debit and credit lines posted to accounts in one legal entity and period. Once posted it is never edited; it is corrected by another entry. | [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) |
| General ledger (GL) | The complete set of accounts and balances of a legal entity, from which its financial statements are produced. | [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) |
| Book of record | The legally authoritative accounting record of a legal entity: its balanced entries and balances by account, dimension, ledger and period. It is the special case of a system of record for accounting balances; each entity has one per accounting basis. | [Anatomy of a Financial Systems Landscape](#finance-landscape) |
| Subledger | A detailed ledger for one kind of item (receivables, payables, partner balances, assets) whose total is carried in the general ledger as a single control account. | [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) |
| Control account | The general-ledger account whose balance must equal the sum of the open items in a subledger, such as `Partner payables`. | [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) |
| Subledger-to-GL reconciliation | The periodic proof that each subledger's total equals its control account in the general ledger. One of the most important key controls in a finance estate; Northmere runs it daily. | [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) |
| Summarization key | The attributes on a summarized ledger line (date, entity, account combination, batch) that let the subledger list every line behind it. A summary without one is where traceability is lost. | [Anatomy of a Financial Systems Landscape](#finance-landscape) |
| Drill-back key | The identifier on a posted entry that leads from the ledger back to the source transaction or subledger lines that produced it. | [Designing Custom Ledgers and Financial Event Processing](#designing-financial-ledgers) |
| Chart of accounts | The list of accounts a legal entity posts to, with their structure and hierarchy. A group chart is mapped to local and operational charts by rule. | [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) |
| Natural account | The part of the account combination that says *what* an amount is: cash at bank, trade receivables, commission revenue. Kept few and stable. | [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) |
| Dimension (segment) | An attribute on a ledger line that says *where, for whom and why*: entity, brand, business model, market, cost center, intercompany counterparty. Analytical variety belongs in dimensions, not in new accounts. | [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) |
| Cost of revenue / R&D / G&A (technology cost) | The income-statement lines a technology cost can land in. No standard prescribes the split; common practice puts customer-serving production in cost of revenue, development and test in research and development, and corporate systems in general and administrative expense, so the environment tag becomes a gross-margin control. | [Technology Spend as Financial Data: FinOps Meets the Ledger](#finops) |
| Trial balance | A list of every account's balance at a date, with total debits equal to total credits. A balanced trial balance proves the double-entry invariant held, not that the books are complete or right. | [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) |
| Accrual | An entry that recognizes revenue earned or an expense incurred before it is billed or paid: accrued revenue (an unbilled receivable) or an accrued expense. | [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) |
| Reversing entry | An entry that exactly negates an earlier one, linked to its original. Corrections in a book of record are reversals plus new entries, never edits. | [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) |
| Commitment discount (prepaid cloud commitment) | A discount bought by committing to a level of cloud spend or capacity for one or three years, such as an AWS Savings Plan, a reserved instance or a Google Cloud committed use discount. An upfront payment is usually a prepaid expense amortized over the term, not a software asset; unused commitment is still expense in the period. | [Technology Spend as Financial Data: FinOps Meets the Ledger](#finops) |
| Revenue recognition | Deciding when and how much revenue to record. Under ASC 606 and IFRS 15, revenue is recognized as the entity satisfies its performance obligations to a customer. | [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) |
| Principal (revenue) | An entity that controls a good or service before it is transferred to the customer. It reports revenue **gross**, with the supplier's share as a cost. Collecting the cash does not make you the principal; control is the test. | [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) |
| Agent (revenue) | An entity that arranges for another party to provide the good or service. It reports revenue **net**: its commission or margin. Contrast principal, and contrast merchant of record, which is a payments role, not an accounting one. | [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) |
| Agency model / merchant model | Stayloft's two ways of selling stays. In the agency model the property collects the traveler's money and Northmere invoices its commission; in the merchant model Stayloft collects at booking, holds the funds and pays the property net of commission. Holding the cash changes payments and safeguarding, not necessarily the revenue answer: both are commonly agent. | [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) |
| Deferred revenue / contract liability | Consideration received or due before the entity has performed, recognized as revenue as the obligation is satisfied, such as a subscription billed in advance. | [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) |
| Funds held for customers | Cash collected from customers for services not yet delivered that will mostly be paid on to suppliers or refunded, not earned. Travel filings often call it deferred merchant bookings; Northmere's account is `Traveler funds held`. It is not deferred revenue. | [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) |
| Clearing account | A balance-sheet account where money waits between two known events, such as a provider's settlement and the bank credit. It should return to zero when the second event happens, and every item in it has an identity and an expected zero date. | [Payments-to-Ledger: Connecting Providers, Brand Platforms and the ERP](#payments-to-ledger) |
| Suspense account | An account holding amounts already posted whose destination is not yet known. An accountant must clear it. Contrast clearing account (destination known, waiting) and dead letter (a technical failure that never reached the books). | [Payments-to-Ledger: Connecting Providers, Brand Platforms and the ERP](#payments-to-ledger) |

Each one needs an owner and a decision on whether it flows back to the entity books. | [OneStream: Consolidation, Planning and Performance Management](#onestream) |
| Record-to-Report (R2R) | The process from recording transactions through close, consolidation and reporting. | [The End-to-End Finance Processes](#finance-processes) |
| Procure-to-Pay (P2P) | The process from purchase request and order through supplier invoice, approval and payment. | [The End-to-End Finance Processes](#finance-processes) |
| Order-to-Cash (O2C) | The process from customer order through invoice, collection and cash application. Northmere specializes it into Book-to-Settle, Commission-to-Cash and Subscribe/Usage-to-Cash. | [The End-to-End Finance Processes](#finance-processes) |
| Book-to-Settle / Commission-to-Cash | Northmere's two travel variants of order-to-cash. In Book-to-Settle (merchant model) Northmere holds the customer's money from booking until it pays the partner out, net of commission; in Commission-to-Cash (agency model) the partner collects from the customer and Northmere invoices and collects its commission. | [The End-to-End Finance Processes](#finance-processes) |

## Payments

The vocabulary of card and alternative payments, payment providers and the reconciliation of money with records. [Payments for Finance Architects: The Lifecycle of Money Movement](#payments-fundamentals) owns the lifecycle and roles; [Adyen: Single-Platform Acquiring and Platform Payments](#adyen) and [Stripe: Programmable Payments, Billing and Connected Accounts](#stripe) own their account structures; [Payments-to-Ledger: Connecting Providers, Brand Platforms and the ERP](#payments-to-ledger) owns clearing design and the three-way match.

| Term | Definition | Chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Authorization | The first money step of the payment lifecycle (authorization, capture, clearing, settlement, payout, then possibly refunds and chargebacks): the issuer approves an amount and holds it on the cardholder's funds. No money moves, and authorizations expire. | [Payments for Finance Architects: The Lifecycle of Money Movement](#payments-fundamentals) |
| Capture | The merchant's instruction to collect all or part of an authorized amount. | [Payments for Finance Architects: The Lifecycle of Money Movement](#payments-fundamentals) |
| Clearing (payments) | The exchange of transaction details between acquirer and issuer through the scheme that fixes the obligation, including interchange and scheme fees. Not the same as a clearing account. | [Payments for Finance Architects: The Lifecycle of Money Movement](#payments-fundamentals) |
| Settlement | The scheme nets positions between issuers and acquirers, and funds move from issuer to acquirer. | [Payments for Finance Architects: The Lifecycle of Money Movement](#payments-fundamentals) |
| Payout | The acquirer or PSP pays the merchant in batches, net of fees, refunds, chargebacks and reserve movements. | [Payments for Finance Architects: The Lifecycle of Money Movement](#payments-fundamentals) |
| Refund / void | A void cancels an authorization before capture and moves no money; a refund returns captured money and can settle in a later batch or fail weeks later. | [Payments for Finance Architects: The Lifecycle of Money Movement](#payments-fundamentals) |
| Dispute / chargeback | A dispute is the cardholder's challenge through their issuer; a chargeback is the forced reversal that follows, debited from the merchant through the acquirer. Chargebacks can arrive months after the sale; the merchant contests one by representment, submitting evidence. | [Payments for Finance Architects: The Lifecycle of Money Movement](#payments-fundamentals) |
| Issuer | The bank that issued the card: it approves payments, bills the cardholder, receives interchange and raises chargebacks. | [Payments for Finance Architects: The Lifecycle of Money Movement](#payments-fundamentals) |
| Acquirer | The licensed institution that contracts the merchant, receives funds from the scheme and pays the merchant. It carries merchant credit risk. | [Payments for Finance Architects: The Lifecycle of Money Movement](#payments-fundamentals) |
| Card scheme | A card network such as Visa or Mastercard that sets rules, routes authorization and clearing, and charges scheme fees. | [Payments for Finance Architects: The Lifecycle of Money Movement](#payments-fundamentals) |
| Payment service provider (PSP) | A provider that packages acquiring (its own or partners'), gateway, risk and reporting into one service for merchants, such as Adyen or Stripe. | [Payments for Finance Architects: The Lifecycle of Money Movement](#payments-fundamentals) |
| Payment orchestrator | A layer over several PSPs for routing, retries, normalized data and sometimes a card vault. It never holds funds unless it also acquires. | [Payments for Finance Architects: The Lifecycle of Money Movement](#payments-fundamentals) |
| Payment facilitator (PayFac) | A master merchant that onboards sub-merchants under its own acquiring contract and carries their losses and know-your-customer duties. | [Payments for Finance Architects: The Lifecycle of Money Movement](#payments-fundamentals) |
| Merchant of record (MoR) | The entity whose name is on the cardholder's statement and that carries the payment side of the sale: refunds and chargebacks. A payments role; whether the entity is principal or agent for revenue is a separate accounting test. | [Payments for Finance Architects: The Lifecycle of Money Movement](#payments-fundamentals) |
| Interchange++ (IC++) / blended pricing | Interchange is the fee the acquirer pays the issuer per card transaction, set by the scheme. IC++ passes interchange and scheme fees through at cost plus an explicit markup; blended pricing charges one rate that hides the cost drivers. | [Payments for Finance Architects: The Lifecycle of Money Movement](#payments-fundamentals) |
| Alternative payment method (APM) | Any non-card payment method, such as a wallet, bank transfer or local scheme. Each needs its own cash-recognition and aging rules. | [Payments for Finance Architects: The Lifecycle of Money Movement](#payments-fundamentals) |
| PSP token / network token | A PSP token stands in for a card inside one provider's vault and works only there; a network token is issued by the card scheme and updated when the card is reissued. Portability is the architect's concern. | [Payments for Finance Architects: The Lifecycle of Money Movement](#payments-fundamentals) |
| Rolling reserve | A percentage of a merchant's funds held back by the provider for a rolling period to cover risk. Reserved funds belong to the merchant but cannot be used. | [Payments for Finance Architects: The Lifecycle of Money Movement](#payments-fundamentals) |
| Presentment currency / settlement currency | The currency the customer is charged in and the currency the merchant is paid in. Foreign exchange can occur at several moments between them. | [Payments for Finance Architects: The Lifecycle of Money Movement](#payments-fundamentals) |
| Company account / merchant account (Adyen) | Adyen's company account represents the business and holds merchant accounts; a merchant account is where payments are processed and paid out, and where reconciliation starts. | [Adyen: Single-Platform Acquiring and Platform Payments](#adyen) |
| Balance Platform (Adyen) | The structure behind Adyen for Platforms: account holders (with legal entities and KYC data), balance accounts holding funds, transfer instruments for payouts, and split instructions that divide each payment. | [Adyen: Single-Platform Acquiring and Platform Payments](#adyen) |
| Payable batch (Adyen) | The unit Adyen pays out in: if a batch's payable balance is positive when it closes, Adyen instructs a net payout. | [Adyen: Single-Platform Acquiring and Platform Payments](#adyen) |
| Settlement details report (Adyen) | Adyen's transaction-level report of every credit and debit in a payable batch, with fees; the primary input for settlement matching. | [Adyen: Single-Platform Acquiring and Platform Payments](#adyen) |
| PSP reference / merchant reference | The provider's key for a payment and the merchant's own key sent in the payment request. Matching depends on both traveling end to end. | [Adyen: Single-Platform Acquiring and Platform Payments](#adyen) |
| In-flight receivable / batch receivable / cash in transit | Northmere's three clearing accounts for provider money: captured but unsettled; settled into a batch but not paid out; paid out but not yet credited by the bank. | [Adyen: Single-Platform Acquiring and Platform Payments](#adyen) |
| Balance transaction (Stripe) | Stripe's record of the effect of any charge, refund, fee, transfer or payout on a Stripe balance, with gross, fee, net and availability date. The reconciliation unit for Stripe. | [Stripe: Programmable Payments, Billing and Connected Accounts](#stripe) |
| Stripe Connect charge types | Stripe Connect's three ways to charge for a platform: a direct charge on the connected account, a destination charge on the platform with an immediate transfer, or separate charges and transfers. The choice moves fees, refunds and loss exposure. | [Stripe: Programmable Payments, Billing and Connected Accounts](#stripe) |
| Webhook | A provider's HTTP notification of an event. Treat it as early, not final: verify signatures, expect duplicates, tolerate disorder and confirm against reports. | [Stripe: Programmable Payments, Billing and Connected Accounts](#stripe) |
| Payments-to-ledger | The design that turns provider records, bank statements and the enterprise's own payment records into complete, matched ledger entries, with clearing accounts that go to zero. | [Payments-to-Ledger: Connecting Providers, Brand Platforms and the ERP](#payments-to-ledger) |
| Three-way match | Proving one payment complete across three independent records (the enterprise's sale record, the provider's settlement line and the bank's statement line) through three joins, each with its own key. | [Payments-to-Ledger: Connecting Providers, Brand Platforms and the ERP](#payments-to-ledger) |
| Expected receivable | The open item created when a payment is captured: what the provider should pay, when, with the keys to match it and an expected settlement date. | [Payments-to-Ledger: Connecting Providers, Brand Platforms and the ERP](#payments-to-ledger) |
| Reconciling item | A known difference on a reconciliation with an explanation, an amount and a date by which it will clear. | [Payments-to-Ledger: Connecting Providers, Brand Platforms and the ERP](#payments-to-ledger) |
| Break | A reconciling item whose expected date has passed without the matching event; an exception someone must investigate. | [Payments-to-Ledger: Connecting Providers, Brand Platforms and the ERP](#payments-to-ledger) |
| Minimum settlement data standard | The settlement-line data Northmere requires payment providers to return so that every line can be matched, written into its provider contracts in 2027. | [Payments-to-Ledger: Connecting Providers, Brand Platforms and the ERP](#payments-to-ledger) |
| Payment factory | A central service that executes outgoing payments: the ERPs decide what to pay and when, the factory decides how. It screens, deduplicates, approves, formats and routes payment instructions to banks and returns status. | [Around the Core: Tax, Treasury, Procurement and Reporting Platforms](#satellite-platforms) |

## Controls, Resilience and Regulation

Internal control over financial reporting, audit evidence, operational resilience and the regulations that shape finance data. [Controls, Auditability and Security by Design](#controls-and-auditability) owns the control vocabulary; [Resilience, Observability, Security and Recovery for Finance Systems](#resilience-and-security) owns financial incidents; regulatory specifics sit in the chapters named in each row. Regulatory status is as of 2026.

| Term | Definition | Chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Internal control over financial reporting (ICFR) | The processes that give reasonable assurance that financial statements are reliable. Under SOX section 404, management assesses it and, for larger public companies, the auditor attests to it. | [Controls, Auditability and Security by Design](#controls-and-auditability) |
| Sarbanes-Oxley Act (SOX) | The 2002 US law that requires certification of financial reports (section 302) and assessment of internal control (section 404). | [Controls, Auditability and Security by Design](#controls-and-auditability) |
| Key control | A control the company relies on to prevent or detect a material misstatement, and that the auditor tests. | [Controls, Auditability and Security by Design](#controls-and-auditability) |
| Preventive / detective control | A preventive control stops an error before it is recorded; a detective control finds it afterwards. | [Controls, Auditability and Security by Design](#controls-and-auditability) |
| Application control | A control performed automatically by a system, such as a three-way match or a posting validation. | [Controls, Auditability and Security by Design](#controls-and-auditability) |
| IT general controls (ITGCs) | Controls over access, change management, operations and development that let the auditor rely on application controls and reports. | [Controls, Auditability and Security by Design](#controls-and-auditability) |
| Segregation of duties (SoD) | Ensuring no one person can both initiate and approve, or record and conceal, a transaction. Cross-system SoD checks conflicts across platforms, not only within one. | [Controls, Auditability and Security by Design](#controls-and-auditability) |
| Information produced by the entity (IPE) | Any report, query, extract or spreadsheet the company produces and uses to perform a control or as audit evidence. The auditor must be satisfied it is complete and accurate. A key report is IPE that a key control depends on. | [Controls, Auditability and Security by Design](#controls-and-auditability) |
| Evidence by design | Building systems so the evidence that a control operated is produced automatically, completely and immutably as a by-product of operating it. | [Controls, Auditability and Security by Design](#controls-and-auditability) |
| Continuous control monitoring (CCM) | Automated, ongoing testing of control operation across the full population rather than a sample. | [Controls, Auditability and Security by Design](#controls-and-auditability) |
| SOC 1 / SOC 2 | Service auditor reports: SOC 1 covers a vendor's controls relevant to customers' financial reporting (ISAE 3402 internationally); SOC 2 covers security and availability. SOC 2 is not a substitute for SOC 1. A Type I report covers design at a date; Type II covers operation over a period. | [Controls, Auditability and Security by Design](#controls-and-auditability) |
| Complementary user entity controls (CUECs) | Controls the customer must operate for a vendor's controls to achieve their objectives, listed in the vendor's SOC 1 report. | [Controls, Auditability and Security by Design](#controls-and-auditability) |
| Bridge letter | A vendor's statement that no significant changes or failures occurred between the end of its SOC 1 period and a later date. | [Controls, Auditability and Security by Design](#controls-and-auditability) |
| Material weakness / significant deficiency | Under PCAOB AS 2201, a material weakness is a reasonable possibility that a material misstatement will not be prevented or detected in time, and leads to an adverse ICFR opinion; a significant deficiency is less severe but merits governance attention. | [Controls, Auditability and Security by Design](#controls-and-auditability) |
| Compensating control | An additional control that addresses a risk while the normal control is absent or deficient, such as during a waiver. | [Architecture Governance That Speeds Decisions](#architecture-governance) |
| Control impact assessment | The record of which key controls a change adds, alters or retires and what evidence each will produce, completed with Internal Controls before the decision. | [Controls, Auditability and Security by Design](#controls-and-auditability) |
| Financial incident | An event in which records or money movements are, or may be, wrong, incomplete, late or unauthorized, whether or not any system is down. Resolved by accounting entries with an approver. | [Resilience, Observability, Security and Recovery for Finance Systems](#resilience-and-security) |
| Business observability | Monitoring the correctness of financial outcomes (balances, completeness, aging, invariants) as metrics, not only system health. | [Resilience, Observability, Security and Recovery for Finance Systems](#resilience-and-security) |
| Recovery time objective (RTO) / recovery point objective (RPO) | How long a function may be unavailable, and how much recently written data may be lost, before the consequences are unacceptable. Set per function; DORA requires financial entities to determine them. | [Resilience, Observability, Security and Recovery for Finance Systems](#resilience-and-security) |
| Point-in-time restore | Returning a database to an earlier moment. For a ledger it is the last resort: it erases good work and evidence; correction by entries is the default. | [Resilience, Observability, Security and Recovery for Finance Systems](#resilience-and-security) |
| Safeguarding | The obligation on e-money institutions (such as Northmere Payments B.V.) and payment institutions to protect customer funds, typically in segregated accounts protected from other creditors, under the E-Money Directive. | [Accounting for Architects: Ledgers, Journals and Revenue](#accounting-for-architects) |
| PSD2 / PSD3 and PSR | The EU's second Payment Services Directive, in force, which introduced strong customer authentication (SCA), two-factor authentication for most electronic payments; and its proposed successors, the third directive and the Payment Services Regulation, not yet in force as of 2026. | [Payments for Finance Architects: The Lifecycle of Money Movement](#payments-fundamentals) |
| PCI DSS | The Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard for storing, processing and transmitting card data. Tokenization reduces the scope. | [Payments for Finance Architects: The Lifecycle of Money Movement](#payments-fundamentals) |
| Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA) | Regulation (EU) 2022/2554, applying since 17 January 2025, on ICT risk, incident reporting, resilience testing and third-party risk for financial entities, including payment and e-money institutions. At Northmere it reaches Northmere Payments B.V., not the whole group. | [Resilience, Observability, Security and Recovery for Finance Systems](#resilience-and-security) |
| Continuous transaction controls (CTC) | Regimes in which invoice data reaches the tax authority at or near the time of the transaction. Under clearance, a document is not legally an invoice until a government platform accepts it, which puts the invoice on the critical path. | [Around the Core: Tax, Treasury, Procurement and Reporting Platforms](#satellite-platforms) |
| Peppol / four-corner model | An international e-invoicing network in which seller and buyer each connect through an accredited service provider and the providers exchange the document. | [Around the Core: Tax, Treasury, Procurement and Reporting Platforms](#satellite-platforms) |
| VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) | The EU VAT package in force since 14 April 2025 that phases in digital reporting, e-invoicing and, from 1 July 2028, deemed-supplier status for platforms facilitating short-term accommodation rental and passenger road transport. Deemed-supplier status for VAT does not make a platform the principal for revenue. | [Many Entities, Currencies, Jurisdictions and Brands](#global-scale) |
| Pillar Two | The OECD global minimum tax of 15% per jurisdiction for large groups, which needs an effective-tax-rate dataset per entity and jurisdiction reconciled to consolidation. | [Many Entities, Currencies, Jurisdictions and Brands](#global-scale) |
| EU AI Act | The EU regulation classifying AI systems by risk; most finance-process agents are likely not high-risk, while credit scoring of natural persons is. | [AI, Automation and Agentic Finance Operations](#ai-in-finance) |
| Retention | The period for which records and their traceability must stay readable, set by law per record type, such as ten years for German books. Modernization ends at retrievability, not at go-live. | [Modernization Patterns for Legacy Finance Systems](#modernization-patterns) |

| Technical debt | The extra cost, risk and delay an enterprise carries because its systems were built, or have aged, in ways that make them harder to change or to trust than they need to be. | Carrying cost | What a debt item costs while it remains, in three columns read side by side and never added: cost carried, risk carried and speed lost. | [Technical Debt, Architectural Fitness and Modernization Priorities](#technical-debt-and-fitness) |

| FinOps | The FinOps Foundation's operational framework and cultural practice for maximizing the business value of technology through collaboration between engineering, finance and business teams. Since the 2026 update it covers public cloud, SaaS, data center, data cloud platforms and AI, not only cloud. | [Technology Spend as Financial Data: FinOps Meets the Ledger](#finops) |
| Technology Business Management (TBM) | The IT financial-management discipline that allocates all technology cost from the general ledger down to consumers on a periodic, finance-led cycle, using the TBM Council's taxonomy. FinOps works from billing data up, near real time; TBM works from the ledger down. | [Technology Spend as Financial Data: FinOps Meets the Ledger](#finops) |
| Cloud cost allocation | Assigning each billing row to a legal entity, cost center, brand, product and environment through governed, effective-dated rules: the provider's account hierarchy first, tags to refine, and named methods for shared pools. Allocation inputs are master data validated against the finance masters. | [Technology Spend as Financial Data: FinOps Meets the Ledger](#finops) |
| Sub-account register | The governed record of every cloud account, project and subscription with its legal entity, brand, product, environment and default cost center; no sub-account is created without an entry. | [Technology Spend as Financial Data: FinOps Meets the Ledger](#finops) |
| Tag policy / unallocated spend | A tag policy sets the required tag keys and validates their values against the finance masters at provisioning; unallocated (untagged) spend is the cost that cannot be traced to an owner. A valid default and an exception to the owner replace any spread by headcount. | [Technology Spend as Financial Data: FinOps Meets the Ledger](#finops) |
| Showback / chargeback (technology cost) | Showback reports allocated technology cost to its consumers as information only; chargeback moves the cost into their budgets through the books. Across legal entities the cost moves by intercompany recharge instead. Contrast a card chargeback, a payment dispute. | [Technology Spend as Financial Data: FinOps Meets the Ledger](#finops) |
| Unit cost | Technology cost divided by a business volume, such as cost per Stayloft booking, run as a governed metric: the numerator ties to the closed ledger and the denominator is a certified volume. Shown as cost to serve (cost-of-revenue lines only) or fully loaded. | [Technology Spend as Financial Data: FinOps Meets the Ledger](#finops) |
| Exception layer | The finance work that rules, reports and robotic process automation (RPA) never reached: settlement lines without references, invoices without purchase orders, unexplained balance movements. | Agent control card | The approved record an AI agent needs before it enters a financially relevant process: an owner, a least-privilege service identity, versioned prompts and models under change management, an evidence log that reconstructs each run, and monitoring. | Model Context Protocol (MCP) | An open protocol, introduced by Anthropic in 2024, for connecting AI agents to tools and data; a reasonable standard for an agent tool gateway. | [AI, Automation and Agentic Finance Operations](#ai-in-finance) |

| Stressor / residue / attractor | A stressor is anything that could happen to the estate's environment; the residue is what remains working after it hits; an attractor is the predictable bad state a system falls into under stress, such as a mapping spreadsheet becoming permanent. | [Evolving the Finance Estate Over a Decade](#long-term-evolution) |
