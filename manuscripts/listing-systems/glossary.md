# Glossary: The Language of Listing Systems

![Glossary: The Language of Listing Systems — logo](listing-systems-architecture/posts/glossary/assets/images/glossary/logo.jpeg)

> **IN THIS SECTION, YOU WILL:** Find the book's vocabulary in one place: read a plain-language definition grouped with the other terms of the same part, follow the link to the chapter that owns the term, or look a term or abbreviation up in the alphabetical index at the end.

> **KEY POINTS:**
>
> * **Look a term up, then read its neighbors.** Each section collects one part's terms, so the rows around a term carry the contrasts that cause real design errors: listing versus underlying entity, publication state versus enforcement overlay, full snapshot versus delta feed, contact versus conversation versus lead, entitlement versus effect, system of record versus read model.
> * **One term, one meaning, one owning chapter.** Every row links to the chapter that owns or best explains the term, and the chapters are written to follow these definitions. When a chapter and this page disagree, one of them needs correcting, and the chapter's worked example is the better test of which.
> * **Regulatory and company terms date quickly.** Regulations, standards, product names and their status are given as of 2026, following the sources cited in the chapters. Check the current source before a definition here goes into a contract, a policy or a design decision.

> **[FAILURE PATTERNS THIS SECTION ADDRESSES](#where-listing-systems-go-wrong):**
>
> * **Vanity Liquidity** — when "contact" means a message in one warehouse, a lead in another and a phone reveal in a third, the group steers on numbers that cannot be compared; an owned definition is the first step out.
> * **The Attribute Junkyard** — the same failure at the level of words: one concept under several names, or one name for several concepts, until filters, feeds, models and teams disagree about what a listing is.

## How to Use This Glossary

The sections follow the book's structure. The first collects the **evidence labels** every chapter uses and the **fifteen failure patterns**; the next eight follow **Parts I to VIII**, from the marketplace business through the listing model, supply, discovery, trust, money and the platform underneath to the Chief Architect's leadership agenda; the last collects the recurring **Veldmark names**. Part IX's case studies apply these terms rather than adding new ones. The **alphabetical index** at the end lists every term, abbreviation and common alias with a link to the section that defines it.

Each section opens with the chapters that own its terms, then a `| Term | Definition | Chapter |` table. The definitions are short teaching descriptions in plain language, not legal text, standards or vendor documentation; the chapter in the last column gives the full treatment, the sources and the worked example. Rows that combine two or more terms with a slash define all of them, because the difference between them is the point. Veldmark, named in some rows and in the last section, is the fictional marketplace group this book follows ([Meet Veldmark: Four Brands, One Platform to Build](#meet-veldmark)); its numbers are illustrations, not facts about any real company.

## Part I — Marketplace

The business vocabulary: what kind of marketplace this is, what it sells, how it is measured and how it is decomposed. [Classifieds, Leads, Transactions and Managed Models: A Marketplace Taxonomy](#marketplace-taxonomy) owns the business models; [Liquidity Is the Product: Network Effects, Local Markets and Marketplace Metrics](#liquidity-and-network-effects) owns liquidity and its metrics; [From Newspaper Columns to Portfolios: How the Classifieds Industry Evolved](#industry-evolution) owns the industry's history; [Map What the Marketplace Does Before What Runs It](#business-capabilities) and [Bounded Contexts for Marketplaces: Where One Listing Becomes Many Models](#bounded-contexts) own the two decompositions; [Same Platform, Different Physics: How Listing Verticals Differ](#vertical-requirements) owns what differs between verticals.

| Term | Definition | Chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Listing marketplace** | An intermediary that publishes offers from many suppliers and helps buyers find them. Its architecture should be judged by how reliably it turns fresh, trustworthy supply into matches with demand. | [Classifieds, Leads, Transactions and Managed Models: A Marketplace Taxonomy](#marketplace-taxonomy) |
| **Endpoint** | The last step of the transaction for which the platform takes responsibility: a contact, a delivered lead, a completed payment and delivery, or a guaranteed outcome. The most useful single axis for classifying a marketplace. | [Classifieds, Leads, Transactions and Managed Models: A Marketplace Taxonomy](#marketplace-taxonomy) |
| **Classifieds** | A marketplace whose job ends when a buyer contacts a seller. The platform never sees the money, so it monetizes visibility and access: insertion fees, paid visibility, professional subscriptions and advertising. | [Classifieds, Leads, Transactions and Managed Models: A Marketplace Taxonomy](#marketplace-taxonomy) |
| **Lead-generation marketplace** | A marketplace (or portal) whose job ends when a qualified enquiry is delivered to a professional such as a dealer, estate agent or employer. The professional pays because the lead has value, so the platform must qualify, count, route and attribute leads. | [Classifieds, Leads, Transactions and Managed Models: A Marketplace Taxonomy](#marketplace-taxonomy) |
| **Transactional marketplace** | A marketplace whose job ends when payment and delivery are complete on the platform. Revenue becomes a take rate on gross merchandise value, and the platform takes on payments, disputes and buyer-protection losses. | [Classifieds, Leads, Transactions and Managed Models: A Marketplace Taxonomy](#marketplace-taxonomy) |
| **Managed marketplace** | A marketplace that takes inventory, quality or outcome risk itself, for example by buying the items it sells. It carries price and inventory risk on its balance sheet. | [Classifieds, Leads, Transactions and Managed Models: A Marketplace Taxonomy](#marketplace-taxonomy) |
| **Horizontal / vertical marketplace** | A horizontal marketplace lists many unrelated categories under one brand and one category tree; a vertical marketplace specializes in one domain, such as property, vehicles or jobs, with data, tools and monetization built for it. | [Classifieds, Leads, Transactions and Managed Models: A Marketplace Taxonomy](#marketplace-taxonomy) |
| **C2C / B2C / B2B** | Consumer to consumer, business to consumer and business to business: the seller-type axis, which decides supply channels, the account model, trader obligations and the unit of monetization. | [Classifieds, Leads, Transactions and Managed Models: A Marketplace Taxonomy](#marketplace-taxonomy) |
| **Marketplace profile** | The combination of answers on six axes (endpoint, scope, seller type, item identity, money flow, risk ownership) that describes one marketplace in one country and category group. A brand is not a profile: one brand can run several. | [Classifieds, Leads, Transactions and Managed Models: A Marketplace Taxonomy](#marketplace-taxonomy) |
| **Gross merchandise value (GMV)** | The total value of goods sold through a marketplace in a period. Only a transactional marketplace observes its true GMV; a classifieds site can at best estimate it. | [How Listing Marketplaces Make Money](#monetization-models) |
| **Two-sided market** | A market in which a platform serves two distinct groups whose value depends on each other: buyers and sellers, renters and landlords, job seekers and employers. | [Liquidity Is the Product: Network Effects, Local Markets and Marketplace Metrics](#liquidity-and-network-effects) |
| **Network effect** | A participant's value from the platform depends on how many others use it. Cross-side effects link the two sides (more buyers attract sellers); same-side effects link participants on one side, and can be negative (more sellers compete for the same buyers). | [Liquidity Is the Product: Network Effects, Local Markets and Marketplace Metrics](#liquidity-and-network-effects) |
| **Liquidity** | The probability that, within a time window that matters to users, a listing receives a qualified contact and a buyer finds a suitable, available listing, measured for a given place and category. What the marketplace actually sells. | [Liquidity Is the Product: Network Effects, Local Markets and Marketplace Metrics](#liquidity-and-network-effects) |
| **Cell (liquidity cell)** | A geographic area that buyers will travel or search across, crossed with a category whose items substitute for each other. Liquidity is a property of a cell, not of a site, and leadership is won cell by cell. | [Liquidity Is the Product: Network Effects, Local Markets and Marketplace Metrics](#liquidity-and-network-effects) |
| **Chicken-and-egg problem / seeding** | Buyers do not come without supply and sellers do not list without buyers. Seeding breaks the loop with free private listings, professional feeds, one dense cell first, or distribution from an existing network. | [Liquidity Is the Product: Network Effects, Local Markets and Marketplace Metrics](#liquidity-and-network-effects) |
| **Multi-homing** | Participating in several competing platforms at once. Professional sellers multi-home cheaply through feeds; buyers multi-home at first and then concentrate on the site with the freshest, most complete supply. | [Liquidity Is the Product: Network Effects, Local Markets and Marketplace Metrics](#liquidity-and-network-effects) |
| **Disintermediation (leakage)** | Parties who met on the platform complete their deal, or their next one, outside it. It costs fees, hides outcomes and is the route scammers use to escape the platform's protections. | [Liquidity Is the Product: Network Effects, Local Markets and Marketplace Metrics](#liquidity-and-network-effects), [From Interest to Contact: Messaging and Lead Management](#messaging-and-leads) |
| **Liquidity metrics** | The measurements that tell a marketplace whether matches are happening, per cell, fast enough: fresh available supply, early exposure, contact rate, sell-through, seller return rate, each paired with a counter-metric. | [Liquidity Is the Product: Network Effects, Local Markets and Marketplace Metrics](#liquidity-and-network-effects) |
| **Time to first contact** | The time from a listing becoming searchable (not from submission) to its first qualified contact. Measuring from searchable separates publication speed from demand. | [Liquidity Is the Product: Network Effects, Local Markets and Marketplace Metrics](#liquidity-and-network-effects) |
| **Contact rate / sell-through** | Contact rate is the share of new listings with at least one qualified contact within a window (48 hours for goods, longer for cars and property). Sell-through is the share of listings sold within N days, hard to measure because most sellers never mark an item sold. | [Liquidity Is the Product: Network Effects, Local Markets and Marketplace Metrics](#liquidity-and-network-effects) |
| **Counter-metric / ghost share** | A counter-metric guards a liquidity metric against dishonest improvement. Ghost share, the share of contacts and alerts that reach listings already sold or removed, is the counter-metric for supply. | [Liquidity Is the Product: Network Effects, Local Markets and Marketplace Metrics](#liquidity-and-network-effects) |
| **Portfolio model** | A marketplace group's strategy of owning many local marketplaces, usually one leader per country and vertical, bought or launched one by one, and sharing some capabilities beneath their brands. The group's value is the sum of its local leaderships plus whatever the shared layer saves or enables, which is why groups inherit several stacks of everything. | [From Newspaper Columns to Portfolios: How the Classifieds Industry Evolved](#industry-evolution) |
| **Copy-paste market entry** | Launching a marketplace in a new country by deploying a copy of an existing marketplace's codebase and operating model, then localizing it; widely associated with Schibsted's international expansion. Fast to start and expensive later, because the copies diverge: the classic origin of the Copy-Paste Country. | [From Newspaper Columns to Portfolios: How the Classifieds Industry Evolved](#industry-evolution), [Adevinta: From Schibsted's Copy-Paste Model to a Portfolio Platform](#case-adevinta) |
| **Carve-out / transitional services agreement (TSA)** | A carve-out separates a business from its group for sale; the TSA is the contract under which the seller keeps providing shared services (hosting, platforms, back office) to the sold business for a period. Every shared layer becomes a line in it. | [From Newspaper Columns to Portfolios: How the Classifieds Industry Evolved](#industry-evolution), [After the Deal: Consolidating Platforms After Acquisitions](#platform-consolidation) |
| **Business capability** | What the business does, described independently of how, where and by whom: "Saved searches and alerts", not "the alert cron". Capability labels contain no system, vendor, team or channel names. | [Map What the Marketplace Does Before What Runs It](#business-capabilities) |
| **Capability map** | A stable, implementation-free hierarchy of capabilities, comparable across brands. The book's marketplace map has ten level-1 areas: Supply Acquisition, Listing Management, Discovery, Matching and Contact, Trust and Safety, Transactions, Monetization, Professional Services, Data and Insights, and Platform. | [Map What the Marketplace Does Before What Runs It](#business-capabilities) |
| **Value stream** | The end-to-end sequence of stages that delivers an outcome to a customer: list-to-sell, search-to-contact, lead-to-deal and professional onboarding. Streams carry outcome metrics; capabilities carry activity. | [Map What the Marketplace Does Before What Runs It](#business-capabilities) |
| **Heat map (differentiation, maturity, duplication)** | Scoring each capability for how much it makes customers choose us (scored by product), how well it works today with dated evidence (scored by engineering) and how many implementations the group runs (counted by architecture). The combination suggests consolidate, promote, buy, split, invest, protect or keep separate. | [Map What the Marketplace Does Before What Runs It](#business-capabilities) |
| **Core / supporting / generic subdomain** | Core subdomains are where the business competes and the best people should work; supporting subdomains are specific and necessary but not where it wins; generic subdomains are needed by every business. Discovery is core everywhere; lead management is core in verticals. | [Bounded Contexts for Marketplaces: Where One Listing Becomes Many Models](#bounded-contexts) |
| **Bounded context** | The boundary within which one model and its vocabulary apply consistently. In a marketplace "the listing" is a different model in feed ingestion, the catalog, search, trust and safety, monetization and analytics. | [Bounded Contexts for Marketplaces: Where One Listing Becomes Many Models](#bounded-contexts) |
| **Ubiquitous language** | The vocabulary used consistently inside one bounded context, in conversation, documents and code. | [Bounded Contexts for Marketplaces: Where One Listing Becomes Many Models](#bounded-contexts) |
| **Context map** | A map of the bounded contexts and the relationships between them: who is upstream, who adapts to whom, and where translation happens. | [Bounded Contexts for Marketplaces: Where One Listing Becomes Many Models](#bounded-contexts) |
| **Partnership / shared kernel / customer–supplier / conformist** | Context relationships: two teams planning an integration together; a small model two teams co-own (listing identifiers and tenant codes); an upstream that plans for its downstream's needs; a downstream that adopts the upstream model as it is (a checkout conforming to its payment provider). | [Bounded Contexts for Marketplaces: Where One Listing Becomes Many Models](#bounded-contexts) |
| **Anti-corruption layer (ACL)** | A translator a downstream context builds so that a foreign model does not leak into its own: feed ingestion in front of 160 vendors' formats, or a translator in front of a monolith. | [Bounded Contexts for Marketplaces: Where One Listing Becomes Many Models](#bounded-contexts) |
| **Open host service / published language** | An open host service offers one well-defined protocol to many consumers; a published language is the documented shared vocabulary it speaks. The listing event contract is the marketplace's main published language. | [Bounded Contexts for Marketplaces: Where One Listing Becomes Many Models](#bounded-contexts) |
| **Aggregate** | A cluster of domain objects treated as one unit for changes, with one root and one owner; transactions do not cross aggregate boundaries. | [Bounded Contexts for Marketplaces: Where One Listing Becomes Many Models](#bounded-contexts), [The Listing Aggregate: Identity, Lifecycle and Invariants](#listing-aggregate) |
| **Domain event** | A past-tense, immutable statement in business language that something meaningful happened in a context, such as `ListingSold`, published so other contexts can react. | [Bounded Contexts for Marketplaces: Where One Listing Becomes Many Models](#bounded-contexts), [Every Change Is an Event: Event-Driven Listing Architecture](#event-driven-listings) |
| **Vertical requirement profile** | A one-page description of a vertical's physics, written with numbers, against which architecture decisions can be checked. It covers fourteen dimensions, including supply mix, item identity, listing lifetime, attribute density, decision horizon, lead value, monetization unit, regulatory load and fraud typology. | [Same Platform, Different Physics: How Listing Verticals Differ](#vertical-requirements) |
| **Shared-versus-specific decision** | Placing a capability, or part of one, in one of four layers with stated reasons: shared platform; shared engine with vertical content; vertical capability, group-owned; or brand-local product. "The machinery is generic, the content is specific." | [Same Platform, Different Physics: How Listing Verticals Differ](#vertical-requirements) |
| **External system of record** | Where the authoritative listing lives outside the marketplace, in a dealer management system, an agency CRM or a multiple-listing service, so the marketplace holds a mirror and the source wins. | [Same Platform, Different Physics: How Listing Verticals Differ](#vertical-requirements), [Many Doors In: Forms, APIs and Bulk Feeds](#listing-ingestion) |

## Part II — Listing

The domain model of the thing being sold. [The Listing Aggregate: Identity, Lifecycle and Invariants](#listing-aggregate) owns the listing, its identity and lifecycle; [Category Trees That Can Change: Designing Marketplace Taxonomies](#categories-and-taxonomies) and [Attributes Without Chaos: Schemas, Dictionaries and Validation](#attribute-schemas) own classification and structured facts; [Photos Sell: Media Pipelines for Listings](#media-pipeline) and [Where Is It? Location, Geocoding and Privacy](#location-and-geo) own photos and place; [Private Sellers, Dealers and Agencies: Modeling Who Lists](#sellers-and-accounts) owns who lists.

| Term | Definition | Chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Listing** | A seller's public, time-bounded offer about a specific thing: an item to sell, a vehicle, a property to sell or let, a job to fill. It references the underlying thing; it is never the thing itself. | [The Listing Aggregate: Identity, Lifecycle and Invariants](#listing-aggregate) |
| **Listing aggregate** | The listing treated as one unit of consistency: identity, owner, category, attributes, price, location, media, contact options and state, with one owning service and a set of invariants that hold after every change. Paid visibility and moderation decisions are held as references, not as flags. | [The Listing Aggregate: Identity, Lifecycle and Invariants](#listing-aggregate) |
| **Underlying entity** | The physical or legal thing an offer is about, such as a specific apartment or a specific car, identified by VIN, address or parcel where one exists. It outlives every listing of it. | [The Listing Aggregate: Identity, Lifecycle and Invariants](#listing-aggregate), [One Property, Three Listings: Duplicates and Entity Resolution](#entity-resolution) |
| **Listing ID / public ID** | The listing ID is the aggregate's internal identity: globally unique across brands and countries, never reused, encoding nothing that can change. The public ID is the short, stable identifier shown in URLs and support conversations, mapped one-to-one to it. | [The Listing Aggregate: Identity, Lifecycle and Invariants](#listing-aggregate) |
| **External key / request key / entity reference** | Three further keys: the partner's own reference, unique per integration and the idempotency key for feeds; a short-lived key that stops a flaky mobile connection creating a listing twice; and an optional link to the canonical property, vehicle or product. | [The Listing Aggregate: Identity, Lifecycle and Invariants](#listing-aggregate) |
| **Relisting** | Putting an item on the market again. Renewals and a sale falling through keep the same listing ID; a delete-and-repost or a property returning a year later gets a new ID, linked to the old one through entity resolution. | [The Listing Aggregate: Identity, Lifecycle and Invariants](#listing-aggregate) |
| **Invariant / policy** | An invariant is a rule that holds after every successful command on the aggregate, such as classification in a valid leaf. A policy is a rule enforced elsewhere or later, such as the seller's slot quota or same-seller duplicates. | [The Listing Aggregate: Identity, Lifecycle and Invariants](#listing-aggregate) |
| **Listing lifecycle** | The states a listing can be in, the transitions between them, who may trigger each, and what each state means for visibility, contact and retention. Seller intent, the platform clock and trust and safety change it for different reasons. | [The Listing Aggregate: Identity, Lifecycle and Invariants](#listing-aggregate) |
| **Publication state / enforcement overlay** | The book models the lifecycle as two dimensions: a publication state owned by the listing (draft, pending review, active, paused, reserved, sold, expired, withdrawn, archived) and an enforcement overlay owned by trust and safety (none, held, restricted, blocked, restored). Visibility, contactability and alerting are derived from both, never stored separately. | [The Listing Aggregate: Identity, Lifecycle and Invariants](#listing-aggregate) |
| **Outcome record** | The reason attached to every terminal transition: sold on platform, sold elsewhere, sold through a contact here but paid off-platform, no longer available, or unknown. Without it, sell-through cannot be measured. | [The Listing Aggregate: Identity, Lifecycle and Invariants](#listing-aggregate) |
| **Listing version** | An immutable snapshot of the aggregate's seller-visible content after one accepted change, numbered with an integer that increases per listing. It orders events, guards concurrent writes, preserves evidence and drives re-moderation. | [The Listing Aggregate: Identity, Lifecycle and Invariants](#listing-aggregate) |
| **Material change** | An edit that alters what was approved (category, price beyond a threshold, images, title, payment terms or contact details) and must therefore pass the moderation gate again. | [The Listing Aggregate: Identity, Lifecycle and Invariants](#listing-aggregate), [From Submitted to Searchable: The Publication Pipeline](#publication-pipeline) |
| **Sold-to-gone latency** | The time each consumer of the listing stream takes to remove a listing after it leaves the active state, declared in that consumer's contract and monitored per consumer. | [The Listing Aggregate: Identity, Lifecycle and Invariants](#listing-aggregate) |
| **Category taxonomy** | A controlled hierarchy of classes into which every listing is placed, with the rules that hang off each class. The category selects the contract a listing must meet: schema, fees, moderation rules, facets and ad segments. | [Category Trees That Can Change: Designing Marketplace Taxonomies](#categories-and-taxonomies) |
| **Strict tree / polyhierarchy / faceted classification** | A strict tree gives each node one parent; a polyhierarchy allows several; faceted classification avoids deep trees by classifying along independent dimensions. The recommended default is a strict, leaf-only classification tree, with polyhierarchy only in navigation. | [Category Trees That Can Change: Designing Marketplace Taxonomies](#categories-and-taxonomies) |
| **Classification tree / navigation tree** | The classification tree is canonical, strict and leaf-only, with stable IDs; navigation trees are derived per brand and country, with aliases, curated groupings and localized labels and slugs that are properties of a node, never its identity. | [Category Trees That Can Change: Designing Marketplace Taxonomies](#categories-and-taxonomies) |
| **Canonical tree with country views** | One canonical set of nodes with stable IDs, of which each country enables a subset with its own labels, slugs and navigation. It shares meaning where it exists and keeps local fit where it does not. | [Category Trees That Can Change: Designing Marketplace Taxonomies](#categories-and-taxonomies) |
| **Reference data** | Data that other data points at to give it meaning, that changes rarely and must be identical wherever it is used: currencies, country codes, categories, vehicle makes and models, energy classes. | [Category Trees That Can Change: Designing Marketplace Taxonomies](#categories-and-taxonomies), [Attributes Without Chaos: Schemas, Dictionaries and Validation](#attribute-schemas) |
| **Taxonomy versioning** | Publishing the taxonomy as immutable, numbered releases with stable, meaningless node IDs, each release shipping its migration; listings and saved searches record the version they were classified under. | [Category Trees That Can Change: Designing Marketplace Taxonomies](#categories-and-taxonomies) |
| **Category migration** | Any taxonomy change that alters which node an existing listing, saved search, URL, rule or metric points at: rename, move, merge, split, retire or add. Merges are deterministic; splits are lossy and need reclassification. | [Category Trees That Can Change: Designing Marketplace Taxonomies](#categories-and-taxonomies) |
| **Category prediction** | Suggesting or assigning a listing's category from its title, description, photos or structured data. Best used to suggest the top three, taxonomy-version-aware, and measured by seller acceptance and later correction. | [Category Trees That Can Change: Designing Marketplace Taxonomies](#categories-and-taxonomies) |
| **Attribute** | A structured, typed fact about the item, such as frame size, mileage or energy class, that drives filters, facets, sorting, ranking, validation and outbound feeds. | [Attributes Without Chaos: Schemas, Dictionaries and Validation](#attribute-schemas) |

| **Paid-organic blending** | How paid placements compete with organic results: fixed slots, a score multiplier, a separate auction merged into floating slots, or unified ranking with a relevance floor and a cap on paid positions. The relevance model never reads entitlements. | [Ranking for Two Sides: Relevance, Freshness and Paid Visibility](#ranking) |
| **Relevance floor / guaranteed exposure** | A floor makes paid listings eligible only above a relevance threshold for the query; guaranteed exposure sells a number of impressions in relevant contexts, with an extension when delivery falls short, instead of an unconditional position. | [Ranking for Two Sides: Relevance, Freshness and Paid Visibility](#ranking) |
| **Ranking transparency** | The obligations and the machinery to disclose the main ranking parameters to business users (P2B Regulation, Art. 5) and consumers (Omnibus Directive), label paid ranking, and explain recommender systems (DSA, Art. 27), generated from the same policy file the ranking runs on. | [Ranking for Two Sides: Relevance, Freshness and Paid Visibility](#ranking) |
| **Interleaving** | Comparing two rankers on the same traffic by merging both rankers' results on one page and crediting each contact to the ranker that contributed the listing; far more sensitive than a standard A/B test. | [Ranking for Two Sides: Relevance, Freshness and Paid Visibility](#ranking), [Testing a Two-Sided Market: Experimentation Platforms](#experimentation) |
| **Recommendation** | A list of listings, searches or sellers the user did not explicitly request, chosen from what is known about the context, the user or both. Surfaces include similar listings, the home feed, recommended searches, low-result fallbacks and recommendation e-mails. | [Recommendations for Inventory That Disappears](#recommendations) |
| **Similar listings** | Recommendations anchored on one listing, usually on its detail page, built from attribute and geography similarity, embeddings and co-view or co-contact neighbors. | [Recommendations for Inventory That Disappears](#recommendations) |
| **Content-based / collaborative filtering** | Content-based methods find nearest neighbors on attributes, price, location, text and images and work from minute one; collaborative methods (item-to-item co-views, matrix factorization, session models) learn from behavior and are absent for new listings. | [Recommendations for Inventory That Disappears](#recommendations) |
| **Candidate generation** | The stage that selects a few hundred plausible items from the whole live inventory, from several sources, before filtering and re-ranking. | [Recommendations for Inventory That Disappears](#recommendations) |
| **Serving-time filter / send-time check** | The availability, eligibility, trust, duplicate, already-contacted and consent checks applied to every candidate when it is served, using the same status read model as search; for e-mail and push, applied again when the message is sent. | [Recommendations for Inventory That Disappears](#recommendations), [Tell Me When It Appears: Saved Searches and Alerts](#saved-searches-and-alerts) |
| **Cold start** | Recommending without the interaction history collaborative methods need, in three forms: a new listing, a new or anonymous user (where the session is the user), and a new market or category. | [Recommendations for Inventory That Disappears](#recommendations) |
| **Map search** | Discovery in which the result set is displayed on, and scoped by, a map. It is a separate search client with its own latency budget and query mix: dozens of queries per session, counts and clusters at every zoom. | [Searching by Place: Map, Polygon and Commute Search](#map-search) |
| **Bounding box (viewport) / drawn polygon / named area** | Three geo scopes: the rectangle of the visible map, cheap but cacheable only if snapped to tiles; a polygon the user traces, which must be normalized before querying; and a place ID filter, the cheapest and most cacheable. | [Searching by Place: Map, Polygon and Commute Search](#map-search) |
| **Isochrone / isochrone search** | An isochrone is a polygon enclosing every point reachable from an origin within a time budget for a mode of transport and departure time; isochrone search filters listings by travel time instead of distance, alternatively by computing per-listing travel times after retrieval. | [Searching by Place: Map, Polygon and Commute Search](#map-search) |
| **Geo clustering** | Grouping nearby results into a marker with a count that splits as the user zooms in, by server-side grid aggregation, client-side clustering or pre-rendered vector tiles. Aggregations can leak precise locations that pins hide. | [Searching by Place: Map, Polygon and Commute Search](#map-search) |
| **Saved search** | A persisted, normalized search query with an owner, a notification policy and a lifecycle, stored with the taxonomy and gazetteer versions it was written against, never as a URL or engine string. | [Tell Me When It Appears: Saved Searches and Alerts](#saved-searches-and-alerts) |
| **Percolation** | Matching a document against stored queries rather than a query against stored documents; also called reverse or prospective search. At scale it needs partitioning and pre-filter keys (category, place cells, price band) to select candidates. | [Tell Me When It Appears: Saved Searches and Alerts](#saved-searches-and-alerts) |
| **Match set** | For one saved search, the set of live, alertable listings that satisfy its query right now. Listings enter, stay in and leave it. | [Tell Me When It Appears: Saved Searches and Alerts](#saved-searches-and-alerts) |
| **Notify on entry, retract on exit** | The core alert rule: send a notification when a listing enters a match set, cancel pending notifications and digest items when it leaves, and ignore changes that keep it in. | [Tell Me When It Appears: Saved Searches and Alerts](#saved-searches-and-alerts) |
| **Watcher / price-drop alert** | A watcher is a user who has favorited a specific listing; a price-drop alert notifies watchers (and saved searches whose price limit the new price now meets) when a listing's price falls. | [Tell Me When It Appears: Saved Searches and Alerts](#saved-searches-and-alerts) |
| **Alert fan-out** | Turning matches into notifications across push, e-mail and in-app channels, with deduplication against a match ledger, frequency caps, quiet hours, consent and provider quotas. | [Tell Me When It Appears: Saved Searches and Alerts](#saved-searches-and-alerts) |
| **Digest** | A scheduled message collecting matches since the last digest; it needs retraction and a send-time availability check, or it becomes the most visible form of Ghost Inventory. | [Tell Me When It Appears: Saved Searches and Alerts](#saved-searches-and-alerts) |
| **Alert latency / alert-to-contact rate / ghost-alert rate** | Alert metrics: time from a listing becoming searchable to the notification being accepted by the provider; contacts within 48 hours per alert sent; and the share of alert items whose listing is unavailable when opened. | [Tell Me When It Appears: Saved Searches and Alerts](#saved-searches-and-alerts) |
| **Alert hold / risk gate** | A short delay before a new account's first listings fan out, and a rule that listings above a risk threshold are not alertable until cleared, because alerts amplify whatever they carry, including scams. | [Tell Me When It Appears: Saved Searches and Alerts](#saved-searches-and-alerts) |

## Part V — Trust

Keeping the marketplace safe and lawful. [Trust and Safety as a Platform](#trust-and-safety-architecture) owns the moderation pipeline, enforcement and T&S metrics; [Scams, Fakes and Account Takeovers: Fraud Prevention for Listings](#fraud-prevention) owns risk scoring and friction; [Who Is on the Other Side? Identity and Verification](#identity-and-verification) owns identity levels and badges; [Rules of the Marketplace: DSA, DAC7 and Vertical Regulation](#marketplace-regulation) owns the legal obligations and compliance by design. Regulatory names and status are given as of 2026; the regulation's own text wins over these summaries.

| Term | Definition | Chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Trust and safety (T&S)** | The capability that keeps listings, users, messages and transactions safe and lawful, in five functions (policy, detection, decision, enforcement, review and redress) plus measurement and transparency across all five. | [Trust and Safety as a Platform](#trust-and-safety-architecture) |
| **Policy / policy registry** | A policy is a named, versioned rule of conduct with a scope, a rationale and a legal or contractual ground. The registry holds policies as data, with country overlays, so that rules, models, reviewer guidelines, notices and reports cite the same policy ID and version. | [Trust and Safety as a Platform](#trust-and-safety-architecture) |
| **Moderation pipeline** | The components that evaluate content and behavior against policies and produce decisions: synchronous gates, asynchronous detectors, random sampling, human review queues and a decision service. | [Trust and Safety as a Platform](#trust-and-safety-architecture) |
| **Synchronous gate / asynchronous detectors** | The gate runs rules, list lookups and a light model inside the user's action (publish, edit, send, log in) and returns allow, hold or reject within a strict budget; detectors run on events afterwards (image models, stolen-photo search, LLM classifiers, price anomalies, graph features). | [Trust and Safety as a Platform](#trust-and-safety-architecture) |
| **Risk-tiered moderation** | Choosing pre- or post-moderation, and which checks run where, by category risk, seller trust level and listing risk score; a risk decision, not a technical one. | [Trust and Safety as a Platform](#trust-and-safety-architecture) |
| **Random sampling** | Drawing unbiased samples of live listings and messages for blind human labeling; the only way to measure prevalence and recall, and to correct the selection bias of labels from flagged items. | [Trust and Safety as a Platform](#trust-and-safety-architecture) |
| **Decision service / decision record** | The only component that writes moderation decisions, combining rule hits, scores, actor context and human input into an action; the decision record holds the decision, evidence, policy and model versions and reviewer, and every regulatory output is a view of it. | [Trust and Safety as a Platform](#trust-and-safety-architecture) |
| **Case / case management** | A case is the unit of human work: the items, evidence, decisions and communications that belong together. Case management provides queues, prioritization by expected harm times uncertainty, reviewer tooling and service-level timers. | [Trust and Safety as a Platform](#trust-and-safety-architecture) |
| **Enforcement action** | The concrete restriction a decision imposes: warning, reach limit, demotion, hold, removal, suspension or payment freeze, ordered on an enforcement ladder. | [Trust and Safety as a Platform](#trust-and-safety-architecture) |
| **Demotion** | Pushing content down in ranking for trust reasons. Under DSA Article 17 it is a restriction that needs a statement of reasons, so actor-trust down-ranking belongs behind the decision service, not inside a ranking feature. | [Trust and Safety as a Platform](#trust-and-safety-architecture), [Rules of the Marketplace: DSA, DAC7 and Vertical Regulation](#marketplace-regulation) |
| **Enforcement propagation** | Publishing `EnforcementApplied` and `EnforcementLifted` events and tracking each consumer's acknowledgement, so a removal reaches search, alerts, caches and SEO pages, and a reversal restores the listing and its ranking state. | [Trust and Safety as a Platform](#trust-and-safety-architecture) |
| **Notice and action** | The DSA mechanism by which anyone can notify a platform of illegal content, and the platform must process the notice and inform the notifier and the affected user. A notice is an entity with a lifecycle, not an e-mail. | [Trust and Safety as a Platform](#trust-and-safety-architecture), [Rules of the Marketplace: DSA, DAC7 and Vertical Regulation](#marketplace-regulation) |
| **Trusted flagger** | An organization granted that status by a national Digital Services Coordinator, whose notices platforms must process with priority. | [Trust and Safety as a Platform](#trust-and-safety-architecture) |
| **Appeal (internal complaint)** | A user's challenge to a decision through the free, electronic complaint system the DSA requires for at least six months after the decision. An appeal creates a new decision linked to the original, which is never overwritten. | [Trust and Safety as a Platform](#trust-and-safety-architecture) |
| **Statement of reasons** | The clear and specific explanation a platform must give a user for each restriction (removal, demotion, payment restriction, suspension), including facts, automation used, ground and redress, and submit without personal data to the DSA Transparency Database. | [Rules of the Marketplace: DSA, DAC7 and Vertical Regulation](#marketplace-regulation), [Trust and Safety as a Platform](#trust-and-safety-architecture) |
| **Prevalence / exposure-weighted prevalence** | The share of live listings or delivered messages that violate a policy, estimated from a blind random sample; and the same share weighted by views or contacts, which measures harm as buyers experience it. | [Trust and Safety as a Platform](#trust-and-safety-architecture) |
| **Time to action** | Time from first availability or first report to an enforcement acknowledged by all consumers, not merely decided. | [Trust and Safety as a Platform](#trust-and-safety-architecture) |
| **Precision / recall (T&S)** | Precision is the share of enforcement actions that were correct, from second review and appeals; recall is the share of violating items the pipeline caught, which only the random sample can estimate. | [Trust and Safety as a Platform](#trust-and-safety-architecture) |
| **Appeal overturn rate** | Reversed decisions as a share of appealed decisions, reported per policy and per detector. A reversed automated decision is a confirmed false positive and the best label the platform gets. | [Trust and Safety as a Platform](#trust-and-safety-architecture) |
| **Fraud signal** | An observable property of an item, actor, session or relationship that changes the probability of fraud: device, network, account, behavior and velocity, content, price, media reuse, graph links and external reports. | [Scams, Fakes and Account Takeovers: Fraud Prevention for Listings](#fraud-prevention) |
| **Risk score** | A calibrated estimate of the probability of fraud for a specific action, such as publishing a listing, sending a message, logging in or releasing a payout. | [Scams, Fakes and Account Takeovers: Fraud Prevention for Listings](#fraud-prevention) |
| **Risk scoring service** | A shared service that answers "how risky is this action, by this actor, right now?" for many callers, reading precomputed features and combining rules (fast, backtested, shadowed before enforcement) with models (durable). | [Scams, Fakes and Account Takeovers: Fraud Prevention for Listings](#fraud-prevention) |
| **Step-up friction** | An additional check imposed in proportion to risk, which genuine users pass easily and fraudsters do not, such as phone verification for a new account sending many first contacts. The threshold is set by an explicit cost model owned by the business. | [Scams, Fakes and Account Takeovers: Fraud Prevention for Listings](#fraud-prevention) |
| **Identity graph** | Links between accounts through shared devices, phone numbers, payment accounts, IP ranges, image hashes and near-identical text, used to propagate known fraud, detect rings and scope enforcement to a whole ring. | [Scams, Fakes and Account Takeovers: Fraud Prevention for Listings](#fraud-prevention) |
| **Authorized push payment (APP) fraud / purchase scam** | Fraud in which the victim willingly sends money, usually by bank transfer, to someone who is not who they claim to be. A purchase scam, paying for goods that never arrive, is the most common APP type, which is why classifieds controls are mostly pre-payment. | [Scams, Fakes and Account Takeovers: Fraud Prevention for Listings](#fraud-prevention) |
| **Payment-link phishing** | A fake buyer sends the seller a link or QR code to a counterfeit "payment received" or courier page that harvests card or bank details, often impersonating the platform's own payment product. | [Scams, Fakes and Account Takeovers: Fraud Prevention for Listings](#fraud-prevention), [From Interest to Contact: Messaging and Lead Management](#messaging-and-leads) |
| **Account takeover (ATO)** | Credentials phished or stuffed so that an aged, reputable account posts scam listings or redirects payouts. Defended with login risk, phishing-resistant authentication and step-up on sensitive actions. | [Scams, Fakes and Account Takeovers: Fraud Prevention for Listings](#fraud-prevention), [Who Is on the Other Side? Identity and Verification](#identity-and-verification) |
| **Verification of Payee** | A check, mandatory for euro-area payment providers before credit transfers since 9 October 2025 under the Instant Payments Regulation, that the payee name matches the account. It helps but does not stop a victim paying a real person acting as a mule. | [Scams, Fakes and Account Takeovers: Fraud Prevention for Listings](#fraud-prevention) |
| **Identity / account / profile / claim** | The identity is the person or organization the platform has evidence about; the account is the login; a profile is a brand-specific persona on an account; a claim is a statement about an identity with its method, source, assurance, time and expiry. | [Who Is on the Other Side? Identity and Verification](#identity-and-verification) |
| **Identity assurance / authenticator assurance** | Two separate confidences, after NIST SP 800-63-4 (2025): how sure the service is that a person is who they claim to be, and how sure it is that the person presenting the credential is the account holder. A single "verified" flag hides which one is missing. | [Who Is on the Other Side? Identity and Verification](#identity-and-verification) |
| **Identity level** | A named combination of claims that a policy can require, derived and recomputed from claims rather than stored. The book's ladder runs from anonymous and contactable (confirmed e-mail) through reachable, protected (passkey or second factor) and identified (proof of person) to payee-verified. | [Who Is on the Other Side? Identity and Verification](#identity-and-verification) |
| **KYB / KYC** | Know your business: verification that an organization exists and is who it claims, on a business ladder from declared trader to registered business to accredited professional. Know your customer: the payments-industry checks a regulated payment provider must run before a user can receive money. | [Who Is on the Other Side? Identity and Verification](#identity-and-verification) |
| **Verified badge** | A public statement the marketplace makes to one user about another, such as "identity verified" or "registered business". It must say what was checked and disappear when the claim expires or is revoked. | [Who Is on the Other Side? Identity and Verification](#identity-and-verification) |
| **Passkey** | A phishing-resistant authentication credential based on FIDO standards that the user unlocks with a biometric, PIN or pattern on their device; bound to the site, with no shared secret to steal. | [Who Is on the Other Side? Identity and Verification](#identity-and-verification) |
| **Pre-hijacking** | An attack in which someone creates an account with a victim's e-mail address before the victim does and keeps access afterwards. Merging accounts across brands by matching e-mail addresses recreates it. | [Who Is on the Other Side? Identity and Verification](#identity-and-verification) |
| **eIDAS 2.0 / EU Digital Identity Wallet** | The revised EU regulation on electronic identification and its wallet, through which citizens can prove identity attributes; private platforms must accept it only in defined cases and after transition periods. Bank-based schemes such as BankID play the same role in some countries. | [Who Is on the Other Side? Identity and Verification](#identity-and-verification) |
| **Digital Services Act (DSA)** | Regulation (EU) 2022/2065, applicable to all platforms since 17 February 2024: notice and action, statements of reasons and the Transparency Database, trader traceability, internal complaints, transparency reports, trusted flaggers, ad and recommender transparency, and extra duties for very large platforms. | [Rules of the Marketplace: DSA, DAC7 and Vertical Regulation](#marketplace-regulation) |
| **Hosting service / online platform / very large online platform** | The DSA's nested categories: a service storing information for users; one that also disseminates it to the public; and one with more than 45 million average monthly EU users, which carries additional risk-assessment and audit duties. | [Rules of the Marketplace: DSA, DAC7 and Vertical Regulation](#marketplace-regulation) |
| **DSA Transparency Database** | The European Commission's public database of statements of reasons, accepting single statements or batches of up to 100 that fail as a whole if one is invalid. | [Rules of the Marketplace: DSA, DAC7 and Vertical Regulation](#marketplace-regulation) |
| **P2B Regulation** | Regulation (EU) 2019/1150 on fairness for business users of online intermediation services: clear terms, disclosure of the main ranking parameters and of any differentiated treatment of the provider's own services, and access-to-data descriptions. | [Rules of the Marketplace: DSA, DAC7 and Vertical Regulation](#marketplace-regulation), [Ranking for Two Sides: Relevance, Freshness and Paid Visibility](#ranking) |
| **Omnibus Directive / UCPD** | Directive (EU) 2019/2161 amended EU consumer law, including the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive (UCPD): undisclosed paid ranking is always unfair, ranking parameters must be disclosed, review authenticity claims need checks, and price reductions must show the prior 30-day lowest price. | [Rules of the Marketplace: DSA, DAC7 and Vertical Regulation](#marketplace-regulation) |
| **General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)** | Regulation (EU) 2016/679. For listing systems its main architectural principles are data minimization, storage limitation and accountability, plus erasure rights and limits on solely automated decisions. | [Rules of the Marketplace: DSA, DAC7 and Vertical Regulation](#marketplace-regulation) |
| **DAC7** | Council Directive (EU) 2021/514 on tax reporting by digital platforms: a reporting platform operator collects and verifies seller information and reports each reportable seller's consideration per quarter to the tax authority by 31 January of the following year. | [Rules of the Marketplace: DSA, DAC7 and Vertical Regulation](#marketplace-regulation) |
| **Reporting platform operator / excluded seller** | Under DAC7, an operator that connects sellers with users for relevant activities (sale of goods, rental of property or transport, personal services) beyond mere listing; a seller with fewer than 30 sales of goods and no more than €2,000 consideration in the period is excluded. | [Rules of the Marketplace: DSA, DAC7 and Vertical Regulation](#marketplace-regulation) |
| **Obligation / control / evidence** | The three artifacts of compliance: a duty in a legal source with its article, jurisdiction and conditions; the system behavior that meets it; and the data and query that prove the control worked. | [Rules of the Marketplace: DSA, DAC7 and Vertical Regulation](#marketplace-regulation) |
| **Compliance by design / compliance by project** | Building controls into platform components with the evidence query written at the same time, versus building one exporter per new obligation. The first is cheaper from the second law onward. | [Rules of the Marketplace: DSA, DAC7 and Vertical Regulation](#marketplace-regulation) |
| **UK Online Safety Act / DMCC Act / US INFORM Act** | Non-EU rules with marketplace consequences: the UK's online-safety duties, the UK Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024's consumer-protection powers, and the US INFORM Consumers Act's verification and disclosure duties for high-volume third-party sellers. | [Rules of the Marketplace: DSA, DAC7 and Vertical Regulation](#marketplace-regulation) |
| **Fair housing rules** | US rules (24 CFR Part 100) that prohibit housing advertising indicating a preference on protected grounds; they constrain listing text, ad targeting, recommendations and neighborhood data. | [Rules of the Marketplace: DSA, DAC7 and Vertical Regulation](#marketplace-regulation), [Searching by Place: Map, Polygon and Commute Search](#map-search) |

| **Lead contract** | One versioned event and API schema for leads across a group: stable lead ID, source channel, timestamps, listing and seller references, consented contact data, typed qualification fields and the routing decision, with each delivery adapter implementing it. | [From Interest to Contact: Messaging and Lead Management](#messaging-and-leads) |
| **Lead qualification / lead attribution** | Qualification adds what the professional needs to judge a lead (financing, trade-in, pre-qualification); attribution connects a lead to a sale, mandate, lease or hire and reports it in a way both sides accept. | [From Interest to Contact: Messaging and Lead Management](#messaging-and-leads) |
| **Transactional pivot** | A classifieds marketplace moving its endpoint from contact to completed payment and delivery, typically starting with shipping and protected payment for private sales; leboncoin, Kleinanzeigen and Marktplaats have all added transactional layers on top of classifieds. | [Beyond the Contact: Payments, Shipping and Buyer Protection](#transactions-and-payments) |
| **Marketplace payments** | Taking payment from a buyer, holding it and paying it out to a seller, usually through a payment service provider's marketplace product so that the provider, not the marketplace, is the licensed party. | [Beyond the Contact: Payments, Shipping and Buyer Protection](#transactions-and-payments), [Payments for Finance Architects: Eleven Reasons the Numbers Disagree](https://zeljkoobrenovic.github.io/spec-driven-journals/finance-systems-architecture/payments-fundamentals.html) |
| **Payment service provider (PSP)** | A company that processes payments for merchants and platforms; its marketplace product handles onboarding and KYC of payees, split payments, holds and payouts. | [Beyond the Contact: Payments, Shipping and Buyer Protection](#transactions-and-payments) |
| **Merchant of record** | The party whose name is on the buyer's payment statement and which carries the payment-side obligations, refunds and chargebacks. In protected private sales the seller usually remains the seller and the platform an intermediary. | [Beyond the Contact: Payments, Shipping and Buyer Protection](#transactions-and-payments), [Payments for Finance Architects: Eleven Reasons the Numbers Disagree](https://zeljkoobrenovic.github.io/spec-driven-journals/finance-systems-architecture/payments-fundamentals.html) |
| **Buyer protection** | A promise that the buyer gets their money back if the item does not arrive or is not as described, funded by a buyer fee. It is a liability to provision, not only a fee to collect. | [Beyond the Contact: Payments, Shipping and Buyer Protection](#transactions-and-payments), [How Listing Marketplaces Make Money](#monetization-models) |
| **Hold and release (escrow-like hold) / payout** | Keeping the buyer's payment in a held balance at the licensed provider until a release event (buyer confirmation, a delivery scan plus a waiting period, a handover code), then making it available for payout to the seller's bank account. Often called escrow informally, though legally it usually is not; payouts and payout-account changes are prime fraud targets. | [Beyond the Contact: Payments, Shipping and Buyer Protection](#transactions-and-payments) |
| **Order state machine** | The states of a protected sale, held on the order rather than the listing: placed, paid (listing reserved), awaiting hand-in or pickup, in transit, delivered, completed, disputed, refunded, cancelled or returned, with a card chargeback possible after completion. Every waiting state has a deadline, and each transition emits events for the ledger, trust and safety and messaging. | [Beyond the Contact: Payments, Shipping and Buyer Protection](#transactions-and-payments) |
| **Dispute / chargeback** | A dispute is opened with the platform under its buyer protection; a chargeback is opened by the buyer's card issuer under card-scheme rules. Both can occur for the same order, on different clocks. | [Beyond the Contact: Payments, Shipping and Buyer Protection](#transactions-and-payments) |
| **Monetization model** | The combination of who pays, for which unit, at which moment in the funnel and on what pricing basis. Each part becomes a system, and each model spends a different part of liquidity. | [How Listing Marketplaces Make Money](#monetization-models) |
| **Price structure / unit of charge** | Two-sided markets set a price structure, which side pays and for what, not just a price level. The unit of charge is the thing whose quantity sets the bill: a listing, a slot, a lead, a sale, an impression. | [How Listing Marketplaces Make Money](#monetization-models) |
| **Average revenue per advertiser (ARPA)** | Rightmove's KPI: monthly revenue from agency and new-homes advertisers divided by the number of advertisers, averaged over the year. Autotrader's equivalent per retailer forecourt is ARPR (average revenue per retailer). | [How Listing Marketplaces Make Money](#monetization-models) |
| **Take rate** | Marketplace revenue divided by gross merchandise value, as eBay defines it: the share of transaction value the platform keeps. | [How Listing Marketplaces Make Money](#monetization-models) |
| **Value-added services (VAS)** | The industry name for paid options bought on top of a basic listing: bumps, top or featured positions, highlights and galleries, longer durations. The core of the freemium paid-visibility model. | [How Listing Marketplaces Make Money](#monetization-models) |

It is an architectural event, planned as a workstream separate from any platform migration; organic search traffic is the main risk. | [After the Deal: Consolidating Platforms After Acquisitions](#platform-consolidation) |
| **Marketplace team topology** | The assignment of team types to a marketplace group's journeys, bounded contexts and shared capabilities, together with the interaction mode between each pair of teams. | [Who Owns What: Organizing Marketplace Engineering](#organizing-marketplace-engineering) |
| **Stream-aligned / platform / complicated-subsystem / enabling team** | Team Topologies' four types: teams on a flow of work, such as a buyer or seller journey; teams providing an internal product with a contract; teams owning work that needs deep expertise, such as ranking or AVMs; and temporary teams that help others adopt or migrate. | [Who Owns What: Organizing Marketplace Engineering](#organizing-marketplace-engineering) |
| **Architecture governance** | The decision rights, forums, records, principles and standards through which architectural decisions are made, communicated and revisited. | [Who Owns What: Organizing Marketplace Engineering](#organizing-marketplace-engineering) |
| **Decision rights / decision record** | Who may decide what (group, platform, brand, team); and the short document capturing a decision's context, decision, status, consequences and review date, kept with the code. An architecture decision record (ADR) is the common form. | [Who Owns What: Organizing Marketplace Engineering](#organizing-marketplace-engineering) |
| **Advice process** | Andrew Harmel-Law's model in which anyone can make an architectural decision, provided they first seek advice from everyone meaningfully affected and from relevant experts, and record it. Advice is public and decisions are recorded. | [Who Owns What: Organizing Marketplace Engineering](#organizing-marketplace-engineering) |
| **Platform funding models** | Ways to pay for platforms: a central program budget, fixed allocation (a "platform tax"), showback of usage-based cost without charging, chargeback by usage, or an internal market. Policy-driven platforms such as identity and trust and safety suit fixed allocation. | [Who Owns What: Organizing Marketplace Engineering](#organizing-marketplace-engineering) |
| **Conway's law** | The observation that systems tend to mirror the communication structure of the organization that builds them, which makes team design an architectural decision. | [Who Owns What: Organizing Marketplace Engineering](#organizing-marketplace-engineering) |
| **Strangler fig** | Replacing a legacy system piece by piece by routing traffic for one capability at a time to new components, until the old system can be switched off. | [Changing the Engine in Flight: Modernizing a Live Marketplace](#modernization-and-migration) |
| **Branch by abstraction / event interception** | Two migration techniques: putting an abstraction in front of a capability so old and new implementations can be swapped behind it; and capturing the legacy system's changes as events so new components can be fed without changing its code. | [Changing the Engine in Flight: Modernizing a Live Marketplace](#modernization-and-migration) |
| **Parallel run / shadow traffic / canary** | Three ways of running old and new side by side. Shadow traffic (a dark launch) sends the new system a copy of real requests or events and discards its output after comparison; a parallel run has both systems compute on real requests, with one authoritative and the other compared; a canary serves a small share of real traffic from the new system. | [Changing the Engine in Flight: Modernizing a Live Marketplace](#modernization-and-migration) |
| **Migration stage gate** | A decision point in moving one slice of a system, with exit criteria agreed beforehand and a defined rollback, that must be passed before more traffic or responsibility moves. Veldmark uses five per slice: contract, shadow, canary, ramp and retire; the move to retire is the recorded point of no return. | [Changing the Engine in Flight: Modernizing a Live Marketplace](#modernization-and-migration) |
| **Relevance parity** | The condition that a new search path produces outcomes for buyers and sellers that are not worse than the old path's, within agreed tolerances, measured on real queries (replayed comparisons of top results, then online tests); not identical results. | [Changing the Engine in Flight: Modernizing a Live Marketplace](#modernization-and-migration) |
| **URL migration** | The controlled change of public URLs, or the deliberate decision not to change them, with old-to-new mappings, permanent redirects, consistent canonicals and sitemaps, and monitoring of crawl and traffic that preserve search-engine signals. | [Changing the Engine in Flight: Modernizing a Live Marketplace](#modernization-and-migration), [Found by Google: Web, App and SEO Architecture for Listings](#web-and-seo-architecture) |
| **Feature parity trap** | The mistake of insisting that a new system reproduce every feature of the old one before launch, including features nobody uses, which delays migration indefinitely. The remedy is a behavior catalog in which each legacy behavior is carried, changed or retired. | [Changing the Engine in Flight: Modernizing a Live Marketplace](#modernization-and-migration) |
| **AI listing assistant** | A feature in the insertion flow that proposes listing content (category, attributes, title, description, price) from photos, voice or short text, for the seller to accept, edit or reject. | [AI Across the Listing Lifecycle](#ai-in-listing-systems) |
| **Structured-output compiler** | The pattern in which a model translates unstructured input into a structure the marketplace already has (an attribute set, a filter query, a policy label), so the structured system of record stays in charge. | [AI Across the Listing Lifecycle](#ai-in-listing-systems) |
| **Agentic search** | Search in which a model plans steps and calls tools (keyword or vector search, filters, comparisons, external lookups) over several turns, rather than returning one ranked list for one query; suited to high-consideration journeys. | [AI Across the Listing Lifecycle](#ai-in-listing-systems) |
| **Agent-to-marketplace interface / Model Context Protocol (MCP)** | An interface through which external AI agents call the marketplace's capabilities as tools; MCP is an open protocol, introduced by Anthropic in 2024, for connecting agents to tools and data. | [AI Across the Listing Lifecycle](#ai-in-listing-systems) |
| **Autonomy level** | How far an AI feature acts without a person: it suggests, drafts for confirmation, acts with confirmation, or acts autonomously. | [AI Across the Listing Lifecycle](#ai-in-listing-systems) |
| **AI governance for marketplaces** | The register, rules and controls that decide which AI uses may run, at what autonomy, with which data, evaluated how, at what cost and with whom accountable. | [AI Across the Listing Lifecycle](#ai-in-listing-systems) |
| **AI gateway / evaluation harness** | The gateway is the control point every model call passes (authentication, routing, rate limits, cost metering, logging, a kill switch per feature); the harness runs golden sets per language, category and use before every prompt or model change and on sampled production traffic. | [AI Across the Listing Lifecycle](#ai-in-listing-systems) |
| **EU AI Act** | Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, with obligations phased in from 2025: transparency for generated content (Article 50), duties for general-purpose AI model providers including respecting text-and-data-mining reservations, and stricter rules for high-risk uses. | [AI Across the Listing Lifecycle](#ai-in-listing-systems) |
| **Stressor analysis** | Listing what could happen to a marketplace group (a sale, a law, a fraud wave, a lost channel), recording for each the attractor, what breaks in the current architecture and the residue that would survive it, then counting which components and contracts carry the most residues so they get the most care. Adapted from Barry M. O'Reilly's residuality theory. | [Built to Be Replaced: Evolving a Marketplace Platform Over a Decade](#long-term-evolution) |
| **Stressor / residue / attractor** | A stressor is anything that could happen to the business or its environment, likely or not, technical or not; the attractor is the state the business is pushed into when it hits (how people, partners and the market will actually behave, such as a carve-out pushing it into transitional services); the residue is what remains of the architecture afterwards, and the structural change that would let it survive. | [Built to Be Replaced: Evolving a Marketplace Platform Over a Decade](#long-term-evolution) |
| **Marketplace fitness function** | A measured, owned, regularly evaluated statement about a stage of the liquidity machine, such as "a sold listing disappears from every registered consumer within two minutes at p95". Unlike a business KPI it has an architectural owner and remedy; unlike an SLO it measures the whole machine, not one service. Veldmark's scorecard has twelve. | [Built to Be Replaced: Evolving a Marketplace Platform Over a Decade](#long-term-evolution) |
| **Optionality / separability** | Keeping future choices open: listing data and events in portable, documented forms, and every tenant able to leave the platform, so a sale, a merger or a new vertical does not force a rebuild. | [Built to Be Replaced: Evolving a Marketplace Platform Over a Decade](#long-term-evolution), [One Platform, Many Marketplaces: Multi-Country and Multi-Brand Architecture](#multi-country-platforms) |
