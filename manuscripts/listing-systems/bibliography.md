# Bibliography: Sources and Evidence

![Bibliography: Sources and Evidence — logo](listing-systems-architecture/posts/bibliography/assets/images/bibliography/logo.jpeg)

> **IN THIS SECTION, YOU WILL:** Find where the book's evidence comes from: each source once, with its year, its evidence type and the chapters that rely on it, findable by topic or by chapter.

> **KEY POINTS:**
>
> * **Sources are listed because they were cited.** Every entry was harvested from a page's **Sources and Evidence** section, and its **Cited in** line names those pages. Nothing is added for completeness, and a listing does not mean every page of a long document was read.
> * **Dates and evidence types tell you how far to trust a claim.** A company's engineering post records what it said it built in that year, a filing records reported numbers, a regulation records a requirement and trade press records an event. Weigh each claim by the kind and age of the source behind it.
> * **Keep documented facts, inferences and Veldmark distinct.** The sources support the documented facts. Reference architectures for real companies are labeled inferences, the recommendations are my judgement, and Veldmark, the marketplace group the book follows, is fictional: no source here describes it.

> **[FAILURE PATTERNS THIS SECTION ADDRESSES](#where-listing-systems-go-wrong):**
>
> * **The Forced Fit** — company engineering posts are the largest evidence type on this page, and each describes one group's platform at one date; reading the year and the citing context keeps a published design from becoming a mandate for markets it was not built for.

This page lists the external sources the chapters cite. The research was conducted in **October 2026**, and most entries were consulted then; where a chapter recorded an access date, a version or a "last updated" date, the entry keeps it. Some company pages blocked automated access during the research, and a few facts are known only through **secondary summaries** (a trade-press article or a third-party digest of an engineering post); the entries say so where the citing chapter did. Ownership and brand names follow the sources as of 2026: Schibsted is now Vend, eBay Kleinanzeigen is now Kleinanzeigen, and the former eBay Classifieds Group is part of Adevinta.

## How to Read This Register

**R** marks a consulted source; the numbers identify, they do not rank. The register holds **587 sources** harvested from the **Sources and Evidence** sections of 55 pages, deduplicated by normalized URL (scheme, `www.`, trailing slash, fragment, `.html` suffix and arXiv version ignored); where chapters cited the same legal text or page under two URLs, the entry keeps both. Identifiers were assigned in topic order for this first edition and stay stable: **new sources append from the next free R-number and are never renumbered**, and a removed source's number is retired rather than reused, so a reference to an identifier keeps meaning the same document.

Each entry gives the identifier, the linked title, the author or publisher, the **publication year as the citing chapter gave it** ("n.d." where the source carries no date, with access or update dates kept where the chapter recorded them), one clause on what the source supports, the evidence type and the chapters that cite it. Company engineering evidence carries its year because old posts may describe systems that have since been replaced; a post more than about three years old is a record of that date, not of today. Where chapters cited one source with slightly different metadata, the entry keeps the wording most chapters used, and where they gave different years it keeps the most specific citation.

Those briefs are working notes, not public evidence, and are not listed; wherever a brief's fact matters, the chapter cites the public source behind it, and that source is listed below.
