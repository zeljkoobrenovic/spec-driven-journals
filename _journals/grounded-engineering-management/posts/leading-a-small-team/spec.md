# Spec: Leading a Small Team

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State my operating model for a team where I directly manage every member,
grounded in the small-team chapter of Julie Zhuo's *The Making of a Manager*:
trust as the foundation everything else runs on (tested by "would my team
choose to work for me again?"), weekly 1:1s owned by the report's priorities
and growth rather than status, transparent expectations and early performance
conversations, the skill–motivation–fit diagnosis before any fix,
disproportionate investment in strengths and strong performers, zero
tolerance for brilliant jerks, and quick, respectful moves when fit is
genuinely wrong. The load-bearing idea: on a small team management is a
handful of relationships, trust is the channel all management information
travels through, and every hard call (performance, attention allocation,
exits) gets a diagnosis before a treatment.

## Audience

First-line managers in my organization (the week-to-week bar I hold them to);
my own reports (what they can expect from my 1:1s and my honesty about
performance); peer executives comparing operating models. First-person
declarative.

## Success criteria

- [x] **Principle is quotable** — highlight names trust as the foundation,
      the "work for me again" test, growth-not-status 1:1s, transparent
      performance, the skill/motivation/fit diagnosis, strengths investment,
      and quick respectful exits.
- [x] **The 1:1 shape survives** — weekly per report, focused on priorities/
      growth/obstacles, questions before advice, and the three questions
      ("What's top of mind?", "How can I help?", "What was most useful?").
- [x] **The diagnosis survives** — every performance problem is classified
      as skill, motivation, or fit before acting, with different responses
      for each.
- [x] **The strengths stance survives** — name strengths specifically,
      create opportunities, disproportionate time on strong people, low
      performance may not consume all attention.
- [x] **Culture and fit survive** — no brilliant jerks, one divider reduces
      the whole team, better-fit roles explored first, exits quick and
      respectful.
- [x] **Credit is explicit** — How to Read This and References name Julie
      Zhuo, *The Making of a Manager* (Portfolio/Penguin, 2019).

## Non-goals

- Not [[feedback]] — that record covers the craft of giving and receiving
  feedback; this one only requires that it flows both ways in the 1:1.
- Not [[first-three-months]] — that record covers the ramp that builds the
  initial trust; this one is the steady state it hands off to.
- Not [[managing-a-growing-team]] — this record assumes I directly manage
  every member; what changes when that stops being true lives there.
- Not [[career-conversations]] (people journal) — the one-off deep career
  conversation is its own tool; this record's weekly 1:1 consumes its
  output rather than replacing it.
