*The working checklist behind this record — the one I hand an engineer mapping their career terrain. The Article tab carries the rationale and anti-patterns.*

## 1. Understand your company type

- [ ] I have identified what kind of company I work for or am targeting:
  - Big Tech
  - Medium-to-large tech company
  - Scaleup
  - Startup
  - Traditional company with a tech division
  - Technology-heavy traditional company
  - Small non-venture-funded company
  - Public sector
  - Nonprofit
  - Consultancy / outsourcing / agency
  - Academia or research lab
- [ ] I have compared the company type against my goals for compensation, stability, growth, impact, and work-life balance.
- [ ] I have talked to people who work at similar companies to understand the real day-to-day experience.
- [ ] I remember that team quality can matter as much as company category.

## 2. Choose a realistic career path

- [ ] I have decided whether I am more interested in a path as an individual contributor or in management.
- [ ] I know whether my company has a single-track career ladder or a dual-track IC and manager ladder.
- [ ] I have identified the highest realistic IC level at my current company.
- [ ] I have checked whether advancement beyond senior/staff requires moving into management.
- [ ] I have considered whether I might switch between IC and manager tracks over time.

## 3. Think beyond the "standard" path

- [ ] I have considered which career pattern fits me best:
  - Lifelong software engineer
  - Specialist
  - Generalist/specialist pendulum
  - Engineer in a niche field
  - Contractor or freelancer
  - Tech lead
  - Engineering manager
  - Founder or business owner
  - Non-software role such as PM, DevRel, TPM, recruiting
  - Engineer/manager pendulum
  - Combination path
  - Non-linear career path
- [ ] I do not assume there is only one "good" career path.
- [ ] I have reviewed examples of people with non-linear careers.

## 4. Understand compensation tiers

- [ ] I can identify whether a company is likely Tier 1, Tier 2, or Tier 3 for compensation.
- [ ] I compare total compensation, not just salary: base salary, cash bonus, and equity.
- [ ] I understand whether equity is liquid or illiquid.
- [ ] I am cautious about private-company equity that may never become valuable.
- [ ] I compare compensation expectations with job difficulty and performance expectations.

## 5. Compare tradeoffs between tiers

- [ ] For Tier 1 companies, I have checked whether I value stability, easier hiring access, or better work-life balance.
- [ ] For Tier 2 companies, I have checked whether I want stronger pay with more demanding expectations.
- [ ] For Tier 3 companies, I have checked whether I am prepared for very high hiring difficulty and demanding performance standards.
- [ ] I have compared expected career growth: can ICs progress beyond senior? Beyond staff? Are manager paths clearer than IC paths?
- [ ] I have considered whether the highest pay is worth the pressure.

## 6. Evaluate profit center vs. cost center

- [ ] I know whether my team directly generates revenue.
- [ ] I know whether my team's impact is visible to executives.
- [ ] I know whether my work supports a core product, internal tooling, compliance, infrastructure, or operations.
- [ ] I watch which teams receive funding, headcount, and executive attention.
- [ ] I have assessed how my team's position affects promotions, bonuses, internal transfers, attrition risk, and job security.
- [ ] I am learning to succeed in both profit centers and cost centers.

## 7. Broaden how you measure career progress

- [ ] I do not evaluate my career only by title, company name, and compensation.
- [ ] I also evaluate:
  - The people I work with
  - My manager relationship
  - My position within the team and company
  - Company culture
  - Company mission
  - Professional growth
  - Mental and physical health
  - Flexibility of time and place
  - Ability to disconnect from work
  - Stress level
  - Life outside work
  - Personal motivation
- [ ] I have decided what balance matters most for my current life stage.
- [ ] I revisit my priorities before changing jobs.

## 8. Own your career development

- [ ] I do not assume my manager will handle my career progression for me.
- [ ] I know what promotion and performance expectations are at my company.
- [ ] I have asked what additional activities are needed for recognition.
- [ ] I make my impact visible.
- [ ] I seek regular feedback.
- [ ] I build relationships with peers, managers, and senior engineers.
- [ ] I track accomplishments and outcomes.
- [ ] I reassess my path regularly as my goals change.
