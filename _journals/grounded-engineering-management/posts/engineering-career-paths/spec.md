# Spec: Engineering Career Paths

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State how I want engineers to understand the terrain their careers run
across — because career advice that ignores company type, ladder shape, and
compensation tier is noise. The post turns the *Software Engineer's
Guidebook*'s Engineering Career Paths chapter into an operating principle: know
what kind of company you are at (Big Tech through public sector through
consultancy), know the ladder (single vs dual track, highest realistic IC
level), take non-standard paths seriously (specialist pendulums,
engineer/manager pendulums, founders, non-linear careers), understand
compensation tiers and the tradeoffs each tier prices in, know whether your
team is a profit center or a cost center and what that does to promotions
and job security, and measure career progress on more axes than title,
company, and compensation. The load-bearing idea: careers run on terrain,
and I coach the terrain honestly — including the parts my company cannot
offer.

## Audience

Engineers on my teams (so career conversations run on a shared, honest map);
managers I hold to the same coaching standard; peer executives comparing
operating models. Manager's voice in the article; the Checklist tab is the
checklist I hand the engineer, in the engineer's first person.

## Success criteria

- [x] **Principle is quotable** — highlight states that careers run on
      terrain (company type, ladder, tier, profit/cost center) and that I
      coach the terrain honestly, including what my company can't offer.
- [x] **Company types survive** — the source's taxonomy is represented
      (Big Tech, medium-to-large, scaleup, startup, traditional-with-tech,
      tech-heavy traditional, small non-VC, public sector, nonprofit,
      consultancy, academia), with team quality mattering as much as
      category.
- [x] **Ladder realism survives** — single vs dual track, highest realistic
      IC level, whether advancement past senior/staff requires management,
      IC/manager switching over time.
- [x] **Non-standard paths survive** — the source's list of career patterns
      and the warning against assuming one "good" path.
- [x] **Tiers and tradeoffs survive** — Tier 1/2/3, total compensation
      (base, bonus, equity; liquid vs illiquid), and the tradeoffs between
      stability, pay, hiring difficulty, and pressure.
- [x] **Profit vs cost center survives** — revenue linkage, executive
      visibility, and the effect on promotions, bonuses, transfers,
      attrition risk, and job security.
- [x] **Broader measures survive** — the source's list of non-title axes
      (people, manager, culture, health, flexibility, stress, life outside
      work), plus revisiting priorities per life stage.
- [x] **Credit is explicit** — References name Gergely Orosz, *The Software
      Engineer's Guidebook*.
