# Spec: Thriving in Different Environments

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State the expectation that engineers read their environment and adapt to it,
because standout work is defined by context: product teams reward product
thinking and outcome validation, platform teams reward customer empathy for
internal users; wartime rewards speed and pragmatism, peacetime rewards
quality and alliance-building; Big Tech rewards tracked individual impact
and relationships, scaleups reward applied tenure and knowledge, startups
reward initiative toward product-market fit. The post turns the *Software
Engineer's Guidebook*'s Thriving in Different Environments chapter into an
operating principle: identify your team type, recognize the company's mode,
adjust behavior to the mode, understand your company type — and hold the
universal set (know what the company values, do standout work by its
standards, help others succeed, keep relationships, avoid burning bridges,
reassess when the environment shifts). The load-bearing idea: there is no
context-free "great engineer" — the same behavior that earns promotion in
one environment stalls a career in another, and reading the environment is
itself a core skill I coach and hold engineers to.

## Audience

Engineers on my teams (so they understand why expectations differ across
teams and company phases); managers I hold to the same coaching standard;
peer executives comparing operating models. Manager's voice in the article;
the Checklist tab is the checklist I hand the engineer, in the engineer's
first person.

## Success criteria

- [x] **Principle is quotable** — highlight states there is no context-free
      "great engineer": standout work is defined by the environment, and
      reading the environment is itself a core skill.
- [x] **Team types survive** — product-team behaviors (user problem,
      outcome validation, product intuition) and platform-team behaviors
      (internal customers, proactive communication, downstream impact).
- [x] **Wartime/peacetime survives** — the recognition signs for each mode
      and the distinct thriving behaviors for each, including stress and
      burnout management in wartime and avoiding stagnation in peacetime.
- [x] **Company types survive** — Big Tech (tracked impact, evidence,
      relationships), scaleup (tenure, knowledge, cost/profit-center
      awareness, alertness in restructuring), startup (product-market fit,
      autonomy, founder visibility, value over tasks).
- [x] **The universal set survives** — know what the company values, adapt
      the working style, standout work by the company's standards, help
      others, keep relationships, resolve conflict professionally, avoid
      burning bridges, reassess mode shifts.
- [x] **Credit is explicit** — References name Gergely Orosz, *The Software
      Engineer's Guidebook*.

## Non-goals

- Not [[engineering-career-paths]] — that record is choosing and evaluating
  environments; this one is thriving inside the one you are in.
- Not [[changing-jobs]] — when the environment and the engineer are truly
  mismatched, that record covers the move.
- Not a company-strategy record — wartime/peacetime is used here as a
  read-and-adapt skill for engineers, not as executive doctrine.
