*The working checklist behind this record — the one I hand an engineer landing in a new team or watching the company change around them. The Article tab carries the rationale and anti-patterns.*

## 1. Identify your team type

**Product team**

- [ ] I understand the customer/user problem my team is solving.
- [ ] I know how my work affects product, business, or user outcomes.
- [ ] I regularly talk with product managers, designers, data scientists, support staff, and users.
- [ ] I suggest product improvements, not just implement specs.
- [ ] I think through edge cases and tradeoffs before shipping.
- [ ] I validate whether a feature worked after launch.
- [ ] I build product intuition over time by learning from outcomes.

**Platform team**

- [ ] I understand who uses my platform and what they need.
- [ ] I know whether my customers are internal engineers, product teams, business teams, or end users.
- [ ] I make it easy for users to contact me and give feedback.
- [ ] I communicate proactively with teams building on the platform.
- [ ] I work side by side with platform customers when useful.
- [ ] I spend time with product teams to understand downstream impact.
- [ ] I balance deep focus with urgency when priorities change.

## 2. Recognize company mode: wartime or peacetime

**Wartime signs**

- [ ] The company faces existential or intense competitive pressure.
- [ ] Shipping quickly matters more than perfect quality.
- [ ] Deadlines are treated as mandatory.
- [ ] Meetings are reduced in favor of execution.
- [ ] Conflict is tolerated if it helps get important work done.
- [ ] Processes may be bypassed to move faster.
- [ ] Work-life balance is weak or deprioritized.

**Peacetime signs**

- [ ] The business has little immediate pressure.
- [ ] Quality, alignment, and validation matter more than speed.
- [ ] Deadlines are useful but not always critical.
- [ ] Meetings are common to maintain alignment.
- [ ] Conflict is discouraged.
- [ ] Process is expected to be followed.
- [ ] Work-life balance is a visible priority.

## 3. Adjust your behavior to the mode

**To thrive in wartime**

- [ ] I prioritize getting important work done quickly.
- [ ] I accept "good enough" when speed matters more than perfection.
- [ ] I don't take blunt feedback or conflict personally.
- [ ] I build only the alliances needed to unblock execution.
- [ ] I stay aligned with my manager and skip-level leadership.
- [ ] I focus on business-critical priorities.
- [ ] I treat job stability as uncertain and keep options open.
- [ ] I manage stress and burnout actively.

**To thrive in peacetime**

- [ ] I prioritize high-quality execution.
- [ ] I avoid unnecessary conflict.
- [ ] I build relationships and alliances across teams.
- [ ] I manage upward, sideways, and downward.
- [ ] I work on longer-term initiatives that benefit the business.
- [ ] I use stability to improve systems and processes.
- [ ] I keep momentum without stagnating.
- [ ] I avoid becoming too comfortable or bored.

## 4. Understand your company type

**Big Tech / large tech company**

- [ ] I know whether my team is closer to product or platform work.
- [ ] I track my individual impact clearly.
- [ ] I keep evidence of achievements for reviews and promotion.
- [ ] I build relationships across the organization.
- [ ] I help others, knowing relationships may matter later.
- [ ] I understand how performance reviews and promotions work.

**Mid- or late-stage scaleup**

- [ ] I understand whether tenure is valuable in my organization.
- [ ] I use company knowledge to create impact.
- [ ] I help improve onboarding or knowledge sharing.
- [ ] I know whether my team is viewed as a cost center or profit center.
- [ ] I understand the company's growth phase and risk level.
- [ ] I stay alert during slowing growth or restructuring.

**Early-stage startup**

- [ ] I understand how my work helps the company reach product-market fit.
- [ ] I talk with founders, teammates, and customers to find high-impact work.
- [ ] I know the company may be risky or unstable.
- [ ] I use autonomy to move fast and take initiative.
- [ ] I make sure founders and leaders are aware of impactful work.
- [ ] I focus on creating value, not just completing assigned tasks.
- [ ] I prioritize getting the most important things done quickly.

## 5. Universal checklist for any company

- [ ] I know what my company values most: speed, quality, impact, reliability, or growth.
- [ ] I adapt my working style to the current environment.
- [ ] I do standout work by my company's standards.
- [ ] I help people around me succeed.
- [ ] I maintain strong relationships with peers and former colleagues.
- [ ] I resolve conflicts professionally.
- [ ] I avoid burning bridges.
- [ ] I regularly reassess whether the company has shifted modes.
- [ ] I stay ready to adapt when the environment changes.
