*The working checklist behind this record. The Article tab carries the rationale and anti-patterns.*

## 1. Shift from managing individuals to leading a team

- [ ] Treat team management as a different job, not just "more people management"
- [ ] Spend less time coding, but stay close enough to the work to spot bottlenecks
- [ ] Keep the team focused on the highest-value projects
- [ ] Clear roadblocks that slow the team down
- [ ] Partner closely with product on scope, priorities, and delivery
- [ ] Plan ahead for hiring, headcount, and team needs
- [ ] Set clear expectations for each team member
- [ ] Give frequent feedback, not just during review cycles
- [ ] Communicate timelines, risks, and tradeoffs upward

## 2. Stay technical

- [ ] Maintain enough hands-on involvement to stay technically credible
- [ ] Guide technical decisions even when others own implementation details
- [ ] Keep enough coding/debugging exposure to feel real bottlenecks firsthand
- [ ] Use my technical judgment to evaluate feasibility and estimates
- [ ] Avoid becoming technically obsolete too early
- [ ] Make time to understand the system, not just the meetings around it

## 3. Watch for dysfunction on the team

**Shipping problems**

- [ ] Check whether the team is shipping consistently
- [ ] Look for slow releases, painful deploys, oversized features, or manual testing bottlenecks
- [ ] Push to remove process and tooling bottlenecks
- [ ] Make work small enough to move regularly

**People drama**

- [ ] Address toxic behavior early
- [ ] Do not let persistent negativity linger
- [ ] Use direct, timely conversations instead of hoping issues resolve themselves
- [ ] Ask for help from my own manager when needed

**Overwork**

- [ ] Look for burnout, instability, or repeated crunch periods
- [ ] Protect time for system stability and sustainability work
- [ ] Support the team visibly during stressful periods
- [ ] Learn from crunch instead of normalizing it

**Collaboration problems**

- [ ] Check whether product, design, and partner teams are working well with my team
- [ ] Create regular touchpoints with peers
- [ ] Gather specific feedback about cross-team friction
- [ ] Stay supportive of peers in public

## 4. Manage former peers carefully

- [ ] Acknowledge the awkwardness of the transition directly
- [ ] Be transparent and ask for their help making the transition work
- [ ] Avoid using managerial power to override technical decisions casually
- [ ] Resist micromanaging people who used to be my peers
- [ ] Pick my battles and protect long-term trust

## 5. Be a shield, but not a wall

- [ ] Protect the team from unnecessary distraction and politics
- [ ] Give enough context so people understand why goals matter
- [ ] Do not hide everything; share important realities in a calm, low-drama way
- [ ] Communicate clearly during uncertainty so gossip does not fill the gap
- [ ] Treat the team like adults, not children

## 6. Drive good decisions

- [ ] Accept responsibility for outcomes, even when others own parts of the process
- [ ] Build a data-driven culture for product and technical decisions
- [ ] Track delivery, quality, outages, bugs, and other useful team metrics
- [ ] Develop customer empathy, not just engineering intuition
- [ ] Think ahead about the future roadmap and technical direction
- [ ] Review whether project assumptions actually proved true
- [ ] Run retrospectives on both projects and the team process

## 7. Handle conflict like a manager

- [ ] Do not rely only on consensus or voting for hard decisions
- [ ] Take responsibility for unpopular decisions when needed
- [ ] Set clear decision-making processes and roles
- [ ] Address simmering issues early
- [ ] Separate problem-solving from gossip and drama
- [ ] Avoid blaming other teams when things go wrong
- [ ] Be kind, not merely "nice"
- [ ] Notice when fear is making me avoid necessary conversations
- [ ] Get curious about my own conflict-avoidance patterns

## 8. Protect team cohesion

- [ ] Build psychological safety so people can take risks and make mistakes openly
- [ ] Encourage friendliness and trust across the team
- [ ] Watch for people who make others feel unsafe or ineffective
- [ ] Refuse to tolerate destructive behavior from "brilliant jerks"
- [ ] Address information-hiding and noncommunication quickly
- [ ] Confront lack of respect toward me or teammates directly
- [ ] Remove persistent toxic behavior if it cannot be corrected

## 9. Manage projects at the team level

- [ ] Decide which projects to take on and when to push back
- [ ] Plan at a higher level than the team's day-to-day agile execution
- [ ] Assume less than a full quarter of uninterrupted engineering time
- [ ] Budget roughly 20% for sustaining engineering work
- [ ] Cut scope as deadlines approach
- [ ] Know when to accept a hack and when to insist on the right implementation
- [ ] Use rough estimates carefully; plan more deeply for larger work
- [ ] Shield engineers from constant random estimation requests

## 10. Onboard myself into a new team

- [ ] Get a walkthrough of the architecture, systems, release flow, and testing process
- [ ] Learn the codebase enough to talk about real work, not abstractions
- [ ] Plan to work on a couple of features early
- [ ] Watch code reviews and pull requests
- [ ] Participate in support/release work if that is part of the team's responsibilities
- [ ] Build technical credibility through real engagement with the team's work

## 11. Self-audit questions

- [ ] Have I stopped doing enough old work to make room for management responsibilities?
- [ ] Do I understand the team's day-to-day friction in coding, deploying, and support?
- [ ] Is the team completing work regularly?
- [ ] When was the last time I personally touched the code or paired with someone?
- [ ] Are one or two people driving most of the negativity?
- [ ] Does the team seem engaged with one another?
- [ ] Do we have a clear decision-making process?
- [ ] When did we last review whether a finished project achieved its goals?
- [ ] Does the team understand why it is working on its current projects?
- [ ] When was the last time I cut scope effectively?
