# Spec: Managing a Team

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State what changes when I stop managing individuals and start owning a
team's output — and what I hold every team manager accountable for at that
rung. The post turns the "Managing a Team" chapter of Camille Fournier's
*The Manager's Path* into an operating principle: team management is a
different job, not "more people management"; the manager stays technical
enough to feel real bottlenecks; watches for the four dysfunctions (not
shipping, people drama, overwork, collaboration problems); manages former
peers with transparency rather than authority; shields the team without
becoming a wall; drives decisions and owns outcomes; handles conflict
directly instead of hiding behind consensus; protects cohesion by refusing
to tolerate brilliant jerks; and manages projects at the team level with
realistic time budgets and aggressive scope cuts. The load-bearing idea: at
this rung I am accountable for the team's output as a whole, and every
dysfunction on the team is mine to notice and act on early.

## Audience

Managers stepping up from managing individuals to managing a whole team;
managers at this rung in my organization (so they know what I hold them
accountable for); peer executives comparing operating models. First-person
declarative.

## Success criteria

- [x] **Principle is quotable** — highlight states that team management is a
      different job, that the team's output is mine, and that dysfunction is
      mine to notice early.
- [x] **The four dysfunctions survive** — not shipping, people drama,
      overwork, and collaboration problems each appear with their concrete
      signals and first moves.
- [x] **Staying technical is argued, not asserted** — credibility, feeling
      bottlenecks firsthand, evaluating feasibility and estimates.
- [x] **Shield-not-wall, former peers, conflict, and cohesion all survive** —
      including "be kind, not merely nice" and the brilliant-jerk line.
- [x] **Team-level project management survives** — less than a full quarter
      of engineering time, ~20% sustaining work, cut scope as deadlines
      approach.
- [x] **Credit is explicit** — References name Camille Fournier's *The
      Manager's Path* and the chapter this record distills.

## Non-goals

- Not [[managing-people]] — that record covers the craft of managing
  individuals (1:1s, feedback, delegation to a person); this one starts
  where the unit of accountability becomes the team.
- Not [[managing-multiple-teams]] — that record covers the next rung, where
  production code is gone and the job is time, priorities, and building
  leaders across teams.
- Not [[tech-lead]] — that record covers technical project leadership
  without people accountability; this rung adds ownership of people, health,
  and output together.
