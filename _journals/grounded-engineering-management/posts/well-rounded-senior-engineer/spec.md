# Spec: The Well-Rounded Senior Engineer

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State what I hold senior engineers to and how I coach the developer-to-
engineer shift — grounded in the Well-Rounded Senior Engineer chapters of
Gergely Orosz's *The Software Engineer's Guidebook*. The load-bearing idea:
senior is a shift, not a bigger version of the same job — thinking in system
lifetimes (extension, operation, debugging, migration), making hidden
complexity visible instead of absorbing it, doing much of the job on the
collaboration surface (reviews, pairing, mentoring, feedback, cross-team
relationships), treating tech debt and testing as economic choices, and
shipping architecture through writing, prototypes, decision-makers, and
business framing. Impact, not raw effort, is the measure. The article is in
the manager's voice; the Checklist tab is the checklist I hand the engineer,
in the engineer's voice.

## Audience

Engineers approaching or operating at the senior level (the bar, in their
own checklist); managers and tech leads who calibrate and coach against it;
peer executives comparing ladder expectations. First-person declarative.

## Success criteria

- [x] **Principle is quotable** — highlight states that senior is the
      developer-to-engineer shift, names the time-horizon change, visible
      complexity, the collaboration surface, and economics-based debt/
      testing/architecture judgment, and closes on impact over raw effort.
- [x] **The mindset shift survives** — system lifetime thinking (extended,
      operated, debugged, monitored, migrated), recurring bugs prevented,
      shortcuts priced, decisions documented; independence at medium/complex
      project scope.
- [x] **The communication expectations survive** — what/why/why-hard
      updates, hidden complexity surfaced, roadblocks early with options
      (reduce scope, workaround, another team), under-promise and
      over-communicate.
- [x] **The collaboration surface survives** — reviews (blocking vs.
      nitpick, tone, automation), pairing that teaches rather than takes
      over, mentoring given and sought, concrete early feedback, cross-team
      mapping and relationships built before crises.
- [x] **Debt, testing, and architecture judgment survive** — debt as
      compounding cost quantified in business impact; test models (pyramid,
      trophy, testing in production) chosen by system needs not dogma;
      architecture shipped via RFCs, prototypes, decision-makers, rollout
      and rollback plans, pre-mortems.
- [x] **Credit is explicit** — References name Gergely Orosz, *The Software
      Engineer's Guidebook* (Pragmatic Engineer, 2023), and the chapters distilled.
