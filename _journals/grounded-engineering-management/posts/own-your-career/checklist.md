*The working checklist behind this record — the one I hand the engineer, in the engineer's own voice. The Article tab carries the rationale and anti-patterns.*

## 1. Take charge of your career

- [ ] I have defined what I want from my career
- [ ] I am not waiting for my manager to drive my growth
- [ ] I regularly communicate my goals and interests
- [ ] I look for opportunities to grow, learn, and improve
- [ ] I create chances for others to give me feedback

## 2. Be seen as someone who gets things done

- [ ] I finish the work I commit to
- [ ] I deliver work of high quality and at a fast pace
- [ ] I focus on impactful work, not just busy work
- [ ] I understand my team's and business's priorities
- [ ] I make sure my manager and team know when I complete important work
- [ ] I share measurable impact when possible

## 3. Keep a work log

- [ ] I record important work each week
- [ ] I track projects, decisions, code reviews, design docs, meetings, and support work
- [ ] I note links to tickets, documents, pull requests, or artifacts
- [ ] I capture work that may be useful for performance reviews or promotions
- [ ] I review my log regularly with my manager
- [ ] I use my log to decide what to prioritize, stop, or say "no" to

## 4. Ask for feedback

- [ ] I ask for feedback on specific work, not vague personal feedback
- [ ] I seek feedback from teammates, managers, product partners, and others I work with
- [ ] I ask what went well and what could be improved
- [ ] I clarify vague feedback with concrete examples
- [ ] I ask about the impact of my actions
- [ ] I use feedback to adjust my behavior or approach

## 5. Give feedback to others

- [ ] I call out good work when I see it
- [ ] I give specific positive feedback, and only when I genuinely mean it
- [ ] I focus critical feedback on situations, observations, and impact
- [ ] I avoid making feedback sound like an order unless I am responsible for managing that person
- [ ] I give constructive feedback in person or by video when appropriate
- [ ] I make it clear that my feedback is my observation, not an absolute truth
- [ ] I end feedback conversations positively

## 6. Make your manager an ally

- [ ] I have regular 1:1s with my manager
- [ ] I share what I have been working on
- [ ] I discuss my goals and growth areas
- [ ] I understand my manager's goals and challenges
- [ ] I deliver what I agree to do
- [ ] I give updates early when I cannot meet a commitment
- [ ] I build trust by being open, honest, and transparent
- [ ] I help my manager recognize my work by making my contributions visible
- [ ] I ask my manager for specific feedback

## 7. Pace yourself

- [ ] I notice when I am stretching, executing, or coasting
- [ ] I take on stretch work when it helps me grow
- [ ] I avoid stretching so much that I risk burnout
- [ ] I spend enough time executing reliably
- [ ] I recognize when I am coasting for too long
- [ ] I address low motivation or lack of challenge instead of ignoring it
- [ ] I ask for support before overload becomes burnout
- [ ] I balance growth, reliability, and recovery over the long term
