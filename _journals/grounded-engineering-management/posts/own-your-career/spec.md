# Spec: Own Your Career

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State the self-directed career management I coach engineers into, from the
manager's side: I can open doors, but I cannot drive anyone's career for
them. The post turns the "Own Your Career" checklist from Gergely Orosz's
*The Software Engineer's Guidebook* into an operating principle with seven
parts: define what you want and take charge, build a reputation for
finishing, keep a work log, ask for feedback on specific work, give feedback
well, make your manager an ally, and pace yourself across stretching,
executing, and coasting. The load-bearing idea: career ownership is a
transferable habit set, not luck or politics — and the engineers who
practice it are the ones I can actually help.

## Audience

Engineers on my teams (so they know what career ownership looks like
concretely and what I will and will not do for them); the managers I hold to
the other side of that contract; senior engineers mentoring others.
First-person declarative, manager's voice; the Checklist tab speaks in the
engineer's first person, as the source does.

## Success criteria

- [x] **Principle is quotable** — highlight states that I cannot drive
      anyone's career for them, and that ownership is a habit set: goals,
      finishing, evidence, feedback, alliance, pacing.
- [x] **All seven habit areas survive** — take charge (define what you want,
      don't wait, communicate goals), get-things-done reputation (finish,
      impact over busywork, make completion known), work log (weekly record,
      links, reviewed with the manager, used to prioritize and say no),
      asking for feedback (specific work, multiple sources, clarify vague
      feedback), giving feedback (specific praise, situation-observation-
      impact, observation not absolute truth), manager as ally (1:1s, early
      updates on slipping commitments, visible contributions), and pacing
      (stretch / execute / coast awareness, support before burnout).
- [x] **The manager's side is explicit** — the article says what I do with
      an engineer who owns their career (advocate, open doors) and links the
      report's side of [[management-101]].
- [x] **The work log's double use survives** — evidence for reviews and
      promotions, and a prioritization tool for deciding what to stop or
      decline.
- [x] **Credit is explicit** — References name Gergely Orosz's *The Software
      Engineer's Guidebook* and the career-ownership chapter this distills.

## Non-goals

- Not [[management-101]] — that record is the two-sided manager–report
  contract; this one is the engineer's own half, coached from my side.
- Not [[performance-review-cycle]] — working the review cycle itself is that
  record; this one is the year-round ownership that makes the cycle easy.
- Not [[promotions]] — building and timing a promotion case lives there;
  the work log here is its raw material.
- Not [[career-conversations]] (people journal) — that is the manager-run
  conversation about a person's arc; this record is what the engineer does
  between such conversations.
