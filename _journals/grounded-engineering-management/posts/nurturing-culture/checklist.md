*The working checklist behind this record. The Article tab carries the rationale and anti-patterns.*

## 1. Understand your current team

- [ ] Write down the first 3 adjectives that describe the team's personality
- [ ] Identify the moments that made me most proud to be part of the team
- [ ] Note what the team does better than most other teams
- [ ] Ask: if random team members were asked "what does our team value?", would their answers be consistent?
- [ ] Compare the team's culture with the broader organization's culture
- [ ] Imagine an outside observer scrutinizing the team — what would they say we do well or poorly?
- [ ] List the top 3 complaints people have about how things work

## 2. Clarify your aspirations

- [ ] Choose the top 5 adjectives I want others to use to describe the team
- [ ] For each adjective, identify the downside or tradeoff of taking it too far
- [ ] List cultural traits I admire in other teams or organizations
- [ ] Note why I admire them and what tradeoffs those teams accept
- [ ] List the cultural traits I do not want to copy from other teams or companies

## 3. Understand the gap

- [ ] Rate the team from 1–9 on how close it is to the ideal culture
- [ ] Identify the strengths the team already has that align with the desired culture
- [ ] List the biggest gaps between the current culture and the desired culture
- [ ] Identify the main obstacles that could block progress
- [ ] Describe how I want the team to work one year from now

## 4. Keep talking about what matters

- [ ] Repeatedly communicate the values and behaviors that matter most
- [ ] Use multiple channels to reinforce the same message
- [ ] Encourage open discussion of tension, disagreement, and hard conversations
- [ ] Assume important messages need to be heard many times in many forms
- [ ] Talk openly not just about values, but also about mistakes and lessons learned

## 5. Walk the walk

- [ ] Check whether my behavior matches the values I say matter
- [ ] Eliminate visible contradictions between what I say and what I do
- [ ] Model the exact behavior I want the team to adopt
- [ ] Ask for feedback in the same way I expect others to
- [ ] Treat trust as fragile and protect it through consistency

## 6. Create the right incentives

- [ ] Review what behaviors are actually rewarded on the team
- [ ] Compare stated values with the decisions people are incentivized to make
- [ ] Watch for the incentive traps: rewarding individual performance over team success
- [ ] Watch for: rewarding short-term gains over long-term investments
- [ ] Watch for: rewarding avoidance of conflict over honest discussion
- [ ] Watch for: rewarding the loudest complainers
- [ ] Ask why people are making the choices they are making
- [ ] Change structural incentives when they push people away from team values
- [ ] Hold people accountable when their behavior violates team values
- [ ] Publicly recognize people who do the hard right thing

## 7. Build traditions that reinforce values

- [ ] Create rituals that help team members bond around shared values
- [ ] Make those rituals specific to this team rather than generic
- [ ] Add recurring moments that celebrate learning, openness, creativity, or support
- [ ] Use traditions to make values visible in everyday team life
- [ ] Pay attention to the small repeated actions that signal what matters

## 8. Final reflection

- [ ] Review what my actions, rewards, and rituals are teaching the team
- [ ] Make sure daily behavior tells the same story as my stated values
- [ ] Adjust until the culture reflects what I genuinely want the team to become
