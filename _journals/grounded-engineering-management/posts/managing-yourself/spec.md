# Spec: Managing Yourself

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State my operating model for self-management as a manager, distilled from
the "Managing Yourself" chapter of Julie Zhuo's *The Making of a Manager*:
a written self-inventory (strengths, weaknesses, inner-critic patterns,
triggers) calibrated against reality by asking my manager and 3–7 trusted
coworkers; imposter-syndrome stories treated as claims to test against
evidence rather than obey; deliberately engineered best-work conditions and
known triggers with a pause before responding; wellbeing boundaries treated
as operational necessity; and a deliberate learning loop (manager as coach,
hard problems brought early, weekly written review, six-month goals) aimed
at better-not-perfect in my own style. The load-bearing idea: self-knowledge
that never leaves my own head is just my inner critic — calibration against
other people's feedback is what makes it real.

## Audience

Managers in my organization, especially new ones (so they see that
self-doubt, triggers, and wellbeing are managed deliberately, not endured
silently); my own manager and reports (so they know what feedback I will
ask of them); peer executives comparing operating models. First-person
declarative.

## Success criteria

- [x] **Principle is quotable** — highlight states that the first person I
      manage is me, and that uncalibrated self-knowledge is the inner critic
      in disguise.
- [x] **The inventory and calibration survive** — written strengths,
      weaknesses, descriptive words, inner-critic patterns, and trigger
      situations; checked with my manager (growth opportunities, what holds
      me back, comparison with the ideal person in the role) and 3–7
      trusted coworkers asking for specific, task-anchored feedback,
      received without defensiveness.
- [x] **The imposter-syndrome discipline survives** — notice the worst-case
      story, ask what evidence exists, consider alternatives, growth over
      fixed mindset, discomfort as learning, early confusion as normal.
- [x] **Conditions, triggers, and wellbeing survive** — best-work
      conditions and calendar structure, sleep and routines, trigger
      awareness with pause-and-cool-down, and boundaries, breaks, exercise,
      and family time as operational necessities.
- [x] **The learning loop survives** — manager as coach with hard problems
      brought early, 1:1s as learning time, peers and mentors, weekly
      written review, six-month goals revisited, better-not-perfect in my
      own style.
- [x] **Credit is explicit** — References name Julie Zhuo's *The Making of
      a Manager*.
