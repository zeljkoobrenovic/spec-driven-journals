# Spec: Managing a Downturn

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State how I lead a team through cost cuts, layoffs, hiring freezes, and
stalled growth. The sequence matters: get my own head straight first (drop
"this is happening to me" and "I should have been perfect", replace with "I
can influence how we navigate this"), read the team's fear/grief/guilt
signals as operating risks, then run the disciplined machinery — a steady
communication cadence built on "what I know, what I don't, when you'll hear
more"; scarcity-mode prioritization with one top goal and an explicit
stop-doing list; layoffs decided on future company needs and executed fast
with equipped managers and defined support; a distinct honest script for each
kind of hard news; and a weekly rhythm that keeps morale grounded in reality.
The load-bearing idea: trust deepens when I show up honestly in hard times —
a downturn led with honesty builds the asset it threatens.

## Audience

Managers in my organization facing a downturn (the playbook I expect them to
run); my own leadership chain (so they know how I will communicate and
prioritize under pressure); peer executives comparing operating models.
First-person declarative.

## Success criteria

- [x] **Principle is quotable** — highlight leads with getting my own head
      straight and lands on trust deepening when I show up honestly in hard
      times.
- [x] **The self-management sequence survives** — named feelings, facts vs.
      self-blame, the two stories to drop and their replacement, focus on
      what is in my control, one concrete next step.
- [x] **The emotional-signal read survives** — fear, grief, and guilt/stress
      signals listed; morale problems framed as trust and purpose problems;
      undercurrents treated as operating risks.
- [x] **The communication cadence survives** — what I know / don't know /
      when updates come; no fake optimism or false certainty; rumor gaps
      closed; the "why" behind priority changes in plain language.
- [x] **Scarcity-mode prioritization survives** — one top goal, defined
      success, explicit non-goals/stop-doing list, paused non-laddering
      work, permission and language to say no, explicit decision paths.
- [x] **Layoffs and hard-news scripts survive** — reason clarity, future-
      needs-based decisions, speed once decided, equipped managers, defined
      support, personal follow-up, intentional support for those who remain;
      distinct scripts for bonuses, hiring freeze, pay cuts, stalled
      promotions, shifting strategy, missed goals; the weekly rhythm.
- [x] **Credit is explicit** — References name Julie Zhuo's *The Making of a
      Manager* and state honestly that the source is supplementary material
      extending the book's themes, not a chapter checklist.
