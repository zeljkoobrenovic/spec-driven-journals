*The working checklist behind this record. The Article tab carries the rationale and anti-patterns.*

## 1. Get your own head straight

- [ ] Acknowledge what I am feeling: guilt, anxiety, isolation, confusion, frustration, grief, stress
- [ ] Separate facts from self-blame
- [ ] Stop telling myself "this is all happening to me"
- [ ] Stop telling myself "I should have been perfect"
- [ ] Replace both with: "I can influence how we navigate this"
- [ ] Focus on progress, not perfection
- [ ] Identify what is in my control right now
- [ ] Commit to one concrete next step

## 2. Read the emotional state of the team

- [ ] Look for fear signals: withdrawal, fixation on layoffs, reduced creativity, silence in meetings
- [ ] Look for grief signals: low energy, loss of enthusiasm, disappointment, mourning lost colleagues or plans
- [ ] Look for guilt/stress signals: overwork, burnout, overcompensation, inability to disconnect
- [ ] Recognize that morale problems are often trust and purpose problems
- [ ] Treat emotional undercurrents as operating risks, not side issues

## 3. Build trust intentionally

- [ ] Explain how the team got here
- [ ] Own my part honestly
- [ ] Speak like a human, not a corporate script
- [ ] Address the big unanswered questions directly
- [ ] Invite questions and suggestions regularly
- [ ] Validate people's feelings without getting defensive
- [ ] Share honestly, but with judgment about audience and detail
- [ ] Ask the team for help explicitly
- [ ] Repeat key context often as conditions change

## 4. Communicate during uncertainty

- [ ] Tell people what I know
- [ ] Tell people what I do not know
- [ ] Tell people when they should expect updates
- [ ] Avoid fake optimism and false certainty
- [ ] Do not let rumor gaps stay open
- [ ] Give the "why" behind changes in priorities
- [ ] Use plain language for difficult topics
- [ ] Keep a steady cadence of updates

## 5. Prioritize ruthlessly

- [ ] Adopt a scarcity mindset: do fewer things, but do them better
- [ ] Define the single most important goal for the next period
- [ ] Identify what success actually looks like
- [ ] Use a prioritization framework: impact vs. effort, RICE, or OKRs
- [ ] Create an explicit "non-goals" or "stop doing" list
- [ ] Pause work that does not ladder to the top goal
- [ ] Recheck priorities whenever conditions change

## 6. Help the team move faster

- [ ] Give people permission to say no to low-priority work
- [ ] Provide language they can use to push back
- [ ] Encourage escalation when teams are stuck in disagreement
- [ ] Make quick decision paths explicit
- [ ] Call out changes in priority in real time
- [ ] Reinforce what matters most every week
- [ ] Keep the team aligned on why the work matters now

## 7. If layoffs may happen

- [ ] Be clear on the reason: percentage-based cost cuts, or strategy/role reshaping
- [ ] Decide based on future company needs, not comfort or recency
- [ ] Identify critical projects, skills, and roles first
- [ ] Move quickly once a decision is made
- [ ] Equip managers with context, scripts, and FAQs
- [ ] Define support clearly: severance, benefits, transition support
- [ ] Write down all logistics and reference materials
- [ ] Communicate timeline and process with maximum clarity
- [ ] Follow up personally with impacted people
- [ ] Support the people who remain just as intentionally

## 8. Deliver hard news well

**Bonuses reduced or removed**

- [ ] Acknowledge disappointment directly
- [ ] Explain the business rationale
- [ ] Reinforce appreciation in non-monetary ways

**Hiring freeze**

- [ ] Reassess workload
- [ ] Cut lower-priority work
- [ ] Check team sustainability

**Pay cuts**

- [ ] Be transparent about why
- [ ] Explain what recovery looks like
- [ ] Reaffirm the person's value

**Promotion/role growth stalls**

- [ ] Reframe growth beyond title changes
- [ ] Offer stretch work, mentorship, or cross-functional exposure
- [ ] Be honest about timelines

**Strategy keeps shifting**

- [ ] Explain why priorities changed
- [ ] Connect each shift to company goals
- [ ] Ask for input
- [ ] Create pockets of stability where possible

**Missed goals**

- [ ] Acknowledge disappointment
- [ ] Highlight effort and learning
- [ ] Run retrospectives without blame
- [ ] Translate lessons into next-quarter actions

## 9. Keep morale grounded in reality

- [ ] Remember morale is not cheerleading
- [ ] Protect trust
- [ ] Protect purpose
- [ ] Recognize contributions publicly
- [ ] Show how today's work connects to survival or recovery
- [ ] Avoid sugarcoating
- [ ] Reinforce that people's work still matters

## 10. Weekly operating rhythm

- [ ] Re-state the top priority
- [ ] Review what was deprioritized
- [ ] Surface risks and rumors early
- [ ] Share any new facts or decisions
- [ ] Invite questions
- [ ] Check for overload on key people
- [ ] Recognize progress, however small
- [ ] Confirm next steps and owners

## 11. Personal leadership reminders

- [ ] Be real
- [ ] Be clear
- [ ] Be fast when needed
- [ ] Be compassionate
- [ ] Be consistent
- [ ] Do not confuse discomfort with failure
- [ ] Remember: trust deepens when I show up honestly in hard times
