*The working checklist behind this record. The Article tab carries the rationale and anti-patterns.*

## 1. Own the role

- [ ] Accept that my job is no longer primarily to write production code
- [ ] Stay technically credible through reviews, debugging, incidents, and design conversations
- [ ] Keep at least one protected block of time each week for creative or technical work
- [ ] Make sure I am building managers, tech leads, and future leaders, not just shipping work

## 2. Prioritize what matters

- [ ] Separate **important** work from merely **urgent** work
- [ ] Reserve time every week for important but not urgent work
- [ ] Review my calendar and remove low-value meetings
- [ ] Avoid treating email, chat, and scheduled meetings as automatically important
- [ ] Make sure every recurring group meeting has a clear purpose and expected outcomes

## 3. Manage time deliberately

- [ ] Protect uninterrupted time for strategic thinking
- [ ] Follow up on key deliverables without becoming a bottleneck
- [ ] Regularly ask: "Am I spending too much time on urgent but low-value work?"
- [ ] Make room for hiring plans, team health, roadmaps, and organizational development

## 4. Delegate on purpose

- [ ] Delegate **simple + frequent** tasks
- [ ] Handle **simple + infrequent** tasks myself when explaining them would take longer
- [ ] Use **complex + infrequent** tasks as coaching opportunities for rising leaders
- [ ] Delegate **complex + frequent** work to develop stronger team ownership
- [ ] Identify tasks only I can do, and teach the rest

## 5. Build leaders under me

- [ ] Give tech leads and emerging managers real responsibility, not just helper tasks
- [ ] Coach people to handle planning, coordination, and follow-through themselves
- [ ] Use delegation to grow capability, not just reduce my workload
- [ ] Check whether my teams could keep operating well if I stepped away for a week

## 6. Watch for warning signs

- [ ] Notice disengagement, sudden withdrawal, or unusual silence from team members
- [ ] Watch for tech leads who report "everything is fine" but skip 1:1s or provide vague updates
- [ ] Pay attention when meetings feel lifeless or one-sided
- [ ] Notice teams whose priorities change constantly with no stable goals
- [ ] Look for teams that are overly narrow, fragmented, or resistant to learning outside their area

## 7. Say no like a leader

- [ ] Use "yes, and" instead of reflexive rejection
- [ ] Clarify tradeoffs: what slips if we say yes?
- [ ] Create policies when I find myself saying "no" repeatedly
- [ ] Ask "help me say yes" questions to test weak proposals
- [ ] Use time and budget constraints honestly
- [ ] Avoid vague "not right now" answers unless I truly intend to revisit later
- [ ] Deliver clear no's quickly when needed

## 8. Measure team health beyond output

- [ ] Track **release frequency**
- [ ] Track **frequency of code check-ins / small-batch progress**
- [ ] Track **incident frequency** and operational load
- [ ] Look for process friction that slows delivery
- [ ] Check whether engineers have what they need to do good work
- [ ] Check whether people have enough focus time to build rather than just react

## 9. Improve the system of work

- [ ] Push for smaller releases and faster feedback loops
- [ ] Reduce manual steps, handoffs, and slow approvals
- [ ] Improve tooling, automation, and developer experience
- [ ] Prevent incident work from consuming all engineering time
- [ ] Balance risk reduction with speed; do not let "safety" become a permanent drag

## 10. Build the right team culture

- [ ] Avoid "us vs. them" identity-building
- [ ] Align teams to company purpose, not team superiority
- [ ] Encourage openness to ideas from outside the team
- [ ] Focus on being a strong partner across product, design, and peer teams
- [ ] Treat my peer leaders as my "first team," not just my direct reports
- [ ] Build teams that survive leadership changes and reorganizations

## 11. Model sustainable leadership

- [ ] Push for efficiency, automation, and simplification instead of longer hours
- [ ] Avoid sending after-hours signals that normalize overwork
- [ ] Queue non-urgent weekend/night messages for later when possible
- [ ] Ask whether faster means smarter, not just more exhausting
- [ ] Protect myself and my teams from burnout

## 12. Review myself regularly

- [ ] What did I spend time on this week that created little value?
- [ ] What important work did I postpone because urgent work crowded it out?
- [ ] What did I delegate recently, and did it help someone grow?
- [ ] Who are my rising leaders, and what am I doing to prepare them?
- [ ] Are releases, code flow, and incident handling improving?
- [ ] Did I send signals this week that reward focus and sustainability, or chaos and overwork?

## 13. Weekly manager reset

- [ ] Review team health, risks, and staffing needs
- [ ] Review the calendar for low-value meetings
- [ ] Identify one thing to delegate
- [ ] Identify one important-but-not-urgent item to move forward
- [ ] Check in with tech leads and future leaders
- [ ] Remove one friction point from the team's workflow

## 14. Monthly leadership check

- [ ] Are my teams getting more independent?
- [ ] Are my leads and I spending time on the right level of work?
- [ ] Is our culture collaborative, or is it drifting into silo behavior?
- [ ] Are our operating metrics improving?
- [ ] Am I building an organization that can scale without me in the middle of everything?
