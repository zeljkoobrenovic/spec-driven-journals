# Spec: Managing Multiple Teams

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State what changes when I manage multiple teams — and what I hold every
multi-team manager accountable for. The post turns the "Managing Multiple
Teams" chapter of Camille Fournier's *The Manager's Path* into an operating
principle: my job is no longer to write production code, and my output is
leaders, priorities, and a system of work that improves without me. The
mechanics: deliberate time management that separates important from merely
urgent work; a delegation grid (simple/complex × frequent/infrequent) used
on purpose to grow leaders, not just to offload; saying no like a leader
("yes, and", tradeoffs, policies, quick clear no's); measuring team health
beyond output (release frequency, check-in frequency, incident load, focus
time); improving the system of work; building culture without "us vs. them";
modeling sustainable pace; and running a weekly manager reset plus a monthly
leadership check. The load-bearing idea: at this rung my calendar and my
delegation choices ARE the job — if the teams cannot run for a week without
me, I am failing at it.

## Audience

Managers stepping up from one team to several; multi-team managers in my
organization (so they know the bar); peer executives comparing operating
models. First-person declarative.

## Success criteria

- [x] **Principle is quotable** — highlight states that production code is
      no longer my job, that my output is leaders and a system of work, and
      that the week-away test is the bar.
- [x] **The delegation grid survives** — all four quadrants
      (simple/complex × frequent/infrequent) with their assigned moves.
- [x] **Important-vs-urgent discipline survives** — weekly reserved time,
      calendar review, meetings with clear purpose.
- [x] **Saying no survives** — "yes, and", tradeoff clarification, policies
      for repeated no's, "help me say yes", quick clear no's.
- [x] **Team-health signals survive** — release frequency, code check-in
      frequency, incident load, friction, focus time — plus the warning
      signs (disengagement, "everything is fine" tech leads, lifeless
      meetings, churning priorities).
- [x] **The cadences survive** — weekly manager reset and monthly
      leadership check appear in the checklist.
- [x] **Credit is explicit** — References name Camille Fournier's *The
      Manager's Path* and the chapter this record distills.

## Non-goals

- Not [[managing-a-team]] — that record covers the single-team rung, where
  the manager still shares the team's day-to-day technical life; this one
  starts where production code ends.
- Not [[managing-managers]] — that record covers the next rung, where the
  directs are themselves managers and skip-levels become the ground-truth
  channel.
- Not a time-management method in general — the record covers the specific
  prioritization and delegation discipline this rung requires, not personal
  productivity systems.
