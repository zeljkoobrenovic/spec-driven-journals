# Spec: Performance Reviews

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State how I coach engineers to work the performance review cycle from its
first week, not its last: learn how the system actually works (who decides,
who gives input, when calibration happens), set goals the manager can
endorse and get confirmation they would meet or exceed expectations, build
evidence habits through the whole cycle (wins recorded weekly, glue work
tracked, no assumption the manager already knows), prepare a self-review
grounded in artifacts, and interpret outcomes with rater biases and
perspective in mind. The post turns the "Performance Reviews" checklist from
Gergely Orosz's *The Software Engineer's Guidebook* into an operating
principle from the manager's side. The load-bearing idea: the review is
written all year; the engineer who starts at the cycle's end has already
lost the information war — and I refuse to let my reports' outcomes depend
on my memory.

## Audience

Engineers on my teams (so they know how I expect them to work the cycle and
why it is not gaming the system); managers who run reviews for their own
reports; senior engineers coaching others through their first cycles.
First-person declarative, manager's voice; the Checklist tab addresses the
engineer directly.

## Success criteria

- [x] **Principle is quotable** — highlight states that the review is
      written all year and that starting at the end is already too late.
- [x] **The early phase survives** — learn the system (who decides, who
      gives input, when calibration happens, what the company rewards), set
      goals that support engineer/team/manager/company, revise them until
      the manager can endorse them, record them officially, and ask whether
      achieving them meets or exceeds expectations.
- [x] **The habit phase survives** — wins recorded weekly or biweekly with
      evidence, work log, regular 1:1s without assuming the manager already
      knows, glue work tracked and bounded, feedback sought well before
      review time.
- [x] **The preparation phase survives** — assess the manager as advocate,
      ask how you are performing before the review, know deadlines and
      calibration dates, give context before decisions are finalized, and
      write a self-review with results over time, goals and outcomes,
      competencies, qualitative examples, and peer feedback.
- [x] **The interpretation phase survives** — the review is a snapshot, not
      the career; the named rater biases (recency, strictness, leniency,
      horns, halo, similarity, central tendency, contrast) survive; engineer
      and manager are ideally on the same side.
- [x] **Credit is explicit** — References name Gergely Orosz's *The Software
      Engineer's Guidebook* and the performance-review chapter this
      distills.
