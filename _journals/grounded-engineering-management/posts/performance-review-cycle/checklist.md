*The working checklist behind this record — the one I hand the engineer. The Article tab carries the rationale and anti-patterns.*

## 1. Start early: gather context and set goals

- [ ] Ask your manager what the team's goals are and how they connect to company goals
- [ ] Ask what your manager personally cares about and what matters most to them
- [ ] Talk with tenured teammates about what the company rewards
- [ ] Listen to leadership priorities from all-hands, emails, presentations, or planning docs
- [ ] Identify influential people involved in your review process
- [ ] Understand what performance review system your company uses
- [ ] Clarify who makes the final review decision, and who gives input on your review
- [ ] Find out when review decisions, calibration, and outcomes happen
- [ ] Ask peers for advice on how to ensure a fair review
- [ ] Set goals that support you, your team, your manager, and the company
- [ ] Share goals with your manager and ask for feedback
- [ ] Revise goals until your manager can support and endorse them
- [ ] Record agreed-upon goals somewhere visible or official
- [ ] Ask whether achieving your goals would meet or exceed expectations

## 2. Build strong habits throughout the cycle

- [ ] Record wins weekly or biweekly
- [ ] Capture completed projects, outcomes, metrics, screenshots, praise, and evidence
- [ ] Keep a work log with notable work and impact
- [ ] Hold regular 1:1s with your manager
- [ ] Share progress, wins, challenges, and what is on your plate
- [ ] Avoid assuming your manager already knows everything you are doing
- [ ] Balance speed, quality, and meaningful impact
- [ ] Build a reputation for getting important work done
- [ ] Help teammates, your manager, and the company where appropriate
- [ ] Track "glue work" and other helping work so it is visible
- [ ] Watch for spending too much time helping others at the expense of core responsibilities
- [ ] Ask for specific feedback well before review time
- [ ] Seek feedback after meetings, incidents, presentations, projects, reviews, or new initiatives
- [ ] Treat feedback seriously, but decide thoughtfully what to act on

## 3. Prepare before the review

- [ ] Assess whether your manager will be a strong advocate for you
- [ ] Ask your manager how they think you are performing
- [ ] Understand your standing relative to expectations and peers
- [ ] Consider your manager's level of trust, influence, and tenure
- [ ] Identify review deadlines, calibration dates, and decision timelines
- [ ] Give your manager important context before decisions are finalized
- [ ] Gather peer feedback from people who know your work well
- [ ] Write down positive feedback and growth feedback you receive
- [ ] Create a self-review document
- [ ] Include results delivered over time, not just recent work
- [ ] Include qualitative examples such as helping others, mentoring, and judgment
- [ ] Include original goals and outcomes
- [ ] Include examples of competencies for your role or level
- [ ] Include feedback from peers, praise, and positive feedback
- [ ] Decide whether to rate yourself in the self-review
- [ ] Consider writing an honest self-assessment even if you do not assign a rating

## 4. During and after the review

- [ ] Remember the review is a snapshot, not your whole career
- [ ] Set realistic expectations about ratings and outcomes
- [ ] Avoid making major plans based on an uncertain bonus or rating
- [ ] Be aware of common biases: recency, strictness, leniency, horns, halo, similarity, central tendency, and contrast
- [ ] Consider whether your manager is skilled at giving feedback
- [ ] Ask for specifics if feedback is vague
- [ ] Reflect on negative feedback before deciding whether to act on it
- [ ] Remember that you and your manager are ideally on the same side
- [ ] Focus on long-term growth, relationships, skills, and impact
- [ ] Keep the review in perspective within the larger arc of your career
