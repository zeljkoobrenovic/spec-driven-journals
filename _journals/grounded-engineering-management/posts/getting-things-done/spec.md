# Spec: Getting Things Done

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State how teams I manage execute, grounded in the "getting things done"
chapter of Julie Zhuo's *The Making of a Manager*: a concrete vision that
survives retelling (different people describe the goal the same way), a
believable plan built on the team's real strengths and constraints with few
priorities and explicit buffer, named ownership and deadlines for every
decision and task, fast decisions with commitment afterward and prompt
reassessment when new information arrives, a portfolio balanced across
short-, medium-, and long-term horizons, and blameless debriefs that turn
repeated work into playbooks. The load-bearing idea: execution is a loop —
direction, plan, ownership, decision, learning — and a team running the
whole loop gets stronger after every mistake.

## Audience

Managers in my organization (this is the execution discipline I hold their
teams to); my own teams (so the expectations on ownership, commitment, and
debriefs are explicit); peer executives comparing operating models.
First-person declarative.

## Success criteria

- [x] **Principle is quotable** — highlight carries the retelling test for
      vision, the believable-plan standard, decide-commit-reassess, and the
      debrief-to-playbook loop.
- [x] **The direction test survives** — concrete, measurable outcome; easy
      to repeat, hard to misinterpret; verified by whether different people
      describe the goal the same way.
- [x] **The plan discipline survives** — real strengths and constraints,
      few priorities, deliberate cuts, milestones, buffer.
- [x] **The decision discipline survives** — named owners and deadlines,
      fast calls on sufficient information (especially reversible ones),
      commitment after disagreement, prompt reassessment on new
      information.
- [x] **Horizons and learning survive** — the short/medium/long portfolio
      with both failure modes (over-optimizing the immediate term, clinging
      to an outdated long-term plan), and blameless debriefs producing
      playbooks; the "signs of good execution" list ships in the checklist.
- [x] **Credit is explicit** — References name Julie Zhuo's *The Making of
      a Manager* and the chapter.
