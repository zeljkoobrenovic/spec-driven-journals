# Spec: Managing Managers

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State how I operate when my direct reports are themselves managers — and
what I hold every manager-of-managers accountable for. The post turns the
"Managing Managers" chapter of Camille Fournier's *The Manager's Path* into
an operating principle: at this rung information arrives pre-filtered, so I
actively seek ground truth instead of waiting for it — regular cadences
with each manager, skip-level meetings run to learn (not to undermine), and
follow-up on small signals until I know whether they matter. I hold
managers accountable for their teams' health and output without absorbing
their responsibility; I watch for manager failure patterns (overpromising,
people-pleasing, over-shielding, yes-to-everyone, micromanagement, skipped
1:1s); I inspect new managers closely in their first six months and manage
experienced managers deliberately; I hire managers with rigor (role-play,
philosophy, diagnosis skill, reference checks); when managing outside my
expertise I lean in harder, not less; and I debug dysfunctional teams the
way I debug systems — hypothesis, data, observation, and asking why until
the real issue surfaces. The load-bearing idea: I am accountable for the
health of teams I no longer see directly, so deliberate inspection replaces
ambient awareness.

## Audience

Directors and managers-of-managers in my organization (so they know the
bar); managers reporting to me (so they know how I will inspect and
support them); peer executives comparing operating models. First-person
declarative.

## Success criteria

- [x] **Principle is quotable** — highlight states that information now
      arrives filtered, that deliberate inspection replaces ambient
      awareness, and that I hold managers accountable without absorbing
      their responsibility.
- [x] **Skip-levels survive** — run to learn, not to undermine; the
      question set (health, blockers, morale, support needed, concerns
      about manager/direction/coordination); 1:1 and group formats.
- [x] **Manager failure patterns survive** — overpromising,
      people-pleasing, over-shielding, yes-to-everyone, micromanagement,
      skipped 1:1s and evaded specifics.
- [x] **New vs. experienced manager handling is distinct** — close
      inspection and corrective feedback in the first six months for new
      managers; culture-fit assessment and strategy-level coaching for
      experienced ones.
- [x] **Manager hiring rigor survives** — role-play of 1:1s and difficult
      situations, management philosophy, diagnosis ability, technical
      credibility with the team, thorough reference checks.
- [x] **Debugging teams as systems survives** — hypothesis, data,
      observation, goal understanding, dynamics, asking why.
- [x] **Credit is explicit** — References name Camille Fournier's *The
      Manager's Path* and the chapter this record distills.

## Non-goals

  themselves managers and the information distance doubles.

  scope); this one is the director-level craft of running managers.
- Not a hiring record in general — manager hiring appears here only as this
  rung's specific rigor; the broader hiring system is covered by
