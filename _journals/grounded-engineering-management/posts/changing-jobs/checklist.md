*The working checklist behind this record — the one I hand an engineer weighing or making a move. The Article tab carries the rationale and anti-patterns.*

## 1. Exploring new opportunities

- [ ] I have decided whether I am actively looking, passively open, or happy but curious.
- [ ] I am clear on what would make a new opportunity worth pursuing.
- [ ] I have updated my LinkedIn/profile if actively looking.
- [ ] I respond selectively to inbound recruiter messages.
- [ ] I use conversations to understand my market value.
- [ ] I compare opportunities against compensation, role, growth, team, mission, flexibility, and long-term upside.

## 2. Promotion vs switching jobs

- [ ] I have assessed whether I am already performing at the next level.
- [ ] I understand whether my company's promotion process is slow or backward-looking.
- [ ] I have compared internal promotion odds with external offers.
- [ ] I have considered that switching jobs may reset my trust, context, and influence.
- [ ] For senior roles, I have weighed the value of tenure and internal relationships.
- [ ] I am not optimizing only for title or compensation; I think about 5–10 year career impact.

## 3. Preparing for tech interviews

- [ ] I have asked the recruiter what the interview process looks like.
- [ ] I have confirmed whether there will be coding, system design, behavioral, domain deep-dive, or bar-raiser interviews.
- [ ] My resume clearly highlights relevant experience and measurable impact.
- [ ] I practice coding and debugging interviews.
- [ ] I prepare for system design or architecture interviews.
- [ ] I have behavioral stories ready about motivation, conflict, hard situations, and values.
- [ ] I ask clarifying questions during technical interviews.
- [ ] For take-home tasks, I confirm time expectations and whether feedback will be provided.
- [ ] For staff+ roles, I prepare for deeper domain, product, director-level, or second system-design rounds.

## 4. Handling down-leveling

- [ ] I compare both title and compensation, not just one.
- [ ] I have asked what the offered level means and what the next level requires.
- [ ] I understand whether this company has higher expectations than my current company.
- [ ] I have asked whether the hiring committee can reconsider my level if I disagree.
- [ ] I have decided whether the lower title is acceptable given pay, growth, company, and opportunity.
- [ ] I am prepared to reject the offer if the level feels significantly misaligned with my career goals.

## 5. Handling up-leveling

- [ ] I have confirmed that the higher level comes with clear expectations.
- [ ] I have asked what success looks like at that level.
- [ ] I understand why the company believes I am ready.
- [ ] I have made sure I have the support needed to succeed.
- [ ] I use competing offers or strong references carefully if negotiating.
- [ ] I am prepared to meet higher expectations from day one.

## 6. Onboarding to a new job

- [ ] I celebrate the offer, then prepare seriously for onboarding.
- [ ] I have asked my future manager how to prepare before starting.
- [ ] I have clarified goals for the first month, first 3 months, and first 6 months.
- [ ] I keep a work log or brag document from the beginning.
- [ ] I keep a weekly journal of what I learned and what is unclear.
- [ ] I have learned how performance reviews and promotions work here.
- [ ] I have found an onboarding buddy or trusted peer.
- [ ] I keep a cheat sheet for acronyms, systems, commands, people, and resources.
- [ ] I am learning the tech stack and team priorities.
- [ ] I meet key teammates and stakeholders early.
- [ ] For senior+ roles, I clarify current and future projects, priorities, and business context.
- [ ] For staff+ roles, I meet peer staff engineers, understand expectations, and spend time with product and cross-functional partners.
