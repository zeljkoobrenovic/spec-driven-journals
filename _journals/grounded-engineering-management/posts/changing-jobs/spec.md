# Spec: Changing Jobs

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State how I coach engineers through the full job-change arc — as a manager
who would rather lose someone well than keep them badly. The post turns the
*Software Engineer's Guidebook*'s Changing Jobs material into an operating
principle covering the whole arc: exploring opportunities deliberately
(active, passive, or curious; knowing your market value), weighing promotion
against switching (including what a switch resets — trust, context,
influence), preparing for tech interviews as a learnable skill, handling
down-leveling and up-leveling offers with clear eyes, and onboarding to the
new job as seriously as the interview. The load-bearing idea: a job change
is a career decision spanning 5–10 years, not a compensation event — and an
engineer who understands the arc makes the move (or the stay) deliberately
instead of reactively.

## Audience

Engineers on my teams (so they know I expect deliberate career moves and
will coach them honestly, even toward the door); managers I hold to the same
standard; peer executives comparing operating models. Manager's voice in the
article; the Checklist tab is the checklist I hand the engineer, in the
engineer's first person.

## Success criteria

- [x] **Principle is quotable** — highlight states that a job change is a
      5–10 year career decision, not a compensation event, and that I coach
      the whole arc honestly, including toward the door.
- [x] **The explore stage survives** — active/passive/curious stance,
      clarity on what would make a move worth it, selective recruiter
      responses, using conversations to learn market value, comparing on
      compensation, role, growth, team, mission, flexibility, long-term
      upside.
- [x] **Promotion vs switching survives** — already performing at the next
      level, slow or backward-looking internal processes, internal odds vs
      external offers, what a switch resets (trust, context, influence),
      tenure's value for senior roles.
- [x] **Interview prep and leveling survive** — ask what the process looks
      like, prepare per interview type, staff+ extra rounds; down-leveling
      handled on title *and* compensation with the option to push back or
      walk; up-leveling handled with clear expectations and support.
- [x] **Onboarding survives** — prepare before starting, 1/3/6-month goals,
      work log from day one, cheat sheet, buddy, early stakeholder
      meetings, senior+/staff+ extras.
- [x] **Credit is explicit** — References name Gergely Orosz, *The Software
      Engineer's Guidebook*.
