# Spec: Managing Remote Teams

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State how I run a remote or substantially distributed team: replace the
office's ambient channel with explicit structure — weekly priorities,
written roles and expectations, response-time norms, documented
decision-sharing, async as the default — keep a searchable decision log as
the team's memory, and run deliberate rhythms (stand-ups, all-hands with
recordings, office hours) that get reviewed and refined. Trust and connection
are engineered too: regular 1:1s with open-ended questions, no negative
assumptions about silence or tone, bonding rituals and casual spaces, and
in-person time spent on relationships instead of packed agendas. The
load-bearing idea: a remote team runs on what is written down and repeated —
overcommunicating is the correct calibration, and when the quick self-review
returns a "no", the system gets fixed, not the person.

## Audience

Managers of remote or distributed teams in my organization (the operating
basics I hold them to); my own remote reports (so they know the norms are a
system with a self-review, not personal style); peer executives comparing
operating models. First-person declarative.

## Success criteria

- [x] **Principle is quotable** — highlight states that a remote team runs
      on what is written down and repeated, and that overcommunicating is
      the correct calibration.
- [x] **The foundations survive** — weekly priorities, explicit roles and
      expectations, response-time norms, documented decision/knowledge
      sharing, async by default.
- [x] **The trust discipline survives** — regular 1:1s with open-ended
      questions on workload/morale/concerns, space for honest feedback,
      shared uncertainty when appropriate, watching for disengagement and
      isolation, no negative assumptions about silence or tone.
- [x] **The written-memory machinery survives** — decisions, owners, and
      next steps summarized in writing; dedicated project threads; a
      searchable decision log; repetition across places; clear meeting
      norms.
- [x] **Rhythms, connection, and in-person time survive** — stand-ups
      (done/next/blockers), all-hands with recordings, office hours, rhythms
      reviewed regularly; bonding rituals, public wins, peer recognition,
      casual spaces; in-person time prioritizing relationships over packed
      agendas. The quick self-review closes the checklist.
- [x] **Credit is explicit** — References name Julie Zhuo's *The Making of a
      Manager* and state honestly that the source is supplementary material
      extending the book's themes, not a chapter checklist.

## Non-goals

- Not [[amazing-meetings]] — the general meeting-quality record; this one
  decides what deserves synchronous time at all on a distributed team.
- Not [[internal-communication]] (executive journal) — the organization-level
  communication architecture; this record is the team-level operating model
  that plugs into it.
- Not a remote-work policy (who may work remotely, hybrid ratios,
  compensation by location) — this record governs how I manage a team that
  is distributed, not whether it should be.
