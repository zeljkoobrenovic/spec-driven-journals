# Spec: Promotions

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State how I run promotions on my teams, from the manager's side of the table:
promotion is an evidence-and-support problem, not a reward for waiting. The
post turns the *Software Engineer's Guidebook*'s Promotions chapter into an
operating principle: I expect engineers to understand how their company's
promotion system actually works, to demonstrate next-level work *before* the
promotion, to build the case themselves — produce, organize, publish — to
secure my support and broader support early, and to stay realistic about
headcount, budget, and approval rates at senior levels. The load-bearing
idea: the promotion decision is made on evidence assembled long before the
cycle opens, and the engineer — not the manager — owns assembling it; my job
is to be honest about gaps, co-own the plan, and never let a promotion case
surprise either of us.

## Audience

Engineers on my teams (so they know what I need from them to argue their
case); managers I hold to the same coaching standard; peer executives
comparing operating models. Manager's voice in the article; the Checklist tab
is the checklist I hand the engineer, in the engineer's first person.

## Success criteria

- [x] **Principle is quotable** — highlight states that promotion is an
      evidence-and-support problem: next-level work first, evidence
      assembled continuously, support secured early, realism about headcount
      and approval rates.
- [x] **The system-awareness expectation survives** — know how promotions
      are decided (informal, lightweight, heavyweight, committee), who
      influences the decision, what current and next levels expect, and
      whether a terminal level exists.
- [x] **Produce, organize, publish survives** — the three verbs from the
      source are named and explained, including "not relying only on the
      manager to make impact visible."
- [x] **The support structure survives** — early feedback, manager
      alignment on a realistic path with milestones, and broader support
      from peers, senior engineers, and skip-levels.
- [x] **Realism survives** — promotion is not guaranteed; business need,
      headcount, and budget gate it; senior promotions have lower approval
      rates; long-term career health is not defined by title.
- [x] **Credit is explicit** — References name Gergely Orosz, *The Software
      Engineer's Guidebook*.
