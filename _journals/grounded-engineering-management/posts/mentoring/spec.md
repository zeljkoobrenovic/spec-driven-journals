# Spec: Mentoring

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State how I run mentoring as a designed first leadership role rather than an
informal favor. The post turns the "Mentoring" chapter of Camille Fournier's
*The Manager's Path* into an operating principle: the mentor prepares before
the mentee's day one (purpose, success definition, environment, access,
docs, a level-appropriate project broken into milestones, a check-in plan),
runs a deliberate orientation in the first days, then tapers support toward
independence with listening, clear communication, and small course
corrections — with explicit variants for interns (a real, specific,
non-urgent project sized to the internship, presented at the end) and new
hires (onboarding docs used actively, relationships built across the
company). The load-bearing idea: mentoring done well is meaningful work with
preparation and an end state; mentoring assigned casually and run vaguely
damages both people, so I let people say no rather than do it badly.

## Audience

Engineers taking on their first mentee (so they know what preparation and
conduct I expect); managers who assign mentoring (so they match goals to
mentors and treat it as real work); peer executives comparing operating
models. First-person declarative.

## Success criteria

- [x] **Principle is quotable** — highlight states that mentoring is a
      designed role with prep before day one and a taper to independence,
      not side work, and that saying no beats doing it badly.
- [x] **The pre-day-one preparation survives** — purpose, success for mentee
      and team, environment and access confirmed, onboarding docs, a clear
      level-appropriate project broken into small milestones, a check-in
      plan.
- [x] **The conduct survives** — orientation and introductions in the first
      days, listening before answering, watching for confusion and overload,
      pointing to docs, encouraging independence without abandoning, small
      course corrections.
- [x] **The variants survive** — intern (real, specific, non-urgent project
      sized to the internship, end-of-term presentation coached) and new
      hire (docs used actively and improved, relationships across the
      company, how things really get done).
- [x] **The close survives** — review what was learned and delivered,
      reflect as a mentor, feed improvements back into onboarding, leave the
      mentee with connections and next steps.
- [x] **Credit is explicit** — References name Camille Fournier's *The
      Manager's Path* and the Mentoring chapter.

## Non-goals

- Not [[managing-people]] — the mentor is not the mentee's manager;
  performance management, reviews, and 1:1 mechanics live there.
- Not [[tech-lead]] — that record covers leading a whole project and team
  without authority; this one covers guiding a single person.
- Not the hiring or onboarding system itself — mentoring plugs into
  onboarding ([[engineering-onboarding]], executive journal) but does not
  define it.
