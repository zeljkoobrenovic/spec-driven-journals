![Comic panel: Arlo is told he is mentoring the new hire waiting by the door, looking proud and panicked.](assets/images/mentoring/comic-01-first-mentee.jpeg)
**Panel 1:** *The hook: mentoring is the first leadership role most engineers ever hold — and it usually arrives without warning.*

![Comic panel: a mentee sits at a bare desk with login errors and padlocks while a week crosses off the calendar.](assets/images/mentoring/comic-02-lost-first-week.jpeg)
**Panel 2:** *The problem: a mentee who loses their first week to logistics learns something permanent about how the organization treats people.*

![Comic panel: Arlo walks away saying grab me whenever while the mentee hesitates with a raised hand.](assets/images/mentoring/comic-03-grab-me-whenever.jpeg)
**Panel 3:** *The wrong way: 'just grab me whenever' — no purpose, no plan, until both sides quietly stop trying.*

![Comic panel: Vera shows a pre-start checklist of access, docs, purpose, and a milestone-sliced project before day one.](assets/images/mentoring/comic-04-designed-before-day-one.jpeg)
**Panel 4:** *The principle: mentoring is designed, not improvised — purpose, access, docs, and a milestone-sliced project ready before the mentee arrives.*

![Comic panel: Arlo introduces the mentee to teammates and walks through a code map during orientation.](assets/images/mentoring/comic-05-deliberate-orientation.jpeg)
**Panel 5:** *How it runs: the first days are an orientation run on purpose — people, code, process, and the unspoken norms, with check-ins so nobody sits lost.*

![Comic panel: three scenes of Arlo gradually stepping back as the mentee grows independent.](assets/images/mentoring/comic-06-the-taper.jpeg)
**Panel 6:** *The mechanic: calibrated withdrawal — listen first, point at docs instead of answering everything, and shrink support as the mentee grows.*

![Comic panel: Vera shows that an overloaded mentor should decline, as an engineer politely raises a hand.](assets/images/mentoring/comic-07-say-no-if-you-cant.jpeg)
**Panel 7:** *The cost: mentoring is counted as real work with real boundaries — and a bad mentor is worse than no mentor, so saying no is allowed.*

![Comic panel: Arlo and the mentee close the mentorship with a handshake, improved docs, and next steps, as Vera nods.](assets/images/mentoring/comic-08-close-the-loop.jpeg)
**Panel 8:** *The closer: the loop closes on purpose — what was learned gets reviewed, the docs get better, and the mentee leaves with connections and next steps.*
