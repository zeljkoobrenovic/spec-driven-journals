*The working checklist behind this record. The Article tab carries the rationale and anti-patterns.*

## 1. Before the mentee starts

- [ ] Clarify the purpose of the mentoring relationship
- [ ] Decide what success looks like for the mentee and for the team
- [ ] Prepare the mentee's environment before day one
- [ ] Confirm they will have access to tools, systems, software, and workspace
- [ ] Create or collect the onboarding docs they will need
- [ ] Choose a project that is clear, useful, and appropriate to their level
- [ ] Break the project into small milestones
- [ ] Plan how often I will check in

## 2. In the first few days

- [ ] Help the mentee get oriented to the team, office, and workflow
- [ ] Introduce them to key people and networks
- [ ] Walk through code, tools, and processes with them
- [ ] Make it easy for them to ask questions
- [ ] Check in several times so they do not feel lost or overwhelmed
- [ ] Explain both formal rules and unspoken norms

## 3. While mentoring

- [ ] Listen carefully before jumping in with answers
- [ ] Pay attention to what they mean, not just the words they use
- [ ] Watch for confusion, hesitation, or overload
- [ ] Repeat or rephrase explanations when needed
- [ ] Use diagrams, examples, and walkthroughs to improve understanding
- [ ] Communicate clearly what should happen next
- [ ] Point them to docs and resources instead of answering everything immediately
- [ ] Encourage independence without abandoning them
- [ ] Adjust my support based on their progress and response
- [ ] Keep the mentee moving in the right direction with small course corrections

## 4. For mentoring an intern

- [ ] Make sure the intern has a real project to work on
- [ ] Choose something specific but not urgent
- [ ] Size the project so it can be completed within the internship
- [ ] Leave room for onboarding, training, and intern program activities
- [ ] Plan for the intern to present their work at the end
- [ ] Coach them on how to explain and demo what they built

## 5. For mentoring a new hire

- [ ] Use onboarding documents actively, not passively
- [ ] Ask the new hire to improve docs when they hit surprises
- [ ] Help them build relationships across the company
- [ ] Explain how things really get done, not just how they are documented

## 6. Mentor mindset

- [ ] Stay curious and open-minded
- [ ] Use the mentee's questions to uncover blind spots in my own understanding
- [ ] Practice patience, empathy, and clear communication
- [ ] Treat mentoring as meaningful work, not side work
- [ ] Be explicit about expectations, time commitment, and boundaries
- [ ] Say no to mentoring if I cannot do it well

## 7. Avoid these pitfalls

- [ ] Do not intimidate, belittle, or show off
- [ ] Do not hoard knowledge or use expertise as status
- [ ] Do not assume the mentee should "just figure it out" alone
- [ ] Do not let the relationship become vague or unstructured
- [ ] Do not assign mentoring without matching goals to the right mentor

## 8. At the end

- [ ] Review what the mentee learned and delivered
- [ ] Reflect on what I learned as a mentor
- [ ] Note what should be improved in onboarding, docs, or process
- [ ] Leave the mentee with useful connections and next steps
