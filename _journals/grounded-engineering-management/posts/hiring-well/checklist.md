*The working checklist behind this record. The Article tab carries the rationale and anti-patterns.*

## 1. Set up before I hire

- [ ] Treat hiring as one of the highest-leverage things I do
- [ ] View hiring as building the future of the team, not just filling holes
- [ ] Plan ahead instead of hiring reactively
- [ ] Map where I want the team to be by year-end
- [ ] Identify skill, strength, and experience gaps on the team
- [ ] Decide what kinds of people would strengthen team diversity
- [ ] Define the level and scope for each open role

## 2. Own the hiring process

- [ ] Take personal responsibility for hiring, even with recruiters involved
- [ ] Write the job description myself
- [ ] Be specific about the skills and experiences I want
- [ ] Partner closely with recruiting on sourcing, interviewing, and closing
- [ ] Help craft outreach that feels personal and relevant

## 3. Build the candidate pipeline

- [ ] Create a sourcing strategy before interviewing starts
- [ ] List target backgrounds, companies, titles, and keywords
- [ ] Look beyond standard patterns when searching for talent
- [ ] Ask trusted people for recommendations
- [ ] Use references as a meaningful signal, not a box-checking step
- [ ] Keep in mind that old negative feedback may be outdated

## 4. Run a strong interview process

- [ ] Make the interview experience attentive, focused, and fast
- [ ] Coordinate tightly with recruiters and interviewers
- [ ] Prepare interview questions ahead of time
- [ ] Ask every candidate a consistent core set of questions
- [ ] Tailor additional questions to the role's actual needs
- [ ] Include multiple interviewers with different perspectives
- [ ] Ensure interviewers assess different dimensions, not the same one repeatedly
- [ ] Debrief independently before group discussion to avoid groupthink

## 5. Ask better questions

- [ ] Probe for examples of similar past work
- [ ] Ask candidates to show real output: apps, writing, pitches, portfolios
- [ ] Clarify what part of teamwork they personally owned
- [ ] Ask what kinds of challenges excite them
- [ ] Ask about strengths, areas for growth, and self-awareness
- [ ] Ask where they want to be in five years
- [ ] Ask about a hard conflict and what they learned
- [ ] Ask what has inspired them lately

## 6. Make better decisions

- [ ] Remember that interviews are imperfect and often noisy
- [ ] Don't rely only on gut feel from a short interview
- [ ] Watch for personal bias in evaluation
- [ ] Avoid "weak hire" decisions
- [ ] Look for at least one strong, passionate advocate
- [ ] Reject candidates who show toxic behavior
- [ ] Favor candidates who can grow beyond the current role
- [ ] Prioritize people who raise the team's long-term capability

## 7. Prioritize diversity and perspective

- [ ] Intentionally seek candidates with different backgrounds and viewpoints
- [ ] Avoid hiring only people who look, think, and work like the current team
- [ ] Recognize that diverse teams make better decisions and produce better ideas

## 8. Close candidates well

- [ ] Make candidates feel genuinely wanted
- [ ] Move quickly once I decide to make an offer
- [ ] Stay involved during the closing process
- [ ] Explain why the role matters and why they are a strong fit
- [ ] Paint a compelling picture of the impact they could have
- [ ] Keep communication warm, clear, and frequent

## 9. Hire at scale

- [ ] Understand the funnel metrics from outreach to offer acceptance
- [ ] Expect hiring to require volume and repetition
- [ ] Break the process into manageable parts and improve each one
- [ ] Train interviewers to assess candidates consistently
- [ ] Build systems, not one-off heroic efforts
- [ ] Spend substantial time on hiring when team growth is a priority

## 10. Hire leaders carefully

- [ ] Do extra research before hiring managers or senior leaders
- [ ] Learn what great candidates for that leadership role look like
- [ ] Involve people who understand the function deeply
- [ ] Assess values alignment, not just competence
- [ ] Treat leadership hiring as a long-term investment

## 11. Play the long game

- [ ] Build relationships with great people before roles open
- [ ] Keep in touch with strong candidates who say no
- [ ] Invest in my network and team reputation over time
- [ ] Remember that careers are long and timing changes

## 12. Build a hiring culture

- [ ] Share responsibility for hiring across leaders as the team grows
- [ ] Coach others on what great talent looks like
- [ ] Set a high bar and repeat it often
- [ ] Make team-building everyone's job
- [ ] Create a culture that consistently prioritizes hiring well
