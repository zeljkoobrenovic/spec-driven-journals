*The working checklist behind this record. The Article tab carries the rationale and anti-patterns.*

## 1. Starting with a new report

- [ ] Learn the person's preferences for praise: public or private
- [ ] Ask how they prefer to receive serious feedback
- [ ] Ask what excited them to join and why they chose the company
- [ ] Ask how to recognize when they are stressed, frustrated, or in a bad mood
- [ ] Ask which manager behaviors they dislike
- [ ] Ask about their career goals
- [ ] Ask about any surprises, concerns, or unmet expectations since joining
- [ ] Build trust and rapport early
- [ ] Create a 30/60/90-day plan together
- [ ] Make expectations clear: communication, meeting cadence, work style, escalation, and autonomy
- [ ] Get feedback from the new hire during the first 90 days
- [ ] Involve them in improving onboarding documentation

## 2. Running 1:1s well

- [ ] Set up regular 1:1s
- [ ] Default to weekly 1:1s unless there is a good reason not to
- [ ] Reschedule missed 1:1s rather than skipping them
- [ ] Choose times that work for both schedules and minimize disruption
- [ ] Adjust frequency based on need, not habit
- [ ] Keep doing 1:1s even with strong performers
- [ ] Use 1:1s for more than status updates
- [ ] Leave room to get to know the person as a human being
- [ ] Keep shared notes from each 1:1

## 3. Adapting 1:1 style

- [ ] Decide whether this person needs a to-do list style, catch-up style, feedback style, or progress-report style 1:1
- [ ] Make sure the meeting serves the report, not just the manager
- [ ] Avoid letting 1:1s turn into repeated complaint sessions
- [ ] Give space for the important topics the report wants to raise
- [ ] Mix formats when useful, but protect privacy for sensitive discussions

## 4. Giving feedback continuously

- [ ] Learn each person's strengths, weaknesses, goals, and current level
- [ ] Observe people closely enough to notice patterns
- [ ] Start with lightweight, regular feedback
- [ ] Give praise promptly and specifically
- [ ] Give corrective feedback as soon as possible
- [ ] Do not save important feedback for a formal review
- [ ] Pair feedback with coaching when appropriate
- [ ] Focus on helping people grow, not just judging them
- [ ] Make frequent feedback part of team culture

## 5. Developing people

- [ ] Help each person identify areas for learning and growth
- [ ] Support growth through projects, mentoring, and external learning
- [ ] Discuss career development regularly, not just during reviews
- [ ] Look for promotion-ready opportunities and stretch work
- [ ] Be transparent about how promotion works at the company
- [ ] Help people build a case for promotion when they are ready

## 6. Delegating without micromanaging

- [ ] Delegate ownership, not just tasks
- [ ] Stay involved enough to support success without taking over
- [ ] Use team goals to decide which details matter
- [ ] Ask teams to make progress and success measures visible
- [ ] Pull information from systems first before asking people for updates
- [ ] Avoid asking for information I could easily get myself
- [ ] Adjust my level of involvement based on project stage and risk
- [ ] Set standards for code, systems, and decision-making
- [ ] Treat open sharing of problems neutrally and constructively
- [ ] Protect autonomy because autonomy drives motivation

## 7. Performance reviews

- [ ] Start early and give myself enough uninterrupted time
- [ ] Review the whole year, not just recent months
- [ ] Keep notes throughout the year to support review writing
- [ ] Use concrete examples
- [ ] Incorporate peer feedback thoughtfully
- [ ] Spend real time on accomplishments and strengths
- [ ] Keep areas for improvement focused and clear
- [ ] Avoid big surprises in formal reviews
- [ ] Give the person time to read the review before discussing it
- [ ] Discuss the review in enough depth to be useful

## 8. Handling underperformance

- [ ] Address problems early
- [ ] Follow the rule of "no surprises"
- [ ] Be clear about what good performance looks like
- [ ] Tell the person early and often when they are not meeting expectations
- [ ] Document negative feedback and improvement expectations
- [ ] Put timelines and concrete deliverables in writing when needed
- [ ] Work with HR processes where required
- [ ] Do not wait until frustration boils over
- [ ] Be fair, direct, and specific
- [ ] Do not put someone on a plan unless I am prepared for them to leave

## 9. Coaching someone out

- [ ] Be honest when someone is not progressing to the next level on my team
- [ ] Explain clearly what the next level requires
- [ ] Say directly when the team may not be the right place for that growth
- [ ] Help them explore a better-fit role internally or externally
- [ ] Preserve goodwill and dignity

## 10. Self-check

- [ ] Do I hold regular 1:1s?
- [ ] Have I discussed career development recently?
- [ ] Have I given feedback in the last week?
- [ ] Have I recognized good work publicly or privately?
- [ ] Have I corrected issues promptly and appropriately?
- [ ] Do I understand how promotions work here?
- [ ] Am I delegating well instead of micromanaging?
- [ ] Am I helping people grow, not just evaluating them?
