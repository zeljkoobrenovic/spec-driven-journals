# Spec: Managing People

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State the first-line people-management mechanics I run and hold managers to:
a deliberate start with every new report (preference questions, a joint
30/60/90-day plan, explicit expectations), weekly-by-default 1:1s that are
rescheduled rather than skipped and adapted in style per person, feedback
given continuously so formal reviews contain no surprises, deliberate
development toward promotion, delegation of ownership with
involvement calibrated to stage and risk, performance reviews written early
from year-round notes, underperformance addressed under a strict
no-surprises rule with documentation, and — when someone cannot grow to the
next level here — coaching them out with dignity. The load-bearing idea:
people management is a set of learnable mechanics run on a cadence, and
"no surprises" is the rule that ties all of them together.

## Audience

First-line engineering managers in my organization (this is the mechanics
bar I hold them to); reports (so they know what the machinery around them
should look like); peer executives comparing operating models. First-person
declarative.

## Success criteria

- [x] **Principle is quotable** — highlight states that people management is
      learnable mechanics on a cadence, with "no surprises" as the unifying
      rule.
- [x] **The new-report start survives** — preference questions (praise
      public or private, serious feedback, stress signals, disliked manager
      behaviors, career goals), joint 30/60/90-day plan, explicit
      expectations, feedback collected from the new hire in the first 90
      days, onboarding docs improved.
- [x] **The 1:1 mechanics survive** — weekly by default, reschedule rather
      than skip, keep them even for strong performers, more than status,
      shared notes, style adapted per person (to-do, catch-up, feedback,
      progress-report), meeting serves the report.
- [x] **Feedback, development, and delegation survive** — continuous
      lightweight feedback, praise prompt and specific, nothing saved for
      reviews; promotion transparency and stretch work; delegate ownership
      not tasks, pull information from systems before asking people, adjust
      involvement to stage and risk, protect autonomy.
- [x] **Reviews and underperformance survive** — start early, whole year,
      concrete examples, no big surprises, time to read before discussing;
      underperformance addressed early with documentation, written timelines
      when needed, and no plan unless prepared for the person to leave;
      coaching out with dignity.
- [x] **Credit is explicit** — References name Camille Fournier's *The
      Manager's Path* and the Managing People chapter.

## Non-goals

- Not [[management-101]] — that record is the two-sided contract from the
  report's perspective; this one is the manager-side machinery that
  delivers it.
- Not [[managing-a-team]] — team-level concerns (health, debugging dysfunction,
  shielding, decision-making) are the next rung's record.
- Not [[performance-review-cycle]] — that record is the engineer-side view
  of the review cycle from the Guidebook; this one covers the manager
  writing and delivering the review.
- Not an HR policy manual — formal process details (PIP mechanics, legal
  requirements) stay with HR; this record governs the manager's conduct
  around them.
