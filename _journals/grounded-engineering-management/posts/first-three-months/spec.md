# Spec: Your First Three Months

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State how I enter a new management role and how I coach new managers through
theirs, grounded in the new-manager transition chapter of Julie Zhuo's *The
Making of a Manager*: name the entry path first (Apprentice, Pioneer, New
Boss, Successor — each fails differently), treat the first three months
explicitly as a ramp-up rather than a proving period, build trust through
1:1s, consistency, and the five relationship questions asked of every report,
learn the system before changing it, and run the 30-60-90 arc from listening
through clarifying to targeted change reviewed with manager and team. The
load-bearing idea: the ramp is a discipline with a diagnosis (the path), a
scorecard (written 3/6-month success definition), and an end state (targeted
change) — not a mood of humility that either ends too soon or never ends.

## Audience

New managers in my organization (their first-quarter operating plan); the
managers of new managers (what to hold the ramp to); myself entering any new
role; peer executives comparing operating models. First-person declarative.

## Success criteria

- [x] **Principle is quotable** — highlight names ramp-up-not-proving, the
      four paths, question-first calibration, trust through 1:1s and
      consistency, and the 30-60-90 arc ending in targeted change.
- [x] **The four paths survive with their distinct traps** — Apprentice
      (former peers, IC load), Pioneer (writing down decisions and values
      from scratch), New Boss (the "new person" window, normal vs. real
      issue), Successor (not copying the predecessor, honest ramp with
      ownership).
- [x] **The relationship questions survive verbatim in substance** — support,
      feedback, recognition, previous manager, excellent relationship.
- [x] **The 30-60-90 arc survives** — listen/learn/meet (30), clarify/
      strengthen/coach/identify (60), targeted change/standards/manager
      mode/review (90).
- [x] **The comfort-lags note survives** — feeling fully comfortable may
      take much longer than three months.
- [x] **Credit is explicit** — How to Read This and References name Julie
      Zhuo, *The Making of a Manager* (Portfolio/Penguin, 2019); the record
      also names [[first-90-days]] as the executive-level counterpart.
