*The working checklist behind this record. The Article tab carries the rationale and anti-patterns.*

## 1. First, identify my starting path

Clarify which situation best fits me:

- [ ] Apprentice: managing part of my existing team
- [ ] Pioneer: building a new team or function
- [ ] New Boss: joining an established team as its leader
- [ ] Successor: taking over for a departing manager

## 2. Core checklist for any new manager

- [ ] Accept that the first three months are a ramp-up period
- [ ] Ask lots of questions before trying to prove myself
- [ ] Learn how the team works before changing too much
- [ ] Build trust through one-on-ones and consistency
- [ ] Define what success looks like for my first 3 and 6 months
- [ ] Notice what is working and what is not
- [ ] Get clear on team goals, values, and norms
- [ ] Be open about my learning curve
- [ ] Resist the urge to act like I already know everything
- [ ] Remember that feeling fully comfortable may take much longer than three months

## 3. Relationships

- [ ] Meet key partners and stakeholders early
- [ ] Build one-on-one relationships with each direct report
- [ ] Ask each report how they like to be supported
- [ ] Ask what kind of feedback is most helpful to them
- [ ] Ask how they like to be recognized for great work
- [ ] Ask what worked well with their previous manager
- [ ] Ask what an excellent manager–report relationship would look like to them
- [ ] Invest deliberately in trust, especially if I am new to the team
- [ ] Expect some awkwardness if I am now managing former peers
- [ ] Practice having direct, honest conversations when needed

## 4. Learning

- [ ] Learn how decisions get made on the team
- [ ] Learn what "good" looks like in this team's context
- [ ] Learn the unwritten rules and normal operating patterns
- [ ] Learn current priorities, deadlines, and active projects
- [ ] Learn where the team is strong and where it needs improvement
- [ ] Learn who influences outcomes beyond the org chart
- [ ] Keep a running list of observations, questions, and risks

## 5. Team and process

- [ ] Review current team processes and identify friction points
- [ ] Note anything that feels inefficient, unclear, or outdated
- [ ] Decide what should be preserved versus changed
- [ ] Avoid fixing things just to leave a mark
- [ ] Create a short list of the biggest improvement opportunities
- [ ] Align with my manager on ownership and expectations
- [ ] Clarify responsibilities across the team

## 6. Personal operating checklist

- [ ] Set aside time each week for reflection
- [ ] Get support from my own manager
- [ ] Find peer managers I can learn from
- [ ] Be careful not to over-index on individual contributor work
- [ ] Shift my time toward coaching, clarity, and team outcomes
- [ ] Accept that my job is now different from my old one
- [ ] Measure myself by team success, not just personal output

## 7. Path-specific: if I am the Apprentice

- [ ] Work with my manager on a joint transition plan
- [ ] Clarify how my scope may evolve over time
- [ ] Understand the people I will be managing
- [ ] Identify team goals and processes that need support
- [ ] Use my context to ramp quickly
- [ ] Watch for awkwardness with former peers
- [ ] Prepare to coach people who used to be my equals
- [ ] Practice difficult conversations early
- [ ] Expect people to share less with me at first
- [ ] Make a plan to reduce IC work before it becomes unsustainable

## 8. Path-specific: if I am the Pioneer

- [ ] Write down how I make decisions
- [ ] Define what "good work" means in my function
- [ ] List all responsibilities I used to carry alone
- [ ] Identify what new processes are needed as the team grows
- [ ] Be intentional about team values and culture
- [ ] Decide what qualities I want in new team members
- [ ] Identify which skills the team needs beyond my own
- [ ] Think ahead about how the team should work a year from now
- [ ] Build an external support network if internal support is limited
- [ ] Avoid trying to keep doing all the IC work myself

## 9. Path-specific: if I am the New Boss

- [ ] Use my "new person" window to ask basic questions
- [ ] Meet as many people as possible early on
- [ ] Ask for context before offering strong opinions
- [ ] Treat the fresh start as a chance to reset my identity
- [ ] Give people the benefit of the doubt
- [ ] Ask reports what support they want from me
- [ ] Listen for what is normal versus what is a real issue
- [ ] Focus the first months on listening, learning, and calibration
- [ ] Invest heavily in building trust
- [ ] Share enough of myself to make openness possible

## 10. Path-specific: if I am the Successor

- [ ] Acknowledge the increased responsibility openly
- [ ] Ask for support from my new manager and peers
- [ ] Tell the team there may be bumps during the transition
- [ ] Avoid trying to be a copy of the previous manager
- [ ] Give myself permission to lead differently
- [ ] Focus on what the team needs now, not just preserving the past
- [ ] Be honest about my ramp-up while still showing ownership

## 11. Simple 30-60-90 focus

**First 30 days**

- [ ] Listen
- [ ] Learn
- [ ] Meet everyone
- [ ] Understand priorities and norms

**Days 31–60**

- [ ] Clarify expectations
- [ ] Strengthen relationships
- [ ] Start coaching more actively
- [ ] Identify a few key improvements

**Days 61–90**

- [ ] Make targeted changes
- [ ] Reinforce team standards
- [ ] Shift fully into manager mode
- [ ] Review progress with my manager and team
