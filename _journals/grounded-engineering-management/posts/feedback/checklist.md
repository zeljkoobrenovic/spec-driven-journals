*The working checklist behind this record. The Article tab carries the rationale and anti-patterns.*

## 1. Before the work starts

- [ ] Explain what success looks like
- [ ] Define what a strong result looks like versus a weak one
- [ ] Share advice for getting started well
- [ ] Point out common pitfalls to avoid
- [ ] Set expectations early, not only after problems appear

## 2. Give feedback regularly

- [ ] Give feedback frequently, not just during reviews
- [ ] Comment on recent work while it is still fresh
- [ ] Balance praise with improvement feedback
- [ ] Include feedback on skills, growth, and career trajectory, not only tasks

## 3. Make feedback specific

- [ ] Describe the exact behavior, action, or output
- [ ] Use concrete examples
- [ ] Explain why it was effective or ineffective
- [ ] Avoid vague statements like "this was confusing" without details

## 4. Include behavioral feedback

- [ ] Look for patterns across multiple situations
- [ ] Share observations about habits, tendencies, and working style
- [ ] Support behavioral feedback with examples
- [ ] Deliver it thoughtfully — it feels more personal than task feedback

## 5. Gather multiple perspectives

- [ ] Collect input from others when useful
- [ ] Use 360-style feedback for a fuller picture
- [ ] Ask collaborators what the person should do more of
- [ ] Ask collaborators what the person should change or stop doing

## 6. Check whether the feedback is being heard

- [ ] Make the other person feel safe enough to listen
- [ ] Show that my goal is to help, not attack
- [ ] Ask for their perspective
- [ ] Confirm understanding by asking for their takeaways and next steps
- [ ] Follow up in writing when clarity would help

## 7. Make it actionable

- [ ] Clarify what success looks and feels like
- [ ] Suggest next steps when appropriate
- [ ] Be clear whether I am setting an expectation or making a suggestion
- [ ] Leave room for the other person to think through solutions too

## 8. Deliver critical feedback well

- [ ] Address the issue directly and calmly
- [ ] Focus on the work or behavior, not the person's character
- [ ] Avoid accusatory or emotionally loaded language
- [ ] Do not give critical feedback while upset
- [ ] State the concern plainly
- [ ] Explain what led me to that concern
- [ ] Invite the other person's perspective
- [ ] Discuss how to resolve it together

## 9. Avoid common mistakes

- [ ] Don't wait until review time to raise serious issues
- [ ] Don't assume task feedback alone is enough
- [ ] Don't hide the message in a "compliment sandwich"
- [ ] Don't make the conversation so soft that the real issue is unclear
- [ ] Don't present decisions as a discussion if they are already made

## 10. Final test

- [ ] Is this feedback likely to improve things?
- [ ] Is it clear, fair, timely, and respectful?
- [ ] Does the person know what to do next?
