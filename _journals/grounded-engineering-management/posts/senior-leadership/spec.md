# Spec: Senior Leadership

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State what I commit to at the top of the engineering ladder — VP of
Engineering or CTO — and what I hold senior engineering leaders accountable
for. The post distills the "Big Leagues" chapter of Camille Fournier's *The
Manager's Path* into an operating principle: the job reduces to four core
tasks (gather and synthesize information, nudge rather than command, decide
under conflicting and incomplete inputs, role-model the company's values),
run through a role whose actual shape — VP versus CTO, execution versus
strategy — must be clarified rather than assumed from the title. The
load-bearing idea: at this level everything I say and do echoes — words
carry extra weight, casual comments redirect work, anxieties dumped on the
team multiply — so the deliberate disciplines (changing priorities cleanly,
delivering bad news personally, defining a True North, building trust rather
than fear) are not optional polish, they are the job.

## Audience

Senior engineering leaders in my organization and leaders growing toward
that level (so they know what I hold the role to); my peers and my boss (so
they know how I operate at this level); AI tools using this journal as
context. First-person declarative.

## Success criteria

- [x] **Principle is quotable** — the highlight states that at this level
      the job is the four core tasks run through an echo: everything a
      senior leader says and does carries extra weight, so discipline in
      communication, priorities, and trust is the work itself.
- [x] **The four core tasks survive** — information gathering and
      synthesis, nudging instead of over-commanding, deciding from
      conflicting and incomplete inputs, and role-modeling values.
- [x] **The VP-versus-CTO distinction survives** — VP owns day-to-day
      execution and operational health; CTO is a business-strategy job seen
      through technology; titles do not mean the same thing at every
      company, so the role is clarified and reassessed as the company grows.
- [x] **The communication disciplines survive** — changing priorities well
      (confirm, decide what stops, repeat the message), delivering bad news
      personally, managing a nontechnical boss, presenting a united front
      with senior peers.
- [x] **True North and trust survive** — explicit standards for judgment
      calls, risk analysis instead of absolutism, and trust-not-fear as the
      leadership climate.
- [x] **Credit is explicit** — References name Camille Fournier, *The
      Manager's Path*, and the "Big Leagues" chapter.

## Non-goals

- Not [[managing-managers]] — that record covers managing through a layer
  of managers; this one covers the executive seat itself, where the boss is
  often nontechnical and the peers run other functions.
- Not [[management-101]] — the base contract with every report still
  applies at this level, but this record is about the responsibilities that
  only exist at the top.
- Not [[operational-excellence]] — that record carries the concrete
  operational bar; this one carries the True North commitment that makes me
  defend such a bar.
