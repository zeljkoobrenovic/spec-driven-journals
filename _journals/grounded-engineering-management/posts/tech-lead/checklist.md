*The working checklist behind this record. The Article tab carries the rationale and anti-patterns.*

## 1. Role and mindset

- [ ] I understand that my job is not just to write code, but to help the team deliver
- [ ] I balance technical work with leadership, communication, and project planning
- [ ] I focus on team outcomes, not just my own individual output
- [ ] I am willing to influence without formal authority
- [ ] I know when to step in directly and when to delegate

## 2. Technical leadership

- [ ] I understand the architecture well enough to confidently lead changes
- [ ] I know the project's critical systems, dependencies, and risks
- [ ] I identify which technical decisions I should make, delegate, or escalate
- [ ] I gather input from the team before making major technical decisions
- [ ] I avoid becoming either a bottleneck or totally hands-off

## 3. Planning and execution

- [ ] I can break large projects into smaller deliverables and tasks
- [ ] I can identify what can happen in parallel versus what must happen in sequence
- [ ] I know which items are critical and which are optional
- [ ] I keep the project moving, not just the codebase evolving
- [ ] I revisit the plan as new information appears

## 4. Project management

- [ ] I create enough structure for estimates, sequencing, and prioritization
- [ ] I push through ambiguity rather than avoid planning work
- [ ] I communicate project status, risks, and tradeoffs early
- [ ] I use planning to reduce uncertainty, not to pretend uncertainty does not exist
- [ ] I prepare for delivery with a premortem, launch plan, and rollback plan

## 5. Team leadership

- [ ] I help teammates unblock themselves instead of hoarding the most interesting work
- [ ] I delegate work in ways that help the team grow
- [ ] I provide mentorship and guidance, even when I am not their manager
- [ ] I help the team focus on what matters most
- [ ] I avoid heroics that hide problems rather than solve them

## 6. Communication

- [ ] I represent the team clearly to management and partner teams
- [ ] I explain technical issues in language others can understand
- [ ] I listen carefully and repeat back key points to confirm understanding
- [ ] I write clearly, speak clearly, and communicate often
- [ ] I raise concerns early instead of waiting for a crisis

## 7. Team effectiveness

- [ ] I protect the team's focus time from unnecessary meetings and interruptions
- [ ] I help stakeholders respect the team's need for deep work
- [ ] I make sure meeting load does not overwhelm individual contributors
- [ ] I remove obstacles that slow the team down
- [ ] I prioritize sustainable productivity over constant urgency

## 8. Process and judgment

- [ ] I use process as a tool, not as a substitute for leadership
- [ ] I do not assume there is one perfect process that fixes everything
- [ ] I optimize for people and interactions, not just rules and tools
- [ ] I adapt the process to the team and the work
- [ ] I look for self-regulating systems instead of policing people

## 9. Personal growth

- [ ] I accept that being a tech lead requires learning new skills beyond coding
- [ ] I am building skills in planning, prioritization, and coordination
- [ ] I am getting better at handling ambiguity and responsibility
- [ ] I regularly ask the team what they need from me most right now
- [ ] I assess honestly whether I want to stay on the technical track, grow as a tech lead, or move into management

## 10. Reflection questions

- [ ] Do I know what my manager expects from a tech lead?
- [ ] Do I know what my team expects from a tech lead?
- [ ] Am I spending too much time coding, or not enough?
- [ ] Am I making the team more effective, or just staying busy?
- [ ] Am I leading in a way that others trust and want to follow?
