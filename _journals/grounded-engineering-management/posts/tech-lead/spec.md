# Spec: Tech Lead

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State what I expect of a tech lead: the first role where the job stops being
"write code" and becomes "help the team deliver," held without formal
authority. The post turns the "Tech Lead" chapter of Camille Fournier's *The
Manager's Path* into an operating principle: balance technical work with
leadership, communication, and project planning; know the architecture and
risks well enough to lead changes; sort technical decisions into make,
delegate, or escalate; push projects through ambiguity with real planning
(estimates, sequencing, critical versus optional, premortem, launch and
rollback plans); represent the team clearly outward; protect its focus time;
and use process as a tool rather than a substitute for judgment. The
load-bearing idea: the tech lead is measured by team outcomes, not personal
output — becoming neither a bottleneck who owns every decision nor a
hands-off figurehead.

## Audience

Engineers stepping into or considering a tech lead role (so they know the
job and the mindset shift); managers who appoint tech leads (so both sides
agree on expectations); peer executives comparing operating models.
First-person declarative.

## Success criteria

- [x] **Principle is quotable** — highlight states that the tech lead is
      measured by team outcomes, leads without formal authority, and must
      avoid both the bottleneck and the hands-off extremes.
- [x] **The decision discipline survives** — make / delegate / escalate,
      gathering team input before major technical decisions, knowing
      critical systems, dependencies, and risks.
- [x] **The planning discipline survives** — break projects into
      deliverables, parallel versus sequential, critical versus optional,
      push through ambiguity, revisit the plan, premortem plus launch and
      rollback plans, communicate status and risks early.
- [x] **The team-side duties survive** — unblock rather than hoard
      interesting work, delegate to grow people, protect focus time and
      meeting load, avoid heroics, represent the team clearly outward.
- [x] **The process stance survives** — process as a tool, no one perfect
      process, optimize for people and interactions, prefer self-regulating
      systems over policing.
- [x] **Credit is explicit** — References name Camille Fournier's *The
      Manager's Path* and the Tech Lead chapter.
