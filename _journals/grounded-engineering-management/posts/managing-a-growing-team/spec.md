# Spec: Managing a Growing Team

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State how my management changes when a team grows past what direct
involvement can cover, grounded in the growing-team chapter of Julie Zhuo's
*The Making of a Manager*: the shift from the front-line view to the macro
view (vision, org design, delegation, communication), rationing deep
involvement to where attention matters most, deliberately making truth
easier to hear as authority rises (invited dissent, rewarded messengers,
publicly owned mistakes), delegation as a sign of trust with public
ownership and coaching that never takes the work back, judging managers as
multipliers by outcomes with the "would I hire this person again?"
question, and putting myself out of a job so the freed time goes to unique
leverage — cross-org communication, top-talent hiring, priority conflicts,
and escalations. The load-bearing idea: a growing team changes the job
whether I accept it or not; the only choice is whether I change with it.

## Audience

Managers in my organization whose teams are scaling (this is the shift I
expect them to make); the managers who report to me (so they know the
multiplier standard they are judged by); peer executives comparing
operating models. First-person declarative.

## Success criteria

- [x] **Principle is quotable** — highlight carries the macro shift, the
      rising price of truth, the multiplier test with "would I hire this
      person again?", and putting myself out of a job as how the job grows.
- [x] **The perspective shift survives** — front-line to macro, time on
      vision / org design / delegation / communication, attention rationed
      deliberately between diving in and stepping back.
- [x] **The dissent discipline survives** — assume hesitation under
      authority, explicitly invite dissent, reward uncomfortable truths,
      own mistakes publicly, use risk-lowering language.
- [x] **The delegation discipline survives** — big problems as trust,
      public clarity on ownership, coaching without taking the work back,
      self-reliant teams; 1:1s that help the other person rather than
      gather information.
- [x] **The management standard survives** — manager as positive
      multiplier, judged by outcomes not effort or intent, the rehire
      question, the hard move when the answer is no; plus the
      put-yourself-out-of-a-job / unique-leverage pairing and success
      measured by what the whole team achieves.
- [x] **Credit is explicit** — References name Julie Zhuo's *The Making of
      a Manager* and the growing-team chapter.
