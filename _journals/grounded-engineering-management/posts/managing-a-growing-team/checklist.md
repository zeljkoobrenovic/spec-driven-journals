*The working checklist behind this record. The Article tab carries the rationale and anti-patterns.*

## 1. Shift my perspective

- [ ] Move from a front-line view to a macro view
- [ ] Spend time on vision, org design, delegation, and communication
- [ ] Accept that management changes as the team grows

## 2. Adjust how I manage

- [ ] Stop trying to support everyone only through direct involvement
- [ ] Recognize when I have too many reports to stay deep in every detail
- [ ] Find the right balance between diving in and stepping back
- [ ] Pick and choose where my attention matters most

## 3. Make truth easier to hear

- [ ] Assume people may hesitate to challenge me once I have authority
- [ ] Explicitly invite dissent and disagreement
- [ ] Reward people who tell me uncomfortable truths
- [ ] Own my mistakes publicly
- [ ] Use language that lowers the risk of speaking up

## 4. Get better at context switching

- [ ] Prepare for meetings in advance
- [ ] Build a reliable note-taking and task-management system
- [ ] Create regular time for reflection
- [ ] Accept that rapid context switching is part of the job

## 5. Become more people-centric

- [ ] Spend less time proving individual expertise
- [ ] Spend more time getting the best out of other people
- [ ] Focus on hiring strong leaders
- [ ] Build self-reliant teams
- [ ] Communicate clearly and often

## 6. Delegate well

- [ ] Treat delegation as a core leadership skill
- [ ] Give people big problems as a sign of trust
- [ ] Believe my reports can solve hard problems
- [ ] Publicly clarify who owns what
- [ ] Stay available to coach without taking the work back

## 7. Build a shared vision

- [ ] Use 1:1s to help the other person, not for information-gathering
- [ ] Align on top priorities
- [ ] Align on people, purpose, and process
- [ ] Keep reinforcing why the work matters
- [ ] Help my managers establish healthy processes for their own teams

## 8. Address weak management honestly

- [ ] Remember that a manager should be a positive multiplier
- [ ] Notice when a manager is slowing projects, making poor calls, or blocking team growth
- [ ] Judge based on outcomes, not just effort or intent
- [ ] Ask: "Would I hire this person again for this role?"
- [ ] Make the hard move when the answer is no

## 9. Put myself out of a job

- [ ] Look for responsibilities that someone else can do as well as me or better
- [ ] Delegate work that is important but not uniquely dependent on me
- [ ] Invest my time where my unique value is highest
- [ ] Keep expanding my leadership capacity year over year

## 10. Use my unique leverage

- [ ] Identify and communicate what matters across the organization
- [ ] Help hire top talent
- [ ] Resolve priority conflicts between teams
- [ ] Escalate decisions when my managers lack the full picture

## 11. Keep growing with the team

- [ ] Expect my role to evolve as the team grows
- [ ] Create stretch opportunities for my leaders
- [ ] Keep taking on bigger, less familiar challenges
- [ ] Measure success by how much more the whole team can achieve
