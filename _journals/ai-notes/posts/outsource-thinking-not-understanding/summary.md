You can let AI handle your thinking — the drafting, creating, and producing that AI now does cheaply and well. You cannot let AI handle your understanding — the human sense of how something was made and whether it has value. Thinking creates something; understanding is where that thing connects to real life, and it remains yours.

This matters now because of a difference: AI keeps getting better on a set schedule; humans stay mostly the same. AI's ability doubles regularly (the length of tasks AI can do has doubled about every seven months); human focus, memory, and understanding speed stay almost fixed. This gap does not remove humans — it moves the main challenge to understanding, the one thing AI cannot provide for you. As Andrej Karpathy said, "I am becoming the bottleneck of even knowing what we are trying to build, why it is worth doing, and how to direct my agents."

**What changes**

- See "the output appeared" and "we understand it" as different situations. Having a correct design, migration, or analysis is not the same as truly understanding it — and only understanding lets you take ownership, protect, or fix it.
- Build understanding by figuring it out yourself, not just accepting it. Don't take a generated design as is; break it down step by step — start with the simplest version, then handle one problem and one decision at a time — until you fully understand every part. (The system-design-interview format is an example.)
- Use AI actively, not just passively. Asking questions helps build understanding; just handing off tasks weakens it — even when you still feel confident.

**What it costs**

- The "last human mile" — linking a general product to your specific situation and people — does not get smaller as AI improves; it gets bigger, and needs people and money to handle it.
- Understanding happens at human speed. Figuring things out yourself instead of just accepting them is slower at first — but cheaper than dealing with problems later.

**What we are not doing**

- Not arguing against AI or against outsourcing thinking — outsourcing thinking is the point. This is about what you must *keep* when you do.
- Not claiming AI "makes us stupid." The cognitive-offloading evidence is a caution about *passive* use, not a verdict against the tool.
