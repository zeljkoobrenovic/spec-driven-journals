# Spec: Part I — FOUNDATIONS: When a System Should Be Agentic

## Intent

Orient the reader to Part I: where a model should choose the next step, and how much that choice may do. State the part's argument as one line of reasoning across its four chapters (find the model-held decision points, test whether each should exist, size each model-chosen action by consequence and reversibility, choose the execution style and its limits), and frame it through the Ostrell events the chapters use (the January 2027 decision-point maps, the Weekend Fan-out, the March 2027 business-case review, the Double Credit, the tier register).

## Audience

Chief architects, principal engineers and technical leaders who must decide whether, and where, a system should be agentic before designing its components.

## Success criteria

- [ ] Opens with IN THIS PART, WHY THE BUSINESS CARES, WHY ARCHITECTS SHOULD CARE, exactly three KEY POINTS and a FAILURE PATTERNS THIS PART ADDRESSES callout with exact pattern names linked to where-agentic-systems-go-wrong.html.
- [ ] States the argument as one chain across the four chapters, not a list of summaries.
- [ ] Lists the questions the reader will be able to answer.
- [ ] Ostrell numbers match the chapters and ledger (€8,700 and 41 hours; €206,000 vs €38,000; 91% vs 98.4% on 250 cases; 212 customers and €31,400).
- [ ] One mermaid diagram with a Figure caption.
- [ ] Ends with `## Chapters in this part`: one `[[permalink]]` per chapter in config order, saying what it decides and naming its Ostrell case, then hands off to [[what-makes-a-system-agentic]].
- [ ] 500–900 words.

## Non-goals

Teaching the definitions, the ladder, the grid or the bounded loop (the chapters own them); the Across Domains comparison; new citations or external factual claims.
