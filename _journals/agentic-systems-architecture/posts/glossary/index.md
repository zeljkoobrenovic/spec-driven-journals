> **KEY POINTS:**
>
> * **One term, one meaning, one owning chapter.** Every row links to the chapter that defines the term in depth, and the definition follows that chapter and the book's consistency rulings. If a chapter and this page disagree, one of them needs correcting.
> * **The near-synonyms are where designs go wrong.** A turn is not an exchange, a handoff is not a delegation, a receipt is not a dry run, a kill switch is not a quarantine, an approval gate is not an approval record, and an exactly-once effect is not exactly-once delivery. Read a term's neighbors and its "see also" links.
> * **Ostrell names are fictional; protocol and regulation entries are dated.** Keelson's components, the seven agents and the recurring incidents belong to the book's running example. Protocols, standards and laws are described as of October 2026; check the current text before relying on a definition here in a design or a contract.

> **[FAILURE PATTERNS THIS SECTION ADDRESSES](where-agentic-systems-go-wrong.html):**
>
> * **The Lost Handoff** — the same failure at the level of words: when "task", "run" or "approval" means one thing to the agent team and another to the risk owner, the meaning falls out on the way, as the contract id did in free-text handoffs.
> * **The Fire-and-Forget Action** — receipts, idempotency keys, unknown outcomes and exactly-once effects have exact meanings; using them loosely is how a team ends up unable to say whether an action happened once, twice or not at all.

<br>

Most of this book's vocabulary is ordinary distributed-systems language with **one narrower meaning added for agents**. A *receipt* is the receiver's evidence that it accepted an effect, not a log line saying a call returned. A *budget* terminates or holds work; a number on a dashboard is not one. A *harness* is the deterministic code around the model, never the evaluation runner. This glossary pins each term to the definition its owning chapter gives, so that a design review, an incident report and an audit query use the words the same way.

## How to Use This Glossary

Entries are in **one alphabetical list**, grouped by first letter; the jump list below goes straight to a letter. A leading "The" is ignored in sorting, so *The Lost Handoff* sits under L. Each row has three columns: the term, a short plain-language definition, and the chapter that owns the term, sometimes followed by chapters worth reading next.

- **Book terms** are the terms each chapter owns in the plan, plus the rulings that fix shared vocabulary across chapters (*harness*, *turn* versus *exchange*, *exactly-once effect*, *unknown outcome*, *approval record*, *hold*, *staging store*). A qualifier in parentheses, such as *(agent)*, marks the narrower sense this book uses.
- **Acronyms** are listed with their expansion and a one-line note on how the book uses them. Where the full term has its own entry, the acronym row points to it.
- **Failure patterns** are the sixteen patterns of [[where-agentic-systems-go-wrong]], marked *(failure pattern)*, each linked to that guide and to the chapters that repair it.
- **Ostrell entries**, marked *(Ostrell, fictional)*, cover the running example of [[meet-ostrell]]: the autonomy tiers A0 to A3 and consequence bands, the Keelson platform components, the seven agents and the recurring incidents. Ostrell Group, Keelson and every name and number attached to them are fictional.
- **Evidence labels** are the seven kinds of statement every chapter marks, explained in [[introduction]].

The definitions are teaching descriptions, not specification text. The owning chapter has the full treatment, the sources and the dates.

## <a id="g-a"></a>A

| Term | Definition | Chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A0 — Suggest** *(Ostrell, fictional)* | Ostrell's lowest autonomy tier: the agent proposes and a human performs the action. Ostrell sets decisions about an employee (rating, pay, termination, hiring) at A0, so no agent proposes them as action objects. | [[autonomy-and-consequence]]<br>See also [[meet-ostrell]] |
| **A1 — Draft for approval** *(Ostrell, fictional)* | The agent prepares an executable action object and a human approves it before execution. Irreversible external commitments (signatures, third-party payments, legal filings) are capped at A1 by policy, whatever the evidence. | [[autonomy-and-consequence]]<br>See also [[human-oversight-and-bounded-autonomy]] |
| **A2 — Act within limits** *(Ostrell, fictional)* | The agent executes within hard limits on amount, scope and rate, and its actions are reviewed after the fact by sampling. Answerdesk credits of €250 or less moved from A1 to A2 in October 2027. | [[autonomy-and-consequence]]<br>See also [[evidence-for-autonomy]] |
| **A2A (Agent2Agent)** | An open protocol, launched by Google in April 2025 and now a Linux Foundation project, by which agents of different vendors or organizations exchange tasks, messages and artifacts and describe themselves with agent cards. | [[coordination-and-handoffs]]<br>See also [[tool-ecosystems-and-mcp]] |
| **A3 — Act autonomously** *(Ostrell, fictional)* | The agent executes within a budget; humans watch metrics and incidents only. Typical A3 classes are reads, internal drafts and edits inside a sandbox. | [[autonomy-and-consequence]] |
| **Abstention** | A defined "cannot answer, not enough evidence" exit that routes the work to a person. Every agent in the book has one, and a correct abstention counts as success in evaluation. | [[evaluating-agentic-systems]]<br>See also [[domain-high-stakes-decisions]] |
| **ACI** | Short for **agent-computer interface**. | [[tool-contracts]] |
| **Action class** | A set of actions that share an effect type (read, draft, reversible write, compensable write, irreversible write, external commitment), a consequence band and an undo mechanism, so they can share one autonomy tier and one set of limits. "Issue a credit of €250 or less" is an action class; "use the billing tool" is not. | [[autonomy-and-consequence]]<br>See also [[side-effects-and-external-actions]] |
| **Action object** | The typed, versioned, immutable record of one intended external effect, built by deterministic code from the model's proposal before anything executes. It separates intent from execution, and approvals, idempotency keys and receipts all refer to it by its content hash. | [[side-effects-and-external-actions]]<br>See also [[retries-idempotency-and-concurrency]] |
| **Action space** | What the deterministic code around the model will actually execute. The action space, not the prompt, sets the limits of what an agent can do. | [[what-makes-a-system-agentic]]<br>See also [[tool-contracts]] |
| **Activity** | A recorded step of a durable workflow that may do nondeterministic or side-effecting work, such as a model call or a tool call. The engine retries it under a policy and, on replay, returns its recorded result instead of running it again. | [[durable-agent-execution]] |
| **Agent** | A component in which a model holds the locus of control at one or more decision points over several steps: it runs in a loop, chooses its next action from a defined action space, observes the result and chooses again until it stops or is stopped. | [[what-makes-a-system-agentic]] |
| **Agent card** | A JSON document describing a remote agent's identity, capabilities, skills, endpoint and authentication requirements, published at `/.well-known/agent-card.json` in A2A. Clients should verify its signature before trusting it. | [[coordination-and-handoffs]] |
| **The Agent Committee** *(failure pattern)* | Failure pattern: several agents talk to each other where one would do, so tokens, latency and lost accountability rise and nobody can say which agent decided. | [[where-agentic-systems-go-wrong]]<br>See also [[single-and-multi-agent-designs]] |
| **Agent identity** | A registered principal, with an accountable owner, by which downstream systems, policy engines and audit logs recognize an agent as an actor distinct from the user it serves and the infrastructure that runs it. | [[agent-identity-and-least-privilege]] |
| **Agent integration pattern** | One of four ways an agent attaches to a system of record: a tool over an existing API, an event-triggered agent, an agent step inside a workflow, or an agent as a service inside an application. The patterns differ in who starts the run and who writes business state. | [[agents-in-the-enterprise]] |
| **Agent loop** | The cycle every agent runs: the harness puts an observation into the model's context, the model proposes a next action or a final answer, the harness checks and executes the action, and the result becomes the next observation. | [[what-makes-a-system-agentic]]<br>See also [[agent-runtime]] |
| **Agent owner** | The one named person accountable for what an agent does in production: its outcomes, budget, eval suite, on-call rotation and retirement. At Ostrell the agent owner is a business leader, not the platform team. | [[owning-agentic-systems]] |
| **Agent registry** | The control-plane record of every agent and agent version (owners, lifecycle state, action classes and tiers by manifest hash, budgets, outcome metric, retirement plan), acting as the state machine that decides what may run. Keelson's Agent Registry is Ostrell's (fictional) instance. | [[owning-agentic-systems]]<br>See also [[agentic-reference-architecture]] |
| **Agent run** | One execution of one agent version on one task, from acceptance to a terminal state. It has a run id, a principal and on-behalf-of identity fixed at creation, a pinned version manifest, a budget and a task record that only the runtime writes. | [[agent-runtime]]<br>See also [[agent-observability]] |
| **Agent runtime** | The deterministic service that executes agent runs: it owns their lifecycle, enforces budgets and limits, mediates every model call and tool call, and records what happened. In a coding tool the same code is called the harness. | [[agent-runtime]] |
| **Agent trace** | The structured, time-ordered record of one agent run: a tree of spans sharing one trace id, covering every step the harness took on the model's behalf. | [[agent-observability]] |
| **Agent version manifest** | An immutable, content-addressed record naming the exact version of everything that determines an agent's behavior: model id, prompts, tool schemas, policies, retrieval index snapshot, memory schema and orchestration code. Its hash is the agent's version identity. | [[versioning-agent-systems]]<br>See also [[staged-rollout-and-rollback]] |
| **Agent-computer interface (ACI)** | The set of tools an agent holds, with their descriptions, argument shapes, results and errors, treated as an interface designed for a model as its user. The term comes from the 2024 SWE-agent paper. | [[tool-contracts]] |
| **Agentic system** | A system that contains at least one agent, together with the deterministic code, data, policies and people around it. Workflows that route through a model count as agentic systems too. | [[what-makes-a-system-agentic]] |
| **The Agent-Shaped Hammer** *(failure pattern)* | Failure pattern: an agent loop is used where a fixed workflow, a single model call or plain code would do, and nobody can say what the model decides that code could not. | [[where-agentic-systems-go-wrong]]<br>See also [[when-agents-are-justified]] |
| **Alias drift** | A behavior change caused by a name that resolves to different artifacts over time with no change on the consumer's side: a provider's model alias, a tool fetched by name, a prompt referenced by label, an index called "current". | [[versioning-agent-systems]]<br>See also [[models-and-the-model-gateway]] |
| **The Alias Drift** *(Ostrell, fictional)* | Ostrell incident, August 2027: Closewise's classification step called a provider alias that moved to a newer model; accuracy fell from 96.1% to 89.4% and controller rejections rose from 4% to 11% over 4 days. A1 approval held, and Keelson banned aliases in September 2027. | [[versioning-agent-systems]]<br>See also [[meet-ostrell]] |
| **Ambient authority** | Authority a component holds because of where it runs or what it was configured with, rather than authority attached to the specific request. An agent running under a service account or a user's full credentials has it. | [[agent-identity-and-least-privilege]]<br>See also [[agent-threat-model]] |
| **Ambient Authority** *(failure pattern)* | Failure pattern: the agent acts with a service account or the user's full credentials, so any tool call, including an injected one, can reach anything that identity can. | [[where-agentic-systems-go-wrong]]<br>See also [[agent-identity-and-least-privilege]] |
| **Amnesiac Execution** *(failure pattern)* | Failure pattern: task state lives only in the conversation, so a crash, deploy or timeout restarts the task from scratch or repeats its side effects. | [[where-agentic-systems-go-wrong]]<br>See also [[task-state-and-checkpoints]], [[durable-agent-execution]] |
| **Answerdesk** *(Ostrell, fictional)* | Ostrell's customer-support agent for Ostrell Field (fictional): about 2.6 million conversations a year, a bounded loop that answers, updates tickets, schedules revisits and issues capped service credits, owned by Lena Brandt. | [[meet-ostrell]]<br>See also [[domain-customer-operations]] |
| **Approval fatigue** | The fall in what an approver actually checks as approvals become frequent, similar and mostly correct. It shows as a rising approval rate, falling review time and a falling catch rate on known-bad cases. | [[human-oversight-and-bounded-autonomy]] |
| **Approval gate** | A policy enforcement point at which execution of one specific action object waits for a recorded decision by an authorized human. A deterministic rule, never the model, decides whether a proposal needs approval. | [[human-oversight-and-bounded-autonomy]] |
| **Approval record** | The record of an approval, bound to the exact action object version (content hash), with approver identity, the evidence shown, a scope and an expiry. It is re-checked just before execution and is void if the action, the policy version or relevant facts changed. | [[human-oversight-and-bounded-autonomy]]<br>See also [[side-effects-and-external-actions]] |
| **Architecture review (agent)** | The review that approves a new agent envelope or a change to one. It is distinct from the evaluation gate, which decides whether a version may run inside an envelope already approved. | [[owning-agentic-systems]]<br>See also [[evidence-for-autonomy]] |
| **Assumption (evidence label)** | An illustrative volume, latency target or cost that is neither an Ostrell fact nor documented, marked "Assume…" or *(assumption)*. | [[introduction]] |
| **Automation bias** | Over-reliance on automated advice, documented in novices and experts alike and not removed by training. It is why an approval gate must show evidence and be measured. | [[human-oversight-and-bounded-autonomy]]<br>See also [[autonomy-and-consequence]] |
| **Autonomous agent** | An execution style in which the model chooses the next step over a long horizon, plans for itself and often works with no human watching. Scout is Ostrell's most autonomous design. | [[workflows-loops-and-autonomous-agents]] |
| **Autonomy by Default** *(failure pattern)* | Failure pattern: the agent may take every action its tools allow, because autonomy was never sized to consequence and reversibility or earned with evidence. | [[where-agentic-systems-go-wrong]]<br>See also [[autonomy-and-consequence]], [[evidence-for-autonomy]] |
| **Autonomy tier** | The degree of human involvement required before or after one class of action takes effect, defined by what the human does and what the system must provide. Tiers are granted per action class, not per agent; Ostrell's register has four, A0 to A3. | [[autonomy-and-consequence]]<br>See also [[evidence-for-autonomy]] |

| **Topology (agent)** | The graph of agents and the meaning of its edges: who may start whom, whether control returns and what context travels. Common shapes are single agent with tools, orchestrator–worker, supervisor, peer and pipeline. | [[single-and-multi-agent-designs]] |
