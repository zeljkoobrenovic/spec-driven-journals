# Spec: Operating Principle: Leadership Styles

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State how I lead: by choosing the style the situation demands rather than
defaulting to habit — policy for recurring decisions that must scale without
me in the room, consensus when context is distributed and buy-in drives
execution, conviction when a high-stakes, non-recurring decision is stalled
and someone must decide. The post turns the "Developing Leadership Styles"
checklist into a durable operating principle: the goal is adaptability, not
purity — any style can be misused, no single style works everywhere, and I
deliberately practice my weaker styles (and run the micromanagement
self-check) to stay fluent in all three.

## Audience

Engineering leaders (including future me) who want to see how I decide *how*
to decide; my own team and peer executives who want to predict which mode I
will bring to a given decision. Declarative first-person register — a vision
statement, not a how-to tutorial.

## Success criteria

- [ ] **Principle is quotable** — the highlight blockquote states the
      style-follows-situation, adaptability-not-purity stance in one
      paragraph.
- [ ] **Selection criteria are concrete** — the when-to-use conditions for
      policy, consensus, and conviction appear as explicit triggers, so a
      reader can predict which style a given decision gets.
- [ ] **Execution is shown, not just named** — each style's execution
      checklist (codify and roll out policy; frame, hear, and close
      consensus; pressure-test and document conviction) is rendered as what
      I actually do.
- [ ] **The checklist survives intact** — every section of the source PDF
      (choose the right style for the situation, style execution checklists,
      micromanagement self-check, development checklist, balance &
      engagement check, core principle reminder) appears in the Checklist tab (`checklist.md`),
      regrouped as bullets.
- [ ] **Anti-patterns are concrete** — at least three (e.g. consensus as
      conflict avoidance, conviction as ego, policy as bureaucracy for
      one-off decisions).
- [ ] **Credit is explicit** — Authoritative References names Larson's *The
      Engineering Executive's Primer* and the lethain.com companion essay.

## Non-goals

- Not a taxonomy of personality-based leadership archetypes — the three
  styles are decision-making modes, not identities.
- Not about delegation and verification depth — [[inspected-trust]] covers
  how I check work without micromanaging; here the self-check only guards
  the conviction style.
- Not about workload and sustainability — [[managing-energy]] covers whether
  my role is too challenging or not challenging enough beyond the balance
  check noted here.
