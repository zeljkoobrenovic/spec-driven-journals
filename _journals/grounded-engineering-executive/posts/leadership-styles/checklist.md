*The working checklist behind this principle, keeping the source's own grouping: selection triggers first, then execution per style, then the self-checks that keep the styles honest. The Article tab carries the rationale, implications, and anti-patterns.*

### Choose the Right Style for the Situation

**Lead with Policy when:**

- [ ] The decision recurs frequently.
- [ ] Many people must make the decision consistently.
- [ ] Speed and predictability matter.
- [ ] You want the organization to move without you in the room.

**Lead with Consensus when:**

- [ ] Multiple stakeholders hold a critical context.
- [ ] No one person has the full picture.
- [ ] The decision is important enough to remember in 6 months.
- [ ] Buy-in materially affects execution.
- [ ] The issue isn't well-suited for policy.

**Lead with Conviction when:**

- [ ] The decision is high-stakes and long-lasting.
- [ ] Stakeholders are deeply divided.
- [ ] No one has clear authority or a full context.
- [ ] The decision is unlikely to be repeated.
- [ ] Momentum is stalled, and someone must decide.

### Style Execution Checklists

**Leading with Policy**

- [ ] Identify a frequently recurring decision.
- [ ] Study how strong decision-makers handle it today.
- [ ] Codify the best judgment into a clear written policy.
- [ ] Validate with key contributors.
- [ ] Roll it out intentionally.
- [ ] Set a review point to revisit with data.
- [ ] Iterate as needed.

**Leading from Consensus**

- [ ] Confirm the decision needs broad input.
- [ ] Identify all relevant stakeholders.
- [ ] Create a shared communication channel.
- [ ] Write a framing document outlining known perspectives.
- [ ] Ensure everyone feels heard.
- [ ] Appoint the most senior engaged leader.
- [ ] Set a clear deadline.
- [ ] Follow the agreed decision-making process.
- [ ] Close the loop and communicate the outcome.

**Leading with Conviction**

- [ ] Confirm that the decision is important and non-recurring.
- [ ] Deeply understand the trade-offs.
- [ ] Engage the individuals with the most context.
- [ ] Write down your reasoning.
- [ ] Pressure-test your thinking.
- [ ] Communicate a tentative decision with a short timeout.
- [ ] Finalize and move to execution.
- [ ] Document the rationale for transparency.

### Micromanagement Self-Check

**Ask yourself:**

- [ ] Am I carefully considering feedback from those closest to the work?
- [ ] Am I adding friction to a team that would reach a similar outcome without me?

- If yes to the first and no to the second → you're likely not micromanaging.

### Development Checklist

- [ ] Identify which style you default to.
- [ ] Identify which style you underuse.
- [ ] Once a month, review upcoming decisions.
- [ ] Choose one to intentionally handle using your weaker style.
- [ ] Debrief with someone skilled in that style.
- [ ] Compare outcomes vs. your default approach.
- [ ] Repeat consistently.

### Balance & Engagement Check

- [ ] Am I defaulting to one style out of habit?
- [ ] Has my company context changed (growth, downturn, scale)?
- [ ] Am I too distant from details?
- [ ] Am I over-engaging unnecessarily?
- [ ] Is my role currently too challenging or not challenging enough?

### Core Principle Reminder

- [ ] You can misuse any leadership style.
- [ ] No single style works everywhere.
- [ ] Strong executives develop fluency in all three.
- [ ] The goal is adaptability, not purity.
