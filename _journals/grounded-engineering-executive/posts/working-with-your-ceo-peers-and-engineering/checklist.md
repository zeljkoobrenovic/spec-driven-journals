*The working checklist behind this principle, in the source's ten-practice order: it starts with the honest support diagnosis everything else flows from. The Article tab carries the rationale, implications, and anti-patterns.*

### 1. Diagnose Your Support Level

**Assess whether you are:**

- [ ] **Supported** — peers proactively help make your initiatives succeed.
- [ ] **Tolerated** — others comply but don't actively improve or support your work.
- [ ] **Resented** — your work is seen as a distraction or nuisance.

**If not clearly supported:**

- [ ] Identify which functions feel indifferent or resistant.
- [ ] Schedule 1:1s to understand their concerns.
- [ ] Clarify shared goals and success metrics.

### 2. Navigate Implicit Power Dynamics

- [ ] Avoid over-anchoring to the CEO's narrative.
- [ ] Gather perspectives from:
  - [ ] CEO
  - [ ] Board
  - [ ] Executive peers
  - [ ] Your own team

**Before acting:**

- [ ] Confirm whether Engineering is the problem or reacting to a broader issue.
- [ ] Distinguish symptoms from root causes.
- [ ] Validate assumptions directly with your team.

### 3. Bridge Conflicting Narratives

**When facing cross-functional tension:**

- [ ] List each function's perspective.
- [ ] Identify where perspectives overlap.
- [ ] Create a unified explanation that integrates concerns.
- [ ] Frame solutions as company-first, not function-first.

### 4. Avoid Anchoring to Past Experience

**Do not assume:**

- [ ] Your previous company's playbook will work here.
- [ ] Decision rights are the same.
- [ ] Resource levels are comparable.

**Instead:**

- [ ] Observe how decisions are made.
- [ ] Ask why processes exist.
- [ ] Adapt before implementing change.

### 5. Build an Alignment Habit

**Regularly invite feedback:**

- [ ] After exec meetings, ask: "What could I have done better?"
- [ ] Ask narrow, specific questions.
- [ ] Thank people for feedback.
- [ ] Demonstrate visible improvement.

**Ensure feedback loops exist:**

- [ ] Ask peers periodically.
- [ ] Ask direct reports periodically.

### 6. Focus on a Small Number of Changes

**Before launching new initiatives:**

- [ ] Review outcomes of prior changes.
- [ ] Confirm existing changes are working.
- [ ] Limit concurrent major initiatives.
- [ ] Deliver visible, meaningful wins.

**Avoid:**

- [ ] Starting multiple migrations at once.
- [ ] Rewriting processes without proving value.

### 7. Embrace Healthy Conflict (Resolve Unhealthy Conflict)

**Accept:**

- [ ] Conflict signals growth and complexity.
- [ ] Disagreement ≠ dysfunction.

**Watch for:**

- [ ] Recurring unresolved debates.
- [ ] Repeated re-litigation of decisions.

**When conflict persists:**

- [ ] Clarify each viewpoint.
- [ ] Attempt synthesis.
- [ ] Establish structured escalation if needed.
- [ ] Reflect: "Am I preventing resolution?"

### 8. Use Structured Escalation

- If disagreement cannot be resolved:

- [ ] Step 1: Agree to resolve within 5 days.
- [ ] Step 2: Prioritize time to align directly.
- [ ] Step 3: If still stuck, perform a joint escalation with shared context.
- [ ] Step 4: Commit to the decision made by the escalated authority.

### 9. Survive Peer Panic

**If a peer begins blaming your function:**

- [ ] Assume stress before malice.
- [ ] Seek empathy and understand their pressure.
- [ ] Solve the problem together if possible.

**If behavior becomes disruptive:**

- [ ] Focus on improving execution vs. replacing them (if feasible).
- [ ] Shield your team from unnecessary chaos.
- [ ] Encourage the CEO to clarify leadership decisions when needed.

### 10. Maintain a Long-Term Perspective

**When uncertain, ask:**

- [ ] "What maximizes company impact over the next 3 years?"
- [ ] "Will this build or erode executive trust?"
- [ ] "Am I operating as a company-first leader?"
