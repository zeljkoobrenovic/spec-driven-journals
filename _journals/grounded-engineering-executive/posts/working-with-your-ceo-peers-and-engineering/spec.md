# Spec: Operating Principle: Working with Your CEO, Peers, and Engineering

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State how I balance the three constituencies an engineering executive serves
— the CEO, executive peers, and Engineering itself: I diagnose honestly
whether I am supported, tolerated, or resented; I gather perspectives beyond
the CEO's narrative before acting; I bridge conflicting cross-functional
narratives into company-first framings; I avoid anchoring to my previous
company's playbook; I build an alignment habit of inviting feedback; I limit
concurrent changes; I treat conflict as a signal of growth and resolve the
unhealthy kind through structured escalation; and when uncertain I ask what
maximizes company impact over the next three years. The post turns the
"Working with Your CEO, Peers, and Engineering" checklist into a durable
operating principle: operate as a company-first leader, not Engineering's
ambassador.

## Audience

Engineering leaders (including future me) navigating the executive team; the
CEO and peer executives who want to know how I will engage with them and
handle disagreement. Declarative first-person register — a vision statement,
not a how-to tutorial.

## Success criteria

- [ ] **Principle is quotable** — the highlight blockquote states the
      company-first balancing stance in one paragraph.
- [ ] **Rationale is argued, not asserted** — explains why tolerated is not
      good enough, why over-anchoring to the CEO's narrative misleads, and
      why disagreement is not dysfunction.
- [ ] **The checklist survives intact** — every section of the source PDF
      (diagnose your support level, navigate implicit power dynamics, bridge
      conflicting narratives, avoid anchoring to past experience, build an
      alignment habit, focus on a small number of changes, embrace healthy
      conflict, use structured escalation, survive peer panic, maintain a
      long-term perspective) appears in the Checklist tab (`checklist.md`), regrouped as
      bullets.
- [ ] **Anti-patterns are concrete** — at least three (e.g. importing the
      previous company's playbook, starting multiple migrations at once,
      re-litigating settled decisions, assuming malice when a peer panics).
- [ ] **Credit is explicit** — Authoritative References names Larson's *The
      Engineering Executive's Primer* and the lethain.com companion post.
