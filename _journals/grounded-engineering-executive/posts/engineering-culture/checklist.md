*The working checklist behind this principle. The Article tab carries the rationale, implications, and anti-patterns.*

## 1. Opportunity and Membership

### Define inclusion

- [ ] Ensure individuals have access to **opportunity** and **membership**.
- [ ] Treat **opportunity** as access to professional success and development.
- [ ] Treat **membership** as participation as a version of themselves they feel comfortable with.

### Improve opportunity

- [ ] Create rubrics for important people decisions.
- [ ] Use rubrics for promotions, hiring, performance designations, management transitions, and similar decisions.
- [ ] Use structured processes to select project leads.
- [ ] Make education and conference budgets explicit.
- [ ] Reach out directly to people who may not apply for opportunities on their own.
- [ ] Create training and learning programs available to everyone.
- [ ] Respect the process even when it is inconvenient.
- [ ] Avoid concentrating opportunities among a small group of people.

### Measure opportunity

- [ ] Track retention by cohort.
- [ ] Track usage rate: who is picked for project-selection opportunities.
- [ ] Track level distribution across different backgrounds.
- [ ] Track time at level before promotion.
- [ ] Review whether opportunities are distributed fairly.

### Improve membership

- [ ] Create recurring weekly social events during working hours.
- [ ] Support Employee Resource Groups.
- [ ] Hold team offsites regularly.
- [ ] Use coffee chats to connect people across teams.
- [ ] Encourage team lunches, while being mindful of comfort and accessibility.
- [ ] Offer a portfolio of membership-building activities rather than relying on a single format.
- [ ] Avoid events that exclude people because of diet, physical ability, caregiving, timing, or comfort.

### Measure membership

- [ ] Track retention as a long-term signal.
- [ ] Track referral rate by cohort.
- [ ] Track attendance at recurring events and team lunches.
- [ ] Track quantity and completion rate of coffee chats.
- [ ] Partner with HR to collect and interpret the data.

## 2. Select Project Leads Fairly

- [ ] Define the project's scope and goals in a short document.
- [ ] Specify the time commitment.
- [ ] Specify requirements to apply, or explicitly state there are none.
- [ ] Specify clear selection criteria.
- [ ] Announce the opportunity broadly.
- [ ] Allow people to apply privately.
- [ ] Do not show applicants who else has applied.
- [ ] Give people at least three working days to apply.
- [ ] Personally nudge strong candidates who may not self-select.
- [ ] Select the project lead based on the stated criteria.
- [ ] Consider every applicant against the criteria.
- [ ] Write a short rationale for each applicant where possible.
- [ ] Privately confirm with the selected lead.
- [ ] Assign a sponsor who has successfully completed a similar project.
- [ ] Notify applicants who were not selected.
- [ ] Give helpful feedback to rejected applicants.
- [ ] Kick off the project with the lead, sponsor, and relevant stakeholders.
- [ ] Record the project, selected lead, sponsor, and project brief in a public spreadsheet.
- [ ] Track who gets leaned on for important work over time.

## 3. Make Your Peers Your First Team

- [ ] Learn what your peers are working on.
- [ ] Invest time in understanding peer goals, constraints, and priorities.
- [ ] Spend time together so peers become people, not abstract roles.
- [ ] Avoid projecting motives onto peers you do not know well.
- [ ] Create conditions where peers help one another succeed.
- [ ] Avoid zero-sum culture around scarce rewards.
- [ ] Explicitly discuss shifting identity from "my team only" to "my peer team first."
- [ ] Treat peer collaboration as a core part of management work.
- [ ] Join a rapidly expanding company where peer learning is abundant, when possible.
- [ ] Make your peers your first team.

## 4. Build Inclusive Senior-Role Processes

- [ ] Announce senior roles internally.
- [ ] Ask for internal candidates.
- [ ] Encourage eligible people to apply, especially those who are uncertain.
- [ ] Give candidates a week or two to consider applying.
- [ ] Ensure every internal candidate has a structured way to apply.
- [ ] Evaluate candidates fairly; do not default to preferring internal candidates.

### Evaluate candidates on

- [ ] Partnership with peers and managed teams.
- [ ] Execution and operational excellence.
- [ ] Vision for the team's future.
- [ ] Strategy to move from current state to future state.
- [ ] Spoken and written communication.
- [ ] Stakeholder management.

### Use structured assessment tools

- [ ] Collect feedback from four or five coworkers.
- [ ] Include peers from other teams.
- [ ] Include people the candidate has managed.
- [ ] Include people the candidate would not manage.
- [ ] Ask candidates to write a 90-day transition plan.
- [ ] Provide feedback on the 90-day plan.
- [ ] Ask candidates to write a vision/strategy document.
- [ ] Provide feedback on the strategy document.
- [ ] Have candidates present their strategy to peers.
- [ ] Have candidates present one-on-one to an executive.
- [ ] Use the process as practice and development, not only evaluation.

## 5. Manage Company Freedoms Deliberately

- [ ] Identify the positive freedoms your culture enables.
- [ ] Identify the negative freedoms your culture protects against.
- [ ] Recognize that every positive freedom can reduce a negative freedom.
- [ ] Recognize that every negative freedom can eliminate a positive freedom.
- [ ] Balance freedom with consequence.
- [ ] Avoid treating freedom as an excuse for not taking accountability.
- [ ] Use systems thinking to understand cultural freedoms.
- [ ] Keep processes that are working.
- [ ] Change processes when they no longer fit new projects or circumstances.
- [ ] Avoid blindly importing cultural practices from companies in different contexts.
- [ ] Place positive and negative freedoms in the right circumstances.

## 6. Kill Your Heroes, Stop Doing It Harder

### Watch for hero culture

- [ ] Notice when "work harder" becomes the default answer.
- [ ] Recognize that heroic work often prevents nonheroes from contributing.
- [ ] Watch for burned-out heroes.
- [ ] Watch for alienated teammates.
- [ ] Watch for projects that remain broken despite heroic effort.
- [ ] Do not reward systems that depend on one or two exceptional people.

### Reset broken systems

- [ ] Identify whether the problem is an effort issue or a system design issue.
- [ ] Look for stockpiles of accumulated problems rather than single causes.
- [ ] Accept that projects fail one sprint at a time.
- [ ] Accept that fixing projects also takes time.
- [ ] Stop using short bursts of effort to cover months of bad process.
- [ ] Reset direction when you have the authority to do so.
- [ ] Take responsibility when you set the original direction.
- [ ] Cut losses quickly when decisions were wrong.
- [ ] Help the team reach a reset point when you do not control policy.
- [ ] Step back from broken systems that absorb effort without improving.
- [ ] Avoid creating rifts by pushing others into hero mode.
- [ ] Learn from mistakes and move forward.
