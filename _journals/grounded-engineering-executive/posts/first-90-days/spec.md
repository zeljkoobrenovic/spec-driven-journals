# Spec: Operating Principle: First 90 Days

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State how I operate in the first 90 days of an engineering executive role: I
prioritize learning over change — understand the business, the people, the
work, and the technology before modifying any of them, and limit early changes
to one or two high-leverage improvements. The post turns the "First 90 Days"
checklist into a durable operating principle: why deliberate slowness is the
fastest path to durable impact, and what I concretely do in those 90 days.

## Audience

Engineering leaders (including future me) who want to see how I ramp into an
executive role; peer executives and CEOs who want to know what to expect from
my first quarter. Declarative first-person register — a vision statement, not
a how-to tutorial.

## Success criteria

- [ ] **Principle is quotable** — the highlight blockquote states the
      learning-over-change stance in one paragraph.
- [ ] **Rationale is argued, not asserted** — explains why early visible wins
      are a trap and why trust precedes conflict.
- [ ] **The checklist survives intact** — every section of the source PDF
      (expectations, business, relationships, work, org health, technology,
      hiring, limited changes, reflection, support, sustainability) appears in
      the Checklist tab (`checklist.md`), regrouped as bullets.
- [ ] **Anti-patterns are concrete** — at least three (e.g. "how we did it at
      my last job", quick-win theater, disrupting systems without a working
      alternative).
- [ ] **Credit is explicit** — Authoritative References names Larson's *The
      Engineering Executive's Primer* and the lethain.com companion post.

## Non-goals

- Not a general onboarding guide for engineers or managers — executive role
  only ([[engineering-onboarding]] covers engineer onboarding).
- Not a job-search post — [[getting-the-job]] covers everything before day one.
- No company-specific commitments (dates, org names, metrics targets).
