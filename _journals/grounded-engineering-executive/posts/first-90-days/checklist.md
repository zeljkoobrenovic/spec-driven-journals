*The working checklist behind this principle. It is grouped the way I use it; the guiding principles come first because everything else derives from them. The Article tab carries the rationale, implications, and anti-patterns.*

### Guiding Principles

- [ ] Prioritize learning over early change.
- [ ] Understand systems before modifying them.
- [ ] Focus on durable improvements, not visible quick wins.
- [ ] Build trust before conflict.
- [ ] Move slower than your instincts tell you.

### Clarify Expectations (Week 1–2)

- [ ] Ask your manager (CEO/CTO) to write explicit expectations for your role.
- [ ] Ask: "What will success look like in 6–12 months?"
- [ ] Identify any urgent, dire issues needing immediate attention.
- [ ] Align on measurable goals for your first 90 days.

### Understand the Business

- [ ] Understand how the business makes money.
- [ ] Know where money is spent and the current financial runway.
- [ ] Identify what must be true in 12 months for step-function growth.
- [ ] Identify knowledge gaps between Engineering and the business.
- [ ] Learn the company's core domain deeply.

### Build Relationships and Trust

- [ ] Go on a listening tour — meet individuals and teams.
- [ ] Meet cross-functional peers and stakeholders.
- [ ] Set up recurring 1:1s (directs and skip-levels).
- [ ] Share weekly or monthly updates on what you are learning.
- [ ] Attend routine forums before changing them.
- [ ] Shadow support tickets, customer and partner meetings, and user testing sessions.

### Learn How Work Gets Done

- [ ] Map how ideas become shipped work.
- [ ] Understand how work is assigned and prioritized.
- [ ] Identify who handles emergencies.
- [ ] Determine whether execution systems are working and scaling.
- [ ] Identify internal engineering velocity metrics and external business-impact metrics.

### Understand Organizational Health

- [ ] Assess team morale and inclusion.
- [ ] Identify who is succeeding and who isn't — and why.
- [ ] Document undocumented processes.
- [ ] Spot-check compensation and inclusion health.
- [ ] Pay attention to non-product engineering roles (TPM, SRE, and similar).

### Understand the Technology

- [ ] Evaluate whether the existing tech stack is effective; identify major friction points.
- [ ] Learn how major technical decisions are made.
- [ ] Build and deploy a small, trivial change yourself.
- [ ] Join an on-call rotation (as observer if needed) and attend incident reviews.
- [ ] Document the technology history, the context behind decisions, and the implicit engineering strategy.

### Understand and Improve Hiring

- [ ] Review hiring funnel metrics and pipeline; establish a recurring recruiting meeting.
- [ ] Shadow interviews and onboarding.
- [ ] Identify the 1–3 highest-priority missing roles and personally help close top candidates.
- [ ] Evaluate whether the hiring process needs an overhaul.
- [ ] Kick off engineering brand efforts (blog, talks, social presence).

### Make the Right (Limited) Changes

- [ ] Limit early changes to 1–2 high-leverage improvements.
- [ ] Avoid disrupting systems without a working alternative.
- [ ] Consider adding a weekly operational review.
- [ ] Evaluate org structure for execution impediments.
- [ ] Plan next year's org growth (size, roles, transitions).

### Learn Through Reflection

- [ ] Follow a "20–40 rule" for problem-solving before asking for help.
- [ ] Create a regular thinking space to slow down.
- [ ] Avoid rushing to judgment without context, and avoid referencing "how we did it at my last job".
- [ ] Write down what you are learning.

### Build External Support

- [ ] Build a peer support group of similar leaders.
- [ ] Consider engaging an executive coach.
- [ ] Create space for self-care: sleep, exercise, therapy, hobbies.

### Protect Long-Term Sustainability

- [ ] Assess whether the team's pace is sustainable.
- [ ] Identify energy drains — for you and for the team.
- [ ] Add energizing practices deliberately, and avoid burnout cycles early.
