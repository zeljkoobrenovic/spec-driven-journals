# Spec: Operating Principle: Personal and Organizational Prestige

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State how I approach prestige — mine and the organization's: I first check
whether I need to invest at all (can I hire senior people, do recruiters
call, does my network expand naturally?), and when I do invest, I optimize
for prestige — ambient, positive, easily discoverable familiarity — not
audience size: a small number of timeless, high-quality artifacts (a blog
post or talk born from real experience), distributed simply, kept
discoverable, repeated only occasionally, and never measured in pageviews or
followers. The post turns the "Personal & Organizational Prestige" checklist
into a durable operating principle for both the individual and the
organization: meaningful engineering problems and a little excellent content
beat a heavy content machine.

## Audience

Engineering leaders (including future me) tempted to build an audience;
my own organization when it asks how we present engineering externally;
recruiters and hiring managers reading this as an artifact of the very
principle it states. Declarative first-person register — a vision statement,
not a how-to tutorial.

## Success criteria

- [ ] **Principle is quotable** — the highlight blockquote states the
      prestige-not-audience stance (small amount, high quality, timeless,
      discoverable) in one paragraph.
- [ ] **Rationale is argued, not asserted** — explains why prestige ≠ brand
      ≠ followers, why the invest-at-all check comes first, and why content
      volume is the wrong axis.
- [ ] **Bad metrics are named** — pageviews, follower counts, content
      volume, and sales are explicitly rejected in favor of timeboxed effort
      and observed opportunity expansion.
- [ ] **The organizational half survives** — the "For Organizations" section
      (attractive problems, highlighting prestigious work, low-frequency
      quality content) is a full peer of the personal half, not a footnote.
- [ ] **The checklist survives intact** — every section of the source PDF
      (decide if you need to invest, understand the goal, timeless topic,
      right format, high-quality content, distribute simply, discoverable,
      repeat lightly, avoid bad metrics, for organizations, core principle)
      appears in the Checklist tab (`checklist.md`), regrouped as bullets.
- [ ] **Credit is explicit** — Authoritative References names Larson's *The
      Engineering Executive's Primer* and the lethain.com companion essay.

## Non-goals

- Not a networking post — [[your-network]] covers relationships and
  outreach; prestige is the passive-awareness complement, and writing as a
  networking channel stays there.
- Not an employer-branding or recruiting-marketing playbook — [[hiring]]
  covers the hiring process itself; here the organization's prestige is
  about the work, not the funnel.
- No commitment to a personal publishing cadence or platform — the principle
  explicitly rejects the content machine.
