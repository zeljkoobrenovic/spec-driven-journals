*The working checklist behind this principle, grouped by phase. The Article tab carries the rationale, implications, and anti-patterns.*

### Before You Start

- [ ] I'm confident in my **diagnosis** of the organization.
- [ ] I'm **willing and able to enforce** this strategy.
- [ ] I believe this strategy will create **real leverage**, not just sound good.
- [ ] I'm ready to **personally write** the strategy, not fully delegate it.

### Define the Structure

- [ ] **Diagnosis** — what is actually going on?
- [ ] **Guiding policies** — what principles will govern decisions?
- [ ] **Coherent actions** — what concrete actions implement the policies?

### Diagnosis

- [ ] Review the roadmap, financial plan, and business priorities.
- [ ] Incorporate survey data (developer productivity, culture, and similar).
- [ ] Identify root causes, not just symptoms.
- [ ] Validate the diagnosis with senior engineering managers, staff+ engineers, product leadership, and executive peers.
- [ ] Actively seek out skeptics; avoid copying assumptions from previous roles.
- [ ] Make sure the diagnosis clearly explains the real constraints.

### Guiding Policies

- [ ] **Resource allocation** — explicitly defined, clearly mapped back to the diagnosis, investing in unowned but critical areas (security, reliability, developer productivity).
- [ ] **Fundamental rules** — clear organization-wide standards (tech stack, lifecycle, review process), exceptions with a defined approval path, and an explanation of why each rule matters.
- [ ] **Decision-making model** — what teams decide independently, what requires review or approval, a defined escalation path, and the *why* behind the structure.

### Guiding Policies Quality Check

- [ ] **Applicable** — useful in real trade-offs.
- [ ] **Enforced** — there is accountability.
- [ ] **Leverage-creating** — produces compounding impact.
- [ ] If any policy fails one of these, reconsider it.

### Coherent Actions

- [ ] **Enforcement** — a process to maintain policies, an ongoing process for evaluating exceptions, clear enforcement ownership.
- [ ] **Escalations** — a small, clear escalation path; teams know how to challenge a policy constructively.
- [ ] **Transitions** — defined current → future state, explicit resource shifts, clear migration plans, organizational changes defined where needed.

### Writing Process

- [ ] Identify 3–5 trusted stakeholders for early feedback; iterate the diagnosis 1:1 with them; refine guiding policies with them.
- [ ] Consider private feedback from 2–3 external engineering executives.
- [ ] Share the diagnosis and guiding policies with key stakeholders; socialize before announcing; remove sensitive content before wider circulation.
- [ ] Meet 1:1 with likely strong dissenters; incorporate valid feedback without weakening the core intent.

### Launch

- [ ] Schedule a strategy presentation meeting and explain the rationale behind decisions.
- [ ] Set a short feedback window (about one week), finalize the document, and send a formal announcement.
- [ ] Commit to reviewing impact in about two months, and set an annual refresh cadence.

### Missing Company Strategy

If broader company strategies are undocumented, clarify — even informally:

- [ ] The business strategy, the product strategy, and the investment thesis across functions.
- [ ] Cash-flow constraints, competitive threats, the distribution model, and how success is measured.

### Ongoing Review

- [ ] Policies are being followed; exceptions are handled consistently; the escalation process works.
- [ ] Resource allocation reflects stated priorities.
- [ ] The strategy is creating visible leverage, and impact is reviewed at defined intervals.
