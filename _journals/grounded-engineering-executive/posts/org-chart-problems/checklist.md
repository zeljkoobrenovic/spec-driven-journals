## Team Design Foundations

- [ ] Apply **Conway's Law** when designing software systems.
- [ ] Design **software architecture and team interactions together**.
- [ ] Clarify each team's **purpose and responsibilities**.
- [ ] Use a **humanistic approach** that treats teams as central to software delivery.
- [ ] Align team structures with long-term organizational adaptability.

## Org Charts and Communication Flow

- [ ] Recognize that organizations tend to produce designs that reflect their **communication paths**.
- [ ] Do not rely only on the formal org chart to understand how work really happens.
- [ ] Identify the organization's:
  - [ ] Formal structure
  - [ ] Informal structure
  - [ ] Value creation structure
- [ ] Look for gaps between the org chart and actual communication patterns.
- [ ] Avoid excessive matrix management and unclear reporting lines.
- [ ] Reduce unnecessary communication paths.
- [ ] Define clear, intentional team interactions.
- [ ] Choose software architectures that support **team-scoped flow**.
- [ ] Optimize for the whole system, not just local team efficiency.
- [ ] Use team structures that encourage modular, decoupled systems.
- [ ] Design the organization only when there is a compelling reason.
- [ ] Develop multiple design options before choosing one.
- [ ] Choose the right time to redesign.
- [ ] Watch for signs that teams, responsibilities, or communication paths are out of alignment.
- [ ] Stay alert to future business, technology, and regulatory changes.

## Cognitive Load and Team Boundaries

- [ ] Treat the **team**, not the individual, as the primary unit of delivery.
- [ ] Keep team size within effective collaboration limits.
- [ ] Limit multi-team groupings based on **Dunbar's number**.
- [ ] Match team responsibilities to the team's cognitive capacity.
- [ ] Avoid assigning too many domains, services, or responsibilities to one team.
- [ ] Establish clear team boundaries.
- [ ] Protect teams from excessive context switching.
- [ ] Ensure teams have enough autonomy.
- [ ] Give teams space to build mastery.
- [ ] Connect team work to a clear purpose.
- [ ] Change the team working environment when teams are struggling.
- [ ] Optimize for flow of change and fast feedback from running systems.
- [ ] Review whether current team responsibilities are causing delivery bottlenecks.

## Overall Review Checklist

- [ ] Are team responsibilities clear?
- [ ] Are communication paths intentional and limited?
- [ ] Does the architecture match the team structure?
- [ ] Are teams empowered to make decisions?
- [ ] Is cognitive load actively managed?
- [ ] Are teams organized around value delivery rather than functions?
- [ ] Are dependencies between teams visible and manageable?
- [ ] Is the organization designed for speed and stability?
- [ ] Are teams able to adapt as technology and business needs change?
