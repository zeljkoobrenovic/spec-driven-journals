# Spec: The Problem with Org Charts

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State how I read the org chart as an executive: as a filing system, not a
model of how work happens. The post turns the *Team Topologies* "Org Chart
Problems" checklist into an operating principle: the organization actually
runs on three overlapping structures (formal, informal, value creation),
communication paths — not reporting lines — shape the systems we build,
and my job is to design for the flow of change: intentional, limited team
interactions; architecture and team structure designed together; team-scoped
cognitive load; decisions made in context rather than escalated up the
chart; and redesign only when there is a compelling reason, at the right
time, with multiple options on the table.

## Audience

My leadership bench and peer executives (so they know why I distrust
chart-first reorgs and where decisions should actually be made); engineering
leaders comparing operating models. First-person declarative.

## Success criteria

- [ ] **Principle is quotable** — highlight states "the org chart is not
      the organization" and "design for the flow of change" in one
      paragraph.
- [ ] **The three structures are explicit** — formal, informal, and value
      creation structures each named, with the gap between chart and actual
      communication treated as the diagnostic.
- [ ] **Conway's Law and Dunbar's number survive** — architecture and team
      interactions designed together; multi-team groupings limited by
      Dunbar's number; cognitive load matched to responsibilities.
- [ ] **Decisions in context** — trust-based decision-making close to the
      work argued against escalation up the reporting line.
- [ ] **Redesign discipline** — only with a compelling reason, at the right
      time, with multiple design options developed first.
- [ ] **The checklist survives intact** — all four PDF sections reproduced
      in the Checklist tab (`checklist.md`).
- [ ] **Credit is explicit** — References name Skelton & Pais, *Team
      Topologies*, and point to teamtopologies.com.

## Non-goals

- Not a taxonomy of the four fundamental team types and three interaction
  modes — sibling records cover the operating model in depth.
- Not [[organizational-design]] — that appendix record covers sizing,
  team states, and investment sequencing; this one covers what the chart
  can and cannot tell me.
- Not a ban on org charts — they remain necessary for reporting,
  compliance, and accountability; the claim is about what they model.
- No specific reorg proposal for any company.
