# Spec: Evolve Team Structures

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State how I keep the team topology alive as an executive: the org design is
never finished, and I evolve it deliberately rather than letting it decay or
reorganizing on a calendar. The post turns the *Team Topologies* "Evolve
Team Structures" checklist into an operating principle: map the current team
system (topologies and interaction modes), keep collaboration purposeful and
timeboxed, mature stable boundaries into X-as-a-Service, platformize where
it improves flow, watch for defined evolution triggers (software too big for
one team, delivery cadence slowing, many business services piling onto many
lower-level services), build organizational sensing with teams as the
sensors, feed operations back into development, and review boundaries every
few months — not every week, and not never.

## Audience

My leadership bench and peer executives (so topology changes are legible as
sensed, triggered moves rather than restlessness); engineering leaders
comparing operating models. First-person declarative.

## Success criteria

- [ ] **Principle is quotable** — highlight states that the topology is a
      living design, teams are the organization's sensors, and evolution
      happens on defined triggers, not a reorg calendar.
- [ ] **The triggers are explicit** — software grown too large for one
      team, delivery cadence slowing, many business services relying on
      many lower-level services — each with its telltale symptoms.
- [ ] **The collaboration → X-as-a-Service lifecycle is argued** —
      collaboration is for discovery and learning, is timeboxed with exit
      criteria, and matures into a service boundary once stable.
- [ ] **Sensing is framed as an executive duty** — teams and their
      interaction friction are treated as signal; weak signals are caught
      before they become structural problems.
- [ ] **The review rhythm survives** — boundaries reviewed every few
      months, not every day or week; different parts of the organization
      allowed different interaction modes simultaneously.
- [ ] **The checklist survives intact** — all ten PDF sections reproduced
      in the Checklist tab (`checklist.md`), sub-groupings preserved.
- [ ] **Credit is explicit** — References name Skelton & Pais's *Team
      Topologies* and point to the authors' materials at
      teamtopologies.com.

## Non-goals

- Not a taxonomy lecture — the four fundamental topologies and three
  interaction modes are defined in the sibling records
  [[static-team-topologies]] and [[team-interaction-modes]]; this record
  covers how the whole arrangement changes over time.
- Not [[organizational-design]] — that record covers sizing and staffing
  the system; this one covers reshaping its boundaries and interactions.
- Not a reorg playbook or a target org chart for any company.
- No claim that constant restructuring is healthy — the record argues for
  triggered evolution, explicitly not permanent flux.
