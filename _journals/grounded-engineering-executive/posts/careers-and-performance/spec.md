# Spec: Careers, Hiring, and Performance

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State how I run the *systems* that shape careers in my organization: careers
run on luck more than we admit, and my job is to reduce luck's share with
fair, instrumented processes. The post turns the *Elegant Puzzle* "Careers,
Hiring, Performance Management" checklist into an operating principle: a
humane, instrumented hiring funnel (identify → motivate → evaluate → close,
extended into onboarding, productivity, and retention); interview loops that
test real skills against rubrics rather than polish; cold sourcing that
widens the pool beyond homogeneous networks; career ladders with crisp level
boundaries; performance designations calibrated against the ladder rather
than against each other; and deliberate caution around career-level risks
and specialized roles.

This is an **appendix record**: grounded in Will Larson's earlier,
manager-focused book *An Elegant Puzzle* rather than the Primer, and read
through an executive lens — I own these systems' design and fairness, even
where managers run them day to day.

## Audience

My direct reports and engineering managers (so they know what systems they
inherit and what fairness bar they are held to); recruiters and People
partners; peer executives; engineering leaders comparing operating models.
First-person declarative.

## Non-goals

- Not [[hiring]] — that Primer-grounded record covers how I personally engage
  in hiring as an executive; this record covers the systems underneath.
- Not a compensation philosophy or a specific ladder document.
- Not a claim that process eliminates luck — the claim is that fair process
  reduces luck's share of career outcomes.
- Not a legal or HR-compliance guide.
