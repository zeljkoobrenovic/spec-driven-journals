# Spec: Operating Principle: Engineering Onboarding

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State how I run engineering onboarding: a program with clear goals (a rising
share of engineers productive within three months, with "productive" defined
and measured) and, above all, clear ownership — an executive sponsor, a
program orchestrator with protected time, the team manager, and an onboarding
buddy each with explicit duties; a curriculum that starts with three core
sessions (values and strategy, architecture, dev setup) and is strengthened
by surveys rather than up-front over-design; attendance required at every
level; and investment sized to hiring volume. The post turns the "Engineering
Onboarding" checklist into a durable operating principle: onboarding fails
from unclear ownership and over-engineering, not from starting simple.

## Audience

Engineering leaders and managers who own a piece of onboarding under my
sponsorship; the program orchestrator role I appoint; peer executives who
want to know how new engineers become productive. Declarative first-person
register — a vision statement, not a how-to tutorial.

## Success criteria

- [ ] **Principle is quotable** — the highlight blockquote states the
      ownership-and-simplicity stance in one paragraph.
- [ ] **Rationale is argued, not asserted** — explains why diffuse ownership
      is the root failure mode and why the worst programs come from trying
      too hard rather than starting simple.
- [ ] **The checklist survives intact** — every section of the source PDF
      (define clear goals, assign clear ownership — sponsor, orchestrator,
      team manager, buddy —, design the curriculum, decide who attends,
      avoid common failure modes, coordinate with company-wide onboarding,
      decide when to invest heavily, measure & improve, quick start version)
      appears in the Checklist tab (`checklist.md`), regrouped as bullets.
- [ ] **Anti-patterns are concrete** — at least three (e.g. an orchestrator
      with no protected time, senior hires skipping onboarding, an
      over-engineered curriculum nobody can sustain).
- [ ] **Credit is explicit** — Authoritative References names Larson's *The
      Engineering Executive's Primer* and the lethain.com companion post.

## Non-goals

- Not a hiring post — [[hiring]] covers everything up to the accepted offer;
  this post starts on day one.
- Not about onboarding executives — [[onboarding-peer-executives]] covers
  peer executives, and [[first-90-days]] covers my own ramp; this post is
  about onboarding engineers and engineering managers.
- No company-specific curriculum content, tooling choices, or dates — the
  post states the program shape, not one company's syllabus.
