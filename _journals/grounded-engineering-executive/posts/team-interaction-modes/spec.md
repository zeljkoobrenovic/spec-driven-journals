# Spec: Team Interaction Modes

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State how I make team-to-team interaction an explicit design decision. The
post turns the *Team Topologies* "Team Interaction Modes" checklist into an
operating principle: exactly three modes — **Collaboration** (high-bandwidth
joint discovery, expensive, time-boxed, at most one other team at a time),
**X-as-a-Service** (one team provides, one consumes, minimal coordination,
the service managed like a product), and **Facilitating** (one team grows
another's capability and then leaves) — chosen deliberately per team pair,
matched to team types (stream-aligned, enabling, complicated-subsystem,
platform), and evolved over time. Friction and awkwardness in an
interaction are read as **sensing data**: signals that a boundary, a
capability, or the chosen mode is wrong. Collaboration everywhere is named
as the anti-pattern it is.

## Audience

My leadership bench and peer executives (so "why can't these two teams just
collaborate?" has a legible answer); engineering leaders comparing
operating models. First-person declarative.

## Success criteria

- [ ] **Principle is quotable** — highlight states the three modes and
      "friction is sensing data" in one paragraph.
- [ ] **The three modes are explicit** — collaboration, X-as-a-Service,
      and facilitating each appear with their purpose, cost profile, and
      exit condition.
- [ ] **The discipline survives** — collaboration is time-boxed, limited
      to at most one other team at a time, and has a plan to end;
      facilitation is temporary by design; a service is managed like a
      product.
- [ ] **Mode-to-team-type matching is present** — stream-aligned,
      enabling, complicated-subsystem, and platform teams each get their
      typical and occasional modes.
- [ ] **Sensing is argued** — awkward or high-friction interactions are
      framed as organizational signals, not interpersonal failures.
- [ ] **The checklist survives intact** — all seven PDF sections plus the
      Final Review reproduced in the Checklist tab (`checklist.md`).
- [ ] **Credit is explicit** — References name Skelton & Pais, *Team
      Topologies* (IT Revolution Press, 2019) and teamtopologies.com.

## Non-goals

- Not a full account of the four team topologies — that is the sibling
  record [[four-fundamental-team-topologies]]; this one covers how the
  teams interact, whatever their shape.
- Not [[evolve-team-structures]] — that record covers when and how the
  structure itself changes; this one supplies the sensing signals that
  trigger it.
- Not a communication-tooling or meeting-hygiene guide — see
  [[internal-communication]].
- No claim that collaboration is bad — only that it is expensive, and that
  its cost must be spent deliberately.
