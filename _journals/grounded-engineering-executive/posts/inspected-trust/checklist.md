*The working checklist behind this principle. The Article tab carries the rationale, implications, and anti-patterns.*

### Mindset Shift: From Blind Trust to Inspected Trust

- [ ] Avoid relying on trust alone as a management technique.
- [ ] Separate judgment and process quality from outcomes.
- [ ] Recognize the four categories of performance: good process + good decision + good outcome; good process + good decision + bad outcome (*good error*); bad process + bad decision + good outcome (*bad success*); bad process + bad decision + bad outcome.
- [ ] Treat inspection as support and accountability — not suspicion.
- [ ] View discomfort with inspection as a signal to explore alignment issues.

### Avoid Common Trust Pitfalls

- [ ] Don't assume the initial "trust reservoir" will sustain itself.
- [ ] Avoid letting new leaders thrash without feedback.
- [ ] Don't confuse empowerment with the absence of oversight.
- [ ] Intervene early, before misalignment compounds; prevent "damage before feedback" cycles.

### Practice Inspected Trust When Concerns Arise

- [ ] Assume there may be a real issue, and independently validate the situation.
- [ ] Seek multiple perspectives, cross-functional if needed; consider missing context.
- [ ] Form your own view before prescribing solutions.
- [ ] Close the loop with the original reporter after validation.

### Use Effective Inspection Tools

- [ ] **Inspection forums** — weekly or monthly metric reviews; track goals regularly (not quarterly-only); ensure executive engagement; focus on outcomes *and* process quality.
- [ ] **Learning spikes** — go directly to individuals doing the work; talk across functions; avoid relying solely on summarized narratives; look for omitted or filtered context.
- [ ] **Engage directly with data** — don't rely exclusively on heavily prepared summaries; understand how data was prepared; identify reporting bias; cross-reference against known sources.
- [ ] **Intolerance for misalignment** — investigate confusion immediately; dig into unclear decisions and contradictory statements; resolve ambiguity before it compounds; treat persistent confusion as a trust risk.

### Introduce Inspection into a Low-Inspection Culture

- [ ] Start with 1–2 critical problems; don't expand everywhere at once.
- [ ] Confirm executive peer tolerance before rollout.
- [ ] Frame inspection as a long-term investment in team success; be explicit that inspection ≠ distrust.
- [ ] Expect and withstand defensive reactions; reinforce that feedback is part of leadership maturity.

### Protect and Build Real Trust

- [ ] Pair trust with validation; catch errors early to enable learning.
- [ ] Reward strong process even when outcomes fail; avoid mistaking good errors for incompetence; avoid rewarding bad successes.
- [ ] Make inspection reciprocal — allow your own work to be inspected too.

### Ongoing Leadership Habits

- [ ] Evaluate leadership bench strength on judgment, not the randomness of outcomes.
- [ ] Maintain small, diverse data sources.
- [ ] Continually clarify strategy and alignment.
- [ ] Treat inspection as a gift — it means taking the work seriously — and stay consistent even when it feels uncomfortable.

### Quarterly Self-Assessment

- [ ] Am I relying on trust alone anywhere?
- [ ] Where might I be mistaking outcomes for judgment?
- [ ] What misalignments am I tolerating?
- [ ] Where do I need a learning spike?
- [ ] Are my inspection forums producing clarity, or just preparation work?
