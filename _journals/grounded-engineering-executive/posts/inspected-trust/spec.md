# Spec: Operating Principle: Inspected Trust

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State how I handle trust as an executive: I trust first, then verify — trust
is paired with inspection, not replaced by it. Blind trust is abdication;
inspection is support and accountability, not suspicion. The post turns the
"Inspected Trust" checklist into an operating principle: separating process
quality from outcomes (good errors vs bad successes), the inspection tools I
use (forums, learning spikes, direct engagement with data, intolerance for
misalignment), and how I introduce inspection into a culture that reads it as
distrust.

## Audience

My direct reports and leadership bench (so inspection is legible as support,
not surveillance); peer executives; engineering leaders comparing operating
models. First-person declarative.

## Success criteria

- [ ] **Principle is quotable** — highlight states trust-first-then-verify in
      one paragraph, including "inspection ≠ distrust".
- [ ] **Process/outcome separation is explicit** — the four performance
      categories appear, with good-error and bad-success named and their
      consequences (reward strong process, don't reward bad successes).
- [ ] **Tools are concrete** — inspection forums, learning spikes, direct
      data engagement, and immediate investigation of misalignment each get a
      practical description.
- [ ] **Reciprocity is stated** — my own work is inspectable too.
- [ ] **The checklist survives intact** — all PDF sections (mindset shift,
      pitfalls, practice, tools, low-inspection culture, protecting real
      trust, ongoing habits, self-assessment) regrouped in the Checklist tab (`checklist.md`).
- [ ] **Credit is explicit** — References name Larson's *Primer* and the
      lethain.com companion essay.

## Non-goals

- Not a performance-management or PIP process description.
- Not [[calibrating-your-standards]] — that record covers what "good" looks
  like; this one covers how I verify without destroying trust.
- No claim that micromanagement is the goal — the essay title "better to
  micromanage than be disengaged" is a provocation, not the principle.
