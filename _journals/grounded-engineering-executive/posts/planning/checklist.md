*The working checklist behind this principle, grouped by planning phase — mindset first, then the three sequenced phases, the process, and the pitfalls. The Article tab carries the rationale, implications, and anti-patterns.*

### Planning Mindset

- [ ] Treat planning as an ongoing, dynamic process (not a one-time event).
- [ ] Separate financial planning, functional allocation, and roadmap discussions.
- [ ] Sequence decisions instead of debating everything at once.
- [ ] Accept that plans will evolve as reality changes.
- [ ] Prefer durable constraints over flexible ones.

### Phase 1: Establish the Financial Plan

**Annual Financial Foundation**

- [ ] Align on revenue targets by business line.
- [ ] Define expense targets by function.
- [ ] Finalize headcount plan (roles + hiring timing).
- [ ] Ensure Engineering is aligned on total budget constraints.
- [ ] Agree whether the financial plan is fixed or adjustable during the year.

**Engineering Cost Management**

- [ ] Segment Engineering expenses:
  - [ ] Headcount
  - [ ] Production (cloud, vendors)
  - [ ] Development (CI/CD, tooling)
- [ ] Review business lines where expenses grow faster than revenue.
- [ ] Document hypotheses for improving underperforming segments.

**Capitalizing Engineering Costs (if relevant)**

- [ ] Agree with Finance on capitalization approach:
  - [ ] Ticket-based
  - [ ] Project-based
  - [ ] Role-based
- [ ] Ensure approach:
  - [ ] Is auditor-defensible
  - [ ] Works for Finance reporting timelines
  - [ ] Minimizes overhead for Engineering

**Headcount Discipline**

- [ ] Decide whether Engineering headcount growth constrains company growth.
- [ ] Model org structure before requesting additional headcount.
- [ ] Compare the hiring plan against the historical recruiting capacity.
- [ ] Avoid treating headcount as the universal solution.

**Vendor Contracts**

- [ ] Identify renewal deadlines early.
- [ ] Check actual usage vs. contracted volume.
- [ ] Push for:
  - [ ] Volume discounts
  - [ ] Shorter commitments (unless a meaningful tradeoff)
  - [ ] Service credits for past reliability issues
- [ ] Align the negotiation strategy before engaging the vendor.

### Phase 2: Determine Functional Portfolio Allocation

**Set Allocation Strategy**

- [ ] Define steady-state allocation percentages (e.g., Infra / DevEx / Product).
- [ ] Explicitly decide % for:
  - [ ] Cross-functional work
  - [ ] Functional/internal priorities
- [ ] Review Engineering investments annually.
- [ ] Update allocation as impact data improves.

**Allocation Discipline**

- [ ] Keep allocation fairly steady (avoid frequent churn).
- [ ] Make changes narrowly and intentionally.
- [ ] Choose appropriate granularity (large buckets > tiny slices).
- [ ] Avoid overreacting to early results.

**Unscoped vs Scoped Work**

- [ ] Separate validated projects from exploratory initiatives.
- [ ] Allocate a fixed % for validating new ideas.
- [ ] Avoid comparing the raw potential of unscoped ideas to scoped work.
- [ ] Keep validation budget long-running and explicit.

### Phase 3: Agree on the Roadmap

**Roadmap Construction**

- [ ] Ensure Product, Engineering, and Design collaborate.
- [ ] Validate roadmap with stakeholders (Sales, Marketing, etc.).
- [ ] Resolve conflicts in open group discussions (not 1:1 side deals).
- [ ] Avoid coupling roadmap changes directly to budget shifts.

**Scope Discipline**

- [ ] Avoid mixing concrete projects with vague ideas.
- [ ] Emphasize outcomes first, projects second.
- [ ] Maintain flexibility for teams to adjust implementation details.
- [ ] Prevent planning from disempowering teams.

**Prevent Common Roadmapping Failures**

- [ ] Avoid over-planning detailed task lists.
- [ ] Avoid a roadmap driven by executives' "exciting ideas."
- [ ] Avoid turning planning into idea-generation season.
- [ ] Don't let cross-functional work crowd out functional priorities.

### Planning Process & Timeline

- [ ] The annual budget is prepared before the year starts.
- [ ] Functional allocation is reviewed on a rolling basis.
- [ ] Quarterly planning is done in the weeks preceding each quarter.
- [ ] Keep planning time-boxed (don't let it expand indefinitely).
- [ ] Improve the process each year.

### Pitfalls to Watch For

**Planning as Checkbox Ritual**

- [ ] Plans are referenced after the planning cycle ends.
- [ ] Focus is on the quality of decisions rather than the formatting.
- [ ] Clear executive sponsor owns trade-offs.

**Inefficient Resource Allocation**

- [ ] Executives optimize for the company, not their own function.
- [ ] Budget allocation is respected after being set.
- [ ] The least efficient teams are not disproportionately rewarded.
- [ ] Velocity discussions don't default to "we need more headcount."

**Shiny Project Bias**

- [ ] Planning is not centered on what executives find most interesting.
- [ ] Compliance, security, and reliability receive proper attention.
- [ ] Functional responsibilities aren't neglected.

**Diminishing Team Ownership**

- [ ] Executives prioritize outcomes, not micromanaged projects.
- [ ] Planning doesn't generate large volumes of new, unscoped work.
- [ ] Teams retain autonomy in execution decisions.

### Final Executive Sanity Check

- Before finalizing your plan, ask:

- [ ] Are financial constraints clear and stable?
- [ ] Is the engineering allocation explicit and steady?
- [ ] Is the roadmap aligned with both company goals and real capacity?
- [ ] Have we avoided mixing budgeting, allocation, and roadmap debates?
- [ ] Does this plan empower teams rather than constrain them?
- [ ] Are we improving the system, not just this cycle?
