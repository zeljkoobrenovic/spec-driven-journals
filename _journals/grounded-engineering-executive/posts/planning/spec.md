# Spec: Operating Principle: Planning

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State how I plan as an engineering executive: I treat planning as an ongoing
process, not an annual event, and I sequence it into three deliberately
separated phases — establish the financial plan, determine functional
portfolio allocation, agree on the roadmap — rather than debating everything
at once. I keep allocation steady, prefer durable constraints, emphasize
outcomes over micromanaged projects, and guard against the classic failures:
checkbox rituals, headcount as the universal answer, shiny projects, and
planning that disempowers teams. The post turns the "Planning (Budgeting,
Allocation, Roadmaps)" checklist into a durable operating principle: sequence
the decisions, protect team ownership, and improve the system each cycle.

## Audience

Engineering leaders and peer executives (Finance, Product, CEO) who
participate in the planning cycle with me; my own leadership team, who need
to know which debate belongs to which phase. Declarative first-person
register — a vision statement, not a planning-process manual.

## Success criteria

- [ ] **Principle is quotable** — the highlight blockquote states the
      three-phase, sequence-the-decisions stance in one paragraph.
- [ ] **Rationale is argued, not asserted** — explains why mixing budget,
      allocation, and roadmap debates ruins all three, and why steady
      allocation beats reactive churn.
- [ ] **The checklist survives intact** — every section of the source PDF
      (planning mindset, financial plan, functional portfolio allocation,
      roadmap, planning process & timeline, pitfalls to watch for, final
      executive sanity check) appears in the Checklist tab (`checklist.md`), regrouped as
      bullets.
- [ ] **Anti-patterns are concrete** — at least three (e.g. planning as
      checkbox ritual, "we need more headcount" as the default velocity
      answer, executive shiny-project bias).
- [ ] **Credit is explicit** — Authoritative References names Larson's *The
      Engineering Executive's Primer* and the lethain.com companion post.

## Non-goals

- Not an engineering strategy post — the long-term direction that planning
  serves is [[engineering-strategy]]'s territory; this record is about the
  yearly/quarterly resourcing machinery.
- Not a measurement post — the impact data that feeds allocation decisions
  is covered in [[measuring-engineering-organizations]].
- No specific budget percentages, team names, or vendor commitments — the
  record states the operating stance, not this year's plan.
