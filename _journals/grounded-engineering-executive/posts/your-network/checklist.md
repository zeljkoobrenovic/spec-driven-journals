*The working checklist behind this principle, grouped from mindset through building and maintaining. The Article tab carries the rationale, implications, and anti-patterns.*

### Adopt the Right Mindset

- [ ] Treat networking as an exchange of value, not extraction.
- [ ] Assume it will take consistent effort over time.
- [ ] Focus on being useful to others, not just getting help.
- [ ] Respect people's time and social capital.

### Clarify Your Networking Goals

- [ ] Identify current challenges where an outside perspective would help.
- [ ] Define 1–2 specific learning goals (e.g., org design, pricing, hiring).
- [ ] Prioritize relationships aligned to what you're actively learning.

### Leverage Your Network Effectively

**Use your network for:**

- [ ] Benchmarking team sizes and org structure.
- [ ] Vendor pricing and renewal negotiation insights.
- [ ] Sensitive executive-team advice.
- [ ] Compensation calibration and market signals.
- [ ] Job market and career move perspective.
- [ ] Backchannel references on senior hires.

**After receiving help:**

- [ ] Share outcomes and context on what worked.
- [ ] Thank them and acknowledge specific advice.

### Build Your Network Intentionally

**Work Together (High-Leverage Path)**

- [ ] Spend time at a fast-growing company in a major tech hub (if early career).
- [ ] Invest in strong peer relationships while working together.

**Cold Outreach (Done Well)**

- [ ] Reach out to "near network" contacts (mutual connections).
- [ ] Keep messages to 2–4 concise sentences.
- [ ] Ask one clear, specific question.
- [ ] Avoid vague asks like "Can we chat?"
- [ ] Do not demand time; offer flexibility instead.
- [ ] Avoid repeated follow-ups if there is no reply.
- [ ] Reuse effective outreach templates thoughtfully.

### Consider Community Building

- [ ] Join or create a small, curated peer learning circle.
- [ ] Select thoughtful, strong communicators.
- [ ] Establish a code of conduct.
- [ ] Commit to maintaining and operating the group.
- [ ] Prefer small, private groups for executive-level discussions.

### Be Realistic About Large Communities

- [ ] Use large forums to learn broadly.
- [ ] Avoid sharing sensitive executive details publicly.
- [ ] Don't expect high-quality advice on complex issues in open spaces.
- Reminder: easier access often means lower signal.

### Be Strategic About Writing & Speaking

- [ ] Write or speak publicly if you enjoy it.
- [ ] Don't rely on content creation as your primary networking strategy.
- [ ] Weigh time investment vs. direct outreach and events.

### Avoid Common Mistakes

- [ ] Avoid vague time-based asks ("grab some time to chat?").
- [ ] Avoid confusing or multi-part requests.
- [ ] Avoid asking for introductions without clear mutual value.
- [ ] Don't overconsume others' communities without contributing.
- [ ] If outreach isn't working, get feedback on your approach.

### Build Other Critical Networks

**Founders**

- [ ] Build relationships with founders for perspective.
- [ ] Connect through startups or angel investing (if appropriate).
- [ ] Seek the founder's advice if navigating founder dynamics.

**Venture Capitalists**

- [ ] Build pattern-level relationships with VCs.
- [ ] Ask portfolio-level or industry-pattern questions.
- [ ] Use CEO/board connections to meet a few trusted VCs.

**Executive Recruiters**

- [ ] Build relationships before you need a job.
- [ ] Connect during hiring cycles.
- [ ] Maintain periodic light-touch contact.

### Maintain a Sustainable Rhythm

- [ ] Set a modest goal (e.g., meet 1 new person per month).
- [ ] Track who you've connected with.
- [ ] Follow up periodically with useful context or updates.
- [ ] Keep networking important — but not your top priority.
