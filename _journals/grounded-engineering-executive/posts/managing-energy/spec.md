# Spec: Operating Principle: Managing Priorities and Energy

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State how I decide what to work on and how I stay effective doing it: company
→ team → self as the default priority order, deliberately softened by energy
management — a long-term executive career requires both impact and energy, so
I keep some energizing work in the mix even when it is not the maximally
important thing, as long as it is orthogonal to (never opposed to) company
needs. The post turns the "Managing Priorities & Energy" checklist into an
operating principle covering the priority hierarchy, positive-sum energy
management, strategic rule-breaking with guardrails, the eventual-quid-pro-quo
frame, context awareness, and flexibility over rigid adherence.

## Audience

Engineering leaders (including future me) reading how I sustain the role;
my team, so my occasional "orthogonal" choices are legible as deliberate
policy rather than inconsistency. First-person declarative.

## Success criteria

- [ ] **Principle is quotable** — highlight states company→team→self plus the
      energy counterweight in one paragraph.
- [ ] **The tension is honest** — the post names the conflict between strict
      prioritization and sustainability, and resolves it via orthogonal-not-
      opposed, rather than pretending there is no conflict.
- [ ] **Guardrails are concrete** — modest and controlled deviations, no
      burden shifted onto others, sanity-check gray areas with a peer,
      ~1-year imbalance triggers reassessing the role.
- [ ] **The checklist survives intact** — all PDF sections (baseline
      alignment, impact vs engagement, energy management, rule-breaking,
      quid pro quo, context awareness, orthogonal-not-opposed, flexibility,
      weekly reflection) regrouped in the Checklist tab (`checklist.md`).
- [ ] **Credit is explicit** — References name Larson's *Primer* and the
      lethain.com companion essay.

## Non-goals

- Not a personal-productivity or time-management system (no calendars, GTD,
  tooling).
- Not burnout clinical advice; sustainability here is an operating constraint,
  not health guidance.
- Team-level workload policy only appears as it derives from the principle.
