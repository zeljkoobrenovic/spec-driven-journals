# Spec: Operating Principle: Onboarding Peer Executives

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State how I onboard a new peer executive: I treat it as my job, not HR's — I
commit personally to their success, share my mental framework of the company
in their first two weeks (real data, urgent problems, what's working, the
power map, processes, cultural quirks), make role expectations and escalation
paths explicit rather than implicit, and invest in a durable partnership
cadence. The post turns the "Onboarding Peer Executives" checklist into a
durable operating principle: why executive onboarding is chronically ad hoc,
and why early, deliberate support is the cheapest way to prevent a mediocre
peer from lingering.

## Audience

Peer executives joining a company where I work (this is what they can expect
from me); CEOs who want to know how I support new executive hires; engineering
leaders building their own onboarding practice. Declarative first-person
register — a vision statement, not a how-to tutorial.

## Success criteria

- [ ] **Principle is quotable** — the highlight blockquote states the
      personal-commitment-plus-explicit-expectations stance in one paragraph.
- [ ] **Rationale is argued, not asserted** — explains why executive
      onboarding is neglected, why it differs from engineer onboarding, and
      why mediocre-but-pleasant executives linger without early support.
- [ ] **The mental framework is concrete** — the first-two-weeks briefing
      (data & reality, immediate focus, budget & headcount, what's working,
      team assessment, power map, processes, cultural norms) is rendered as
      something I actually walk a new peer through.
- [ ] **The checklist survives intact** — every section of the source PDF
      (why onboarding matters, executive vs. engineer difference, mental
      framework, defining roles explicitly, partnership cadence, EA
      partnership, monitoring progress, guiding principles) appears in
      the Checklist tab (`checklist.md`), regrouped as bullets.
- [ ] **Anti-patterns are concrete** — at least three (e.g. leaving
      onboarding to HR, letting implicit role expectations fester, ignoring
      red flags like old-company playbooks).
- [ ] **Credit is explicit** — Authoritative References names Larson's *The
      Engineering Executive's Primer* and the lethain.com companion essay.
