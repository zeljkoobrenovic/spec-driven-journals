*The working checklist behind this principle, grouped the way the onboarding unfolds — from commitment, through the mental-framework briefing, to the ongoing cadence. The Article tab carries the rationale, implications, and anti-patterns.*

### Align on Why Onboarding Matters

- [ ] Acknowledge that onboarding executives is ad hoc and often neglected.
- [ ] Commit personally to helping the new executive succeed.
- [ ] Align with the CEO (if possible) on shared expectations for the new hire.
- [ ] Clarify what success looks like in the first 90 days.

### Understand the Difference (Executive vs. Engineer Onboarding)

- [ ] Help them understand the company landscape (business, team, product, process).
- [ ] Emphasize current critical issues and priorities.
- [ ] Encourage broad learning before big changes.
- [ ] Watch for red flags:
  - [ ] Inaction on key issues.
  - [ ] Changing what's already working.
  - [ ] Reverting to old-company playbooks prematurely.

### Share Your Mental Framework (First 2 Weeks Priority)

**Data & Reality**

- [ ] Show where to find real data (dashboards, board reports, analytics).
- [ ] Encourage validation over narratives.

**Immediate Focus**

- [ ] Clarify the top 2–3 urgent problems.
- [ ] Explain current friction areas across teams.

**Budget & Headcount**

- [ ] Discuss norms around budget and hiring requests.
- [ ] Explain tradeoffs and historical context.

**What's Working Well**

- [ ] Identify strong processes and high-functioning areas.
- [ ] Steer them away from "fixing" what isn't broken.

**Honest Team Assessment**

- [ ] Provide transparent strengths and gaps on their team.
- [ ] Clarify the historical context behind performance issues.

**Power & Influence Map**

- [ ] Introduce long-tenured employees.
- [ ] Identify unofficial influencers.
- [ ] Clarify implicit power structures.

**Company Processes**

- [ ] Walk through planning cadence (quarterly/annual).
- [ ] Explain the budgeting & headcount process.
- [ ] Review performance management systems.
- [ ] Outline recurring business reviews.

**Surprises & Cultural Norms**

- [ ] Flag unique company quirks.
- [ ] Explain documentation expectations.
- [ ] Prepare them for recurring rituals.

### Define Roles Explicitly

**Role Clarity**

- [ ] Independently write expectations of each other's roles.
- [ ] Compare and discuss differences.
- [ ] Resolve accidental misalignments.

**Handling Conflict**

- [ ] Agree on how to manage public disagreement.
- [ ] Define when to resolve privately.

**Escalation Path**

- [ ] Explicitly define the escalation process.
- [ ] Agree on when/how to involve the CEO.
- [ ] Remove ambiguity around decision ownership.

### Establish Ongoing Partnership Cadence

**Relationship Investment**

- [ ] Schedule non-transactional time (lunch/dinner).
- [ ] Build personal rapport.

**Structured 1:1**

- [ ] Schedule weekly 60-min structured 1:1.
- [ ] Use the shared doc for the agenda.
- [ ] Review 2–3 meaningful issues deeply.

**Joint Forums**

- [ ] Identify recurring meetings where your teams intersect.
- [ ] Attend key planning/review sessions together.
- [ ] Debrief disagreements afterward.

### Executive Assistant Partnership (If Applicable)

**Hiring & Timing**

- [ ] Evaluate when an EA is needed.
- [ ] Align on the scope of support.

**Delegation**

- [ ] Offload calendar management.
- [ ] Delegate communications drafting.
- [ ] Delegate recurring meeting coordination.
- [ ] Use EA support for offsites & all-hands.

**Partnership Mindset**

- [ ] Treat the EA relationship as a two-way partnership.
- [ ] Support their growth and success.

### Monitor Progress (First 3–6 Months)

- [ ] Observe early signals of effectiveness.
- [ ] Provide candid feedback early.
- [ ] Reinforce strengths publicly.
- [ ] Address missteps before they compound.
- [ ] Continue investing even if things feel "fine".

### Guiding Principles

- [ ] Go broad before going deep.
- [ ] Make implicit expectations explicit.
- [ ] Prevent mistakes rather than recover from them.
- [ ] Build trust through repeated, small commitments.
- Reminder: mediocre but pleasant executives can linger — early support matters.
