**Panel 3:** *The principle: every team is one of four types — stream-aligned, enabling, complicated-subsystem, platform.*

![Comic panel: Vera points at many horizontal stream arrows flowing toward a customer, with a single small supporting shape beside them.](assets/images/four-fundamental-team-topologies/comic-04-stream-aligned-majority.jpeg)
**Panel 4:** *The majority: stream-aligned teams own a flow of change end to end — roughly six to nine for every other team.*

![Comic panel: Vera shows a thin platform bar with plug-in sockets under stream arrows that now have speed lines.](assets/images/four-fundamental-team-topologies/comic-05-platform-as-a-product.jpeg)
**Panel 5:** *The platform: an internal product with users and feedback — just big enough to make the streams faster.*

![Comic panel: an enabling figure hands a glowing skill to a stream team and walks away, while one small specialist octagon sits apart.](assets/images/four-fundamental-team-topologies/comic-06-enabling-grows-then-leaves.jpeg)
**Panel 6:** *The rare types: enabling teams time-box their help; complicated-subsystem teams stay few and justified.*

![Comic panel: Vera and Leo sort old team boxes into four bins shaped like the four topologies, one ops box morphing into a platform bar.](assets/images/four-fundamental-team-topologies/comic-07-converting-the-zoo.jpeg)
**Panel 7:** *The conversion: legacy teams get an explicit fate under the four types — no quiet exemptions.*

![Comic panel: close-up of Vera and Leo before the finished four-type diagram as Vera closes her notebook.](assets/images/four-fundamental-team-topologies/comic-08-easy-to-explain.jpeg)
**Panel 8:** *The closer: an organization whose team boundaries are easy to explain — and whose ambiguities are the executive's to fix.*
