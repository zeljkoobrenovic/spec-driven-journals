# Spec: Operating Principle: Gelling Your Engineering Leadership Team

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State how I build and run my engineering leadership team: I debug the team in
the first 60–90 days — assessing who should stay, which relationships are
broken, and whether the structure brings the right leaders and domains into
the room — and act decisively on what I find; I then operate the team on
explicit values (how we decide, how we handle conflict, when we escalate),
clear structure and rituals, and deliberate space for human connection; I set
explicit expectations for how members lead their teams, work with peers, and
navigate me; I referee defections from values immediately and prevent
collaboration from drifting into internal competition; and I maintain the
team continuously rather than assuming it stays gelled. The post turns the
"Gelling Your Engineering Leadership Team" checklist into a durable operating
principle: a leadership team is built and maintained, never assumed.

## Audience

Members of my engineering leadership team, present and future, who want to
know what I expect and how the team operates; engineering leaders building
their own teams ("turtles all the way down"). Declarative first-person
register — a vision statement, not a how-to tutorial.

## Success criteria

- [ ] **Principle is quotable** — the highlight blockquote states the
      debug-then-operate-then-maintain stance in one paragraph.
- [ ] **Rationale is argued, not asserted** — explains why early decisive
      personnel action resets team dynamics, and why unaddressed value
      violations and peer competition dissolve a team.
- [ ] **The checklist survives intact** — every section of the source PDF
      (debugging & establishing the team in the first 60–90 days, operating
      the leadership team, setting expectations for team members, preventing
      competition among peers, ongoing maintenance) appears in the Checklist tab (`checklist.md`), regrouped as bullets.
- [ ] **Anti-patterns are concrete** — at least three (e.g. tolerating
      repeated behavioral drift, purely transactional relationships, letting
      collaboration curdle into zero-sum internal competition).
- [ ] **Credit is explicit** — Authoritative References names Larson's *The
      Engineering Executive's Primer* and the lethain.com companion post.

## Non-goals

- Not about the executive table around me —
  [[working-with-your-ceo-peers-and-engineering]] covers the CEO and my
  peers; this post covers the team that reports to me.
- Not a first-quarter plan — [[first-90-days]] covers my whole executive
  ramp; this post takes only the team-debugging slice and then goes long-term.
- Not an organization-wide values post — [[organizational-values]] covers
  values for all of engineering; this post covers the working values of the
  leadership team itself.
