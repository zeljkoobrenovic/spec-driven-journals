*The working checklist behind this principle, following the source's lifecycle arc: debug the team first, then operate it, set expectations, prevent competition, and maintain. The Article tab carries the rationale, implications, and anti-patterns.*

### Debugging & Establishing the Team (First 60–90 Days)

**Assess the Current Team**

- [ ] Identify members who may need to move on (behavioral issues or active damage vs. simple underperformance).
- [ ] Identify broken relationship pairs (internal or with key stakeholders).
- [ ] Evaluate whether the org structure brings the right leaders into the room.
- [ ] Ensure key domains (e.g., Data, Security, Infrastructure) are properly represented.

**Form Initial Hypotheses**

- [ ] Develop a clear point of view for your leadership team.
- [ ] Actively try to disprove your early assumptions.
- [ ] Validate perceptions through 1:1s and observation.

**Take Decisive Action**

- [ ] Move out individuals who won't be part of the long-term team.
- [ ] Set clear expectations for those who remain.
- [ ] Make personnel decisions quickly to reset team dynamics.
- [ ] Consider starting with a broader team early on.
- [ ] Ensure that the technical perspective is represented (e.g., by senior engineers or architects).

### Operating the Leadership Team

**Define Team Values**

- [ ] Clarify how decisions are made.
- [ ] Define how conflicts are navigated.
- [ ] Align on when to escalate vs. resolve directly.
- [ ] Align team values with broader company values.
- [ ] Document values (if helpful) and enforce them consistently.

**Establish Team Structure**

- [ ] Define recurring meetings and rituals.
- [ ] Clarify information flow from exec team to leadership team.
- [ ] Establish clear escalation pathways.
- [ ] Decide on update cadence (weekly reports, chat updates, etc.).

**Create Space for Human Connection**

- [ ] Build informal, unstructured interaction time.
- [ ] Avoid purely transactional relationships.
- [ ] Encourage relationship-building outside structured meetings.

**Referee Defection from Values**

- [ ] Address value violations immediately.
- [ ] Hold members accountable consistently.
- [ ] Avoid tolerating repeated behavioral drift.
- [ ] Reinforce that values apply to everyone.

### Setting Expectations for Team Members

**Leading Their Teams**

- [ ] Clarify how leaders will be evaluated (execution vs. firefighting vs. vision).
- [ ] Align on performance expectations.
- [ ] Ensure they understand business-level goals.

**Communicating with Peers**

- [ ] Set expectations for cross-functional engagement.
- [ ] Avoid siloed, heads-down leadership behavior.
- [ ] Define how to handle peer conflicts.

**Staying Aligned with Peers**

- [ ] Clarify when to resolve vs. escalate.
- [ ] Define escalation norms (together vs. separately).
- [ ] Encourage shared ownership over competing priorities.

**Building Their Own Leadership Teams**

- [ ] Ensure each leader develops their own strong team.
- [ ] Reinforce "turtles all the way down" leadership consistency.
- [ ] Encourage cascading communication of priorities and values.

**Navigating You (the Executive)**

- [ ] Clarify your working style (risk tolerance, process preference).
- [ ] Share decision-making philosophy.
- [ ] Make stakeholder prioritization explicit.
- [ ] Consider creating a personal README.

### Preventing Competition Among Peers

- [ ] Watch for collaboration drifting into internal competition.
- [ ] Address perceived lack of opportunity.
- [ ] Encourage non-zero-sum growth opportunities.
- [ ] Reinforce that future capacity > current capacity.
- [ ] Avoid tolerating value violations that fuel competition.

### Ongoing Maintenance

- [ ] Revisit expectations regularly.
- [ ] Periodically re-evaluate team composition.
- [ ] Reinforce values in weekly meetings.
- [ ] Address small tensions before they grow.
- [ ] Invest consistently in relationships.
