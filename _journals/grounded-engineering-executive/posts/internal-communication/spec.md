# Spec: Operating Principle: Internal Communication

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State how I communicate inside the organization: a steady, predictable drip of
short updates rather than rare grand announcements; extracting the real
concern behind executive feedback instead of debating incidentals; testing
significant messages with reviewers before broadcasting; structuring every
important communication as a packet (summary, canonical source, place for
questions); keeping it short; and repeating the message across every channel.
The post turns the "Internal Communication" checklist into a durable operating
principle: communication is an operating system I run deliberately, not a
byproduct of doing the work.

## Audience

Engineering leaders and my own leadership team who want to know how I
communicate and what I expect of their communication; peer executives who
receive my updates. Declarative first-person register — a vision statement,
not a writing tutorial.

## Success criteria

- [ ] **Principle is quotable** — the highlight blockquote states the
      drip-over-broadcast stance in one paragraph.
- [ ] **Rationale is argued, not asserted** — explains why consistency beats
      intensity (trust compounds from predictable updates) and why extracting
      the kernel prevents losing executives in incidental detail.
- [ ] **The checklist survives intact** — every section of the source PDF
      (maintain the drip, extract the kernel, test before broadcasting, build
      the packet, keep it short, use every channel, final quick audit) appears
      in the Checklist tab (`checklist.md`), regrouped as bullets.
- [ ] **Anti-patterns are concrete** — at least three (e.g. communicating only
      when something is wrong, broadcasting untested controversial messages,
      the single-channel announcement that half the org never sees).
- [ ] **Credit is explicit** — Authoritative References names Larson's *The
      Engineering Executive's Primer* and the lethain.com companion post.

## Non-goals

- Not a meeting-design post — [[meetings]] covers how I run and prune the
  meeting portfolio; this post covers the written and multi-channel layer.
- Not a metrics-reporting post — [[measuring-engineering-organizations]]
  covers what I report upward and how; this post covers how messages travel.
- Not a general writing course — no prose-style guidance beyond what the
  checklist itself demands (brevity, summaries, structure).
