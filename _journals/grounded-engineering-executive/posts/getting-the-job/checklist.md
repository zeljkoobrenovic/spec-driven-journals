*The working checklist behind this principle, grouped by search stage — from deciding to pursue the role to handling rejection. The Article tab carries the rationale, implications, and anti-patterns.*

### Decide Whether to Pursue an Executive Role

- [ ] Clearly articulate why you want an executive role.
- [ ] Ensure your rationale is positive (growth, impact, learning — not ego or status).
- [ ] Pressure-test your reasoning with trusted peers/mentors.
- [ ] Confirm you're motivated by the work — not just the title.

### Prepare for a "One-of-One" Search

- [ ] Accept that executive searches are unstructured and variable.
- [ ] Avoid over-generalizing from past interview experiences.
- [ ] Recognize that confidence is common — discern substance from bravado.
- [ ] Expect role definitions to vary significantly across companies.

### Finding Roles

**Internal**

- [ ] Evaluate realistically whether internal promotion makes sense.
- [ ] Consider political dynamics and peer relationships.
- [ ] Assess whether you'll receive adequate support in transition.
- [ ] Understand internal transitions may be harder than external ones.

**External**

- [ ] Strengthen and activate your professional network.
- [ ] Build relationships with executive recruiters.
- [ ] Maintain an updated LinkedIn profile.
- [ ] Respond thoughtfully and professionally to recruiter outreach.
- [ ] Seek warm introductions via investors, founders, or operators.
- [ ] Don't rely solely on recruiters (best roles often never reach them).
- [ ] Be cautious about roles publicly posted — investigate context.

### Interview Process

**General Preparation**

- [ ] Prepare to be evaluated on fit, communication, and perceived leadership.
- [ ] Show energy and authentic excitement for the company.
- [ ] Use structured storytelling (e.g., STAR method).
- [ ] Send timely, thoughtful follow-ups.

**Interview Stages**

- [ ] Recruiter screen: clarify expectations, logistics, and concerns.
- [ ] CEO/executive call: demonstrate preparation and strategic thinking.
- [ ] Deep founder discussions: assess mutual working fit.
- [ ] Meet peer executives and key team members.
- [ ] Prepare a clear, structured executive presentation (vision + first 90 days).

**Presentation**

- [ ] Focus on high-impact priorities.
- [ ] Leave time for discussion.
- [ ] Ask what has worked well in past candidate presentations.
- [ ] Align your plan with company priorities.

### Negotiating the Contract

**Compensation**

- [ ] Benchmark compensation with peers.
- [ ] Review comparable public executive compensation (if applicable).
- [ ] Clarify base, bonus structure, and equity details.

**Equity**

- [ ] Understand form (options, RSUs, etc.).
- [ ] Review vesting schedule and cliffs.
- [ ] Negotiate vesting terms if appropriate.
- [ ] Understand post-termination exercise window.
- [ ] Evaluate tax implications with an advisor.

**Acceleration**

- [ ] Clarify single vs. double trigger acceleration.
- [ ] Understand acquisition/change-of-control scenarios.

**Severance**

- [ ] Negotiate severance terms upfront.
- [ ] Confirm severance duration and conditions.

**Bonus**

- [ ] Clarify bonus size and structure.
- [ ] Understand individual vs. shared goal metrics.

**Other Terms**

- [ ] Negotiate parental leave terms (if relevant).
- [ ] Agree on a realistic start date.
- [ ] Negotiate role support (e.g., executive assistant, hiring budget).
- [ ] Don't over-negotiate to the point of damaging trust.

### Deciding Whether to Accept

- [ ] Spend sufficient time with the CEO.
- [ ] Ensure you trust their leadership and judgment.
- [ ] Speak with at least one board member.
- [ ] Meet every executive you'll regularly work with.
- [ ] Review the company's recent financials (P&L).
- [ ] Confirm your key questions have been fully answered.
- [ ] Investigate recent executive departures.
- [ ] Be willing to walk away if something feels off.
- [ ] Ask yourself:
  - [ ] Am I still excited?
  - [ ] Can I see myself thriving here?
  - [ ] Have trusted advisors validated this decision?

### If You Don't Get the Job

- [ ] Reframe rejection as filtering toward a better fit.
- [ ] Remember: the goal is not *an* executive job — it's the *right* one.
- [ ] Maintain relationships even when processes end.
