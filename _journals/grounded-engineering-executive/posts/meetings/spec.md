# Spec: Operating Principle: Meetings

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State how I run meetings in an engineering organization: every meeting has a
clear purpose — decision, review, development, alignment, or Q&A — and fills
a communication gap rather than being the default. I run a deliberate core
cadence (weekly team, leadership, tech spec review, and incident review;
monthly managers, Staff+, and org-wide Q&A), anchor meetings to written
documents with discussion prioritized over presentation, give every
recurring session a clear owner, and hold each one to a quality bar that
includes the willingness to cancel it when it stops adding value. The post
turns the "Engineering Org Meetings" checklist into a durable operating
principle: a small portfolio of well-run meetings that distribute context,
communicate culture, and surface concerns.

## Audience

Engineering managers and Staff+ engineers in my organization, who attend and
increasingly own these meetings; peer executives who want to know how
Engineering's meeting portfolio is structured. Declarative first-person
register — a vision statement, not a facilitation handbook.

## Success criteria

- [ ] **Principle is quotable** — the highlight blockquote states the
      "purposeful cadence, document-anchored, cancel when value stops"
      stance in one paragraph.
- [ ] **Rationale is argued, not asserted** — explains why meetings must
      fill communication gaps rather than be the default, and why written
      documents plus discussion beat presentations.
- [ ] **The checklist survives intact** — every section of the source PDF
      (foundations: why you're running meetings; core meeting cadence;
      additional high-value meetings; scaling guidelines; ownership &
      leadership; quality bar) appears in the Checklist tab (`checklist.md`), regrouped as
      bullets.
- [ ] **Anti-patterns are concrete** — at least three (e.g. status theater
      in the leadership meeting, splitting meetings prematurely because of
      one or two difficult participants, recording Q&A sessions so nobody
      asks real questions).
- [ ] **Credit is explicit** — Authoritative References names Larson's *The
      Engineering Executive's Primer* and the lethain.com companion post.

## Non-goals

- Not an internal-communication post — the full written/async communication
  system around meetings is [[internal-communication]]'s territory; this
  record covers the synchronous portfolio.
- Not a process-ownership post — who runs engineering processes in general
  is covered in [[run-engineering-processes]]; here ownership appears only
  per meeting.
- No company-specific calendar (exact days, durations, attendee lists) —
  the record states the cadence and quality bar, not a schedule.
