### 1. Work the Policy, Not the Exceptions

- [ ] Define the goal the policy is meant to serve.
- [ ] Identify constraints that actually limit behavior.
- [ ] Avoid writing policies that do not constrain choices.
- [ ] Treat exception requests as signals, not one-off problems.
- [ ] Batch exception requests rather than handling each one immediately.
- [ ] Periodically refresh the policy based on collected exceptions.
- [ ] Avoid changing policy so often that policy becomes indistinguishable from exceptions.

### 2. Say No Effectively

- [ ] Explain your team's constraints clearly.
- [ ] Separate velocity problems from prioritization problems.
- [ ] Use a delivery process view, such as a kanban board, to show where work gets stuck.
- [ ] Translate vague problems like "technical debt" into concrete, measurable examples.
- [ ] Identify whether the real constraint is capacity, prioritization, or relationships.
- [ ] When prioritizing, document incoming asks.
- [ ] Define guiding principles for selecting work.
- [ ] Test priorities with stakeholders.

### 3. Build Your Management Philosophy

- [ ] Treat management as an ethical profession.
- [ ] Consider the impact of decisions on people, not just business outcomes.
- [ ] Build strong relationships before trying to solve hard problems.
- [ ] Prefer people over process when process is failing.
- [ ] Do the hard thing now rather than postponing it.
- [ ] Make decisions in this order: company, team, yourself.
- [ ] Think independently instead of blindly copying inherited practices.
- [ ] Revisit and evolve your philosophy over time.

### 4. Manage In and Outside the Growth Plates

- [ ] Identify whether you are operating in a growth area or a stable area.
- [ ] In growth areas, prioritize execution over endless ideation.
- [ ] Reduce the backlog of obvious ideas before adding more.
- [ ] Accept that some work will be mediocre when constraints are severe.
- [ ] Outside growth areas, focus on fundamentals and steady improvement.
- [ ] Build relationships, develop your team, and prepare for future change.
- [ ] Match your leadership style to the current context.

### 5. Avoid Common Ways Managers Get Stuck

- [ ] Do not only manage down.
- [ ] Do not only manage up.
- [ ] Manage your relationship with your management chain.
- [ ] Avoid optimizing locally at the expense of the company.
- [ ] Do not assume hiring solves every problem.
- [ ] Spend deliberate time building relationships.
- [ ] Define your role broadly enough to fill gaps.
- [ ] Give your manager room to make mistakes.
- [ ] Pause before "fixing" everything when joining a new company.
- [ ] Delegate rather than disappearing from the work.
- [ ] Stay connected to ground truth.
- [ ] Do not confuse team size, title, or authority with real impact.
- [ ] Trust the team enough to delegate.
- [ ] Let others manage their own time.
- [ ] Celebrate what is working, not only what is broken.

### 6. Partner with Your Manager

- [ ] Tell your manager what problems you are solving.
- [ ] Share how you are solving them.
- [ ] Communicate progress and where you are stuck.
- [ ] Explain what you prefer to work on and what you can staff.
- [ ] Share your workload and capacity.
- [ ] Discuss your professional goals and areas for growth.
- [ ] Clarify how you believe you are being measured.
- [ ] Learn your manager's priorities and pressures.
- [ ] Ask how stressed or busy they are.
- [ ] Look for ways to help them.
- [ ] Understand what their manager expects from them.
- [ ] Periodically write a self-reflection or career narrative and share it.

### 7. Find Managerial Scope

- [ ] Do not equate career growth only with managing more people.
- [ ] Identify key gaps in the organization.
- [ ] Take responsibility for increasingly complex work.
- [ ] Pursue hard, cross-cutting problems.
- [ ] Measure scope by impact and responsibility, not title.
- [ ] Build reusable systems that empower others.
- [ ] Stop using team size as the gateway to career growth.

### 8. Set Organizational Direction

- [ ] Recognize when feedback becomes scarce or vague.
- [ ] Accept that senior roles require setting direction with limited input.
- [ ] Mine broadly for ideas across teams, peers, companies, and papers.
- [ ] Separate idea discovery from judgment.
- [ ] Turn a large pile of ideas into a strategy.
- [ ] Identify the key pivots or tradeoffs in the strategy.
- [ ] Write down decisions clearly.
- [ ] Test the strategy with others.
- [ ] Refine confusing parts into smaller, clearer questions.
- [ ] Distill the final direction into a few memorable points.

### 9. Close Out, Solve, or Delegate

- For every recurring or unresolved problem, choose one:
- [ ] **Close out:** Make a clear decision and communicate it to resolve the specific issue.
- [ ] **Solve:** Design a system, norm, or process so this kind of issue does not recur soon.
- [ ] **Delegate:** Redirect the work to someone better positioned or responsible for it.
- [ ] Do not keep solving every problem yourself.
- [ ] Use this method for a week and review whether it improves your effectiveness.
