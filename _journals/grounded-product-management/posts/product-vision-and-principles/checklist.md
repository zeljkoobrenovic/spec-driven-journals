*The working checklist behind this principle. The Article tab carries the rationale, implications, and anti-patterns.*

## Establish the Strategic Context

- [ ] Confirm that the company's mission, objectives, and scorecard are clear.
- [ ] Ensure the product vision supports company objectives.
- [ ] Define how the vision connects to product strategy.
- [ ] Align team topology to the vision.

## Create a Compelling Product Vision

- [ ] Make the vision customer-centric.
- [ ] Describe the future you want to create for customers.
- [ ] Focus on the end state, not the implementation plan.
- [ ] Make it ambitious and meaningful.
- [ ] Ensure it provides a shared "North Star" for all teams.
- [ ] Make the bigger picture understandable to every team.
- [ ] Clarify how each team contributes to the larger whole.
- [ ] Set a long-term horizon, typically **3–10 years**.
- [ ] Leverage relevant industry trends, not fads.
- [ ] Keep the emphasis on customer value, not technology for its own sake.

## Make the Vision Actionable Across the Organization

- [ ] Use the vision to guide product strategy.
- [ ] Use the vision to shape architecture decisions.
- [ ] Use the vision to inform team structure and topology.
- [ ] Give engineering enough clarity about what is coming.
- [ ] Avoid confusing the vision with a feature roadmap.
- [ ] Avoid giving each product team its own separate vision.

## Build the Right Ownership

- [ ] Ensure the head of product owns the development of the vision.
- [ ] Involve the design closely in shaping the vision.
- [ ] Involve technology leadership closely in shaping the vision.
- [ ] Ensure the CEO/GM feels genuine ownership of the vision.
- [ ] Prepare leaders to repeatedly communicate and "sell" the vision internally and externally.

## Communicate the Vision Effectively

- [ ] Tell the story from the user's perspective.
- [ ] Make the vision inspiring, not just informative.
- [ ] Create a visiontype or conceptual prototype.
- [ ] Consider a scripted video to communicate the vision.
- [ ] Use storyboarding where helpful.
- [ ] Show how different user types experience the future product.
- [ ] Involve product designers heavily in crafting the experience and communication.

## Validate the Vision Appropriately

- [ ] Test whether the underlying customer problems are real.
- [ ] Test whether customers care enough about those problems.
- [ ] Test whether customers are open to new solutions.
- [ ] Accept that you can validate demand, but not fully validate the final solution upfront.
- [ ] Treat pursuing the vision as a reasoned leap of faith.

## Use the Vision as a Leadership Tool

- [ ] Use the vision to recruit strong product talent.
- [ ] Use the vision to create meaning and purpose for teams.
- [ ] Use the vision as an evangelism tool across the company.
- [ ] Re-communicate the vision regularly; do not assume one telling is enough.
- [ ] Engage executives, investors, sales, marketing, customer success, and other stakeholders.

## Handle Roadmap Conversations Carefully

- [ ] Prefer sharing the product vision over sharing detailed roadmaps.
- [ ] Stay stubborn on vision but flexible on details.
- [ ] Avoid locking into roadmap promises too early.
- [ ] Be careful with forward-looking commitments to customers.
- [ ] Share only high-integrity commitments when necessary.

## Define Product Principles

- [ ] Write a clear set of product principles that complement the vision.
- [ ] Use principles to guide day-to-day decisions.
- [ ] Make principles explicit enough to help with trade-offs.
- [ ] Explain the reasoning behind each principle.
- [ ] Ensure teams understand which values take priority in conflicts.

## Address Ethics and Trade-Offs

- [ ] Identify the major trade-offs teams are likely to face.
- [ ] Clarify how to balance goals such as ease of use vs. security.
- [ ] Discuss ethics explicitly, not implicitly.
- [ ] Consider how the product could be misused.
- [ ] Define principles for preventing harmful or unethical outcomes.
- [ ] Give teams enough context to make good ethical decisions.

## Final Quality Check

- [ ] The vision is compelling.
- [ ] The vision is customer-centered.
- [ ] The vision is shared across teams.
- [ ] The vision informs strategy, architecture, and team design.
- [ ] The principles support real trade-off decisions.
- [ ] The organization can explain the vision simply and consistently.
