*The working checklist behind this principle — derived from John Cutler's "20 Unfiltered Operating Takes". The Article tab carries the rationale and anti-patterns.*

## Build the Causal Model

- [ ] The team can name the near-term things it directly influences.
- [ ] Those inputs are linked, explicitly, to the long-term outcomes that propel the company.
- [ ] The team can walk a skeptic through the chain — including where it rests on conviction rather than data.
- [ ] Teams far from money have made their chain to money explicit, not assumed.
- [ ] The model is treated as a model: refined as answers arrive, not defended as a theorem.

## Balance Math and Conviction

- [ ] Where measurement is cheap, it is used — no leaps of faith over available data.
- [ ] Where measurement is impossible, conviction and narrative are used openly and labeled as such.
- [ ] Leaps of faith are funded knowingly, with the model saying why the landing zone is plausible.
- [ ] Input metrics get stared at on a rhythm: revised, discussed, monitored, switched up.

## Run the Question Loop

- [ ] The team asks sharp questions about its lanes as part of the weekly motion — not in standalone workshops.
- [ ] Answers are harvested: last week's work visibly sharpens this week's questions.
- [ ] Core assumptions are re-checked on a cadence.
- [ ] Nobody needs the perfect answer to start moving; the loop tolerates "we'll find out".

## Mix the Goals

- **The goal palette is actually used — the current set includes several of:**
  - [ ] Learning goals.
  - [ ] Shipping goals.
  - [ ] Outcome goals — near-term inputs and lagging results.
  - [ ] Practice and continuous-improvement goals.
- [ ] Prescriptive goals where the path is known; descriptive goals where exploring.
- [ ] Goals are set, revisited, and retired on a rhythm — the habit, not the performance.
- [ ] No goal is a bolted-on "success metric" added after the wishlist was written.
- [ ] Goals inform decisions but do not rule them — a goal can be changed when it stops making sense.

## Hold the Bar, Spread the Horizons

- [ ] Powerful ideas proceed with imperfect measurement; fidelity improves over time.
- [ ] No weak idea survives on the strength of its dashboard alone.
- [ ] Multi-horizon thinking is expected from every level — and staffed for on the front line.
- [ ] Experienced ICs are visibly showing others how to do the thing.
