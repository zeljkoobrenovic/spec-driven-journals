# Spec: Goals and Questions

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State what outcome-centricity costs when it is practiced rather than
preached: it is not a mindset but a **built causal model** — near-term
things a team can influence, linked to the long-term results that propel
the company — plus the conviction, storytelling, and leaps of faith that
the math alone cannot supply ("if everything were an A/B test and every
pattern had already happened, you are a phone company"). Four supporting
claims. First: proximity to money is real — the cart team outbids the
design-system team for attention unless the causal chain to money is made
explicit; there is no free lunch. Second: what compounds is **asking
better questions**, not having all the answers — answers sharpen the next
question, and that loop runs on tempo and routines, so teams that turn
"good questions" into a standalone activity drift back to business as
usual. Third: **goals are a habit, not a performance** — set them for
anything (learning, shipping, outcomes, practices, leading, lagging), mix
prescriptive with descriptive and near with long, and refuse both goal
theater and goals-as-afterthought-success-metric. Fourth: **powerful ideas
imperfectly measured beat perfect measures of weak ideas**, and
multi-horizon thinking belongs at every level — experienced ICs on the
front line reasoning across horizons, not a leaders-only privilege.

## Audience

My product leaders and teams (so goal-setting expectations and the
causal-model demand are explicit); peer executives who fund "outcome
transformation" initiatives and deserve the unvarnished version.
First-person declarative.

      a habit, not a ritual.

      outputs-to-outcomes talk named as too theoretical; the record
      states the real mix (math + conviction + storytelling + narrative)

      complaining about attention.

      named.
- [ ] **Goal mixing is specific** — the goal-type palette (learning,

      improvement) and both anti-patterns (goal motions; goals as
      afterthought) appear.
- [ ] **The two closing takes survive** — powerful ideas imperfectly
      measured over perfect measures of weak ideas; horizons for
      everyone, with experienced people ideally on the front line.
- [ ] **Checklist is operational** — derived checklist (causal-model
      build, question rituals, goal-mix audit) in the Checklist tab.
- [ ] **Credit is explicit** — References name John Cutler's TBM 435 as
      the grounding source.

## Non-goals

- Not a restatement of [[outcomes]] — that record defines the outcome
  pair and metrics discipline; this one is about the practice cost of
  living by them (model, conviction, habit).
- Not an OKR framework — [[team-objectives]] covers how objectives get
  assigned and committed; goal *mixing* here is deliberately looser than
  any framework.
- Not a measurement methodology — imperfect measurement is embraced, not
  solved.
- No claim that leaders stop doing long-horizon work — the claim is that
  horizon-thinking is not exclusive to them.
