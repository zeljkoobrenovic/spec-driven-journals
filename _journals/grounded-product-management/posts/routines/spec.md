# Spec: Routines

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State that great products are built on **routines** — the collective habits,
explicit or implicit, that make good work happen weekly. The record carries
five claims. First: routines are the heartbeat; they can be fluid or strict,
but they must exist, and the failure modes are symmetric — processified to
death, or so loose the team cannot reset when something changes. Second:
new teams of experienced people should be bootstrapped with a **V1 way of
working** — decided by anyone, collectively or delegated — and then get
moving, because smart people waste enormous energy reconciling work-style
quirks and back-channel gap-filling when what they want is for someone to
define the game; the trap to avoid is a deck-ified "starter OS" written for
people who don't do the work. Third: the critical moment is week four — the
point where the weekly ritual either becomes a zombie meeting or quietly
stops; pushing through as elephants accumulate is the discipline. Fourth:
lean on **anchor artifacts** — documents you write and revisit — over
perpetually reinventing formats. Fifth: **AI turbocharges both your good
and your bad habits** — most principles survive; AI lets you finally do
what you knew you should (organize research, instrument analytics), but
people who have never seen high-leverage work will use it to scale
mediocrity, so expertise still sets the bar. Close with "boring product":
good product work is undramatic doing, every day, every week — you can
hear when a team has the motion and when it doesn't.

## Audience

My product teams — especially newly formed ones and their leads (the V1
mandate is for them); leaders tempted to answer drift with more process.
First-person declarative.

## Success criteria

- [ ] **Principle is quotable** — highlight states that great products are
      built on routines, that I bootstrap new teams with a V1 way of
      working rather than letting them theorize, and that the routine's
      job is to survive week four.
- [ ] **Both failure modes named** — over-processified and loosey-goosey,
      with the note that experienced people are still vulnerable to
      avoided risks, dodged hard conversations, and "one more week".
- [ ] **The V1 move is concrete** — either write working agreements in one
      meeting or delegate ("Bob, hammer out V1"), explicitly
      non-dictatorial, adapt as you go; the deck-ified showpiece OS named
      as the risk.
- [ ] **The week-four collapse is vivid** — zombie meeting or silent
      death; leaders drifting to far-off planning rooms; 1:1s plotting for
      the expected collapse — and the payoff of pushing through.
- [ ] **Anchor artifacts and AI both land** — revisit written artifacts vs
      reinventing formats; AI as habit-turbocharger with the
      feature-factory and synthetic-persona examples, and expertise as the
      guard.
- [ ] **Boring product closes it** — the day-to-day feel (motion,
      learning, connecting small to big) as the test you can hear.
- [ ] **Checklist is operational** — derived checklist (V1 bootstrap,
      routine health, week-four watch, anchor artifacts, AI leverage) in
      the Checklist tab.
- [ ] **Credit is explicit** — References name John Cutler's TBM 435 as
      the grounding source.

## Non-goals

- Not a meeting-format catalog — [[right-processes]] carries the operating
  processes; this record is about the habit layer that keeps any of them
  alive.
- Not an AI strategy — the AI take here is scoped to habits and leverage;
  organizational AI policy lives elsewhere.
- Not team formation or staffing — [[right-team]] and [[staffing]] cover
  who is in the room; this record starts when they are.
- No claim that implicit routines are inferior — the claim is that the
  routine must exist and be resettable, not that it must be written.
