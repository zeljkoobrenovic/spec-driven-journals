*The working checklist behind this principle. The Article tab carries the rationale, implications, and anti-patterns.*

## Define the Key Customer Outcome

- [ ] We can clearly describe the **outcome customers want**, not just the feature they use.
- [ ] The outcome explains **why customers use the product**, not merely how they use it.
- [ ] The outcome is something customers genuinely care about and are willing to spend money, time, or effort on.
- [ ] We have validated the outcome through customer interviews, surveys, usage data, or sales conversations.
- [ ] We can describe the outcome in the customer's language.
- [ ] The outcome is durable enough to guide product decisions for the next 2–3 years.
- [ ] The outcome is specific enough to help us prioritize what to build.

## Test Whether the Outcome Matters

- [ ] Customers would recognize this as a meaningful improvement in their lives or work.
- [ ] Customers would be willing to switch, pay, renew, or expand as a result of this outcome.
- [ ] We understand what customers currently do instead.
- [ ] We know what "better" looks like from the customer's perspective.
- [ ] We have identified the emotional, financial, or practical value customers receive.
- [ ] We are not confusing internal engagement metrics with real customer value.

## Choose a Strong Customer Outcome Metric

- [ ] The metric measures customer value, not just company activity.
- [ ] The metric is tied to the customer's desired result.
- [ ] It can be measured directly or through a reliable proxy.
- [ ] It avoids vanity metrics such as clicks, views, or raw usage unless those clearly connect to value.
- [ ] It helps us know whether the product is making customers more successful.
- [ ] It would still matter even if our current product changed significantly.

## Aim for a 10x Outcome

- [ ] The chosen outcome is ambitious enough to create meaningful differentiation.
- [ ] We are not settling for a small usability improvement when customers need a much bigger result.
- [ ] We have identified what would make the product dramatically better for customers.
- [ ] We can explain why achieving this outcome would also create business value.
- [ ] The team understands what "10x better" would mean in practical terms for customers.

## Define the Key Business Outcome

- [ ] Leadership agrees on the primary business outcome we are optimizing for.
- [ ] We know whether success means revenue growth, retention, margin improvement, expansion, valuation growth, or another outcome.
- [ ] We have clarified whether growth should come from new customers, existing customers, upsell, retention, pricing, or efficiency.
- [ ] We have resolved competing definitions of success across sales, marketing, product, customer success, and engineering.
- [ ] Product priorities are evaluated against both customer outcome and business outcome.

## Balance Customer Value and Company Value

- [ ] We understand how customer value creates the opportunity for business value.
- [ ] We are not extracting more value from customers than we are delivering.
- [ ] Pricing, monetization, and packaging decisions are tied to customer ROI.
- [ ] We are watching for signs of over-monetization, such as churn, complaints, declining trust, or competitor switching.
- [ ] We are investing in customer value before trying to capture more revenue.
- [ ] We know which product improvements would increase both customer value and business results.

## Build a Customer Outcome Pyramid

- [ ] We have placed the key customer outcome at the top of the pyramid.
- [ ] We have broken that outcome into major drivers.
- [ ] We have identified leading indicators that influence the outcome.
- [ ] We have identified lagging indicators that confirm the outcome was achieved.
- [ ] Each metric in the pyramid has a clear relationship to the metric above it.
- [ ] The pyramid is simple enough for the team to understand and use.
- [ ] The pyramid helps product teams decide what to improve next.

## Build a Business Outcome Pyramid

- [ ] We have placed the key business outcome at the top of the pyramid.
- [ ] We have broken it into the business drivers that create that outcome.
- [ ] We have connected business drivers to customer value where possible.
- [ ] We can see where customer outcomes and business outcomes reinforce each other.
- [ ] We have identified which business metrics product can influence directly.
- [ ] We have identified which business metrics belong primarily to sales, marketing, or customer success.

## Align Teams Around Time Horizons

- [ ] Customer success and account management are focused on renewals, retention, and account growth.
- [ ] Sales is focused on near-term pipeline and closing deals.
- [ ] Marketing is focused on shaping demand and building future pipeline.
- [ ] Product is focused on creating future customer value.
- [ ] The organization understands that product work may affect business outcomes over multiple quarters or years.
- [ ] Product is not judged only by short-term revenue movement.

## Validate with Customers and the Market

- [ ] We have asked customers what result they are trying to achieve.
- [ ] We have asked prospects what metric would make them expand, renew, or switch.
- [ ] We have looked for differences across customer segments or stakeholders.
- [ ] We have identified whether different users, buyers, or customers need different outcome pyramids.
- [ ] We have confirmed that the chosen outcome is not just an internal assumption.
- [ ] We have reviewed competitors to understand whether they deliver the outcome better than we do.

## Use Outcomes to Guide Product Decisions

- [ ] Roadmap items are tied to customer outcome drivers.
- [ ] We can explain how each major product investment improves the outcome pyramid.
- [ ] We avoid optimizing metrics that harm customer trust or value.
- [ ] We prioritize work that increases the ceiling of customer value, not only work that captures more of existing value.
- [ ] We use metrics to learn, not just to prove success.
- [ ] We revisit priorities when metrics show we are optimizing the wrong thing.

## Final Readiness Check

- [ ] We know the key customer outcome.
- [ ] We know the key business outcome.
- [ ] We know how customer value leads to business value.
- [ ] We have outcome pyramids for both customer and business outcomes.
- [ ] We have leading and lagging indicators.
- [ ] We have validated the outcomes with customers.
- [ ] We have aligned the product, sales, marketing, customer success, and leadership teams.
- [ ] We can use this framework to make decisions about roadmap, pricing, and prioritization.
