# Spec: Outcomes

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State how I judge product work: by key outcomes — measurable changes in
customer and business reality — never by shipped output. The post turns the
*Build What Matters* "Key Outcomes" checklist into an operating principle:
defining the key customer outcome in the customer's language, testing that
it matters, choosing a strong outcome metric (not a vanity metric), aiming
for a 10x outcome, defining the key business outcome and balancing customer
value against company value, building customer and business outcome
pyramids with leading and lagging indicators, aligning teams around time
horizons, validating outcomes with customers and the market, and using
outcomes — not ship dates — to guide roadmap, pricing, and prioritization.

## Audience

My product leaders and product managers (so prioritization and kill
decisions are legible as outcome moves, not taste); peer executives and
engineering leaders who fund initiatives and deserve to know what "done"
means. First-person declarative.

## Success criteria

- [ ] **Principle is quotable** — highlight states that every initiative
      names the outcome it moves before it starts, and that an initiative
      that moves no outcome is a cost, however impressive the demo.
- [ ] **The outcome pair is explicit** — key customer outcome and key
      business outcome both appear, with the link from customer value to
      business value argued, not assumed.
- [ ] **Metrics discipline survives** — strong outcome metrics vs vanity
      metrics (clicks, views, raw usage); leading vs lagging indicators;
      outcome pyramids for both customer and business.
- [ ] **The horizon point lands** — product creates future value over
      quarters or years and is not judged only by short-term revenue
      movement.
- [ ] **The 2–3 year durability test appears** — the outcome is durable
      enough to guide decisions for the next 2–3 years and specific enough
      to prioritize with.
- [ ] **The checklist survives intact** — all twelve PDF sections
      reproduced in the Checklist tab (`checklist.md`), numbers preserved.
- [ ] **Credit is explicit** — References name Foster & Nerlikar's *Build
      What Matters* as the grounding source.

## Non-goals

- Not a metrics catalog or an OKR-writing tutorial — [[team-objectives]]
  covers how teams commit to outcomes.
- Not the roadmap mechanics — [[balanced-roadmap]] covers how outcomes,
  vision, and customer requests share a roadmap.
- Not a diagnosis of broken product organizations — [[dysfunctions]]
  covers the failure modes this principle exists to prevent.
- No claim that outputs are worthless — shipping is how outcomes move;
  the claim is only that shipping is never the finish line.
