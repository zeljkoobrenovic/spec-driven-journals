# Spec: Working Small

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State how I think about size and tempo: a team that can **work small** —
deliver continuously, test assumptions, keep risk-reduction tempo high — is
the asset I protect hardest, because it is far easier to teach a
small-working team to think big than to teach a big-thinking team to work
small. The record carries four connected claims. First: working small is
part environment (no constant interruption, WIP overload, strategy churn,
or premature convergence on estimates) and part skill (slicing options for
leverage, shipping, measuring impact) — both must be built. Second: no
planning cadence is "the" cycle — quarters, 2-week sprints, and 6-week
cycles are each simultaneously too long and too short; cycles are forcing
functions, not boxes to fill, and a quarter treated as a big sprint is a
perversion. Third: estimation needs only coarse resolutions — 1–3 days,
weeks, months, quarters, years — applied at the atomic level of the actual
bet, distinguishing the big messy project (needs a de-risking strategy)
from the umbrella bet funded iteratively (houses smaller bets, needs only
the first couple to work). Fourth: real progress can be sensed daily and
weekly even on big, hard things — SHIP, LEARN, don't wait for the quarter.

## Audience

My product teams and their leaders (so the tempo expectation is explicit);
program and planning owners who administer cycles and estimates and deserve
to know which precision I will not pay for. First-person declarative.

## Success criteria

- [ ] **Principle is quotable** — highlight states the asymmetry (small →
      big is teachable; big → small rarely) and the commitment to protect
      teams that can work small.
- [ ] **Environment and skill both named** — the record lists the
      environment killers (interruptions, WIP, strategy churn, premature
      estimate convergence) and the skills to hire for and nurture; "you
      can't make shots you don't take" survives.
- [ ] **The cycle relativity lands** — quarters/sprints/6-week cycles each
      shown too long and too short; "boxes to fill" named as the damage;
      the wholesale-quarterly-rebalance smell called out as a sign of
      working too big.
- [ ] **Estimation resolution is concrete** — the 1–3 d/w/mo/q/y ladder
      appears with the rule that finer precision is waste, plus the two
      shapes of a 1–3-quarter bet (messy project vs iteratively funded
      umbrella) and how their child-bets differ.
- [ ] **Daily/weekly progress is asserted** — real progress on big things
      can be sensed daily and weekly; coaching teams to break work up
      without losing the big picture is named as the leader's job.
- [ ] **Checklist is operational** — derived checklist (environment audit,
      slicing skills, cycle hygiene, estimation ladder) in the Checklist
      tab.
- [ ] **Credit is explicit** — References name John Cutler's TBM 435 as
      the grounding source.

## Non-goals

- Not process mechanics — [[right-processes]] covers the discovery/delivery
  loop; this record is about size, tempo, and cadence relativity.
- Not a Shape Up / Scrum comparison — cycle systems appear only as
  examples of the relativity claim.
- Not the feature-factory critique — [[outcomes]] guards against shipping
  without impact; this record concedes the iterate-to-nowhere risk and
  points there.
- No claim that big bets are bad — the claim is that big bets are made
  safe by small work, not by precise estimates.
