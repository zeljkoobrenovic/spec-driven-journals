*The working checklist behind this principle — derived from John Cutler's "20 Unfiltered Operating Takes". The Article tab carries the rationale and anti-patterns.*

## Audit the Environment

- [ ] The team is not being interrupted mid-cycle with new asks.
- [ ] Strategy is not changing under the team faster than it can ship against it.
- [ ] WIP is capped — the team is not loaded with parallel commitments.
- [ ] Nobody is forced to converge prematurely on estimates.
- [ ] The team can get changes into production and measure their impact.

## Build the Skill

- [ ] The team can rapidly lay out options for a piece of work.
- [ ] The team can slice options to get the most leverage from the smallest change.
- [ ] Slicing skill is part of hiring criteria and internal coaching, not assumed.
- [ ] Teams that already work small are recognized and protected as an asset.
- [ ] Small-working teams are coached to think big — connected to lanes and larger bets.

## Keep Cycles as Tools

- [ ] No single cadence is treated as "the" cycle that all work must fit.
- [ ] Cycle boundaries act as checkpoints and forcing functions, not containers.
- [ ] Shippable work and learning are released when ready, never held for the planning window.
- [ ] Bets are not resized to match the cycle length.
- [ ] A wholesale rebalance at a cycle boundary is investigated as a smell: work too big, prioritization horizon too short.

## Estimate on the Ladder

- [ ] Every bet is sized on the coarse ladder: 1–3 days, 1–3 weeks, 1–3 months, 1–3 quarters, 1–3 years.
- [ ] Estimates are made at the atomic level of the actual bet, not its imagined pieces.
- [ ] No estimation effort is spent on finer resolution than the ladder.
- [ ] Each 1–3-quarter (or larger) bet has its shape named — one of:
  - Big messy project — needs a de-risking strategy and significant progress across its parts.
  - Iteratively funded umbrella — houses smaller bets, with pivot/proceed points; only the first few need to work.

## Sense Progress Weekly

- [ ] Every bet, including the biggest, produces weekly evidence: something shipped, learned, or de-risked.
- [ ] Big work is broken up without losing the big picture — and leaders coach this actively.
- [ ] The weekly conversation covers what was done and learned, not only what is planned.
- [ ] Work with no sensed progress for weeks is surfaced and challenged, not left "in progress".
