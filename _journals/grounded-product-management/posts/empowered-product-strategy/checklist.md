*The working checklist behind this principle. The Article tab carries the rationale, implications, and anti-patterns.*

## Establish Strategic Context

- [ ] Clarify the company's mission, objectives, and scorecard.
- [ ] Define or confirm product vision and principles.
- [ ] Ensure teams understand the broader business context.
- [ ] Confirm that team topology and ownership are clear.

## Create a Real Product Strategy

- [ ] Define the few critical problems the business most needs to solve.
- [ ] Make explicit choices about what matters most.
- [ ] Identify what will **not** be prioritized.
- [ ] Avoid confusing strategy with roadmaps, features, or tactics.
- [ ] Frame strategy as the approach for achieving the goal.

## Focus on the Vital Few

- [ ] Reduce the number of active top priorities.
- [ ] Limit major initiatives to a manageable number.
- [ ] Check whether teams are spread too thin.
- [ ] Ensure attention is concentrated on the highest-impact problems.
- [ ] Revisit whether the current work is creating outcomes or just activity.

## Generate Insights Before Choosing Solutions

- [ ] Study product and business data.
- [ ] Analyze acquisition, retention, funnel, and sales patterns.
- [ ] Conduct regular user research.
- [ ] Look for both evaluative and generative insights.
- [ ] Explore enabling technologies that could unlock new solutions.
- [ ] Track industry, competitive, and adjacent-market trends.
- [ ] Gather insights from multiple sources, not just dashboards.
- [ ] Prepare enough context so that important insights are recognizable.

## Share Insights Across the Organization

- [ ] Aggregate learnings from teams regularly.
- [ ] Share the most important insights with leaders and peers.
- [ ] Ensure insights reach the people making strategic decisions.
- [ ] Connect related learnings across teams.
- [ ] Avoid letting insights remain isolated within one team.

## Turn Insights into Action

- [ ] Convert key insights into clear team objectives.
- [ ] Give teams problems to solve, not just features to ship.
- [ ] Define expected business or customer outcomes.
- [ ] Use OKRs or similar methods only if they support empowered teams.
- [ ] Provide constraints and context without prescribing every solution.
- [ ] Check whether teams are acting like owners, not order-takers.

## Support Empowered Product Teams

- [ ] Confirm each team understands the objective and why it matters.
- [ ] Give teams room to discover and deliver the best solution.
- [ ] Ensure teams have the right skills, people, and access.
- [ ] Avoid stakeholder-driven feature overload.
- [ ] Serve customers in ways that work for the business — not the business by accepting every request.

## Practice Active Management

- [ ] Hold regular 1:1s or coaching sessions with product leaders.
- [ ] Review progress weekly or biweekly.
- [ ] Surface blockers, dependencies, and risks early.
- [ ] Remove obstacles and barriers quickly.
- [ ] Help teams make stakeholder decisions when needed.
- [ ] Provide help without slipping into command-and-control.
- [ ] Track whether teams are actually moving objectives forward.

## Stay Adaptive

- [ ] Expect strategy to meet real-world obstacles.
- [ ] Watch for missed dependencies, missing capabilities, or urgent issues.
- [ ] Reassess priorities when reality changes.
- [ ] Be open to vision pivots when a larger opportunity becomes clear.
- [ ] Do not abandon the vision too quickly just because execution is hard.

## Final Health Check

- [ ] We have a small number of truly important priorities.
- [ ] We are using insights, not opinions alone.
- [ ] Teams are solving problems, not just delivering backlog items.
- [ ] Leadership is actively managing progress and removing impediments.
- [ ] Work is aligned to measurable outcomes.
- [ ] Our strategy reflects choice, thinking, and effort.
