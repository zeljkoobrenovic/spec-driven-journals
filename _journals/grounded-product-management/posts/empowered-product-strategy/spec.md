# Spec: EMPOWERED Product Strategy

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State how I run product strategy in an empowered-teams organization: as a
loop of **focus, insight, and action**. The post turns the *EMPOWERED*
"Product Strategy" checklist into an operating principle: establish
strategic context (mission, vision, topology) before strategizing; create a
real strategy — explicit choices about the few critical problems, including
what will *not* be prioritized — rather than a roadmap in disguise; focus
on the vital few; do the hard work of generating insights from product
data, user research, enabling technologies, and industry trends before
choosing solutions; share insights across the organization so they reach
decision-makers; convert insights into team objectives — problems to
solve, not features to ship; support empowered teams with context and
room; practice active management (weekly progress, blockers removed)
without slipping into command-and-control; and stay adaptive when strategy
meets reality.

## Audience

Product leaders and product managers in my organization (so they know why
I refuse twenty "top priorities" and why objectives arrive as problems,
not task lists); peer executives comparing operating models; engineering
leaders who want the product-side counterpart to my engineering records.
First-person declarative.

## Success criteria

- [ ] **Principle is quotable** — highlight states the focus–insight–action
      loop in one paragraph: choose the few problems that matter, earn real
      insights, convert them into team objectives, and let empowered teams
      find the solutions.
- [ ] **Strategy is defined by contrast** — a real strategy is explicit
      choices about the few critical problems, not a roadmap, feature list,
      or set of tactics.
- [ ] **The insight sources are explicit** — product/business data, user
      research (evaluative and generative), enabling technologies, and
      industry/competitive trends all appear.
- [ ] **Action means objectives, not orders** — teams get problems to
      solve with expected outcomes, constraints, and context; not
      prescribed solutions.
- [ ] **Active management is distinguished from micromanagement** — weekly
      or biweekly progress, blockers surfaced and removed, help without
      command-and-control.
- [ ] **The checklist survives intact** — all ten PDF sections reproduced
      in the Checklist tab (`checklist.md`), items and numbering preserved.
- [ ] **Credit is explicit** — References name Cagan and Jones's
      *EMPOWERED* and note the svpg.com essay origins.
