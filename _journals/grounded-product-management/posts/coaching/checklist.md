*The working checklist behind this principle. The Article tab carries the rationale, implications, and anti-patterns.*

## 1. Coaching mindset

- [ ] Treat developing people as job #1
- [ ] Judge your success by the growth of your people
- [ ] Focus on empowerment, not task control
- [ ] Create ownership of outcomes, not dependency on you
- [ ] Remove impediments, clarify context, and coach judgment
- [ ] Watch for insecurity, ego, or need for recognition
- [ ] Encourage diverse viewpoints and constructive disagreement
- [ ] Build trust intentionally and continuously
- [ ] Praise publicly and criticize privately
- [ ] Address mistakes directly and promptly
- [ ] Ensure every person has an explicit coach

## 2. Skills assessment

- [ ] Assess each person's current capability vs. role expectations
- [ ] Rate both expectations and current capability by skill area
- [ ] Identify the biggest gaps
- [ ] Focus on the top 3 gaps first
- [ ] Reassess periodically
- [ ] Use assessment to guide development, not replace coaching

## 3. Product knowledge coaching

- [ ] Build user and customer knowledge
- [ ] Build data fluency
- [ ] Build industry and domain knowledge
- [ ] Build business and company knowledge
- [ ] Build product operational knowledge
- [ ] Expect the person to become deeply credible in the product

## 4. Process and execution coaching

- [ ] Teach discovery methods
- [ ] Teach optimization methods
- [ ] Teach delivery methods and tradeoffs
- [ ] Teach the product development process in your company
- [ ] Teach risk framing: value, usability, feasibility, viability
- [ ] Ensure they understand their role in discovery and delivery

## 5. People skills coaching

- [ ] Coach team collaboration
- [ ] Coach stakeholder collaboration
- [ ] Coach product evangelism and communication
- [ ] Coach leadership and influence
- [ ] Observe real interactions and coach from them
- [ ] Reinforce trust, respect, and mutual accountability

## 6. Coaching plan

- [ ] Create a written coaching plan for priority gaps
- [ ] Define learning actions: reading, coaching, training, exercises, exposure
- [ ] Give stretch opportunities matched to readiness
- [ ] Develop strengths, not only weaknesses
- [ ] Connect development to future growth and promotion
- [ ] Review progress weekly

## 7. Run strong 1:1s

- [ ] Hold 1:1s at least weekly
- [ ] Protect the meeting from casual cancellation
- [ ] Use it primarily for development, not status
- [ ] Listen more than you talk
- [ ] Build trust and candor
- [ ] Share context and expectations
- [ ] Discuss progress on learning and development
- [ ] Give specific, timely, constructive feedback
- [ ] Help the person think and act like a strong product leader
- [ ] Use the 1:1 to connect dots across teams and issues

## 8. Use stronger coaching tools

- [ ] Use written narratives for major decisions
- [ ] Require clear thinking before presentations
- [ ] Use writing to expose weak logic and missing evidence
- [ ] Review the narrative and FAQ together
- [ ] Coach for clarity, logic, and persuasiveness

## Strategic context

### 9. Teach strategic context explicitly

- [ ] Make strategic context part of onboarding
- [ ] Ensure every PM understands the broader business context
- [ ] Revisit context regularly, not only once
- [ ] Verify the person can explain how their team contributes to company goals

### 10. Ensure understanding of the six strategic context elements

- [ ] Company mission
- [ ] Company scorecard/health metrics / KPIs
- [ ] Company objectives
- [ ] Product vision
- [ ] Product principles
- [ ] Team topology
- [ ] Product strategy

### 11. Company mission

- [ ] Ensure the person knows why the company exists
- [ ] Ensure they can connect their work to the mission
- [ ] Check whether they use the mission in judgment and prioritization

### 12. Company scorecard

- [ ] Teach the key business health metrics
- [ ] Ensure they understand what healthy vs. unhealthy looks like
- [ ] Ensure they understand key funnels and business dynamics
- [ ] Confirm they know which metrics matter most

### 13. Company objectives

- [ ] Teach the current company objectives
- [ ] Emphasize that objectives are outcomes, not outputs
- [ ] Connect key results back to the company scorecard
- [ ] Ensure the person can explain how the team objectives ladder up

### 14. Product vision and principles

- [ ] Ensure the PM understands the future the company is trying to create
- [ ] Ensure they understand why that future matters to customers
- [ ] Teach the product principles that guide tradeoffs
- [ ] Check whether they actually use those principles in decisions

### 15. Team topology and product strategy

- [ ] Ensure the PM understands what each team is responsible for
- [ ] Clarify where their team sits in the bigger picture
- [ ] Teach the product strategy that connects company goals to team objectives
- [ ] Check that the PM can explain why their objectives matter

## Ownership mindset

### 16. Coach's sense of ownership

- [ ] Coach thinking like an owner, not an employee
- [ ] Emphasize responsibility for outcomes, not just activity
- [ ] Reinforce that customers care about results, not effort
- [ ] Teach the PM to own obstacles, not just report them
- [ ] Expect them to bring context, not wait to be told what to do
- [ ] Reinforce taking responsibility when things go wrong
- [ ] Reinforce giving credit when things go well

### 17. Coach outcome orientation

- [ ] Ask whether the PM is solving for output or outcome
- [ ] Evaluate performance by business and customer results
- [ ] Reinforce that ownership includes cross-functional relationships
- [ ] Coach the PM to act with long-term company value in mind

## Managing time

### 18. Protect time for real product work

- [ ] Ensure the PM has substantial time for discovery and problem-solving
- [ ] Help them identify where their time is currently going
- [ ] Distinguish product management work from project management work
- [ ] Block recurring time for deep product work
- [ ] Protect that time from routine meeting sprawl
- [ ] Reduce unnecessary coordination work where possible
- [ ] Consider delivery/project support if PM time is being consumed
- [ ] Coach the PM to spend time ensuring what gets built is worth building

### 19. Diagnose time misuse

- [ ] Check whether the PM is overspending time on project tracking
- [ ] Check whether they are choosing tangible admin work over ambiguous product work
- [ ] Check whether the calendar makes quality thinking impossible
- [ ] Address time management as an explicit coaching topic

## Thinking and judgment

### 20. Coach thinking, not just knowledge

- [ ] Distinguish intelligence from actual thinking
- [ ] Distinguish acquiring knowledge from applying knowledge
- [ ] Expect the PM to think through problems before escalating
- [ ] Teach structured problem solving
- [ ] Check how the PM responds when they do not know the answer
- [ ] Coach them to reason through value, usability, feasibility, and viability together
- [ ] Use writing and discussion to build critical thinking
- [ ] Reinforce that product work is fundamentally problem-solving

## Team collaboration

### 21. Coach true collaboration

- [ ] Define collaboration as solving problems together
- [ ] Clarify that collaboration is not consensus for its own sake
- [ ] Clarify that collaboration is not artifact production
- [ ] Clarify that collaboration is not a compromise that produces mediocre outcomes
- [ ] Reinforce that collaboration aims at solutions that are valuable, usable, feasible, and viable

### 22. Strengthen cross-functional collaboration

- [ ] Build true collaboration between PM, design, and engineering
- [ ] Encourage teams to work around prototypes and shared problem exploration
- [ ] Ensure each discipline brings its expertise rather than deferring blindly
- [ ] Reinforce that no one should try to do the other person's job
- [ ] Expect each person to come prepared, having done the homework
- [ ] Watch for arrogance or premature certainty from the PM
- [ ] Watch for the PM reverting to requirement handoff behavior
- [ ] Coach collaboration with customers and prospects when appropriate

### 23. Diagnose collaboration failures

- [ ] Check whether the PM has done the business and customer homework
- [ ] Check whether arrogance is suppressing team contribution
- [ ] Check whether artifacts are replacing collaborative work
- [ ] Check whether the team is discussing solutions together early enough

## Stakeholder collaboration

### 24. Reframe stakeholder relationships

- [ ] Teach that stakeholders are partners, not obstacles
- [ ] Teach that the PM is not there merely to gather requirements
- [ ] Teach that the PM is not there to dictate solutions to stakeholders
- [ ] Ensure the PM understands each stakeholder's role and constraints
- [ ] Teach how stakeholder input contributes to business viability

### 25. Build trust with stakeholders

- [ ] Identify the critical stakeholder relationships for each PM
- [ ] Encourage relationship-building before moments of conflict
- [ ] Encourage regular 1:1 relationship time with key stakeholders
- [ ] Build rapport, not just transactional contact
- [ ] Help the PM learn how to navigate different personalities
- [ ] Reinforce that mutual trust makes disagreement productive

### 26. Coach executive collaboration

- [ ] Ensure the PM has done enough homework to talk credibly with executives
- [ ] Coach careful listening to executive concerns and constraints
- [ ] Teach the PM to bring solutions, not just problems
- [ ] Prepare the PM for increasingly broad executive expectations

## Imposter syndrome and preparedness

### 27. Coach preparedness, not performative confidence

- [ ] Normalize doubt as a signal to prepare better
- [ ] Teach the PM to use anxiety as a trigger for homework and rehearsal
- [ ] Require preparation for important presentations and reviews
- [ ] Insist on draft reviews and honest feedback before high-stakes meetings
- [ ] Provide progressive exposure for nervous presenters
- [ ] Encourage training and rehearsal where needed
- [ ] Reinforce that executive trust drops when a PM appears unprepared

## Customer-centricity

### 28. Define customer-centricity clearly

- [ ] Protect the meaning of "customer."
- [ ] Distinguish real customers from stakeholders and internal partners
- [ ] Reinforce that the role is to innovate on behalf of customers
- [ ] Teach that product changes, outages, and support moments reveal true customer care

### 29. Build customer contact into the role

- [ ] Expect regular direct customer interaction
- [ ] Encourage at least several meaningful customer conversations each week
- [ ] Review what the PM learned from those conversations
- [ ] Ask for stories, not just summaries
- [ ] Encourage the PM to share customer insights across the company
- [ ] Build their reputation as someone who deeply knows customers

### 30. Coach customer judgment

- [ ] Watch for contempt or dismissiveness toward customers
- [ ] Watch for the PM assuming customers are not smart
- [ ] Teach empathy and respect
- [ ] Teach urgency and calm during customer pain moments
- [ ] Reinforce that customer-centricity is shown in hard tradeoff situations

## Integrity

### 31. Coach integrity as a practical operating standard

- [ ] Treat integrity as foundational to trust
- [ ] Discuss pressures that challenge integrity in day-to-day work
- [ ] Coach for difficult real-world situations, not abstract values only

### 32. Dependability

- [ ] Teach that commitments must be taken seriously
- [ ] Ensure commitments are based on informed judgment
- [ ] Ensure the PM does not overcommit before sufficient discovery
- [ ] Ensure the PM relies on design and engineering expertise before committing
- [ ] Reinforce that what ships must actually solve the problem
- [ ] Coach the PM to build a reputation for dependable commitments

### 33. Company's best interests

- [ ] Teach the PM to act in the best interests of the company
- [ ] Watch for local optimization for only their team
- [ ] Encourage helping other teams when it benefits the company
- [ ] Recognize visible acts of company-first behavior
- [ ] Coach's decisions that may not maximize convenience for the team but are better for the business

### 34. Accountability

- [ ] Teach willingness to take responsibility for mistakes
- [ ] Ask what the PM could have done better, even when others contributed
- [ ] Reinforce that accountability is not blame-seeking
- [ ] Reinforce that accountability means learning, adjusting, and improving
- [ ] Ensure the PM does not hide behind cross-functional complexity

## Decision making

### 35. Build a strong foundation for decisions

- [ ] Reinforce that good decisions rest on integrity
- [ ] Reinforce that decisions must aim at customer and business outcomes
- [ ] Help the PM bring leaders and stakeholders along through rationale and respect
- [ ] Coach for decisions others can understand, even when they disagree

### 36. Right-size decision analysis

- [ ] Teach the PM to judge the level of risk
- [ ] Teach the PM to judge the consequences if wrong
- [ ] Match the depth of analysis to the importance of the decision
- [ ] Identify what information is critical before deciding
- [ ] Avoid over-analyzing low-consequence decisions
- [ ] Avoid under-analyzing high-consequence decisions
- [ ] Consider revenue, legal, stakeholder, and customer implications
- [ ] Encourage a clear plan of attack for consequential choices

### 37. Collaboration-based decision making

- [ ] Clarify that decision-making is not consensus, voting, or benevolent dictatorship
- [ ] Teach when to defer to the tech lead on enabling technology
- [ ] Teach when to defer to product design on customer experience
- [ ] Teach when the PM must work with stakeholders on business viability
- [ ] Reinforce that the hardest choices often concern value tradeoffs

### 38. Resolving disagreements

- [ ] Normalize healthy disagreement
- [ ] Teach when to run a test instead of arguing
- [ ] Coach selection of the least expensive useful test
- [ ] Use prototypes and evidence to resolve uncertainty
- [ ] Escalate only when necessary after collaborative attempts

### 39. Transparency

- [ ] Require a clear rationale for important decisions
- [ ] Use notes for smaller decisions
- [ ] Use written narratives and FAQs for major decisions
- [ ] Anticipate objections and address them explicitly
- [ ] Make reasoning visible to leaders and stakeholders

### 40. Disagree and commit

- [ ] Teach the team to debate honestly
- [ ] Teach them to accept a final decision once made
- [ ] Prevent undermining decisions after the fact
- [ ] Ensure the PM can explain the rationale even when it was not their preferred option
- [ ] Expect the PM to help make the final decision successful

## Warning signs and anti-patterns

### 41. Coaching anti-patterns

- [ ] Coaching is treated as secondary work
- [ ] 1:1s become status meetings
- [ ] The manager talks more than listens
- [ ] Feedback is delayed until reviews
- [ ] Manager micromanages
- [ ] Manager avoids difficult conversations
- [ ] Weak performance is tolerated for too long

### 42. PM anti-patterns

- [ ] PM focuses on activity over outcomes
- [ ] PM acts like a project coordinator rather than a product leader
- [ ] PM lacks strategic context
- [ ] PM has weak customer contact
- [ ] PM has not done the homework
- [ ] PM dominates rather than collaborates
- [ ] PM avoids ownership
- [ ] PM overcommits
- [ ] PM appears unprepared in important forums
- [ ] PM makes opaque decisions
- [ ] PM resists disagreement but fails to commit

## Manager self-audit

### 43. Weekly manager check

- [ ] Did I spend real time coaching this week?
- [ ] Do my people know I care about their development?
- [ ] Have I clarified the strategic context recently?
- [ ] Do I know each person's top development gaps?
- [ ] Did I observe and coach real behavior this week?
- [ ] Did I give honest, useful feedback this week?
- [ ] Am I fostering ownership rather than dependence?
- [ ] Am I protecting time for real product work?
- [ ] Am I coaching thinking and judgment, not only execution?
- [ ] Am I helping PMs become more customer-centric?
- [ ] Am I coaching collaboration across teams and stakeholders?
- [ ] Am I reinforcing integrity and accountability?
- [ ] Am I helping my team make better decisions?

### 44. Run effective meetings

- [ ] Challenge whether a meeting is needed at all
- [ ] Prefer asynchronous communication when it can serve the purpose
- [ ] Remember, meetings are expensive because they are synchronous
- [ ] Be aware that meetings shape how executives judge your team, especially the PM
- [ ] Avoid poorly prepared, slow, low-value meetings

### 45. Be clear on the type of meeting

- [ ] Identify whether the meeting is for communication, decision-making, or problem-solving
- [ ] Do not mix purposes carelessly
- [ ] For communication meetings, verify that the content is too important or complex for async
- [ ] For decision meetings, verify that a real decision is needed beyond what the team can decide alone
- [ ] For problem-solving meetings, verify that the issue is genuinely hard enough to benefit from real-time group thinking

### 46. Organize meetings well

- [ ] State the purpose clearly
- [ ] Decide who is essential vs. optional
- [ ] Keep the attendee list tight
- [ ] Postpone if essential people cannot attend
- [ ] Prepare thoroughly before the meeting starts
- [ ] Ensure the right content, medium, visuals, and data are ready
- [ ] For decision meetings, prepare the written narrative
- [ ] For problem-solving meetings, prepare context, framing, and relevant facts
- [ ] Anticipate likely questions in advance

### 47. Facilitate meetings well

- [ ] Facilitate the needed decision or solution
- [ ] Do not let the meeting drift aimlessly
- [ ] Do not confuse facilitation with policing
- [ ] Keep discussion tied to purpose
- [ ] Make sure the room understands the decision, even if people disagree
- [ ] Close the loop with explicit follow-up and next steps

## Ethics

### 48. Add ethics explicitly to product coaching

- [ ] Teach the classic four product risks: value, usability, feasibility, viability
- [ ] Add a fifth explicit question: Should we build it?
- [ ] Treat ethics as a first-class consideration, not something buried inside viability
- [ ] Make room for uncomfortable questions about consequences
- [ ] Reinforce that product teams have special responsibility because they create and ship new things

### 49. Teach ethical product judgment

- [ ] Ask how the solution affects customers beyond revenue
- [ ] Ask how the solution affects the broader stakeholder community
- [ ] Ask about environmental, social, regulatory, and reputational consequences
- [ ] Ask whether you would be proud to have the work publicly scrutinized
- [ ] Ask whether the product choice could embarrass the company if exposed
- [ ] Reinforce that good intentions can still create harmful unintended consequences

### 50. Coach how to raise ethical concerns

- [ ] Encourage people to raise concerns thoughtfully
- [ ] Coach them to avoid moral grandstanding or accusatory framing
- [ ] Frame concerns in terms of protecting the company's best interests
- [ ] Ensure they understand enough of the business context to speak credibly
- [ ] Help them involve their manager when the situation is sensitive
- [ ] Create an environment where uncomfortable questions are acceptable

### 51. Watch for company-level ethics signals

- [ ] Check whether ethics is regularly discussed in product work
- [ ] Check whether anybody is clearly responsible for ethical oversight
- [ ] Watch whether leadership seems genuinely concerned with integrity and ethics
- [ ] Take seriously the signs that the company consistently ignores ethical implications

## Happiness and sustainability

### 52. Treat team happiness as a management responsibility

- [ ] Accept that managers strongly influence whether product people are fulfilled or miserable
- [ ] Check regularly whether each person feels their work is meaningful
- [ ] Check whether they are progressing in their career and capability
- [ ] Check whether they are building the relationships needed to succeed
- [ ] Learn what is personally meaningful to each person

### 53. Reinforce meaningful work

- [ ] Help people understand why their work matters
- [ ] Connect team contributions to company and customer impact
- [ ] Reinforce meaning both privately and publicly
- [ ] Do not assume people automatically see the significance of their work

### 54. Build personal relationships

- [ ] Build trust through a real personal connection
- [ ] Learn aspirations, motivations, and interests outside work
- [ ] Let people know you are committed to helping them succeed professionally and personally
- [ ] Create the kind of relationship that can sustain honest feedback
- [ ] Aim to become a trusted and valued part of their career story

### 55. Recognize people personally

- [ ] Assume most people want to feel valued
- [ ] Personalize recognition to the individual
- [ ] Use frequent, human recognition beyond compensation and promotion
- [ ] Notice whether someone dislikes public recognition but still values appreciation
- [ ] Recognize effort, growth, and contribution in ways that feel genuine

### 56. Coach healthy work habits

- [ ] Distinguish between an occasional healthy push and chronic overwork
- [ ] Watch for signs of burnout
- [ ] Talk explicitly about sustainability and the long game
- [ ] Reinforce that creative problem-solving requires recovery time
- [ ] Make sure intense pushes do not become the normal operating mode
- [ ] Coach people who cannot disengage from meaningful work

### 57. Model healthy behavior

- [ ] Remember, your actions speak louder than your words
- [ ] Avoid creating pressure through your own unhealthy patterns
- [ ] Be thoughtful about when you send messages and emails
- [ ] Share how you recharge and manage your time
- [ ] Model sustainable intensity rather than performative overwork

### 58. Support career planning honestly

- [ ] Help people think clearly about their career path
- [ ] Acknowledge when someone may need a different role or career
- [ ] Recognize the outsized influence managers have on employees' lives
- [ ] Support long-term flourishing, not only near-term team needs

## Additional manager self-audit items

### 59. Meetings

- [ ] Did I cancel or convert low-value meetings when async would work?
- [ ] Did the meetings I ran have a clear purpose, preparation, and follow-up?
- [ ] Did I use written narratives for important decisions?

### 60. Ethics

- [ ] Did I explicitly ask whether we should build this, not only whether we can?
- [ ] Did I make space for ethical concerns without punishing people for raising them?
- [ ] Did I help my team think through unintended consequences?

### 61. Happiness

- [ ] Do I know what makes each person feel fulfilled?
- [ ] Am I reinforcing meaningful work frequently enough?
- [ ] Am I modeling healthy habits?
- [ ] Am I noticing burnout early?
- [ ] Am I helping people build careers, not just complete tasks?
