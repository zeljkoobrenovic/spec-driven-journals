*The working checklist behind this principle. The Article tab carries the rationale, implications, and anti-patterns.*

## Define the Destination

- [ ] Identify your **Point A**: where the product/customer experience is today.
- [ ] Define your **Point B**: the end-state customer journey vision.
- [ ] State the desired customer outcome clearly.
- [ ] Confirm the vision is compelling, specific, and aligned with the company's larger mission.
- [ ] Make sure the strategy serves the customer journey, not just the current product.

## Work Backward from the Vision

- [ ] Start with the end-state vision and work backward to today.
- [ ] Identify the major steps required to reach the vision.
- [ ] Map the dependencies between those steps.
- [ ] Ask: "What must be true before this next step can happen?"
- [ ] Identify possible dead ends before committing resources.
- [ ] Avoid moving forward just because something is easy to build.

## Conduct a Gap Analysis

- [ ] Compare the ideal customer journey with the current customer journey.
- [ ] Identify gaps in product capabilities.
- [ ] Identify gaps in data, infrastructure, or technology.
- [ ] Identify gaps in user volume, awareness, distribution, or brand.
- [ ] Identify gaps in partnerships, operations, or funding.
- [ ] Prioritize the gaps that most block the customer from achieving the desired outcome.

## Sequence the Work

- [ ] Decide what must happen first, second, third, and later.
- [ ] Consider financial constraints and runway.
- [ ] Identify what proof investors, executives, or stakeholders will need.
- [ ] Prioritize work that both advances the vision and supports the business.
- [ ] Make sure the sequence accounts for technical, competitive, financial, and operational dependencies.
- [ ] Leave room to adjust the plan as new information appears.

## Evaluate Strategy Factors

### Financial situation and fundraising

- [ ] Can you fund the development required to reach the next milestone?
- [ ] Do you need traction before raising more capital?
- [ ] Are milestones designed to prove enough value before money runs out?

### Competitive advantage

- [ ] Have you included the must-haves required to earn the right to play?
- [ ] Have you identified performance features that help you earn the right to win?
- [ ] Have you included delighters that drive loyalty and retention?
- [ ] Does the MVP include a thoughtful combination of must-haves, performance features, and delighters?

### Market segments

- [ ] Should you start with one customer segment before expanding?
- [ ] Is there a specific geography, vertical, customer size, or use case that gives you a better foothold?
- [ ] Can learnings from the first segment help later segments?

### Outcome delivery

- [ ] Can you deliver the larger customer outcome in phases?
- [ ] Are you starting with a single product before expanding into a platform?
- [ ] Does each phase build trust and increase value for the same customer?

### Technology and data dependencies

- [ ] What technical or data assets are required for the future vision?
- [ ] Do you need to build foundational capabilities before launching future features?
- [ ] Are you investing early enough in assets that will become strategic later?

### Strategic leverage

- [ ] Can relationships, partnerships, patents, brand, data, or thought leadership create an advantage?
- [ ] Are there non-product assets that should influence the product strategy?
- [ ] Are you using early traction to strengthen your position before competitors arrive?

## Establish Milestones

- [ ] Break the strategy into clear milestones.
- [ ] Define what each milestone proves.
- [ ] Assign business KPIs to each milestone.
- [ ] Assign customer KPIs to each milestone.
- [ ] Identify what work must be completed before moving to the next phase.
- [ ] Use milestones as natural points to reassess the strategy.

## Review Progress and Adapt

- [ ] Track development accomplishments to date.
- [ ] Summarize results and learnings so far.
- [ ] Identify next steps in the original strategic plan.
- [ ] Document any adjustments you are making or considering.
- [ ] Change the strategy when evidence shows it is necessary.
- [ ] Avoid reacting too literally to every piece of customer feedback.
- [ ] Make sure customer feedback improves the vision rather than replacing it.

## Final Strategy Check

- [ ] Does the plan clearly connect today's product to the long-term vision?
- [ ] Does the strategy explain why this sequence makes sense?
- [ ] Are customer outcomes and business outcomes both represented?
- [ ] Are the biggest risks, dependencies, and constraints visible?
- [ ] Are teams aligned around the same strategic plan?
- [ ] Is the plan flexible enough to evolve without losing direction?
