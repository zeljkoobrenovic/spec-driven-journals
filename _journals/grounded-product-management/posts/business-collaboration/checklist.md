*The working checklist behind this principle. The Article tab carries the rationale, implications, and anti-patterns.*

## Understand the Context

- [ ] Recognize that strong product leaders and empowered product teams are necessary, but not sufficient.
- [ ] Acknowledge that product work happens within the broader company context.
- [ ] Understand that the CEO, executives, and other stakeholders matter to product success.

## Build Cross-Business Relationships

- [ ] Identify the key stakeholders outside the product.
- [ ] Establish strong working relationships with the rest of the business.
- [ ] Approach those relationships with sensitivity and nuance.
  - [ ] Avoid using the term **"stakeholder management."**
  - [ ] Explain that this is not about *managing* people around product, but about working *with* peers across the business in a collaborative way.

## Shift the Team Model

- [ ] Recognize when the company is still operating with feature teams.
- [ ] Help the organization transition toward empowered product teams.
- [ ] Keep the focus on serving customers in ways that also work for the business.

## Change How the Product Works with the Business

- [ ] Move from a subservient model to a collaborative model.
- [ ] Encourage shared problem-solving instead of top-down feature delivery.
- [ ] Reinforce collaboration as the default way of working.

## Support Executive Mindset Change

- [ ] Help senior executives rethink how they view product teams.
- [ ] Build confidence in teams made up of ordinary individuals doing extraordinary work together.
- [ ] Prepare leaders for the organizational implications of this shift.

## Lead the Transformation

- [ ] Recognize that this is a significant company-wide change.
- [ ] Discuss how the change affects the rest of the business.
- [ ] Guide the company through changes in mindset and responsibilities.
- [ ] Continue reinforcing the new collaborative model over time.
