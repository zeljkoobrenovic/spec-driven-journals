# Spec: Business Collaboration

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State how I run product's relationship with the wider business: as a
partnership of equals — not a ticket-taking service to stakeholders, and not
an ivory tower above them. The post turns the *EMPOWERED* "Business
Collaboration" checklist into an operating principle: empowered teams are
necessary but not sufficient, because product work happens inside the broader
company context where the CEO, executives, and other stakeholders matter;
build genuine cross-business relationships (and retire the term "stakeholder
management"); shift the team model from feature teams to empowered product
teams; move the working model from subservient to collaborative; help
executives change how they see product teams; and lead the whole thing as a
company-wide transformation, reinforced over time. Partnership is earned —
by understanding the business's constraints, sharing context openly,
evangelizing the vision, and keeping the commitments we make.

## Audience

Product managers and product leaders in my organization (so the posture
toward sales, marketing, finance, legal, and support is explicit); peer
executives and stakeholders (so they know what to expect from product and
what product expects back). First-person declarative.

## Success criteria

- [ ] **Principle is quotable** — highlight states "partnership of equals:
      not servitude, not arrogance" and names the four trust-earning moves
      (understand constraints, share context, evangelize, keep commitments)
      in one paragraph.
- [ ] **Necessary-but-not-sufficient survives** — the record says plainly
      that strong leaders and empowered teams alone do not make product
      succeed; the company context does.
- [ ] **The vocabulary point lands** — "stakeholder management" is called
      out as a term I avoid, and why: peers are worked *with*, not managed
      around product.
- [ ] **Both failure postures are named** — servitude (feature-team
      ticket-taking) and arrogance (the ivory tower) each get an
      anti-pattern; partnership is argued as the only durable posture.
- [ ] **Transformation framing survives** — executive mindset change and
      "significant company-wide change, reinforced over time" appear, not
      just team-level advice.
- [ ] **The checklist survives intact** — all six PDF sections reproduced in
      the Checklist tab (`checklist.md`), including the nested
      sensitivity-and-nuance sub-items.
- [ ] **Credit is explicit** — References name Cagan & Jones's *EMPOWERED*
      and note the svpg.com essay origins.

## Non-goals

- Not a stakeholder-mapping or RACI exercise — no templates, no grids.
- Not [[team-objectives]] — that record covers how objectives are set; this
  one covers the relationship inside which they are negotiated.
- Not the executive's side of the table — the engineering journal's
  [[working-with-your-ceo-peers-and-engineering]] covers that seat; this
  record is the product organization's posture.
- No claim that collaboration means consensus — partnership includes saying
  no, with context.
