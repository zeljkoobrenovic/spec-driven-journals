# Spec: The Right Team

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State how I build the product team itself. The hiring arc: when a company
needs its first product hire and what that hire should be (a senior PM, not
a premature VP/CPO), how I evaluate PM candidates for a product mindset
using a real homework assignment, and when to add a second PM and a product
executive. The structure around the team: how I separate design from
product and keep them peers, the scaling ratios I plan around (5–7
developers, 1 PM, 0.5 UX designer, 0.3 UI designer, 1–2 QA per team), why I
refuse the PM/product-owner telephone game, when product operations earns
its place, and how I choose an org structure. And the culture — customer
discovery, collaboration, delegated decisions, diversity — that lets the
team do excellent product work. The post turns the *Build What Matters*
"Right Team" checklist into an operating principle.

## Audience

Founders and executives deciding when and whom to hire into product; my own
leadership bench, so product staffing decisions are legible as system moves;
PMs who want to know what I will hold them accountable for. First-person
declarative.

## Success criteria

- [ ] **Principle is quotable** — highlight states that I hire real product
      managers, place them in a real triad with design and engineering, and
      give them the conditions to own outcomes.
- [ ] **First-hire timing survives** — the readiness signals (founder out of
      time, unclear engineering priorities, ~5+ engineers, second product)
      and "senior PM, not VP/CPO too early" are explicit.
- [ ] **The product mindset is enumerated** — the eleven evaluation traits
      appear, and the homework assignment (real challenge, roughly 2–3
      hours) is argued as the hiring instrument.
- [ ] **The numbers survive** — 5–7 developers, 1 PM, 0.5 UX, 0.3 UI, 1–2 QA
      per team; PMs hired ahead of engineering growth.
- [ ] **PM vs product owner is settled** — one person with real
      accountability for product outcomes; no telephone games; PMs never far
      from customers.
- [ ] **Culture is included** — customer discovery, simple 360 feedback,
      delegated decision-making, and diversity framed as conditions for the
      team to succeed, not HR garnish.
- [ ] **The checklist survives intact** — all fourteen PDF sections
      reproduced in the Checklist tab (`checklist.md`), numbers preserved.
- [ ] **Credit is explicit** — References name Foster & Nerlikar's *Build
      What Matters*.

## Non-goals

- Not [[staffing]] — the EMPOWERED-grounded sibling covers staffing product
  teams under Cagan's coaching-first model; this record covers the
  founder-to-scale arc of building the product function from zero, per
  Foster & Nerlikar.
- Not [[coaching]] — that sibling covers developing PMs once hired; this one
  covers deciding whom to hire and what conditions to give them.
- Not the engineering side of hiring — the cross-journal [[hiring]] record
  covers how I hire engineers and executives.
- Not a compensation guide, a leveling framework, or an org chart for any
  specific company.
- No claim that the scaling ratios are laws — they are planning defaults
  that shift the burden of proof.
