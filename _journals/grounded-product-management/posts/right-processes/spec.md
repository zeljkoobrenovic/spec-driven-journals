# Spec: The Right Processes

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State how I run the processes of vision-led product management: process
exists to serve outcomes, never as ceremony. The post turns the *Build What
Matters* "Right Processes" checklist into an operating principle. It
grounds everything in a validated key customer outcome (hypotheses,
customer research, 5 Whys, outcome pyramids), validates the customer
journey vision outside the building, and keeps discovery running
continuously (at least four hours a week, research before deciding what to
build). From there it derives prioritization from strategy through a
transparent framework (idea bank, RICE, innovation/iteration/operation
allocation), sizes reassessment cadences to the horizon they govern
(quarterly for a one-year roadmap, yearly for a three-year vision), matches
the development process to the type of work (innovation sprints, Scrum,
Kanban), communicates the roadmap deliberately inside and cautiously
outside, and measures success by whether shipped work moved the outcome,
not by how much shipped.

The source checklist's strategy-session and vision-communication sections
("Craft the Product Strategy", "Communicate the Outcome, Vision, and
Strategy") overlap territory owned by the sibling records
[[product-strategy]] and [[outcomes]]; the article points at those records
rather than restating them, and the sections are reproduced in the
Checklist tab only so the seventeen-section source survives intact (see
the "checklist survives intact" success criterion).

## Audience

My product leaders and peer executives (so process decisions are legible as
outcome machinery, not bureaucracy); product managers comparing operating
models; engineering leaders who want to see the product-side counterpart of
their own process record. First-person declarative.

## Success criteria

- [ ] **Principle is quotable** — highlight states "processes exist to
      serve outcomes, not ceremony" and "research before deciding what to
      build" in one paragraph.
- [ ] **The loop is explicit** — discovery → validation → prioritization →
      delivery → learning appears as a continuous cycle feeding the
      roadmap, not a one-time phase.
- [ ] **The numbers survive** — at least four hours per week on customer
      research, reassess at the one-third/one-quarter mark of the horizon,
      quarterly for a one-year roadmap, yearly for a three-year vision,
      outcome reviews at least quarterly, top 1–2 stakeholder priorities.
- [ ] **Prioritization derives from strategy** — idea bank, consistent
      framework (RICE), transparency about what is off the roadmap and why,
      and the innovation/iteration/operation balance are argued, not just
      listed.
- [ ] **Process is matched to work** — innovation sprints / Scrum / Kanban
      each appear with the work type they serve.
- [ ] **The checklist survives intact** — all seventeen PDF sections
      reproduced in the Checklist tab (`checklist.md`), numbers preserved.
- [ ] **Credit is explicit** — References name Foster & Nerlikar's *Build
      What Matters* with the full citation.

## Non-goals

- Not a Scrum/Kanban tutorial or a tooling guide — the record chooses
  between methods, it does not teach them.
- Not the engineering-side process machinery — that lives in the
  engineering journal's [[run-engineering-processes]]; this record covers
  the product side.
- Not [[balanced-roadmap]] — that record covers what goes on the roadmap;
  this one covers the processes that produce and maintain it.
- Not [[right-team]] — that record covers who runs these processes; this
  one covers the processes themselves.
- No claim that any single framework (RICE, Scrum) is mandatory — they are
  defaults that make prioritization and delivery legible.
