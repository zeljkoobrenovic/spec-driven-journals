*The working checklist behind this principle. The Article tab carries the rationale, implications, and anti-patterns.*

## Identify the Key Customer Outcome

- [ ] Bring executives together to define the key customer outcome.
- [ ] Ask: **"How do customers know this product is delivering value?"**
- [ ] Treat each answer as a hypothesis, not a decision.
- [ ] Conduct customer research to validate or reject the hypotheses.
- [ ] Use the **5 Whys** to uncover the real customer need.
- [ ] Combine qualitative and quantitative data.
- [ ] Consider future customers, prospects, and market trends.
- [ ] Identify the final decision-maker.
- [ ] Define different outcomes for different customer segments or personas.
- [ ] Document the assumptions, options considered, and rationale.
- [ ] Get alignment from the CEO/founder and executive team.
- [ ] Create outcome pyramids from both the customer and company perspective.
- [ ] Define usage metrics that act as leading indicators of success.

## Craft the Customer Journey Vision

- [ ] Validate the customer outcome with forward-thinking customers.
- [ ] Seek external validation before relying on internal opinions.
- [ ] Gather input from customer-facing teams:
    - [ ] Sales
    - [ ] Marketing
    - [ ] Customer support
    - [ ] Account management
- [ ] Gather input from engineering leadership on feasibility, risks, and complexity.
- [ ] Review third-party sources such as app reviews, communities, industry reports, and review sites.
- [ ] Map the full customer journey:
    - [ ] Trigger
    - [ ] Discovery
    - [ ] Evaluation
    - [ ] Trial
    - [ ] Engagement
    - [ ] Retention
- [ ] Draft the journey vision before asking everyone to create separate versions.
- [ ] Share an early version with stakeholders, customers, and prospects.
- [ ] Collect feedback and revise.
- [ ] Watch for weak consensus or groupthink.
- [ ] Strengthen the vision until it is clear, valuable, realistic, and inspiring.

## Craft the Product Strategy

- [ ] Hold a cross-functional strategy session.
- [ ] Include product, design, engineering, marketing, sales, and customer success.
- [ ] Share customer research before the meeting.
- [ ] Ask participants to prepare ideas in advance.
- [ ] Make the session a discussion, not a presentation.
- [ ] Identify major disagreements or disconnects.
- [ ] Clarify whether disagreements are about:
    - [ ] Risk tolerance
    - [ ] Market direction
    - [ ] Competitive threats
    - [ ] Technical feasibility
    - [ ] Customer needs
- [ ] Agree on the decision-making process before forcing agreement on the decision.
- [ ] Treat stakeholders as advisors and contributors, not voters.
- [ ] Make the CEO or empowered product leader the final decision-maker.
- [ ] Use an offsite or neutral setting when possible.
- [ ] Give the meeting enough time for real discussion.
- [ ] Call out assumptions, risks, pivot points, and open questions.
- [ ] Define success milestones and reassessment milestones.

## Communicate the Outcome, Vision, and Strategy

- [ ] Prepare a clear story, not just a presentation.
- [ ] Practice the presentation before sharing broadly.
- [ ] Anticipate likely questions and objections.
- [ ] Use customer evidence to support the message.
- [ ] Consider letting customers "speak" through recordings, quotes, or interview clips.
- [ ] Explain the key outcome, customer journey, and strategy in plain language.
- [ ] Make the vision exciting and credible.
- [ ] Communicate that the strategy is a living document.
- [ ] Set expectations that reassessment and adjustment will happen over time.

## Maintain Ongoing Customer Validation

- [ ] Build a regular customer discovery habit.
- [ ] Continuously test assumptions in the vision and strategy.
- [ ] Use customer research before deciding what to build, not only after work is prioritized.
- [ ] Use multiple research methods:
    - [ ] Market research
    - [ ] Persona research
    - [ ] Card sorting
    - [ ] Diary studies
    - [ ] Customer advisory boards
    - [ ] Win/loss analysis
    - [ ] Surveys
    - [ ] Interviews
    - [ ] Usability studies
- [ ] Spend at least **four hours per week** on customer research when possible.
- [ ] Hire a dedicated customer research specialist once the product team is large enough.
- [ ] Regularly talk to customers even when there is no immediate project.

## Track Iteration and Product Outcomes

- [ ] Define metrics that show whether product changes are delivering value.
- [ ] Track customer behavior, not just output.
- [ ] Use quantitative feedback loops to guide iteration.
- [ ] Choose durable metrics that reflect value for multiple stakeholders.
- [ ] Run experiments or A/B tests where appropriate.
- [ ] Use results to adjust product direction.

## Reassess the Roadmap and Vision

- [ ] Reassess the roadmap and vision at the **one-third or one-quarter mark** of the total time horizon.
- [ ] For a one-year roadmap, update it quarterly.
- [ ] For a three-year vision, update it at least once a year.
- [ ] Revisit strategy after major milestones.
- [ ] Watch for market changes, competitors, regulations, or customer shifts.
- [ ] Change direction when evidence shows the current strategy is no longer correct.

## Handle Inbound Product Requests

- [ ] Create a process for managing feature requests and ideas.
- [ ] Avoid saying yes to every stakeholder request.
- [ ] Maintain an idea bank for suggestions, including ideas not on the roadmap.
- [ ] Be transparent about what is on the roadmap and what is not.
- [ ] Explain why certain ideas do not make the roadmap.
- [ ] Use a consistent prioritization framework such as **RICE**.
- [ ] Regularly review ideas, scores, and rankings.
- [ ] Share updates with stakeholders who submitted ideas.
- [ ] Ask stakeholders for their top **1–2 priorities**, not a long wish list.
- [ ] Ask why they prioritized those items.

## Collect Roadmap Input by Stakeholder Group

- [ ] Ask founders/CEO about strategic milestones, market trends, and technical stability.
- [ ] Ask finance about investment horizons, efficiency, and the trade-off between growth and profitability.
- [ ] Ask sales about customer needs, deal blockers, and prospect concerns.
- [ ] Ask marketing about market trends, pipeline growth, and automatable tasks.
- [ ] Ask customer success about retention, customer outcomes, and support needs.
- [ ] Ask engineering about technical opportunities, performance, tech debt, and scalability.
- [ ] Ask design about UX issues, customer outcomes, and workflow constraints.

## Balance the Roadmap

- [ ] Categorize roadmap items into:
    - [ ] Innovation
    - [ ] Iteration
    - [ ] Operation
- [ ] Define an allocation target for each category.
- [ ] Tag roadmap items by category.
- [ ] Check whether investment is balanced appropriately.
- [ ] Adjust allocation when one category is over- or underfunded.

## Build and Execute the Product Backlog

- [ ] Do not assume the product backlog appears automatically from strategy.
- [ ] Use vision and strategic planning to feed innovation work.
- [ ] Use customer feedback, new features, and A/B testing to feed iteration work.
- [ ] Use bugs, internal tools, and technical improvements to feed operations work.
- [ ] Prioritize each category intentionally.
- [ ] Convert roadmap priorities into sprint-ready backlog items.
- [ ] Work with engineering to plan delivery.

## Create the Roadmap Document

- [ ] Make clear which initiatives belong to innovation, iteration, or operation.
- [ ] Show allocation targets when possible.
- [ ] Paint a picture far enough into the future for other teams to plan.
- [ ] Explain the rationale behind the work.
- [ ] Focus on problems, value, and outcomes, not just features.
- [ ] Leave room for plans to change.
- [ ] Avoid false precision.
- [ ] Avoid giving every roadmap item the same level of detail.
- [ ] Avoid feature-date commitments unless there is a strong business reason.
- [ ] Avoid forcing too many items into the roadmap.

## Communicate the Roadmap Internally

- [ ] Share the roadmap with executives first.
- [ ] Present the roadmap to major groups separately:
    - [ ] Engineering, QA, and architecture
    - [ ] Sales and marketing
    - [ ] Account management, customer success, and support
    - [ ] HR, finance, and other teams
- [ ] Tailor the message to each group's needs.
- [ ] Give people space to ask questions.
- [ ] Use surveys or anonymous feedback channels after presentations.

## Communicate the Roadmap Externally Carefully

- [ ] Avoid sharing the internal roadmap with customers or sales prospects.
- [ ] Share broad themes rather than detailed commitments.
- [ ] Avoid specific dates unless absolutely necessary.
- [ ] Add buffers when discussing future plans.
- [ ] Preserve flexibility for product discovery and Agile development.
- [ ] Minimize detail and avoid overpromising.

## Choose the Right Development Process

- [ ] Use innovation sprints for bold, future-facing work.
- [ ] Consider longer cycles for innovation work when teams need space to think deeply.
- [ ] Use Scrum for iteration, experimentation, and build-measure-learn loops.
- [ ] Use Kanban for operational issues, production problems, bugs, and urgent technical work.
- [ ] Match the process to the type of work rather than forcing one method on everything.

## Assess Product Development Efficiency

- [ ] Measure progress against roadmap completion, not just story points.
- [ ] Track whether shipped work produced the intended outcome.
- [ ] Ask product managers to show evidence after releases.
- [ ] Review whether launches moved the intended metric or created the desired value.
- [ ] Share evidence with engineering so teams see the impact of their work.
- [ ] Conduct this type of outcome review at least **quarterly**.

## Create and Manage the Product Metrics Dashboard

- [ ] Create a dashboard showing key customer and company outcome metrics.
- [ ] Keep the dashboard simple.
- [ ] Track only a few meaningful metrics tied to the outcome pyramid.
- [ ] Update the dashboard daily where possible.
- [ ] Add temporary launch metrics when needed.
- [ ] Remove temporary metrics after their purpose is complete.
- [ ] Expose the dashboard internally.
- [ ] Review metrics regularly with the organization.
- [ ] Connect team efforts to business and customer outcomes.
