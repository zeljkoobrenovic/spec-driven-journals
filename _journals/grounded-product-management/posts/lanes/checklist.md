*The working checklist behind this principle — derived from John Cutler's "20 Unfiltered Operating Takes". The Article tab carries the rationale and anti-patterns.*

## Write the Lanes

- [ ] Each team has a small number of lanes — durable, outcome-oriented directions, not projects or kanban columns.
- [ ] Each lane carries a couple of metrics and a directional sense of the opportunity.
- [ ] Each lane is written in language the team can actually work with — specific enough to guide action.
- [ ] Each lane names its customer impact and the causal link to a business result.
- [ ] Lanes are expected to stay stable for months to quarters, not weeks.
- [ ] Big cross-team efforts get lanes of their own rather than being smeared across team lanes.

## Test Lane Quality

- [ ] The lane is not a hand-wavy pillar — you can name work it would rule out.
- [ ] The lane is not a bucket — it guides what to do next, not just where to file things.
- [ ] The lane would survive a product redesign — it is about the outcome, not a feature.
- [ ] The team can say what "moving the needle" on this lane looked like last week.
- [ ] The lane's inputs are actionable — the team can influence them directly (the North Star Framework lesson).
- [ ] Removing this lane would change what someone does next week — if it only changes a slide, it is deckware.

## Run the Lane Rhythm

- [ ] The team reviews its lanes weekly: what happened, what's happening, what's working, what isn't.
- [ ] Lane metrics are looked at in the review, not just reported upward.
- [ ] Each lane is revisited on a schedule — and pivoted or retired when it stops making sense.
- [ ] A lane whose metrics have gone quiet gets challenged, not carried.

## Keep Roll-Ups in Their Place

- [ ] Pyramid and pillar slides are made when leaders need them — then set aside.
- [ ] Label taxonomies are used for roll-up reporting only, never for prioritization.
- [ ] No decision is justified by pointing at a pillar; decisions point at lanes and their metrics.
- [ ] Ownership shown on alignment artifacts matches people actually working on the thing.

## Operate at Scale

- [ ] The organization admits its true lane count instead of "simplifying" it away.
- [ ] Each lane's approximate annual investment is known — and treated as a bet worth pressure-testing.
- [ ] Routines, rituals, and AI-assisted review carry the lane volume no single leader can.
- [ ] Leaders sample and read individual lanes regularly — not only the roll-up.
