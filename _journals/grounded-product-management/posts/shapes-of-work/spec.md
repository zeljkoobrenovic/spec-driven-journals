# Spec: Shapes of Work

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State that product work **defies single templates** — initiatives differ in
risk profile, experiment-friendliness, size, and blast radius, and any
serious shape exercise surfaces 5–8 distinct shapes of work, never one.
Two arguments carry the record. First: standardization schemes built for
slide decks flatten differences that matter operationally — the canonical
example is the launch-tier confusion, where marketing's external launch
tier gets used as the coordination tier, even though some work is
internally massive and externally silent (or deliberately secret); the
remedy is to look at the THING, read it, write about it explicitly, and
let people surface the nuances instead of template-filling. Second: the
same flattening instinct applied to org design produces the dark side of
**stable teams** — durable capabilities rationalized as "we'll always need
that" become kingdoms with directors and GMs stacked on products that
never really existed, and because of org-chart physics the only unwind is
layoffs that hurt people; "stable" and "durable" are useful exactly until
they aren't, and knowing the difference — before the org chart hardens —
is a leadership obligation.

## Audience

My product and engineering leaders (who approve templates, launch tiers,
and new-team requests); peer executives who hear "we'll always need that"
in headcount asks. First-person declarative.

## Success criteria

- [ ] **Principle is quotable** — highlight states that I treat shape as a
      property to be read off each piece of work (expect 5–8 shapes, never
      one template), and that I treat team durability as a claim to be
      re-earned, not a default.
- [ ] **The launch-tier example lands** — internal blast radius vs
      external launch demands distinguished; the "yeah, but we need to
      keep it simple" marketing line named as how the flattening spreads
      to executives.
- [ ] **The remedy is concrete** — look at the thing, read it, write
      about it, be explicit, surface differences; slide decks conceded as
      eventually needed but never mistaken for reality.
- [ ] **The stable-teams dark side is told honestly** — the
      kingdom-building sequence (busy people, plentiful money, new team →
      director → GM), the layoff-only unwind and its org-chart physics,
      and the leadership euphemisms ("bureaucracy", "work around the
      work") that cover for over-scaling.
- [ ] **The tension with [[team-topology]] is addressed** — durable teams
      remain the default for ownership and flow; this record adds the
      sunset test and the discipline of not stacking hierarchy on
      unproven "products".
- [ ] **Checklist is operational** — derived checklist (shape exercise,
      launch/coordination tiering, team-durability review) in the
      Checklist tab.
- [ ] **Credit is explicit** — References name John Cutler's TBM 435 as
      the grounding source.

## Non-goals

- Not an anti-template screed — light templates for genuinely uniform work
  are fine; the claim targets one-scheme-fits-all flattening.
- Not org-design mechanics — [[team-topology]] covers how teams are
  shaped and assigned; this record covers when durability claims should
  be doubted.
- Not a layoff post-mortem — layoffs appear only as the cost of ignoring
  the durability question early.
- Not estimation or sizing — [[working-small]] carries the size ladder;
  shape here is about kind, not magnitude.
