## Dysfunction Self-Assessment

Check any that apply.

- [ ] We focus more on **shipping features** than achieving customer or business outcomes.
- [ ] Success is judged mainly by **internal metrics** rather than customer value.
- [ ] Product decisions are made **without** enough **customer research**.
- [ ] We spend too much time **optimizing small details** instead of creating meaningful innovation.
- [ ] Our roadmap is **mostly a backlog** of requested features.
- [ ] Prioritization depends too heavily on **spreadsheets**, **ROI** models, or **theoretical** scoring.
- [ ] **Priorities change** too quickly, creating whiplash for the team.
- [ ] Engineering is **overbuilding** for future scenarios that may never happen.
- [ ] Product meetings become **negotiations** to keep every stakeholder happy.
- [ ] The founder, CEO, or senior leader frequently **overrides** product direction without a clear framework.

**Score:**<br>
0 checked = strong product habits<br>
1–2 checked = some warning signs<br>
3–4 checked = meaningful product dysfunction<br>
5+ checked = urgent product leadership problem

## Product-Driven Company Checklist

- [ ] We know exactly which **customer segment** we are serving.
- [ ] We build for **a broader market**, not one customer at a time.
- [ ] We understand which **customer needs** our product should meet.
- [ ] We also understand which customer requests we should **intentionally ignore**.
- [ ] **Product** and **UX** are closest to the customer.
- [ ] **Customer-facing teams** regularly share insights with product.
- [ ] Product decisions guide the priorities of **sales**, **marketing**, **engineering**, and **support**.
- [ ] We are comfortable **saying "no"** to requests that do not serve the target market.
- [ ] Our product strategy is based on **customer outcomes**, not stakeholder politics.
- [ ] We **avoid custom work** unless it benefits many customers or advances the strategy.

## Customer Feedback Checklist

For each major feedback item, ask:

- [ ] Is this feedback from our target market?
- [ ] Is this customer using the product for the purpose we designed it for?
- [ ] Would solving this help many customers, not just one?
- [ ] Would it improve retention, upsell, activation, or customer success?
- [ ] Does it support our long-term product vision?
- [ ] Is it worth the engineering and opportunity cost?
- [ ] Are we reacting to pressure, or making a strategic decision?

### Decision guide

- [ ] **Act** if it improves value for the target market.
- [ ] **Investigate** if it reveals a broader unmet need.
- [ ] **Defer** if it is useful but not aligned with current priorities.
- [ ] **Decline** if it only serves a one-off or off-market use case.

## Vision-Led Product Management Checklist

### Key Customer Outcome

- [ ] We have identified the main customer outcome we want to improve.
- [ ] We can measure whether customers are getting more value.
- [ ] The outcome matters to customers and supports business success.
- [ ] The team knows why this outcome matters.

### Customer Journey Vision

- [ ] We have mapped the customer journey from discovery to long-term retention.
- [ ] We understand where customers struggle today.
- [ ] We know what the ideal future journey should look like.
- [ ] The vision explains value from the customer's perspective, not as a feature list.

### Product Strategy

- [ ] We know the gap between today's product and the future vision.
- [ ] We have clear milestones toward the vision.
- [ ] Our roadmap connects directly to those milestones.
- [ ] We have defined what we will not do.
- [ ] Engineering, design, sales, marketing, and leadership understand the strategy.

## Roadmap Health Checklist

- [ ] Every roadmap item links to a customer outcome.
- [ ] Every roadmap item supports the target market.
- [ ] We can explain why each item matters now.
- [ ] We have removed low-value stakeholder requests.
- [ ] We are not over-prioritizing the loudest customer or executive.
- [ ] We balance short-term needs with long-term product direction.
- [ ] We give initiatives enough time to succeed before pivoting.
- [ ] We review whether shipped work actually created customer value.

## Leadership Alignment Checklist

- [ ] Leadership agrees on the target customer.
- [ ] Leadership agrees on the customer outcome we are pursuing.
- [ ] Leadership understands the trade-offs behind the roadmap.
- [ ] Product has authority to make prioritization decisions.
- [ ] Stakeholders understand why some requests are declined.
- [ ] The company has a shared product vision that inspires teams.
- [ ] Strategy is stable enough for teams to execute effectively.
- [ ] Product leadership communicates the vision repeatedly and clearly.

## Final Action Checklist

- [ ] Identify which dysfunctions are present.
- [ ] Pick the top 1–2 dysfunctions causing the most harm.
- [ ] Define the target customer segment.
- [ ] Define the key customer outcome.
- [ ] Map the customer journey.
- [ ] Create a future-state product vision.
- [ ] Build a strategy with measurable milestones.
- [ ] Rework the roadmap around customer value.
- [ ] Say no to misaligned requests.
- [ ] Review progress regularly against outcomes, not output.
