*The working checklist behind this principle. The Article tab carries the rationale, implications, and anti-patterns.*

## Start with Strategic Context

- [ ] Team design is anchored to the company's mission, objectives, and scorecard.
- [ ] Team design reflects product vision and principles.
- [ ] Team topology supports product strategy, not just org chart convenience.

## Optimize for Empowerment

- [ ] Team topology is intentionally designed, not inherited by default.
- [ ] We are optimizing for **ownership**, **autonomy**, and **alignment**.
- [ ] Teams are structured to do great work with minimal unnecessary coordination.

## Define the Right Number and Scope of Teams

- [ ] We know how many product teams we need.
- [ ] Each team has a clear scope of responsibility.
- [ ] Each team has the right mix of skills.
- [ ] Team boundaries reduce ambiguity and overlap.
- [ ] Dependencies between teams are understood and acceptable.

## Strengthen Ownership

- [ ] Each team owns something meaningful.
- [ ] Team ownership includes functionality, experience, quality, performance, and technical health, where appropriate.
- [ ] Ownership is broad enough to motivate the team.
- [ ] Ownership is not so broad that it overwhelms the team.
- [ ] Ownership is clear enough that teams know what is theirs and what is not.

## Protect Team Autonomy

- [ ] Teams are given problems to solve, not just tasks to execute.
- [ ] Teams have enough control to choose the best solution.
- [ ] Teams can perform discovery as well as delivery.
- [ ] The topology minimizes blocking dependencies.
- [ ] Teams are not forced into constant coordination for small changes.

## Improve Alignment

- [ ] Team boundaries align with business outcomes.
- [ ] Team boundaries align with the product vision.
- [ ] Team boundaries align with the architecture, where useful.
- [ ] Teams are not trapped by legacy architecture or excessive technical debt.
- [ ] Alignment reduces handoffs and speeds decision-making.

## Use the Right Team Types

- [ ] We distinguish between **platform teams** and **experience teams**.
- [ ] Platform teams provide leverage through shared services, tooling, or abstraction.
- [ ] Experience teams own user/customer-facing outcomes.
- [ ] Platform teams reduce cognitive load for experience teams.
- [ ] Experience teams have as much end-to-end ownership as possible.

## Design Effective Platform Teams

- [ ] Platform teams exist to enable other teams.
- [ ] Platform work is separated from pure "keep the lights on" work where possible.
- [ ] Platform teams have clear objectives.
- [ ] Platform teams collaborate with experience teams through shared objectives when needed.
- [ ] Internal platforms are managed like products when appropriate.

## Design Effective Experience Teams

- [ ] Experience teams are organized around customer/user outcomes.
- [ ] Teams are aligned by the most natural customer dimension, such as:
  - [ ] user type/persona
  - [ ] market segment
  - [ ] customer journey stage
  - [ ] sales channel
  - [ ] KPI/business outcome
  - [ ] geography
- [ ] Teams own meaningful end-to-end slices of the experience.
- [ ] Teams are not fragmented into tiny feature areas.

## Avoid Common Structural Traps

- [ ] Team topology is not dictated by reporting lines.
- [ ] Team topology is not dictated by engineering skill silos alone.
- [ ] We are not "shipping the org chart."
- [ ] Design is embedded in teams, not treated only as an internal service.
- [ ] Product, design, and engineering leaders shape topology together.

## Consider Proximity and Collaboration

- [ ] We have intentionally considered where teams are located.
- [ ] We optimize for the product team first.
- [ ] Product managers, designers, and engineers are co-located or closely connected when possible.
- [ ] We account for proximity to customers, business partners, and other teams.
- [ ] Remote/distributed arrangements have explicit communication support.

## Keep Topology Evolving

- [ ] We review topology as strategy, architecture, and scale change.
- [ ] We revisit topology when dependencies become painful.
- [ ] We revisit topology when teams cannot make decisions or ship simple things.
- [ ] We revisit topology when ownership is too narrow or complexity is too high.
- [ ] We try to keep intact teams together when changing responsibilities.

## Watch for Warning Signs

*Polarity flips in this section: a checked box here is a warning, not a win.*

- [ ] Developers are frequently moved between teams.
- [ ] Leaders often step in to resolve dependency conflicts.
- [ ] Teams complain about too many dependencies.
- [ ] Teams struggle to ship simple changes.
- [ ] Teams have very limited ownership.
- [ ] Teams face too much complexity across too many areas.
- [ ] Major topology changes are happening too often.

## Quick Scorecard

- [ ] Our teams have clear ownership.
- [ ] Our teams can act autonomously.
- [ ] Our teams are aligned with business and architecture.
- [ ] Our platform helps experience teams move faster.
- [ ] Our experience teams own meaningful customer outcomes.
- [ ] Our topology minimizes unnecessary dependencies.
- [ ] Our topology is reviewed and improved over time.
