*The working checklist behind this principle. The Article tab carries the rationale, implications, and anti-patterns.*

## Confirm Why the Roadmap Exists

- [ ] Connect near-term product changes to mid-term milestones and the long-term vision.
- [ ] Help marketing, sales, and other teams plan around product direction.
- [ ] Communicate sequencing of priorities.
- [ ] Create alignment around trade-offs.
- [ ] Give product leadership and PMs a shared view of top-level priorities.

## Classify Every Roadmap Item

- [ ] **Innovation**: Work that moves the product toward the vision.
- [ ] **Iteration**: Improvements to the existing product that add value for customers or businesses.
- [ ] **Operation**: Work required to run, secure, scale, and maintain the product.

## Make a Top-Down Allocation First

- [ ] Decide the percentage of investment to allocate to innovation, iteration, and operations before ranking individual projects.
- [ ] Make the trade-offs explicit to executives and stakeholders.
- [ ] Use the allocation to prevent loud stakeholders from dominating priorities.
- [ ] Compare items only within the same category, rather than ranking unlike work against each other.
- [ ] Revisit the allocation quarterly or when business context changes.

## Pick an Allocation Based on Product Lifecycle

- [ ] **Pre-alpha / idea**: Mostly innovation.
- [ ] **Post-alpha/launch**: Further iteration to address early feedback.
- [ ] **Beta / approaching product-market fit**: Heavy iteration to find PMF.
- [ ] **Scaling after PMF**: More operation to support growth.
- [ ] **Steady state / maintaining**: Balance operation with the next wave of innovation.

## Prioritize Innovation Work

- [ ] Identify the strategic milestone needed to realize the product vision.
- [ ] Prioritize work that directly advances that milestone.
- [ ] Avoid "improvements" that do not meaningfully move the product forward.
- [ ] Involve engineering early to reduce future technical debt.
- [ ] Decide whether the product is still on the growth curve or reaching a plateau.
- [ ] Start planning the next innovation before the current product flattens.

## Prioritize Iteration Work

- [ ] Focus on incremental value for customers, the business, or both.
- [ ] Use RICE or a similar scoring model.
- [ ] Score each item for **Reach**, **Impact**, **Confidence**, and **Effort**.
- [ ] Validate confidence using quantitative data, qualitative research, and competitive intelligence.
- [ ] Review the output instead of accepting scores blindly.
- [ ] Check for bias, weak strategic fit, stakeholder impact, and missed themes.

## Prioritize Operation Work

- [ ] Reserve a clear portion of roadmap capacity for operational work.
- [ ] Include SLAs, privacy requirements, security needs, COGS, tooling, maintenance, technical debt, and bug fixes.
- [ ] Stack-rank operational work separately from innovation and iteration.
- [ ] Define how bugs will be handled: zero-tolerance, SLA-based, severity-based, or quality-threshold-based.
- [ ] Document the operational prioritization process so stakeholders know what to expect.

## Manage Stakeholder Requests

- [ ] Create a clear intake process for one-off requests.
- [ ] Require requesters to understand the cost of using roadmap capacity.
- [ ] Say no to low-value requests that do not justify the investment.
- [ ] Consider a capacity/token system for sales or customer-facing commitments.
- [ ] Protect innovation and operational capacity from being consumed by reactive work.

## Review Before Finalizing the Roadmap

- [ ] Does the roadmap reflect the product vision?
- [ ] Are innovation, iteration, and operation all represented appropriately?
- [ ] Are trade-offs clear and defensible?
- [ ] Are executives aligned on the allocation?
- [ ] Are stakeholders clear on what will not be done?
- [ ] Is there a documented prioritization method for each category?
- [ ] Is the roadmap balanced for the current stage of the product?
