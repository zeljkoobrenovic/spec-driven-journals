Phishing is the front door of most incidents, and my organization defends it with **people, not just filters**. Every employee runs a short verification habit before clicking — was I expecting this, does the sender match, is it manufacturing urgency, is it asking for credentials — and verifies unusual requests through a separate trusted channel. The warning signs are taught in plain language across every medium the attack arrives on, and reporting is fast, easy, and blame-free whether or not anyone clicked. The test: the employee who clicked reports it within minutes — because they know they will be helped, not punished.

**What changes**

* **A pre-click habit becomes universal.** Expectation, sender match, urgency, and credential requests are checked on every message; unusual work requests are verified out-of-band through a known-good channel; doubt means stop and contact the help desk, not proceed carefully.
* **Warning signs are taught by feel, look, and sound.** The bounced login, the misbehaving site, the strange attachment error, the macro prompt, the familiar page on a wrong URL, the found USB drive, the caller from "IT" wanting a password — phishing is trained as a multi-channel con, not an email-only problem.
* **The after-click path is rehearsed and safe.** Stop, enter nothing more, report immediately, disclose exactly what was clicked and whether credentials went in, follow instructions for resets and device checks — with the clicked employee treated as the response's first sensor, never its culprit.
* **Near-misses become intelligence.** Suspicious messages are reported and kept even when nobody clicked — one report protects everyone who received the same campaign — and are never forwarded to coworkers, which spreads the live payload.
* **A few rules become absolute.** No unexpected links, no unexpected attachments, never give a username or password to another person, verify unusual requests, report anything that feels, looks, or sounds funny — and the help-desk, phishing-report, and after-hours contacts are filled in, published, and current.

**What it costs**

* Continuous reinforcement instead of an annual module — the habit is built by repetition through onboarding and ongoing education.
* A visible thank-you culture for reports, including false alarms and clicks — blame is the one expense this record forbids.
* A small standing overhead of out-of-band verification on unusual requests — thirty seconds that defeats attacks no filter catches.

**What we are not doing**

* Not replacing technical email defenses — filters catch the volume; this record catches what is engineered to pass them.
* Not running the post-click investigation here — a reported click hands off to [[incident-response]].
* Not owning the broader curriculum or the credential controls — those live in [[user-education]] and [[authentication]].
