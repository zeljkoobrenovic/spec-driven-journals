![Comic panel: bored employees click through a December training video as a printer piles up identical certificates under a 98 percent complete dashboard.](assets/images/user-education/comic-01-the-december-module.jpeg)
**Panel 1:** *The hook: the annual compliance module — forty-five minutes in December, a certificate, and no measurable change in behavior.*

![Comic panel: a fish-shaped phishing email reaches a grid of employee inboxes before a distant analyst with binoculars can spot it.](assets/images/user-education/comic-02-the-widest-sensor.jpeg)
**Panel 2:** *The problem framed right: the people who receive the phish see it before any analyst does — a trained workforce is a detection sensor at every mailbox.*

![Comic panel: a manager shames one employee for clicking while another hides a suspicious email and an intruder slips deeper into the systems.](assets/images/user-education/comic-03-punishing-the-click.jpeg)
**Panel 3:** *The wrong way: punish the click and the next click goes unreported — the attacker gets hours or days of quiet instead of minutes.*

![Comic panel: Vera plans with exactly two objective cards tailored to real threats while a crossed-out twelve-objective catalog sits in a bin.](assets/images/user-education/comic-04-controls-get-engineered.jpeg)
**Panel 4:** *The principle: people are a security control, and controls get engineered — one or two clear objectives, tailored to the threats we actually face, with executive backing.*

![Comic panel: Nadia paints a baseline starting line on a track and records what users know and do before a target flag ahead.](assets/images/user-education/comic-05-baseline-first.jpeg)
**Panel 5:** *The baseline comes before the program: measure what users currently know and do — otherwise the program can demonstrate activity, but never improvement.*

![Comic panel: an employee who just clicked a bad link presses a big report button and Nadia responds with a thumbs up and a stopwatch.](assets/images/user-education/comic-06-the-big-red-report-button.jpeg)
**Panel 6:** *Reporting is the easy option and never punished — even after the click — because the cheapest incident is the one reported in minutes by the person it happened to.*

![Comic panel: employees train like in a gym, hovering over links and spotting fake invoices in repeated sessions while a framed certificate gathers dust.](assets/images/user-education/comic-07-habits-not-certificates.jpeg)
**Panel 7:** *Training builds habits, not certificates: spaced repetition, realistic examples that resemble real threats, and lessons framed in each person's everyday work.*

![Comic panel: a reported phishing email travels a pipeline through report, triage, and response stations while Nadia patches a leaky joint.](assets/images/user-education/comic-08-the-exercise-tests-us-too.jpeg)
**Panel 8:** *The exercise tests us as much as them: every simulation rehearses the reporting path and response runbook — and the gaps found flow back into the procedures.*

![Comic panel: Vera and Nadia review a chart where reporting rises and clicks fall from a baseline mark, with a completion certificate in the wastebasket.](assets/images/user-education/comic-09-behavior-against-the-baseline.jpeg)
**Panel 9:** *The closer: the test the program is held to — behavior change measured against the baseline, reporting up and clicks down; completion rates appear nowhere in that conversation.*
