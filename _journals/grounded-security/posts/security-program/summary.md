A security program in my organization is designed before anything is bought — **risk before tools**. Purpose and business objectives come first, organized around a recognized framework applied with judgment; responsibility is structured into executive, risk, security, and audit functions; the baseline is documented; risk is calculated and every risk gets an explicit treatment; work is tiered into milestones; and the program rehearses. The test the program is held to: every major risk has an owner, a treatment decision, and a review date in a maintained risk register — and the program itself is periodically reassessed.

**What changes**

* **The program gets a map and named owners.** Security activities are organized around the NIST CSF functions (Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover, Govern); a CIO or CISO carries organization-wide authority and funding; risk, security operations, and independent audit are distinct responsibilities — combined honestly in small organizations, with a plan to separate.
* **The baseline is written down before it is improved.** Policies, endpoints, licenses and certificates, the internet footprint, network infrastructure, logging, ingress and egress, vendors, and critical applications are all inventoried — because nothing downstream works against an unknown estate.
* **Risk becomes arithmetic, not anxiety.** Each risk is documented as threat, vulnerability, asset, and consequence, rated likelihood × impact on a 1–5 scale, and recorded in a register with an owner, controls, actions, due dates, and residual risk.
* **Every risk gets a decision.** Avoided, remediated, transferred, or accepted — and acceptance is the heavyweight option: documented justification, a named approver, compensating controls, and an expiry date, reassessed at least annually.
* **The program rehearses.** Roughly three high-priority attack scenarios become use cases and playbooks; attacks are mapped to the Cyber Kill Chain; tabletop exercises and technical drills (backup restoration, DR, failover) run regularly with findings tracked to completion.

**What it costs**

* Standing governance work — the register, the reviews, the reporting to management and board — is permanent overhead, accepted deliberately because an unmaintained register is decoration.
* Tabletops and drills consume cross-department time from legal, HR, finance, and communications, not just security.
* Quick wins come first, which means some ambitious projects wait in tiers three and four until credibility and prerequisites exist.

**What we are not doing**

* Not selecting tools in this record — no purchase precedes a documented risk it treats.
* Not building the standing asset inventory here — the baseline is a snapshot; the program is [[asset-management]].
* Not writing the policy documents or the incident-response operating model here — those are [[policies]] and [[incident-response]].
