*The working checklist behind this record — physical access, surveillance, media, facilities, people, and the social-engineering defenses, end to end. The Article tab carries the rationale and anti-patterns.*

## 1. Physical Access Controls

- [ ] Restrict access to secure and sensitive areas
- [ ] Use appropriate controls such as locks, PIN pads, RFID/badge readers, biometrics, or security guards
- [ ] Require more than one security control for highly sensitive areas when appropriate
- [ ] Ensure employees lock their computer screens when away from their desks
- [ ] Use computer cable locks where appropriate
- [ ] Enforce a clear-desk policy
- [ ] Store sensitive documents in locked cabinets or other secure storage
- [ ] Restrict access to network jacks, telephone jacks, and other connection points
- [ ] Disable unused network jacks or require authentication before use
- [ ] Keep printers, printer trays, and printer discard boxes free of sensitive information
- [ ] Shred sensitive documents immediately or place them in locked shredding containers

## 2. Video Surveillance

- [ ] Install CCTV cameras at major entrances and exits
- [ ] Monitor sensitive locations such as server rooms and other high-risk areas
- [ ] Position cameras so they can clearly capture faces
- [ ] Position cameras to help correlate badge use with the person using the badge
- [ ] Protect cameras from tampering
- [ ] Mount cameras out of easy reach
- [ ] Use tamper-resistant enclosures where appropriate
- [ ] Review surveillance footage when investigating suspicious events
- [ ] Correlate video with badge-access logs and other evidence when possible

## 3. Authentication and Access Maintenance

- [ ] Regularly audit physical authentication controls
- [ ] Inspect keypads for worn or damaged buttons that could reveal commonly used codes
- [ ] Replace or repair security controls that expose authentication information
- [ ] Immediately recover badges, keys, and other access devices from departing employees
- [ ] Remove former employees' access permissions promptly
- [ ] Change shared door or alarm codes when authorized personnel leave
- [ ] Review access permissions whenever an employee changes roles
- [ ] Avoid relying on shared PINs for highly sensitive areas

## 4. Secure Media

- [ ] Secure USB drives, removable hard drives, CDs, and other portable media
- [ ] Restrict unauthorized access to sensitive information stored on removable media
- [ ] Store sensitive media in locked locations when not in use
- [ ] Secure backup media against loss or theft
- [ ] Periodically inspect media-storage locations for security problems
- [ ] Clearly label media according to its data classification
- [ ] Maintain procedures for distributing media to internal and external users
- [ ] Use secure couriers when transporting sensitive media
- [ ] Track the location and status of sensitive media
- [ ] Require approval before media is removed from secure areas
- [ ] Maintain records of media movement and transfers

## 5. Datacenter and Equipment Security

- [ ] Use lockable server racks and cabinets
- [ ] Keep rack keys in a secure, centrally controlled location
- [ ] Log the checkout and return of rack keys
- [ ] Prevent unauthorized access through walls, ceilings, or adjoining rooms
- [ ] Ensure sensitive-room walls extend from floor to ceiling where needed
- [ ] Evaluate access through suspended ceiling tiles and similar openings
- [ ] Secure routers, switches, domain controllers, and other equipment in remote offices
- [ ] Use locked equipment enclosures when a full server rack is impractical
- [ ] Consider both physical security and fire-safety requirements when designing protected spaces

## 6. Visitors

- [ ] Require all visitors to sign in when entering
- [ ] Require visitors to sign out when leaving
- [ ] Record when visitors entered and exited
- [ ] Record whom each visitor is meeting
- [ ] Escort visitors to and from controlled areas
- [ ] Verify a visitor's purpose before allowing access to technology or sensitive areas
- [ ] Require employee approval before visitors interact with equipment or access information

## 7. Contractors

- [ ] Clearly identify contractors while they are on-site
- [ ] Require and verify photo identification
- [ ] Define contractor access permissions in organizational policy
- [ ] Limit contractor access to areas required for their work
- [ ] Confirm that contractor vetting or background-check requirements have been completed
- [ ] Verify contractor requests before granting access to sensitive systems or locations

## 8. Visitor and Contractor Badges

- [ ] Issue badges that clearly distinguish visitors from employees and contractors
- [ ] Limit visitor badges to the duration of the visit
- [ ] Require badges to be returned when visitors sign out
- [ ] Use automatically expiring or self-voiding badges when appropriate
- [ ] Train employees to recognize legitimate badge types
- [ ] Remain aware that badges can be copied or counterfeited
- [ ] Report lost or stolen badges immediately
- [ ] Disable lost, stolen, or unreturned badges promptly

## 9. Physical Security Training

- [ ] Train employees on physical as well as digital social-engineering risks
- [ ] Teach employees not to allow unauthorized people into restricted areas
- [ ] Reinforce that politeness should not override security procedures
- [ ] Train employees to recognize suspicious behavior involving equipment, doors, badges, and media
- [ ] Regularly reinforce physical-security expectations with signs, reminders, and awareness training

## 10. Tailgating

- [ ] Require each person to authenticate individually at restricted entrances
- [ ] Do not hold secure doors open for unknown or unauthorized people
- [ ] Challenge or report people who enter restricted areas without authenticating
- [ ] Train employees to recognize tailgating and piggybacking attempts

## 11. Badge Cloning

- [ ] Educate employees that RFID and other access badges may be cloned
- [ ] Require stronger authentication for highly sensitive areas
- [ ] Periodically test badge-security controls
- [ ] Investigate suspicious or duplicated badge activity
- [ ] Combine badge access with another authentication method where risk warrants it

## 12. Malicious Removable Media

- [ ] Do not connect unknown USB drives or other removable media to company devices
- [ ] Train employees to recognize malicious-media social-engineering tactics
- [ ] Treat promotional or unexpectedly received USB devices as untrusted
- [ ] Be suspicious of removable media intentionally left in public or employee areas
- [ ] Restrict or disable removable-media ports where practical
- [ ] Use endpoint controls to manage removable-media access when available

## 13. Restricted Access and Pretexting

- [ ] Verify the identity of repair personnel, delivery staff, vendors, and other service workers
- [ ] Confirm that requested work is expected and authorized
- [ ] Verify unusual requests with a manager or responsible department
- [ ] Do not allow someone into a restricted area solely because they appear knowledgeable or appropriately dressed
- [ ] Verify unexpected devices before connecting or installing them
- [ ] Confirm that replacement equipment comes from a trusted source
- [ ] Be cautious of fake work orders, shipping labels, return instructions, and similar pretexts

## 14. Ongoing Physical Security Management

- [ ] Coordinate physical-security activities between information security, facilities, and other responsible departments
- [ ] Include physical security in internal assessments and penetration tests
- [ ] Periodically review physical-security policies and procedures
- [ ] Identify new physical threats and vulnerabilities
- [ ] Correct identified security gaps in a timely manner
- [ ] Apply defense-in-depth instead of relying on a single physical-security control

With the ongoing-management section running as a program — coordinated, tested, and reviewed — the estate is held to the test that runs through the whole checklist: **no single failed control is enough to reach sensitive systems or data.**
