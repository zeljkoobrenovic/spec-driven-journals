*The working checklist behind this record — writing, governing, and maintaining the security policy set. The Article tab carries the rationale and anti-patterns.*

## Policy Foundation

- [ ] Define why the policy is needed and what security objective it supports
- [ ] Ensure the policy promotes consistent behavior across the organization
- [ ] Make the policy accessible so employees can understand applicable rules
- [ ] Clearly define expectations, boundaries, and prohibited behavior
- [ ] Confirm the policy supports applicable regulatory, compliance, and audit requirements
- [ ] Ensure the policy reflects the organization's overall security posture
- [ ] Obtain appropriate management endorsement

## Policy Language

- [ ] Write clear, simple, and concise policy statements
- [ ] Focus policies on **what must be achieved**, leaving detailed implementation steps to procedures or standards
- [ ] Use strong terms such as **must**, **will**, **shall**, and **do** when requirements are mandatory
- [ ] Avoid ambiguous terms such as **should**, **try**, and **mostly** for mandatory requirements
- [ ] Clearly document any permitted exceptions
- [ ] Avoid unnecessary legalese or excessively wordy statements
- [ ] Use short paragraphs or bullet points where appropriate

## Required Document Contents

- [ ] Include a version number
- [ ] Include an effective date
- [ ] Include a revision history or summary of changes
- [ ] Identify the policy owner
- [ ] Identify the policy approver
- [ ] Define roles and responsibilities for implementation, monitoring, compliance, and updates
- [ ] Include executive sign-off where required
- [ ] Provide a brief purpose or overview
- [ ] Clearly define the scope and who or what the policy applies to
- [ ] Include the actual policy statements and requirements
- [ ] Reference related policies, standards, procedures, processes, and forms
- [ ] Reference relevant regulatory, legal, or compliance requirements where appropriate
- [ ] Use consistent document and artifact naming conventions

## Policy Coverage

- [ ] Acceptable use
- [ ] Access control and remote access
- [ ] Password construction and protection
- [ ] Encryption and key protection
- [ ] Email and communications
- [ ] Internet usage
- [ ] Mobile devices
- [ ] Removable media
- [ ] Wireless communications
- [ ] Software installation
- [ ] Workstation security
- [ ] Server security and auditing
- [ ] Router, switch, and network security
- [ ] Malware and antivirus protection
- [ ] Web application security
- [ ] Incident response and recovery
- [ ] Data breach response
- [ ] Disaster recovery
- [ ] Risk assessment
- [ ] Security awareness and social engineering
- [ ] Data/database credentials
- [ ] Logging
- [ ] Equipment acquisition and disposal
- [ ] Employee and third-party responsibilities
- [ ] Ethics and intellectual property
- [ ] Any industry-specific or regulatory policies required by the organization

## Policy Management

- [ ] Create separate, manageable policy documents rather than one large document where practical
- [ ] Align policies with an appropriate security framework or regulatory regime
- [ ] Store policies in a central and easily accessible location
- [ ] Use revision control so the current approved version is identifiable
- [ ] Establish a formal review and approval process
- [ ] Communicate new and revised policies to affected personnel
- [ ] Keep backup copies of policies needed during outages or disaster recovery
- [ ] Maintain physical copies of critical policies when digital access may be unavailable

## Review and Maintenance

- [ ] Treat policies as living documents
- [ ] Review policies at least annually or according to the organization's review schedule
- [ ] Review policies after significant business, technology, regulatory, or security changes
- [ ] Update outdated requirements and references
- [ ] Record what changed during each revision
- [ ] Obtain required approval for revised policies
- [ ] Verify policies remain aligned with current business objectives and the operating environment
- [ ] Remove or archive obsolete versions so users do not follow outdated requirements

With every section green, the policy set is governed, findable, and alive — the mandate layer that [[standards-and-procedures]] turns into technical bars and steps. The test running through the whole sequence: **can any employee find the current approved version of the policy that applies to them and understand what it requires — and is every obsolete version out of reach?**
