Why a policy states what must be achieved — and why "should" is not policy language — in nine panels.

![Comic panel: Nadia hauls a giant dusty tome labeled Security Policy while a frightened employee peeks from behind a desk.](assets/images/policies/comic-01-the-90-page-omnibus.jpeg)
**Panel 1:** *The hook: the 90-page omnibus — one giant policy nobody reads, nobody can revise without a committee, and nobody can map to a real question.*

![Comic panel: an employee stands at a crossroads with blank signposts while coworkers offer conflicting ghost versions of the rule.](assets/images/policies/comic-02-a-policy-nobody-reads.jpeg)
**Panel 2:** *The problem: a policy nobody can find or understand binds nobody — the gap gets decided ad hoc by whoever faces the question first, differently each time.*

![Comic panel: the word Should on a policy page opens like a gap in a fence and an employee with a deadline slips through it.](assets/images/policies/comic-03-the-should-loophole.jpeg)
**Panel 3:** *The wrong way: 'should' is a loophole spelled politely — every one is a decision delegated to whoever reads it under pressure, and pressure argues for the convenient reading.*

![Comic panel: Vera holds a stone tablet reading What Must Be Achieved while Nadia shelves Standards and Procedures binders by a revolving door.](assets/images/policies/comic-04-what-not-how.jpeg)
**Panel 4:** *The principle: policy states the what — durable for a decade; the how lives in standards and procedures, revised every year without touching the mandate.*

![Comic panel: Nadia builds a wall from bricks labeled Must, Will, Shall, Do and bins sponge bricks labeled Should, Try, Mostly; a signed Exception card hangs on a hook.](assets/images/policies/comic-05-must-will-shall-do.jpeg)
**Panel 5:** *The language is mandatory or it is not policy: must, will, shall, and do — and permitted exceptions are documented, because a written exception is a decision and an unwritten one is erosion.*

![Comic panel: two employees wave conflicting policy printouts while Vera points at a header block with version, date, owner, and approver fields.](assets/images/policies/comic-06-the-version-block.jpeg)
**Panel 6:** *Every policy is a governed document — version, effective date, owner, approver, endorsement. When two versions circulate, the version block is the whole argument.*

![Comic panel: a shelf of slim policy booklets with one empty glowing slot through which a USB stick character sneaks in.](assets/images/policies/comic-07-gaps-are-permissions.jpeg)
**Panel 7:** *Coverage is deliberate, not accidental: walk the full list and decide each one — because whatever has no policy is, in practice, allowed.*

![Comic panel: during an outage with dead monitors, Nadia retrieves a printed Incident Response Policy binder from a break-glass wall cabinet.](assets/images/policies/comic-08-the-paper-copy.jpeg)
**Panel 8:** *Managed as a set: central storage under revision control — and paper copies of critical policies, because the response policy locked inside the crashed system is a punchline, not a control.*

![Comic panel: an employee finds the current approved policy in one search while Vera locks an obsolete version into an archive drawer.](assets/images/policies/comic-09-the-findability-test.jpeg)
**Panel 9:** *The closer: the test the set is held to — any employee can find the current approved policy that applies to them, and nobody can find an obsolete one.*
