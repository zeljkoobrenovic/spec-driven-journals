> **IN THIS PART, YOU WILL:** Design the shared platform underneath the listing marketplace: listing events, data management and read models, web and SEO architecture, the data platform, experimentation, machine learning, multi-country tenancy, and reliability, performance and cost.

> **WHY THE BUSINESS CARES:** A sold car that still gets alerts, dead pages in Google's index, a price test with the wrong sign and a search outage at the peak all cost contacts. Users see each as a product failure.

> **WHY ARCHITECTS SHOULD CARE:** One listing write has ten or twenty readers, and every brand and country wants its own variant. The platform decides whether those readers agree, whether a market can change without a fork, and whether a number or a model can be trusted.

> **KEY POINTS:**
>
> * **The platform is a set of published contracts, not a set of products.** One owned listing event stream, a register of every copy with its staleness budget (allowed delay) and rebuild path, and governed metrics let many teams build on shared data without coupling to each other's internals.
> * **Two-sided markets break the defaults.** Buyers and sellers affect each other, so experiments need interference-aware designs (ones that allow for that spillover) and guardrails for the side not being tested, and models need governance by the decision they make, not by their algorithm.
> * **Share beneath the product, and measure by journey.** Make the tenant (one brand in one country) first-class and market differences a matter of configuration, with forks allowed only when they carry an expiry date. Measure reliability as journey objectives with error budgets (the failure each journey may spend), and cost per search, per live listing and per contact.

> **[FAILURE PATTERNS THIS PART ADDRESSES](where-listing-systems-go-wrong.html):**
>
> * **Search as System of Record** — Makes the search index one registered read model (a copy) that can be rebuilt from the event stream.
> * **The Copy-Paste Country** — Replaces copied codebases with a tenancy model, configuration over code and owned extension points (where a market adds its own code).
> * **Set-and-Forget AI** — Gives every production model an owner, a decision tier (what it may decide), a prediction log and monitoring.

<br>
Parts II to VI designed what a marketplace does: model listings, take supply in, match it to demand, keep it safe and make money from it. Part VII turns to **the distributed system that all of those capabilities stand on**. Most groups never designed this layer; it grew from polling jobs, nightly dumps and copied country codebases.

This part argues that **a shared platform earns trust through contracts, not mandates**. Events, copies, URLs, metrics, experiments, models, tenants and service levels each need a written promise, an owner and a check that it is kept. Then the brands above can move at their own pace without breaking each other.

Tenancy decides how all of these capabilities are shared; reliability, spanning every user journey, comes last.

## The Veldmark thread

Veldmark, the fictional group this book follows (see [[meet-veldmark]]), starts 2027 with four messaging technologies, three data warehouses, 146 production models on four training stacks and a PHP monolith full of country branches. On Sunday 17 January 2027 a TV campaign and a scheduled reindex browned out Tradeyard NL search for 48 minutes; no peak calendar (a shared schedule of campaigns and heavy jobs) had flagged the clash. Ingrid Halvorsen's team, with Sven Lindqvist, Priya Raman and Yara Mendes, decides one contract per chapter, listed in chapter order, each with its date:

- *Events:* **Listing event contract v1** on Kafka (Confluent Cloud), live on 1 July 2027;
- *Copies:* a **group retention schedule** and the consumer registry (the register of copies) extended to every listing copy (October 2027);
- *Web:* **server-rendered pages and event-driven sitemaps** for Tradeyard's 40 million indexed URLs (May 2027);
- *Data:* an **Iceberg-based data platform** with tracking plan v1 (September 2027);
- *Experiments:* **Experimentation standard v1** (30 June 2027), after a bump-price test that looked like a win;
- *Models:* a **group model registry** mandatory for every production model (9 November 2027);
- *Tenancy:* **Tenancy model v1**: ten brand × country tenants (9 June 2027);
- *Reliability:* **journey service level objectives** with error budgets and a search degradation ladder, steps that shed load before search fails (April 2027); they answer the brown-out.

## Where to begin

Together, the chapters put each contract under a check, which polling jobs and dumps never had. By the end of Part VII you can say, for any listing fact, where it is true, which copies hold it and how you would rebuild or erase them. Begin with [[event-driven-listings]].
