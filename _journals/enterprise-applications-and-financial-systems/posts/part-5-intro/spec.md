# Spec: PART V — LEARN: Lessons From Public Case Studies

## Intent

Orient the reader to Part V: why the book turns from its fictional running example to real, publicly documented platforms, and how to read them. Distinguish evidence from illustration, name the limits of public engineering writing (survivorship, selective disclosure, dates), set out the three-way reading discipline (documented / interpretation / not public), introduce the current case and present the part as a collection that grows with the living journal.

## Audience

Chief Architects, finance-systems and integration architects, and finance-transformation leaders who use public engineering writing as input to their own designs.

## Success criteria

- [ ] Opens with the IN THIS PART and FAILURE PATTERNS callouts; patterns use exact names, are linked to [[where-finance-architecture-goes-wrong]] and are ordered by strength of connection to chapter 44: The Shadow Ledger, The Brand Exception, Point-to-Point Spaghetti.
- [ ] Explains why real cases after a fictional one: evidence versus illustration, and what a public engineering blog can and cannot prove (survivorship, selective disclosure, dates).
- [ ] States the documented / interpretation / not public discipline, with a small mermaid evidence-boundary diagram and caption.
- [ ] One paragraph on [[case-uber-payments]] drawn from its KEY POINTS.
- [ ] Presents the part as a growing collection without promising specific companies.
- [ ] Explains how to use the cases with the earlier chapters and the [[toolkit]]; closes by pointing to [[case-uber-payments]].
- [ ] 450–700 words of body.

## Non-goals

Re-teaching the Uber case or the ledger, payments and reconciliation patterns (the chapters own them); naming future cases; making any new factual claim about Uber beyond chapter 44.

## Modalities

Article only.
