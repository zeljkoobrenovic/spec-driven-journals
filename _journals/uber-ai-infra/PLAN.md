# RETROFITTED — Book Plan and Chapter Contracts

**RETROFITTED: How Uber Built AI-Native Engineering on a Decade of Developer Platforms**

*A case-study journal of Uber's internal AI developer infrastructure — the model and tool gateways, the harness layer, skills and context graph, background agents, self-verification, review and maintenance loops — read as durable engineering lessons for every company that is not Uber.*

**Status:** first edition in progress · **Created:** 2026-10-20

## Positioning

The starting point is the Pragmatic Engineer podcast episode **"Uber's AI infra, from the inside"** (Gergely Orosz with Uday Kiran Medisetty, Uber Distinguished Engineer, published 14 October 2026; input in `_input/pragmatic-engineer-podcast/`). The episode tours close to two dozen internal systems. The book widens that tour with Uber's public engineering record (blog posts, papers, open-source projects, talks) and the industry and research context, verified in `_research/brief-*.md`.

The central idea: **the harness is the easy part.** Uber's AI tooling looks as if it was built in a year, but nearly every AI system stands on a pre-AI developer-platform investment: Minion on DevPods (2017–), the MCP Gateway on the Thrift/Protobuf IDL registry, Slate routing on Jaeger trace baggage, Capri on standardized language frameworks, Shepherd campaigns on the monorepos. *"All the things that we built to make life easier for engineers: it makes it easier for our agents as well."* The book's job is to make that mapping explicit, explain each mechanism, and turn it into guidance for organizations that have to build the foundations and the AI layer at the same time.

The reader is an engineering leader, platform or developer-experience lead, staff or principal engineer, or CTO deciding what AI developer infrastructure to build. The book does not explain what an API gateway, a monorepo or a coding agent is; it explains why Uber built each piece, how it works as far as the public record shows, what it depends on, and what a smaller company can copy.

The book is **evidence-labeled**. Most facts about Uber's AI systems come from one source — the podcast — and are marked as Uber's own account. Older systems are cross-checked against Uber's engineering blog and papers with publication years. Inferences about internals are labeled. Recommendations are the author's.

### Part I — FOUNDATIONS: The Decade Before AI

**01-build-what-does-not-exist → `build-what-does-not-exist`** — *Build What the Market Cannot Yet Sell: Uber's Pre-AI Platform Bets.* Q: Why did Uber build so much in-house, and what did those systems leave behind? Hypergrowth and the absence of vendors at Uber scale; uOwn (ownership), M3/M3DB (≈500M metrics/s), Cadence (durable workflows; Temporal lineage), Morpheus/XP (experiments, switchbacks, multi-sided marketplaces), Michelangelo (ML platform), incident tooling, design-doc tooling; the build→open-source→buy lifecycle; **the city as a first-class primitive** (city-sharded services, region pinning and failover, logical deployment groups). The lesson: proprietary metadata (ownership, experiments, cities) is what later becomes agent context. Owns: city as primitive, build/open-source/buy lifecycle. Briefs: pre, pod.

**02-monorepos-and-one-build → `monorepos-and-one-build`** — *From Two Services to One Build Command: Microservices, Monorepos and Bazel.* Q: How did Uber go from microservice sprawl to standardized builds, and why does that matter for agents? 2 services → thousands → DOMA and consolidation (similar count to 2019, business ~10×); micro-repos with several build/test systems; per-language monorepos (Go, Java, iOS, Android) 2017–18; Bazel; SubmitQueue and keeping main green; Go/Java standardization; one way to build and test any module → top-level AGENTS.md/CLAUDE.md that work out of the box. Owns: per-language monorepo, standard build contract. Briefs: pre, agents, pod.

**03-devpods → `devpods`** — *DevPods: Cloud Workspaces Built for Engineers, Ready for Agents.* Q: What does a cloud development environment give agents that a laptop cannot? Origin (2017; monorepo scale made laptops impractical); flavors per monorepo (Go, Java, Android, web, ML, base); warm Kubernetes pools, pre-built images, build caches, seconds to start; build/test SLAs; AI tools pre-installed (non-engineers too); the **mega DevPod** with all repositories for cross-repo agents; Minion's flavor. Comparisons: GitHub Codespaces, Gitpod/Ona, Stripe devboxes. Owns: DevPod, mega DevPod, flavor. Briefs: pre, pod, ind.

**04-contracts-and-context-propagation → `contracts-and-context-propagation`** — *The Hidden Foundations: Typed Contracts, Ownership Metadata and Trace Baggage.* Q: Which invisible pieces of infrastructure turned out to be load-bearing for AI? Thrift→Protobuf IDLs in one registry; uOwn ownership graph; Jaeger tracing (2017–18, open source) and baggage propagated across the call graph and understood by the service mesh; standardized language frameworks (one middleware hooks every service); Edge Gateway (successor to RT-API; REST, gRPC, webhooks, GraphQL; required vs optional dependency tags). Each as the enabler of a later AI system (MCP Gateway, context graph, Slate, Capri). Owns: IDL registry, trace baggage routing, standardized language frameworks, Edge Gateway. Briefs: pre, test, pod.

### Part II — GATEWAYS: Agnostic Building Blocks

**05-model-gateway → `model-gateway`** — *One Door for Every Model: The Model Gateway and AI Guard.* Q: Why route every model request in the company through one gateway? Late-2023 decision: model- and harness-agnostic building blocks; built as a GenAI product on Michelangelo; OpenAI- and Anthropic-compatible APIs; project ID + caller token; closed and open-weight models; AI Guard (security-team middleware; ~five fine-tuned models on every request and response under strict latency guardrails); per-user and per-project attribution; audit logs, session traces, cache-hit tracking; the base for routing. Uber's 2024 GenAI Gateway blog as the documented predecessor. Trade-offs: gateway latency and a single point of failure vs. consistent policy. Owns: Model Gateway, AI Guard, model-agnostic principle. Briefs: ai, ind, pod.

**06-aifx-harness-layer → `aifx-harness-layer`** — *AIFX: Making the Coding Harness Swappable.* Q: How do you let engineers switch between Claude Code, Codex and OpenCode without losing their setup? AIFX (AI Foundations) wrapper; personas with default plugins and skills; centrally managed settings plus synced user settings across laptop and DevPods; plugin install updates every harness; status line with tips and spend; `aifx validate` (detail owned by inner-loop chapter). Harness churn as a design assumption. Owns: AIFX, persona, harness-agnostic principle. Briefs: ai, pod, ind.

**07-mcp-gateway → `mcp-gateway`** — *The MCP Gateway: Every Internal Service One Tool Call Away.* Q: How did Uber expose thousands of services and vendor tools to agents? April 2025 start (after Cursor and MCP); built by the API/Edge Gateway team; MCP servers auto-generated from the Thrift/Protobuf IDL registry; request/response schemas and LLM-optimized descriptions (some tools were 3,000–4,000 tokens); custom MCPs proxied; vendor tools (Google Workspace, Slack, Jira, Linear) through the same gateway; **MCPs projected as CLIs** for token efficiency; permissions carried forward. Security: MCP risks (tool poisoning, confused deputy) and how a gateway helps. Owns: MCP Gateway, CLI projection. Briefs: ai, ind, pod.

**08-skills-monorepo → `skills-monorepo`** — *Skills as a Product: The Agent Skills Monorepo.* Q: Why is a skills repository harder than a folder of markdown files? Core platform skills + team skills in one monorepo; portable across harnesses via AIFX; one quality gate; auto-installed marketplace; ~30,000 skill executions/day; the mass rewrite to the CLI pattern (context load of always-on MCPs); skill tips; trace-driven improvement patches and evals in flight; V0 is easy, good is hard. Owns: skills monorepo, skill quality loop. Briefs: ai, ind, pod.

**09-context-graph → `context-graph`** — *The Context Graph and Cortana: Grounding Agents in the Company.* Q: How do you cut the number of turns an agent needs to get grounded? Cortana (Dec 2025; Slack/web/CLI; question answering across all repositories); trace analysis showing turns spent on grounding across siloed systems; the context graph: 100+ node types, 130+ edge types, live (people, assets, PRs, design docs, features, on-call events, config changes, incidents, tables, queries); built with the production graph platform team; agents navigate with Cypher-like queries the way they navigate files with bash; the cash-trips query example; permission preservation and break-glass; 360° service health report. Quality up, cost and latency down (claimed, early). Comparisons: knowledge graphs for RAG, Backstage catalog. Owns: context graph, grounding turns. Briefs: ai, ind, pod.

**10-token-spend-governance → `token-spend-governance`** — *Spend Tiers, Not Spend Caps: Governing Token Costs.* Q: How do you keep AI spend responsible without rationing a productivity tool? Spend tiers per user; manager approval to move up (minimal paperwork, ROI conversation); separate tiers for interactive harnesses and background agents; visibility in every tool and the status line; CostCheck plugin with personalized tips; model sizing and context hygiene; ~50% token-cost reduction over a few months (as reported); FinOps for AI context. Owns: spend tier, CostCheck. Briefs: ai, ind, pod.

### Part III — LIFECYCLE: The Feature Path, Rewired

**11-plans-as-source-of-truth → `plans-as-source-of-truth`** — *Plans Before Prompts: PRDs, ERDs and the Design-Doc Hub.* Q: Where does the spec for agent work live? One place for product and engineering design docs, launch, experiment and production-readiness reviews; status, approvals, owners; links to Jira/Linear; fed into the context graph; agentic skills for writing and reviewing docs; why planning matters more with product and country heterogeneity and physical-world operations; specs as the hand-off to Minion. Owns: design-doc hub, spec as hand-off. Briefs: agents, pod, ind.

**12-minion → `minion`** — *Minion: Background Coding Agents on the Mega DevPod.* Q: What does it take for 15% of production PRs to come from a background agent? Minion (2025; v2 now); web and Slack entry points, plan mode, one-shot prompts, steering in Slack threads ("@Minion follow this thread"), on-call bug fixes in support channels; mega DevPod (all repos, cross-repo PRs); started with maintenance, expanding as self-validation tools are added; separate spend tier. Comparisons: Stripe Minions, Spotify Honk, GitHub Copilot coding agent, Codex cloud. Owns: Minion, background agent. Briefs: agents, ind, pod.

**13-inner-loop-and-self-healing-ci → `inner-loop-and-self-healing-ci`** — *Shift Left or Drown: Inner-Loop Validation and Self-Healing CI.* Q: What happens to CI when PR volume doubles? PR volume up; P50/P90 cycle times inner and outer loop; CI jobs more than doubled in 12 months → internal "code red"; simple CI jobs (lint, coverage, build rules, template rules) moved into `aifx validate`; visual validation (emulators, web, staging); self-healing CI per PR or all PRs; capacity foundations; Sunday scheduling of Fox load. Owns: inner loop vs outer loop, `aifx validate`, self-healing CI, CI code red. Briefs: agents, pre, pod.

**14-slate → `slate`** — *Slate: Letting Agents Test Their Own Changes End to End.* Q: How can an agent verify a change across thousands of services safely? Slate: short-lived personal staging instances deploying a branch; pinned test-account UUIDs; routing through Jaeger baggage and the service mesh; multi-service tests; expiry; predecessors Deputy and Cerberus; agents create and deploy Slate environments via MCP/CLI and skills; agent-browser A/B (main vs branch) with before/after reports, screenshots and videos attached to PRs; iOS, Android, web, backend. Comparisons: Lyft staging overrides, Signadot. Owns: Slate, test-account pinning, agent self-verification. Briefs: test, ind, pod.

**15-capri → `capri`** — *Capri: Capture, Replay and the Combinatorial Test Problem.* Q: How do you test a change against every live combination of product, city and payment type? Hundreds of rider-app screens × business lines × cities; capture middleware in standardized frameworks, inbound and downstream request/responses; dimension-tagging rules; the live combination set; MatchGuard pre-deploy validation in CI/CD; replaying a single session on a Slate env; agents in a fix-and-replay loop; Edge Gateway's capture-replay; data-privacy questions (inference, labeled). Owns: Capri, MatchGuard, dimension tagging. Briefs: test, ind, pod.

**16-code-review-signals → `code-review-signals`** — *Code Inbox: Spending Human Review Where the Risk Is.* Q: If every PR still needs a human, how do you keep review from becoming the bottleneck? Review as the acknowledged bottleneck; Code Inbox (needs action, participating, mergeable); by review time: AI review done, screenshots/videos, build checks, risk score, blast radius; all PRs human-reviewed because incidents are expensive; "green lane" auto-landing for low-risk classes once risk scoring is backtested on years of data (P1) vs signals first (P0); uReview as the documented AI reviewer. Comparisons: Google modern code review, DORA 2025. Owns: Code Inbox, risk score, green lane. Briefs: agents, ind, pod.

**17-shepherd-and-fox → `shepherd-and-fox`** — *Shepherd and Fox: From Migration Campaigns to Self-Improving Codebases.* Q: Who upgrades a library across a monorepo, and who keeps code healthy every week? Shepherd campaigns: OpenRewrite policies for deterministic changes, agentic prompts and skills for non-deterministic ones (e.g. context propagation), per-module PRs routed to owners, YAML campaign files (JUnit 4→5), campaign owner tracking; Fox: services enroll in skills (dead code cleanup, performance, noisy logs), weekly PR on Sunday low-CI-load, opt-in → opt-out, maintenance-PR analysis to create new skills; Piranha as documented predecessor. Comparisons: Google LSCs/Rosie, Spotify Fleet Management. Owns: Shepherd, Fox, campaign, managed agentic loop. Briefs: agents, pre, ind, pod.

**18-wisdom-and-debug-assist → `wisdom-and-debug-assist`** — *From Bug Report to Fix: Wisdom, DebugAssist and the Closed Loop.* Q: How short can the path from a dogfooding bug to a merged fix get? Employee dogfooding of beta apps; in-app bug reports with logs, analytics, network requests, feature flags; AI routing to owning team; repro steps, expected vs observed; network-response replay on a test device by bug UID; DebugAssist managed agent (root cause, impact, evidence timeline); hand-off to Minion; PR attached to Jira with the RCA; corrections as training signal for DebugAssist and Minion. Owns: Wisdom, DebugAssist. Briefs: test, agents, pod.

### Part IV — PRACTICE: Measuring and Leading

**19-feature-velocity → `feature-velocity`** — *From Diff Velocity to Feature Velocity: Measuring Productivity When PRs Are Cheap.* Q: What should you measure when agents can open hundreds of PRs? Pre-AI metrics (diff throughput, DevEx surveys, NPS, incidents per diff, coverage); why diff counts break; PR tagging by type (feature, bugfix, maintenance, testing, docs) and complexity; feature PR share up, maintenance share down; P50 feature time by complexity per half, reconstructed with AI clustering across PRD→ERD→task traces; finding the bottleneck phase (PRD, launch, rollout); capability overhang. Comparisons: SPACE, DevEx, DX Core 4, METR. Owns: feature velocity, PR type tagging. Briefs: agents, ind, pod.

**20-the-engineer-as-context-builder → `the-engineer-as-context-builder`** — *The Engineer as Context Builder: Specs, Parallel Sessions and Verification.* Q: How has the engineer's job changed at Uber? Hands-on Distinguished Engineers making more PRs than ever; context engineering (what context would an agent need; set up the environment); specs and constraints; multitasking across sessions (scope, track, verify); verification and spotting code smells; the meta-shift from building software to building systems that build and verify software; the customer-support policy example (runbook → agent policy, synthetic evals; weeks → much less); risks: review fatigue, deskilling. Owns: context engineering (book-level), the meta-shift. Briefs: pod, ind.

**21-the-retrofit-playbook → `the-retrofit-playbook`** — *The Retrofit Playbook: What to Copy If You Are Not Uber.* Q: In what order should a 200- or 2,000-engineer company build this? Sequenced playbook by company size; minimum viable versions of each system (buy vs build, open-source equivalents); readiness assessment against the AI-ready properties; anti-patterns (harness lock-in, MCP sprawl, unmeasured spend, review by volume, agents without verification); a 12-month roadmap; when not to copy Uber. Owns: retrofit playbook, readiness assessment. Briefs: all.
