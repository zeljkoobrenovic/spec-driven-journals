# PE Technology Capability

Domain ID: `pe-tech-capability`. Group: `vortexcp`. Authored: 11 September 2026.
Primary design source: [INPUT.md](INPUT.md), preserved as supplied.

## Product and boundary

**Technology Value Creation Platform** is the proposed product: a repeatable system
that helps the fund make better technology investment decisions and helps portfolio
companies turn technology into measurable enterprise value. Its principle is
**standardize the questions, not the answers**. Technology choices are judged against
the investment thesis, customer needs, economics, and constraints.

The capability is delivered through people, decision artifacts, reusable accelerators,
and supporting workspaces. It is an internal operating product, with no assumed
commercial SaaS subscription or separate portfolio consulting revenue. The Technology
Principal is its proposed product leader: understanding users, prioritizing the
backlog, testing approaches, measuring outcomes, and building repeatable capability.
This is a role design, not a claim about a named person's position or promotion.

Core scope spans technology market sensing, thesis-led diligence, investment committee
advice, the first 100 days, CTO partnership, targeted interventions, portfolio
intelligence, expert support, acquisition integration, and exit readiness.
The adjacent `venture-capital-and-private-equity` domain owns the broader investment
partnership, fundraising, transaction execution, valuation, and investor cash flows.
Here, those activities consume technology decisions and approved value evidence.

Portfolio companies retain authority for roadmaps, architecture, hiring, budgets,
and production operations. Specialists, candidates, nearshore partners, and company
product/engineering teams participate as delivery partners. Excluded: central IT for
the fund, mandatory stacks, running portfolio products, automated investment approval,
company valuation or return promises, and universal access to company databases.

## Customers and value exchange

| Customer from the input | Progress the product supports |
|---|---|
| Investment Partner | Identify technology advantages and test a defensible investment thesis |
| Deal Team | Resolve material risks and opportunities within the deal timetable |
| Investment Committee | Understand technology choices, investment needs, uncertainty, and decision conditions |
| Portfolio CEO | Prioritize a few technology moves that improve company outcomes |
| CTO/CPTO | Make hard decisions, unblock execution, and strengthen leadership with trusted support |
| Board | Challenge outcome progress and decide when intervention is needed |
| Exit Team | Give buyers a coherent, evidenced technology story without restarting diligence |
| LP | Assess repeatable operating capability from approved, qualified value-creation evidence |

The manager sponsors the capability; participating companies contribute leadership
time and agreed evidence. Who funds specialist invoices and implementation remains
an open commercial decision. Company revenue, EBITDA, risk exposure, and acquisition
benefits remain distinct from manager income and LP returns. Attributed contribution
requires finance review, baseline and period definitions, costs, confidence, and
alternative explanations. Estimated risk reduction is not realized EBITDA.

## Lifecycle and product architecture

Customer work follows **Discover → Diagnose → Decide → Accelerate → Scale → Exit**.
The input's product architecture provides six complementary capability areas:

| Area | Repeatable outputs |
|---|---|
| Invest | Technology Market Lens, Technology Investment Assessment, one-page Technology Investment Thesis, IC recommendation |
| Align | Technology 100-Day Plan with three to five priorities, company owners, baselines, funding decisions, and outcome tests |
| Accelerate | CTO Partnership and bounded AI, engineering, product, leadership, nearshore, cloud, security, and hiring interventions |
| Learn | Vortex Portfolio Technology Intelligence, reusable playbooks, CTO community, and expert network |
| Scale | Buy-and-build integration, platform strategy, and organization design |
| Exit | Technology Exit Readiness, evolving technology narrative, and buyer diligence evidence |

Learning and exit evidence run throughout ownership. Diligence findings retain IDs
through the thesis, priorities, interventions, outcomes, and exit claims. New evidence
can invalidate a decision; revisions retain the original rationale. Handoff is complete
when the receiving company owner accepts the priority and its outcome definition.

The assessment uses seven dimensions: **Product, Architecture, Engineering, Data & AI,
Security & resilience, Organization, Economics**. Each records its thesis question,
evidence, confidence, enabling strengths, constraints, required investment, and next
decision. Missing evidence is not a favorable score; architecture age is not a verdict.

The one-page thesis records technology advantage, main constraint, value-creation
opportunity, leadership, AI opportunity/threat, required investment and timing,
expected business outcome, and exit story. The input's €1.2m over 24 months and 60%
onboarding reduction are illustrative examples, not baselines or commitments.

The 100-day plan selects three to five important moves with finding links, a company
owner, budget decision, and measurable acceptance outcome. Other findings stay in a
visible backlog; material risks still have an escalation path.

The proposed monthly CTO Partnership asks what changed, which decision is hardest,
which initiative is stuck, and what help is needed. Cadence and participation are
agreed with the CTO. Confidential coaching and board escalation have explicit boundaries.

Each minimum viable accelerator provides **diagnostic + benchmark + decision
framework + recommended actions**, with an outcome test and review date. All ten
from the input are represented: AI Opportunity Assessment, Engineering Effectiveness,
CTO Assessment, Nearshore Builder, Cloud Economics, Product/Technology Alignment,
M&A Tech Integration, Cyber Readiness, Exit Readiness, and CTO Hiring. An unavailable
benchmark remains unavailable rather than becoming an invented comparison.

Portfolio intelligence covers cloud cost/revenue, engineering headcount/ARR, release
frequency, support burden, AI adoption/effectiveness, onboarding effort, nearshore
effectiveness, and organization context. Cohorts preserve business model, scale,
product maturity, acquisition stage, period, and metric definitions. AI tool adoption
is distinct from delivery improvement. Small or incompatible cohorts do not support
league tables. The ecosystem includes portfolio CTOs, CTO/VP Engineering candidates,
security, AI, cloud, product and diligence specialists, and nearshore partners.

Exit readiness starts at acquisition and follows leadership, engineering predictability,
architecture capacity, roadmap health, technical debt, cybersecurity, AI strategy,
unit economics, key-person risk, IP evidence, and integration. Unresolved issues remain
visible in approved buyer materials.

## Strategy and measurement

Vision: dependable technology contribution to better investment decisions and company
outcomes while preserving company judgment. Differentiation to test is continuity
from thesis to execution to exit, reinforced by specialist and peer support. Tradeoffs
include diligence depth versus speed, intervention versus autonomy, reuse versus
context, and learning versus confidentiality.

1. **Year 1: prove continuity and trust.** Pilot the market lens, assessment/thesis,
   one 100-day handoff, and opt-in CTO partnership. Establish baselines, evidence
   continuity, a few accelerators, and exit criteria early.
2. **Year 3: make useful interventions repeatable.** Expand validated accelerators,
   peer learning, specialist capacity, and compatible cohorts. Check company outcomes
   and participation burden before expanding coverage.
3. **Year 5: demonstrate durable contribution through scale and exit.** Reuse platform
   and organization patterns, learn from realized experience and failed hypotheses,
   and share approved evidence with buyers and LPs.

Three measurement levels are retained: **adoption** (early deal involvement,
voluntary CTO engagement, proactive requests); **outcomes** (cloud unit cost,
onboarding time, delivery speed and quality, leadership, integration, AI product
value); **enterprise-value contribution** (revenue enabled, EBITDA contribution,
risk exposure, acquisition benefits, evidenced exit claims). Meetings and report
counts are workload diagnostics, not success measures.

Customer KPI pyramids contain measurable definitions, with exact names reused by
strategy horizons and team charters. Leaf `currentValue` fields are null: no private
baseline is supplied. Targets follow baseline agreement. Pyramids express diagnostic
relationships, not automatic additive formulas or proof of causality. Operational
customer trees have deeper drivers; three-level governance and business trees avoid
invented causal precision. No statistics from company marketing become fund returns.

## Delivery and ownership assumptions

Bricks are ownable capabilities delivered through configured workflows, artifacts,
and optional software modules. They do not imply one microservice or staffed squad
each. Shared layer/team vocabularies follow toolkit contracts. Brick status `invest`
denotes a proposed priority, not deployment maturity.

Five logical responsibility teams cover investment technology, CTO partnership,
specialist accelerators, portfolio intelligence, and value evidence/exit. People may
hold multiple responsibilities; staffing counts are omitted. Each brick and asset
has one accountable owner. IC, board, and company management retain decision rights.
The Technology Principal owns the product across these five responsibility areas.

Deployment channels are proposed fund, company, peer-learning, and approved-reporting
workspaces. Vendor, hosting, identity, and integration choices are unverified. GitHub,
AWS, and Jira in the input are examples, not a discovered estate. Permissioned manual
imports can precede APIs. Selected aggregate evidence is sufficient to start.

Data governance is a design assumption: company/fund boundaries, provenance,
candidate privacy, coaching confidentiality, cohort protection, and approval before
buyer/LP disclosure. Retention, residency, contracts, and applicable obligations
need owner confirmation. No compliance certification is asserted. Stressors are hypothetical.
