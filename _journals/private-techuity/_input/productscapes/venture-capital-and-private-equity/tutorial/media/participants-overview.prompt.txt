Use case: scientific-educational
Asset type: one illustration in a plain-language product-domain tutorial for adults.
Domain: Venture capital and private equity, from the beginning
Reader: A curious reader coming from a different field. You do not need a finance background or experience running a company.
Scope: An introduction to investing in businesses through venture, growth, and buyout funds, with software companies as everyday examples. It explains common arrangements rather than any particular investment firm's terms. Fund structures, investor access, and ownership rights vary by country and agreement. The people, businesses, and financial examples are fictional.
Subject: The people involved and how they relate
The fund investors: Organizations such as pension funds, or other eligible investors, pool money in a fund: an arrangement for investing together. In a partnership structure, these investors are called limited partners, shortened to LPs. Cares about: Returns after costs, the possibility of losses, trustworthy reporting, and when money may come back.
The investment manager: Selects investments and oversees them for the fund. You may hear the managing side called the general partner, or GP; precise legal roles depend on the structure. Cares about: Finding worthwhile investments, supporting them, earning fees and a possible share of profits, and retaining investors' confidence.
Founders, selling owners, and company management: Founders may seek money for growth or sell ownership. Management runs the business. These can be the same people, but they need not be. Cares about: Resources, an acceptable price, decision-making freedom, and a workable relationship after the agreement is signed.
Lenders and specialist advisers: Lenders may supply borrowed money. Lawyers, accountants, and other specialists help examine and complete investments; fund administrators maintain records and reporting. Cares about: For lenders, repayment. For advisers and administrators, accurate work within their responsibilities. Neither role is the same as owning the business.
Employees and customers: Keep the business working and buying its services, although they may have little say in an ownership transaction. Cares about: Good jobs, dependable products, fair treatment, and continuity when ownership or strategy changes.

Teaching scene:
Create a spacious map of the people around a private investment. Use a landscape canvas with the main line running left to right: a pair of people labeled "Fund investors", a clearly separate container labeled "Fund", and a company office labeled "Company". An arrow from Fund investors to Fund says "Provide money". A relationship connector from Fund to Company says "Holds shares"; do not decorate this connector with cash, because buying existing shares pays the seller. Above the Fund, put a person labeled "Investment manager" with a connector down to Fund labeled "Manages". Inside the Company office show a founder and colleague labeled "Founder and management", with the small phrase "Run the business". Keep them distinct from the fund manager. At the right, show an optional "Lender" with an arrow to Company labeled "May lend". Below the Fund, place two specialists labeled "Advisers"; connect them to Fund and Company with lines labeled "Checks and advice". Under the Company, place two small groups labeled "Employees" and "Customers", joined to the company by simple short lines, labeled "Work here" and "Buy products" respectively. Make all connections direct and avoid crossed lines. Distinguish people from the fund container and company. Use no acronyms, percentages, legal terms, unrequested labels, or profit charts. The founder is Maya: an adult woman with medium-brown skin, shoulder-length dark curly hair, and a teal jacket. These labels and relationships are the full teaching content of the image; use ample white space and large type.

Style and composition:
- Aspect ratio 16:9, generous margins, bright warm-white background.
- A polished adult educational illustration: flat editorial drawing, crisp navy outlines,
  restrained teal, blue, and warm amber accents, subtle texture, very little shading.
- Make the concept understandable at the width of a reading column and on a phone.
- Use a small number of recognizable objects and people. Keep relationships spatially clear.
- Use an inclusive cast without occupational or cultural stereotypes.
- Short everyday labels are welcome where they teach the distinction. Use only labels
  explicitly requested in the teaching scene; otherwise keep the image free of text.
- Arrows must have a clear meaning and direction; do not connect unrelated comparison
  panels as though they are required stages. Do not imply certainty, causality, proportions,
  cash movements, or guaranteed outcomes that the explanation does not support.
- Keep all important content inside the frame. No headline, watermark, brand logo,
  decorative charts, invented statistics, jargon, tiny writing, photorealism, or 3D rendering.
- Explain the idea visually. The surrounding tutorial provides its full definition and caveats.
