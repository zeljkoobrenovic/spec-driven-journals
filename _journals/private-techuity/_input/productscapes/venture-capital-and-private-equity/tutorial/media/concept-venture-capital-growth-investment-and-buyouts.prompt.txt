Use case: scientific-educational
Asset type: one illustration in a plain-language product-domain tutorial for adults.
Domain: Venture capital and private equity, from the beginning
Reader: A curious reader coming from a different field. You do not need a finance background or experience running a company.
Scope: An introduction to investing in businesses through venture, growth, and buyout funds, with software companies as everyday examples. It explains common arrangements rather than any particular investment firm's terms. Fund structures, investor access, and ownership rights vary by country and agreement. The people, businesses, and financial examples are fictional.
Concept: Venture capital, growth investment, and buyouts
Meaning: Venture capital, usually shortened to VC, commonly backs young businesses with substantial growth potential and uncertainty. Growth investment helps more established businesses expand. A buyout involves acquiring control of an existing business. These categories overlap.

Private equity, or PE, is a broad family that includes venture capital. In everyday industry conversation, people often use PE more narrowly to mean buyout investing.
Concrete example: Funding Maya to prove a young business model is a venture case. Funding expansion after it is established may be a growth case. Buying control from an owner who wants to leave is a buyout case.
Why this matters: Different needs call for different investors, expectations, and ownership arrangements. A company does not have to pass through all three stages.

Middle: an established software team expanding into an additional office, a manager and investor reviewing the expansion; label "Growth" and "Expand a business". Right: an existing business owner handing a symbolic key to a new controlling owner, while the business and employees continue; label "Buyout" and "Change control". These are alternative investment situations, not a required sequence. Avoid plants, rockets, ladders, or success charts.
