### Ideas retained and developed

1. Map the needs of investment partners, deal teams, the investment committee, CEOs, CTOs/CPTOs, boards, exit teams, and LPs. Add employee and end-customer consequences explicitly.
2. Separate the firm, its portfolio-support capability, and the products of operating companies.
3. Connect Invest, Align, Accelerate, Learn, Scale, and Exit, with learning and exit evidence running throughout.
4. Assess product, architecture, engineering, data/AI, security/resilience, organization, and economics around the actual thesis.
5. Keep evidence connected from diligence through priorities, interventions, outcome review, and exit.
6. Make accelerators combine diagnosis, useful comparisons where available, a decision framework, action, and review.
7. Distinguish adoption, company outcomes, and qualified economic contribution.
8. Develop the principle “standardize the questions, not the answers,” including where consistent controls and interfaces still matter.

### Challenges introduced

Owner-associated support is not necessarily voluntary. The customer metaphor must not hide influence, duties, information boundaries, or affected people. A small portfolio can support learning without supporting reliable causal benchmarks. A portal, catalogue, or large shared database can be premature. Reusable support has company effort and coordination costs. Low uptake can mean low value, poor timing, lack of capacity, or lack of need.

The proposed accelerator portfolio and minimum number of priorities are not validated optima. Leadership of the support capability does not confer control over company roadmaps. A progression to a more senior role is an aspiration in the source, not an outcome established by the manuscript.

### Values that must remain illustrative

The input's €1.2 million over 24 months and 60% onboarding-improvement example are illustrative proposal numbers, not verified company results. Null baseline/actual fields must remain unknown. The first draft uses its own clearly fictional Larkspur examples and does not convert model instances, team labels, or capability counts into claims about existing operations.
