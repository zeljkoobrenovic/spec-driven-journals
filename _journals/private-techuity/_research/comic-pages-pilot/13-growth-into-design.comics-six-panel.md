**Comic.** Larkspur is a fictional company that sells scheduling software, and an investor owns a large stake in it. That investor expects fast growth, and the expectation has reached the technology team as a request to make the software “more flexible”, with the reasoning left behind. The panels show Larkspur working out what was actually being asked for, comparing three ways to do it against the same customer need, the same date and the same spending limit, and choosing one — with a cheaper standby plan ready if the chosen one fails.

What “more flexible” turned out to mean: Larkspur must be able to bill, contract with and support customers in a **second country**, under that country’s tax and pricing rules, with the first customers actually **paying within twelve months** — and the investor’s plan expects a **third country within two years**, so each new country has to get cheaper to add than the last.

The people: Morgan advises the investor, Alex leads technology, Priya leads product, Sam leads finance, and Ines is the chief executive, who can approve spending inside the limit the board has set. All are fictional, and every amount and date below is invented for the example.

Two words recur. A **replay** means rerunning last year’s real invoices through newly written code and checking, line by line, that it produces the same bills — the test that catches mistakes before a customer is billed wrongly. A **specialist-week** means one week of work by one of the two people who can safely change the billing code; it measures effort, not how long the calendar takes.
