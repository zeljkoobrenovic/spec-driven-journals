**Comic.** Larkspur, a fictional company, sells scheduling software to firms whose staff work in the field. It expected €4 million of new investment in September. That investment is a **round**: investors put in money and receive **shares**, stakes in the company’s ownership. The work is on schedule, but the money now looks likely in December. Two pieces of work continue: an **onboarding pilot**, a trial of a faster way to set up new customers, and **recovery work**, which makes sure the dispatch service that sends field staff to their jobs can be restored within four hours after a failure. Two hires and a **customer portal**, a website where customers handle routine tasks themselves, were planned but are not yet committed. The panels follow the company as it changes that funding assumption before it becomes a delivery promise: cash by date, the latest useful decision date, three alternatives and one authorized fallback.

Alex leads technology and Priya leads product. Sam, the chief financial officer, leads finance, and Ines, the chief executive, leads the company. Morgan advises the investors on technology and sits in on the board meeting. All are fictional, and every figure is invented. This example is deliberately separate from the Larkspur figures in other chapters.
