**Comic.** An investment firm has put money into Larkspur expecting a financial gain, and it employs its own technology adviser to form a view of the company’s technology and help improve it. That adviser reports to the firm, not to the company — so the company needs to understand both what the adviser has been asked to do and how what they learn may reach the investor. The panels show how the company’s technology leader establishes the assignment, separates influence from authority and handles a change of role.

Morgan is the investment firm’s technology adviser. Inside Larkspur, Alex leads technology, Priya leads product, Sam leads finance, and Ines is the chief executive, who leads the company and can commission work on its behalf. Above them sits the **board**, the group of directors that oversees company leadership and approves certain major decisions. A few terms recur below. The investor’s **existing rights** are the access the company has already agreed to in its investment contracts — typically to inspect records and discuss the business with senior executives. **Coaching** means helping a leader develop their own judgment, not directing company work. **Delivery capacity** is simply the people and time available to carry work out. All the characters are fictional. Comparative scenarios use separate assumptions. Historical cases are discussed through documents; the scenes do not reenact real events.
