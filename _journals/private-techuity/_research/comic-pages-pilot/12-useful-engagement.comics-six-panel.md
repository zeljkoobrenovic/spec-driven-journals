**Comic.** Larkspur is a fictional software company. An investor has put money into it, and now offers to lend Larkspur an **outside specialist** for a few weeks to make customer setup repeatable — setting a new customer up on the software is the work that currently depends on one Larkspur employee. The specialist is paid for out of money the board has already set aside for a customer-setup trial, so the offer is not free help. The panels follow that one engagement from an undefined offer through a written agreement, a change of direction and a tested handover.

Two clocks run. Days are numbered from day 0, the meeting at which Larkspur’s **board** — the directors who approve the company’s major spending — adopted its plan. The specialist starts on day 21, so engagement week three falls around day 40 and week six around day 63. The board reads the result at day 100.

The people are fictional. Alex is Larkspur’s technology leader, Priya its product leader and the person accountable for the trial, and Sam its finance leader. Morgan is the investor’s technology adviser, who made the introduction. The **investor-side sponsor** is the senior person at the investment firm who backs the engagement and secures the specialist’s days.
