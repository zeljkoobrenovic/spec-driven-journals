The decision rule in 16 is "the largest actionable share of remaining effort, supported by a costed step" (40% is not "most").

**Gate 2 definitions added 18 September 2026 (in-depth review of 08, finding R08-006; stated in 08 only, consistent with every chapter that says “waiting time down”).** Customer waiting time runs from the signed contract to the day the customer first schedules real work in the product. Fictional baseline: about ten weeks for the typical customer, in both the twelve earlier implementations and the day-90 cohort (hence “unchanged”). Gate 2 passes only if the second cohort averages ≤ 50 h **and** typical waiting time is eight weeks or less (a two-week improvement is the smallest the board judged worth a further stage). The hours are end to end and include all Larkspur staff time spent checking and correcting customer data, before or during setup; work by the outside validation service and temporary data-entry help bought with the €40,000 (03) is reported beside the hours with its continuing cost. A failed gate means the board approves no further stage; commitments already made (the rest of ONB-1’s €90,000) are still owed. The 80/62/50 hours count all Larkspur staff time on one customer’s setup, not the specialist’s alone; 100 implementations a year is the teaching model’s planning assumption (8,000 h = four FTE of modelled effort, not a staff count).
