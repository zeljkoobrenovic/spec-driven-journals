## What changed the argument

The conditional central thesis remains: technology value depends on the fit among investment assumptions, financing, governance and the company’s capacity to execute and reinvest. This pass makes one missing link explicit: a valuation is an estimate based on business assumptions, not a command to optimize one ratio. Growth can require faster learning and architectural flexibility; sustainable earnings can require cost discipline and simplification. Both need customer value, funding and ongoing investment.

The valuation article is now the second reading step, before investor-return arithmetic. It teaches revenue, EBITDA and its exclusions, profit, cash, enterprise/equity value, market comparisons, DCF and asset-based methods. All financial teaching calculations are labeled fictional. The architecture, cash, incentives, product-value and diligence chapters introduce relevant terms locally and apply the connection without prescribing one architecture per valuation method.
