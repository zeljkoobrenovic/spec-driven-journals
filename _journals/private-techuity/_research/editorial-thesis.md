# Editorial Thesis and Architecture

Decision date: September 12, 2026. Recommended title accepted by the author as the working title.

## The argument to test

Private equity changes the constraints and decision system of a company. Durable product and technology value depends on fit among the investment thesis, financing, governance, and the company's ability to execute and reinvest. A Technology Principal can improve that fit by connecting assumptions to evidence, evidence to funded decisions, and interventions to qualified outcomes. The role cannot remove all conflicts or guarantee success.

This is a synthesis and evaluative proposal, not a causal theorem established by the literature. “Durable” intentionally asks about the company and affected stakeholders beyond one investor's exit. Readers may disagree about acceptable trade-offs; the manuscript should expose those disagreements rather than disguise them as a score.

## Why this structure

Part I establishes the economic grammar before offering operating advice. Part II shows how the arrangement changes authority and behavior. Part III connects familiar technology work to specific mechanisms and financial constraints. Part IV makes that connection the Principal's lifecycle responsibility. Part V develops the author's distinctive product hypothesis after readers can see what it must explain. Part VI tests the argument against positive, mixed, adverse, and empirical evidence.

Twenty-six question-led articles allow independent reading. The repeatable fictional company Larkspur carries worked mechanisms without pretending to be a real case. The real cases provide uneven but traceable evidence, with their causal limitations made explicit. Reference pages hold definitions and practical forms so chapters can remain analytical essays.

The first draft is approximately short-book length in its core text. It needs deeper longitudinal cases and a stronger range of company sizes before it could become a mature commercial book. Adding words without better evidence would not address that need.

## Title decision

The author accepted **Private Techuity: Product and Engineering Leadership in Private Equity**. Preserve the memorable author-created name; the subtitle describes both understanding and action. The original “Demystifying Private Equity for Product and Engineering Leaders” remains a credible alternative but sets a narrower introductory expectation. The earlier alternatives and rationale are retained in title-decision.md.

Revisit the title after the role and capability model have been tested against actual engagements. Do not position Productscapes as proven intellectual property or a universal operating system.

## Editorial distinctions to preserve

- Investor return is not company health, stakeholder welfare, or a demonstrated technology effect.
- Enterprise value is not equity value; a fund is not its manager; a transaction value is not operating cash.
- Adoption is not an outcome; capacity is not cash; a projection is not realization.
- Reported evidence, established definitions, inference, hypotheses, and fictional examples need different language.
- A manager's operating-platform description is interested evidence about an offering, not an independent impact evaluation.
- A Principal's contribution is not an implied right to command portfolio teams or use all company data.
- Standardization should preserve comparison and control where needed while allowing company-specific conclusions.
- Conflicting interests are sometimes real. Do not repair the manuscript into a universally positive-sum story.

## Voice and continuity

Use the intelligent technical reader's existing knowledge of systems, decisions, and organizational change. Explain finance concretely on first use. Prefer an explicit mechanism and an important qualification to a list of fashionable interventions. Keep articles structurally varied; short adaptations should preserve the main caveat rather than become promotional.

Cross-reference the next useful question rather than ending every chapter with the same checklist. Avoid “best practice” without a defined setting and evidence. Keep the six recurring capability areas as a hypothesis, not an obligatory maturity ladder.

Numeric chapter references in the original claim/input notes identify the original folder prefixes, not the new displayed reading positions.

Visma's metric discrepancy is resolved; its causal and stakeholder gaps remain.
