Assess an investor through its **behavior under pressure** and its ability to support this company’s next decisions. Statements about patience, expertise and company leaders’ freedom to decide need concrete evidence before they become planning assumptions. Most product and engineering leaders never sign the term sheet, but the feasibility of their plan depends on how the investor behaves after a missed quarter; if the investor is already in place, the same assessment is used to change one dependency in the plan.

Begin with the company’s next difficult decisions. For fictional Larkspur: an onboarding pilot, a second country and a retiring founder, each needing something specific from the investor: an experienced contributor, follow-on capital on known conditions, and time. Public operating-team descriptions are evidence of stated offerings, not proof of suitability. [S22: KKR Capstone description](https://www.kkr.com/approach/capstone)

Seek references that include difficulty: a failed plan, a disagreement with an operating expert, a request for further investment, support that was discontinued, unfinished work at exit. A reference has its own perspective; look for repeated mechanisms and check alternative explanations. **Make the support offer reviewable**: who is available, for how many days, who pays, who has authority to decide, what would stop it. Examine the financing alongside the support, and distinguish committed capital from expectations of help.

**The evidence changes the choice.** Investor A offers €5 million at the higher valuation and a broad platform; an independent reference reports that a follow-on after a miss took four months through an unwritten committee process. Investor B offers €4.5 million, one operator with two onboarding automations behind him, and funded a reference’s bridge within six weeks while replacing its CFO; its fund is in year seven of ten. Either could be right. Ines chooses B because its uncertainty can be bounded: a reserved €1.5 million follow-on released on pilot evidence, and ten named operator days a quarter. A second former executive with the same CFO story would send her back to negotiate the approval route for management changes before signing.

**For an investor already in place**, change one dependency. In a separate scenario, Alex moves a June hire that assumed an uncommitted co-investor to a dated July board decision, funds the onboarding pilot from operating cash, and labels the investor’s AI help unconfirmed until a named person and days exist. When references or fund information are unavailable, label support unconfirmed, make no funding-dependent commitment, and set a review date.

Observations such as avoided downside scenarios, shifting definitions or instructions that bypass executives warrant investigation, not an allegation; uncomfortable challenge can be useful discipline. Reassess fit periodically, and when conflict emerges take a concrete decision with its evidence and options to the forum authorized to decide ([[decide-who-decides]]).
