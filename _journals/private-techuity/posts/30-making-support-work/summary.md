An **engagement** is an agreed piece of support with a purpose, participants and boundaries. An **engagement charter** is a short record of that agreement. It turns an offer of help into work the company can understand, resource and review. The difficulty is peculiar to investor-backed companies: a consultant the company hires answers to the company, while a specialist sent by the investor answers partly to the owner, and engineers may not know whose instructions they are following.

Start with the useful decision or result. Determine whether the assignment should diagnose a problem, recommend options, implement a change or develop the team’s ability to do the work. A review that produces recommendations needs a different commitment from an assignment expected to deliver and operate an improvement.

Agree the company need, expected result, accountable company leader, named support, resources, decision rights, information use, review and handover. The **engagement sponsor** is the person backing this assignment within the investment firm. Their support does not automatically transfer responsibility for the company’s operating decisions.

**External help needs internal capacity**. In a fictional example, a specialist provides ten days over six weeks, while the company must also provide engineering time, customer-team participation and product review. If the company cannot make those commitments, the specialist’s availability alone does not make the engagement feasible.

Clarify advisory fees, implementation spending and continuing costs. Help described as included in the relationship can still require other funded work. Compare the full arrangement with an outside provider, internal delivery or a smaller first step.

Agree **how information will be used**. Coaching, operational support and formal oversight have different purposes. People should understand what may be shared with the board and how important concerns will reach the responsible decision-maker. Peer learning also needs clear permissions and enough context to interpret the lesson.

A **baseline** records the starting situation. For customer setup, measure effort alongside waiting time, errors and useful customer outcomes. Track other changes that could explain the result. Less staff effort can create capacity without reducing cash spending, and an observed improvement does not isolate the adviser’s contribution.

**Review what changed**, what was learned and what remains. Continue, revise, expand, replace or stop the support according to the evidence. **Escalation** takes a decision to someone with the authority or resources to resolve it; present the options and consequences.

Several investors can offer conflicting help. In fictional Larkspur, Priya and Alex define one company question and an accountable executive before assigning work to two advisers. Corporate support also needs separation from requests chiefly serving the parent’s commercial agenda. Agree who sees findings and what continues after an ownership change. Investor status should not make ineffective help impossible to revise or end through the relevant authority.

A **handover** transfers the knowledge and responsibilities needed to continue. Test whether the company can use the result, or agree the continuing specialist service it needs. The proposed charter succeeds when it improves the work and makes future responsibility understandable, rather than merely documenting activity.
