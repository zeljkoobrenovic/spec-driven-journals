An **engagement** is an agreed piece of support with a purpose, participants and boundaries. An **engagement charter** is a short record of that agreement. The specialist also reports to the investor. Before work starts, agree how that responsibility relates to the company’s priorities and who can direct the assignment.

![A support charter connects the agreed result, company and adviser resources, boundaries, evidence review and handover.](assets/images/30-useful-engagement/summary-at-a-glance.jpeg)
**Figure 1:** *Make support feasible, reviewable and sustainable after the engagement.*

The input is the written request from the previous chapter: make one setup path repeatable, with a specialist alongside a Larkspur engineer for six weeks, and Priya assessing customer outcomes. The fictional charter fills in the fields. The need is diligence finding D-3: onboarding depends on one specialist’s manual configuration, about 80 hours per implementation. The result is a path the engineer and customer team can run without that specialist. Priya is the **accountable company leader**; the **investor-side sponsor** is the firm’s partner responsible for the holding, who secures the specialist’s days. Resources are ten specialist days over six weeks, six engineer-days released from portal research, two customer-team sessions and Priya’s review time. Specialist days are charged to the company from the ONB-1 budget. The specialist recommends, Priya decides, Alex approves code changes, a scope change needs Priya and the sponsor, and a budget change goes to the board. Interviews are scheduled once with a stated purpose; findings are pilot evidence, not assessments of individuals. A baseline from customer records is due by day 20, the review is at week six, and the handover test is an engineer and a customer-team member completing a new setup with the specialist observing only.

**External help consumes internal time.** If the six engineer-days cannot be found, the specialist’s availability does not make the engagement feasible.

**In week three the evidence changes the problem.** The baseline shows that for customers with imperfect data, cleaning their records, not the setup step, decides when a setup completes. Priya proposes a scope change, the sponsor agrees, Alex confirms the effort. Templating the less common customer types stops; the remaining five specialist days define a data-intake check and the engineer builds a validation for the three most common defects. Ten days and six engineer-days are unchanged, no cash is added, and the review date stands. Extending the engagement and stopping to hire are both rejected; a funded data-quality step is a board decision for the day-90 review.

Three boundaries from other chapters apply: the charter names the accountable leader and decision boundaries so no parallel reporting line forms; interviews are evidence, not assessments, unless the firm requests one separately; and hours released are not cash saved, so waiting time and errors are recorded alongside effort.

**At week six** the handover test passes on two new customers, the intake check is running, effort is falling and waiting time has not yet moved. The engagement ends on schedule with no continuing service needed; the data-quality finding goes to the board. Any engagement may legitimately end with a capability, a better decision or an understood continuing service. None should leave a dependency nobody planned to fund.
