**Comic.** Before an investor puts money into a company, it investigates the company’s product, technology, people and risks. This is **technical due diligence**. Here it tests one assumption behind a proposed investment in Larkspur, a fictional company that sells scheduling software: that Larkspur can double **onboarding**, the work of setting each new customer up until the software is ready for real use, without hiring more implementation staff. Eight pages follow one finding, D-3, from a sample of real work to a recorded decision that changes the growth plan, and on to the dates by which that decision will be tested. Each page is three strips; the figures, options and thresholds are drawn into the artwork, and a transcript follows every page. Every figure is fictional.

Morgan is the investor’s technology adviser. At Larkspur, Alex is the chief technology officer, Priya leads product, Sam leads finance and Ines is the chief executive. All are fictional. A deal has two key moments: at **signing** the parties commit to the agreement, and at **closing** the money is paid and the **shares**, the units of ownership in the company, change hands. Larkspur’s leaders took part before signing, when a finding can still change the deal; after closing, their main levers would have been the plan and its funding. The investor’s **investment committee** approves the deal’s terms. Larkspur’s **board** of directors approves the company’s plan and its money. Day 0 is the board meeting after closing.
