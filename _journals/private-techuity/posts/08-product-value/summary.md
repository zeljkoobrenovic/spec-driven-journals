Product work creates business value through changes in customer behavior and operating economics. A roadmap item is a proposed intervention, not a unit of revenue or enterprise value.

Make the chain explicit: intervention, operating change, customer outcome, and economic effect. In the fictional onboarding example, reusable configuration might reduce effort, activate customers earlier, and improve billing or retention. Every connection is a hypothesis. Weak demand, poor adoption, or new maintenance costs can break the chain.

**Distinguish capacity from cash.** Fewer implementation hours can free people for other work without reducing payroll. The company needs a conversion plan: serve additional profitable demand, avoid hiring, reduce external spending, improve quality, or use the capacity another way. Include implementation and ongoing costs before claiming a net benefit.

Identify the value mechanism precisely. Growth, retention, margin, cash timing, risk reduction, and strategic flexibility need different evidence. Avoid adding overlapping benefits: earlier collection is not automatically additional lifetime revenue, and a retained customer may already be included in the forecast.

Product strategy determines where efficiency matters. Begin with the customers and needs the company intends to serve. Apply the same customer and economic test to requests from sales, executives, investors, and acquisitions. User focus and stable priorities are relevant research themes, but they do not calculate a company's roadmap value. [S15: DORA 2024 report](https://dora.dev/research/2024/dora-report/)

Establish a baseline with population, period, data source, exclusions, and cost basis. Compare cohorts or staged rollouts where feasible. Document competing explanations such as pricing, sales incentives, acquisitions, and customer mix. Finance can reconcile calculations and double counting; it cannot create a missing counterfactual.

Protect the future product through measures that could reveal harm. Lower onboarding effort is incomplete if customers wait longer, abandon the process, or create more support demand.

The Principal's contribution is to keep the full chain understandable to investors and teams. A qualified claim of contribution is stronger than an unsupported valuation number. The same evidence should also justify stopping an initiative whose customer or economic hypothesis no longer holds.
