# Spec: Hilton and Skype: A Successful Exit Still Needs Explaining

## Intent

Use the documented ownership periods to distinguish investor proceeds from company capability and product continuity, and to distinguish a capability the company inherited from a change the owner made. Preview the contrast up front: Hilton needs a financing explanation, Skype a rights-and-buyer-value explanation. End on two case-specific operating questions and one shared evidence limit. Treat Skype’s strategic acquisition as a change of decision context, not proof of general strategic-owner behavior.

## Audience

Product and engineering leaders inside companies working under investors, including leaders who inherit an ownership arrangement. Assume no specialist finance background; explain necessary terms before use. Investor-side readers are secondary.

- Explain the title question and its implications for a concrete company decision.

- Give each company an inherited-capability / documented-change / remaining-uncertainty row drawn only from the cited sources; use the dates the sources supply, and keep a dated event distinct from a measured contribution to the return.
- Link related chapters and cite substantive external factual claims close to the text.
- Keep the TL;DR and the comic page scripts consistent with the full article; inspect every generated page against its script.
- Define every finance term at its first use in each reading format, in ordinary words rather than by another finance term: exit, private equity, purchase funded by borrowing, changed borrowing terms, retained seller stake, promise of later payment, public offering, strategic buyer, sponsor, limited partner, fund, fees and carry, adjusted EBITDA (as a company-defined earnings measure that leaves out borrowing costs, tax and the accounting charges that spread asset costs over time), net loss, capitalized software, synergies, valuation and cash actually received. Give the 3.1× multiple as dollars returned per dollar invested. Identify the $2.75 billion as Skype's 2009 valuation wherever it is contrasted with the 2011 price.
- Explain the Hilton financing mechanism from the registration filing's debt note: who owed the money, that the owner's parent supplied $819 million to buy back $1.8 billion of loans below face value and cancelled about $2.0 billion of the most junior loans plus $87 million of unpaid interest, and that the $4.0 billion reduction lowered debt and interest cost rather than supplying cash for technology. Explain that most Hilton-branded hotels belong to third-party owners who pay Hilton for the brand, systems and management.
- Keep implementation chronology separate from causal attribution: acknowledge the software the filing records as placed into service in the fourth quarter of 2012, and state only that the disclosure does not identify the OnQ change or measure its contribution. Report Qik's $121 million as the initial cash payment alongside the roughly $29 million of employee payments tied to future service.
- Keep the Hilton sale timeline accurate in every format: the 2013 initial public offering returned Hilton's shares to public trading and Hilton itself sold shares, while no Blackstone fund sold shares in it; Blackstone's funds sold their own shares later (a June 2014 sale is the dated example) and completed the exit in 2018. Give each component of the 2010 restructuring an identifiable provider and use (owner's $819 million to buy back loans, owner's cancellation of the two lowest-priority loans it held, $76 million from Hilton's own set-aside cash). Describe fund payouts as reduced by fees and the manager's profit share and affected by the fund's other holdings, with timing set by the fund agreement, not as a universal payment order. Explain junior loans as loans repaid last, stock-market indices as measures tracking baskets of publicly traded shares, and, in the comic, a fund and investor cash flows before their labels appear. State the evidence limit as the absence of a comparative assessment of employee and customer outcomes, and present a shared-system outage as a possible network-wide disruption rather than a certain shutdown. Explain a mortgage loan as a loan secured against property, debt securities as investments that represent money lent, and revenue as income from selling services before costs are deducted; in the comic, say what OnQ does before naming it. Present dated chronologies (the Hilton retrospective, Skype's 2010–2011 developments, the eBay 2007 charge) as short separate sentences, with each date, amount, payment condition and source qualification preserved.
- Comic artwork carries only plain-language labels that the visible caption explains: no unexplained acronyms (LBO, IPO, IP), no malformed document text, no invented technical structure presented as historical fact, and the two Skype values, $2.75 billion at the 2009 purchase and $8.5 billion at the 2011 sale, are shown as the same company's value at two dates, never as a return. Every generated page is inspected at full size before acceptance.
