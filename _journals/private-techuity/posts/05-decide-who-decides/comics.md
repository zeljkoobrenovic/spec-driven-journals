**Comic.** An investment can change who is authorized to decide, and a suggestion from someone close to the investor can sound like an instruction. The panels start with a misheard cloud remark, a suggestion that Larkspur move its systems to a different supplier of rented computing, which settles how far an adviser’s influence reaches. They then follow a Larkspur proposal to automate **onboarding**, the setup a new customer needs before it can use the product: the pilot, a small trial that is already funded and in use, the two hires that would speed up the rest of the work, the board meeting that must approve them, and the written January plan the team executes when that approval does not arrive.

Morgan is an investor’s adviser in the fund scenarios; Alex leads technology, Priya leads product, Sam leads finance and Ines is the chief executive, who runs the company and answers to its board. All are fictional. Comparative scenarios use separate assumptions. Historical cases are discussed through documents; the scenes do not reenact real events. Terms used below: shareholders own a company’s shares, the pieces of its ownership; the board is the group of directors that oversees the company for its shareholders; an engineer-week is one engineer’s work for one week, so twelve engineer-weeks is three engineers for four weeks; a template is a reusable set of product settings for one customer type, so a new customer of that type is set up by filling in the template instead of configuring the product from scratch.
