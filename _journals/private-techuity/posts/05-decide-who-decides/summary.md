**Governance** is the arrangement for making decisions, overseeing them and holding people responsible. In the fictional example, Morgan, the investor’s technology adviser, mentions that Larkspur should change its cloud provider; Alex, the CTO, hears an instruction and starts planning. Informal influence exists under any ownership; an investment changes the conditions of the decision by adding board seats, approval thresholds, reserved matters and advisers who seem to speak for a shareholder. Establish the actual authority, funding and deadline before committing.

![A proposal moves through explicit recommendation, decision, funding and delivery roles, with advice and an escalation route.](assets/images/05-decide-who-decides/summary-at-a-glance.jpeg)
**Figure 1:** *Make authority, resources and the route for disagreement explicit.*

**Start by establishing what changed**: who holds shares and which rights come with them, which outcomes are now expected, which decisions need approval and from whom, and who is accountable for each result. Applied to the cloud remark, this settles it quickly. Morgan holds no approval right; Ines, the CEO, may approve spending up to a fictional €500,000; a migration above that goes to the board. The suggestion is an option to cost, not a plan. Keep the decision-makers distinct: the fund’s investment committee approved the investment; the company’s board and executives approve company work; a public operating-support description is a stated model, not a delegation of authority. [S22: KKR Capstone description](https://www.kkr.com/approach/capstone)

**Then fill in the record with names, thresholds and dates.** For the €1 million onboarding proposal: Priya and Alex recommend, Ines resolves trade-offs, Morgan tests the 80-hour implementation figure, the board approves because the amount exceeds Ines’s delegation, and the answer is needed within two weeks. It is not yet a funded commitment; the board needs a funding plan.

With several investors, do not turn **conflicting requests into parallel company plans**; identify the forum that can settle the choice. Ask the board to connect a technical proposal to a business outcome and compare feasible options, not to design the architecture.

**Make conditional commitments explicit and plan the missed deadline.** Alex can deliver in October if two hires start in June, or in January with current capacity; the hiring approval is needed by May. If it has not arrived by May, Alex brings the January option back to Ines, and the team is told in writing that January is the plan it is executing. Customer promises tied to October still require an explicit decision by Ines or the board; nobody keeps working toward October assuming money will appear.

**Report to ask for a decision.** Three sentences suffice: what changed (effort per implementation fell from 80 to 62 hours, not the 50 assumed; about 40% of the remainder traces to customer data quality), what management proposes (a €40,000 data-quality step from the reserve), and the decision requested at this meeting. Engineers should hear one set of priorities. When the adviser, the CTO and the investment team each give instructions, name the accountable company leader and route conflicts to that person; adviser influence and confidentiality are in [[investors-adviser]], the support charter in [[useful-engagement]].
