Positive EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) does not mean a company has money available for a technology program. Interest, taxes, capital investment, working capital, and debt repayment can consume most of the cash generated by operations.

The full article builds a fictional bridge from EBITDA to remaining cash. Its purpose is to make competing obligations visible, not to reproduce a statutory cash-flow statement. Before promising an initiative, understand when its cash is required, which entity pays, and what other commitments it displaces.

**Debt changes the consequences of a miss.** Higher interest costs, a refinancing deadline, or contractual restrictions can reduce room for product investment. A revenue shortfall and a financing shock can occur together. A credible technology plan therefore needs a downside funding path, not just a base-case delivery schedule.

Accounting also affects how work appears. IAS 38 distinguishes research expense from development expenditure meeting capitalization criteria. [S07: IAS 38 overview](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ias-38-intangible-assets/) Eligible capitalization can change an earnings presentation without changing the immediate development cash payment. It is not inherently suspicious, but comparisons need consistent policies and a view of the spending required to sustain the product.

Adjusted EBITDA requires a reconciliation. Ask whether excluded costs recur as part of the company's strategy and whether the next owner will still need to pay them. Do not treat an accounting classification or deferred expenditure as proof of operating improvement.

The practical proposal should offer feasible choices: minimum continuity, staged improvement, and a more ambitious investment where appropriate. Show cash timing, capacity, dependencies, expected outcomes, and stop or expansion conditions. The CFO contributes financial consistency; product and engineering leaders explain what each option makes possible or puts at risk.

A cash constraint deserves analysis rather than automatic acceptance or dismissal. Some spending protects the company's ability to meet future obligations. Some proposed modernization does not justify its transition cost. The decision is which plan provides the strongest feasible ability to serve customers, reinvest, and meet commitments under both expected and adverse conditions.
