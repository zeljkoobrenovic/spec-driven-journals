# Spec: TeamSystem: Each New Owner Inherits Progress and Unfinished Work

## Intent

Use successive ownership transactions to show that each ownership change creates a new financial starting point while product work carries on across it. Separate reporting periods, adjusted earnings and statutory results with the company’s own reconciliation, distinguish announced transaction values from cash received, and end on what the group accumulates: products, knowledge, dependencies and continuing work.

## Audience

Product and engineering leaders inside companies working under investors, including leaders who inherit an ownership arrangement. Assume no specialist finance background; explain necessary terms before use. Investor-side readers are secondary.

## Success criteria

- Add one whole-post overview visual to the TL;DR after its opening paragraph, generated with the Nano Banana article illustrator. Keep the 300–500-word summary prose, bold emphasis and citations; provide alt text and a concise numbered caption.
- Provide a distinctive article header logo and a simple navigation icon with unique asset paths. Keep both consistent with the journal’s visual style.
- Include two explanatory article figures with accurate short labels, alt text and numbered captions. Use restrained bold emphasis for key claims; preserve the words, citations and historical data. The comic modality is a set of comic pages — each image one page of three stacked strips with the dialogue, labels, dated figures and reconciliation lines lettered in the artwork — generated from the `comic-page` blocks in `comics.md`, with consistent fictional characters, a caption and a transcript per page.
- Make the company leader’s decision, authority and funding assumptions explicit. Distinguish the stated ownership arrangement from a claim about every investor.
- Keep historical findings within their source scope and label new comparative scenarios as fictional.
- Introduce necessary terms in ordinary language before using them in explanations, examples, tables, or diagrams; keep opening key points readable without prior study.
- Introduce TeamSystem and the concrete question before restating the ownership-change thesis; explain enterprise value, EBITDA (expansion plus practical meaning) and the majority/minority distinction before the ownership table; explain the relationship between Hg, its funds and HgCapital Trust plc before the first Trust-specific amount, and say that sterling amounts concern the Trust's interest, not the whole company.
- Make the opening true for both a minority investment and a controlling acquisition: each investor starts from a new purchase price and plan, while reporting periods and financing arrangements must be checked case by case; attribute the shortened 2016 comparison to TeamSystem's disclosed change of control.
- Describe 2000–2017 as the operating-history window and label 2021–2024 as a follow-up on investment ownership and proceeds drawn from announcements and the Trust's annual reports; give the detailed 2021–2024 sequence once, in the partial-exit section, with short cross-references elsewhere.
- Present the reconciliation with plain-English explanations beside unfamiliar rows, separate the asset-cost charges, the financing charges and the actual payments, keep the non-cash finance components in a compact supporting table, describe the €31.8 million financing figure as net loan proceeds after repayments (80 + 34.5 − 81.5 − 1.2), and explain the tax credit as an accounting benefit rather than a refund.
- Make the summary and the comic captions stand alone for a lay reader: expand EBITDA and gloss every retained finance term locally; in the comic, define migrations and integration work, state the compared periods concretely (March–December 2016 versus full-year 2017) and keep the qualification that 8.9% still includes newly acquired businesses.
- Move from a concrete question through explanation and example to a practical conclusion; connect the conclusion to the next chapter in the configured reading order.
- Make summaries and comic storyboards understandable on their own; preserve source limits and label fictional scenarios, including changes in example assumptions.
- Establish the historical window and distinguish transaction announcements from completed transactions.
- Separate sponsor accounts from company financial records and the manuscript's causal hypotheses.
- Explain cash proceeds versus retained value, pro forma comparisons, adjusted EBITDA versus statutory earnings, and capitalized development using checked source figures; show the full reported adjusted-EBITDA-to-loss reconciliation, and present the ownership episodes in a table with date/status and what each source establishes, without a synthetic performance ranking.
- Treat later HgCapital Trust events (2021, 2023, 2024) by source: announcements give announced values; the Trust's annual reports give completion status and gross realizations; none gives net investor cash, and none is the 2016 stake.
- Distinguish the accounting finance charge from finance costs actually paid, using the 2017 report's notes 7–10, and keep both distinct from other financing cash outflows.
- Keep the recurring-acquisition-adjustment lesson to one paragraph linking [[visma]]; link [[handover-of-obligations]] for the continuing-obligations handover.
- Relate concrete interventions to testable customer and business mechanisms; expose stakeholder and attribution gaps.
- Preserve qualifications in a 300–500-word TL;DR and in the comic page scripts; inspect every generated page against its script.
- Explain revenue (sales recorded before costs), profit (what remains after the costs a measure includes) and cash (money actually received or paid) in plain words before the opening's first numerical comparison.
- State both assumptions behind the 2016 pro forma base in the article: the March 2016 change of control treated as if it had happened on 1 January 2016, and the businesses bought during 2016 treated as owned from that date; the short formats describe the base as reconstructed for a full year including the businesses bought during 2016, and keep the separate warning that 2017 growth still includes newly acquired businesses.
- Present the 2021–2024 ownership chronology as a compact dated event table rather than one long paragraph; present the 2017 financing payments as three short parts (what was paid, borrowing movements, interpretation) with the components in a supporting table, and keep the main lesson visible before the arithmetic.
- Say that recording a non-cash charge (depreciation, amortization, provisions, impairment, bad-debt allowance) does not itself move cash and that payments on the underlying obligations must be read from the cash-flow statement; never assert payment timing from the charge alone.
- In the summary: define adjusted earnings as a measure that leaves out costs the company selects, not only one-off costs; explain "reconciles", "listed company" and "funds" locally; state that €52.1 million is the finance-cost payment category with other financing payments reported separately; name the 2021 sale and reinvestment before any later proceeds; explain the overview artwork's labels in its caption.
- In further reading: describe the secondary-buyout finding as higher buyer returns when the firms' skills complemented one another, and describe the Trust's 2024 holding as a replacement for the 2016 stake rather than an addition to it.
- Keep the opening blocks non-repetitive: each block adds something (what the reader follows, the investor's price-and-obligations question, the leader's workload), and the three key points separate the ownership lesson, the reporting-period lesson and the earnings-versus-cash lesson. Define adjusted earnings at its first meaningful use as a profit figure calculated with specified costs left out, and let the reconciliation identify the exclusions rather than calling them all one-off.
- One question per paragraph in the dense passages: introduce TeamSystem and the central question before the revenue/profit/cash definitions; split the growth section into the reporting-period problem, the two pro forma assumptions and the resulting comparison, with the purchase-price-allocation sentence moved to the asset-cost discussion; split the later-proceeds interpretation (scope of the 2024 figure, completion status, the three numbers to keep apart); list the non-core components in a compact table; give non-cash charges, the tax credit versus tax paid, and operating cash flow their own paragraphs with direct statements.
- Describe the credit line as borrowing up to an agreed limit with repayments governed by the lending agreement (note 19 of the 2017 report: €65 million facility maturing September 2021), not as repaid at the borrower's discretion.
- In the summary: gloss revenue locally, identify Hg as the manager formerly named HgCapital, describe the overview artwork's migration/integration label as moving customers between products and making acquired businesses work together, explain net cash as money reaching shareholders after fees and other deductions, bound the evidence limit to the sources examined, and keep lesson two in two paragraphs.
- In the comic: explain every meaningful artwork label in the displayed caption or alt text, define revenue, profit and cash on the first page, state directly that fictional people are discussing historical documents (no fund-scenario boilerplate), keep the artwork free of unexplained small documents, and let the build render the displayed captions and transcripts from the page blocks so they cannot drift.
- Explain enterprise value as a price for the whole operating business that counts what belongs to shareholders and what is owed to lenders together, distinct both from one shareholder's stake and from the price of all the shares, which is broadly enterprise value minus the company's debt plus its cash, subject to adjustments agreed in the transaction; give a shorter equivalent in the summary.
- Keep sentence-level readability in the dense passages: give profit and adjusted earnings separate sentences in the opening definitions; in the summary, state the owner chronology before the HgCapital purchase and explain enterprise value in its own sentence; in the further-reading annotation on the July 2024 sale, separate the announced terms, the replacement holding and the later gross figure. Add no new material when splitting.
- On the reconciliation page, explain the non-core classification in ordinary language for the standalone comic: the integration costs the company classified as outside normal operations and excluded from this profit measure, so the reader sees that management chose the classification and that the work still needs funding.
- On the reconciliation and inheritance pages, do not imply that all development and integration spending sits outside adjusted earnings: say the work needs real money whether its costs are counted in the adjusted figure or left out, and name what the 2017 report actually leaves out (development recorded as an asset; the integration costs classed as non-core); the displayed caption is rendered from the page block.

## Non-goals

A current company valuation, complete sponsor cash-flow reconstruction, a claim that all ownership periods had the same strategy or outcome, or validation of Productscapes through resemblance alone. Do not treat a source's undefined profitability measure as EBITDA.

## Open questions

Independent customer and employee histories; full investor cash flows; matched product/cohort evidence; integration outcomes; later ownership-period evidence. The early regional-company setting improves relevance but does not make the entire history a lower mid-market case.
