**Comic.** The morning after a company takes new investment or changes owners, its customer contracts, the half-finished move to a new system and the postponed hire are still there. Seven pages follow the fictional Larkspur record into a handover: keep the evidence during the work, keep money approved, work received and cash paid apart, carry the day-100 record forward, follow who receives cash and who ends up owning what, sort the open obligations by kind, and get the handover accepted by a named receiver. Each page is three strips; the amounts, dates and labels are drawn into the artwork, and a transcript follows every page.

Larkspur sells scheduling software, and every new customer has to be set up with its own settings and data before using it. A company’s owners hold its **shares**, the units that divide up its ownership. Day numbers count from the board meeting that adopted the plan just after the investment. Morgan is an investor’s adviser; Alex leads technology, Priya leads product, Sam leads finance and Ines is the chief executive officer (CEO), who runs the company. The board is the group of directors that oversees the company for its owners. All are fictional, and every figure belongs to the shared Larkspur chain that runs from the investor’s pre-deal investigation through the first hundred days to this handover. Historical cases are discussed through documents; the scenes do not reenact real events.
