**A claim that AI lets the team do more is a capacity claim, and it needs the same evidence as a hire.** Find which stage of the work a tool touches, count only work that completes to the old quality standard, and decide at a gate with rules written in advance.

At fictional Rotaline, the claim arrives in a note from Morgan, the investor’s technology adviser. Other companies report big gains from AI coding tools, Morgan writes, so Rotaline should adopt them and may not need its deferred second team at all.

Ines, the chief executive, starts where the hiring chapter did: with the trace. A standard pricing change takes ten working days. Seven are spent waiting for one of the two specialists who can safely change the invoicing module, two are building and one is release. The published studies measure building, and they disagree: one found a small task done 55.8% faster, another found experienced developers 19% slower. Even if a tool halved building at Rotaline, **it would save one day in ten**.

So the real question is what completes. **Capacity is delivered work, not activity.** Lines of code accepted, seats licensed and tokens used show how busy the tool was, not what customers received. Rotaline counts pricing changes reaching production and customer setups going live, each month, beside the share that caused an error within thirty days, checked exactly as before. More output counts only while that error rate holds.

Two ninety-day pilots follow: fourteen engineers get a coding tool, and three implementation specialists get a tool that drafts each new customer’s configuration. Both are compared with the three months before, and the people who review generated work record their hours, because **review time is a cost**, and at Rotaline the reviewers are the two specialists the plan is trying to protect. The pilots cost about €10,400 in cash, plus about €23,500 of staff time, before any extra review.

“Fewer people” gets the same scrutiny. No funding condition mentions AI, the investor’s director votes only when the roles reach the board, and Morgan’s benchmark is influence, not authority. The second team stays deferred on its three conditions: shown demand, committed money and board approval. Announcing cuts early would also silence the people best placed to report that review time had doubled.

The gate falls on day 120, once every item has had its thirty days. Worse quality, slower delivery or missing evidence withdraws the tool. A gain of 20% or more continues only if Ines can fund the added review hours by naming the work they displace. A smaller gain earns one sixty-day extension, and no gain at all withdraws the tool.

And if the new billing engineer releases a change alone, that proves the transfer, not the tool. The country rules still live in the billing code, which is the choice [[plan-for-growth]] takes up next.
