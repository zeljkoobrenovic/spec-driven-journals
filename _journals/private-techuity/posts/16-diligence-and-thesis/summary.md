Technical diligence is useful when it changes an investment decision or makes the operating plan more credible. A long report is not sufficient if material findings disappear between signing and execution.

Begin with the investment thesis. Identify which assumptions about customers, product, delivery, technology, people, or risk must hold for it to work. Then choose evidence and specialists around those assumptions. Sourcing support can improve the questions before a formal diligence process starts.

The chapter proposes seven assessment dimensions: product, architecture, engineering, data and AI, security and resilience, organization, and economics. These are a coverage aid, not an equally weighted maturity ranking. A weakness matters through its effect on this company's thesis, obligations, and feasible choices.

**Every material finding needs a disposition.** It might change the investment decision, valuation, financing, timetable, contractual protection, or post-close work. Alternatively, it may be an uncertainty consciously accepted by the authorized decision-maker. Label the difference between observed evidence, management assertion, inference, and an untested assumption.

Sampling and access constrain conclusions. Repository access does not establish product-market fit. A system demonstration does not prove recovery. An interview can reveal an important dependency while remaining a claim requiring corroboration. Record what was inspected, when, and what could not be verified.

The fictional Larkspur example turns a broad scalability thesis into a narrower investigation of implementation capacity and customer onboarding. That change affects the investment plan: growth may require knowledge transfer, product simplification, and funded delivery capacity before additional sales can become revenue.

The supplied Principal brief explicitly connects diligence with investment advice and subsequent operating work. [P01: supplied role brief](pt-bibliography.html) The manuscript therefore proposes a traceable handoff: a finding identifier survives into the thesis, initiative, decision record, and outcome review. This is a design proposal, not a proven industry standard.

The test after closing is whether company leaders recognize the problem, have accepted ownership, and can act with adequate resources. Diligence should reduce consequential uncertainty and make the remaining uncertainty governable, rather than creating confidence through the volume of its documentation.
