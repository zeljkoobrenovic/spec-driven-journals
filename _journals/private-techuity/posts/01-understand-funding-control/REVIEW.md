# Editorial review: An Investment Announcement Is Not a Budget

Reassessed 15 September 2026 against the complete current article, summary and comic text, the previous review, revision log and Git changes. See the [collection review](../REVIEW.md).

## Current assessment

The chapter now delivers its practical promise: the completed hiring record identifies the payer, cash received, board approval, contractual conditions and CEO decision. Moving the waterfall to the optional reference and placing the entity diagram beside its explanation makes the fund primer serve the company leader. Preserve the separate firm/fund/holding-company/business vocabulary, the LP cash timeline and the two possible share-sale routes.

The former categorical contrast with founder-owned businesses is corrected in the article and both companions. The current problems are new inconsistencies in the worked record, an inaccurate closing handoff, and the tone of the later investor callout. These need focused correction, not another restructuring. Four questions provide a useful ending, although the several opening highlights repeat the premise before the example begins.

## Active recommendations

- **High — Stop changing the meaning of the €100 million in “From Money and Authority to Value.”** The opening calls it an investment announcement; the conclusion says it “was a valuation before it was anything else.” The chapter never establishes that. A transaction amount and a whole-company valuation can differ, precisely the distinction the next chapter teaches. Replace with: “A headline may name transaction consideration, new funding or a valuation. The next chapter explains how to identify and interpret the valuation.” The summary already avoids this error; retain that precision.
- **Medium — Reconcile the hiring record and its decision paragraph.** “Alex proposed three” becomes “hiring five … Alex's first proposal” without an intervening change. Pick one chronology. Also make the extra approval rule explicit: exceeding the four-hire plan is not necessarily the same as exceeding the €250,000 threshold for spending outside it. At roughly €100,000 per hire, the fifth hire alone would be below that threshold. A separate headcount consent rule would make the stated decision coherent. Do not leave readers to invent the agreement.
- **Medium — Define the cash trigger as a forecast variance.** Reopening the decision whenever “net cash [is] below … €7.6m” would be triggered by normal authorized spending. Use a dated minimum liquidity or variance from the approved forecast. The record's 24-month runway is an assumption attributed to finance, not something demonstrated by the three-hire cost. Label its burn, minimum reserve and other receipts if the figure is meant to be independently reproducible.
- **Medium — Revise “WHY INVESTORS CARE.”** “Investors know exactly” and the accusation that management “has not read its own deal” assign perfect knowledge to one side and blame to the other. The intended reader may have inherited the transaction or lack access to its documents. Suggested wording: “Investors and management need an agreed account of where the money went and which conditions remain before committing to the operating plan.” In the alternative seller-payment paragraph, replace funding “out of existing earnings” with available company cash or forecast operating cash generation; the old earnings/budget shortcut should not survive in the worked example.
- **Low — Remove editorial explanation from the diagram commentary.** “Because beginners should not have to infer” describes the author's process. Simply explain which shares are sold. Keep the two sale routes and avoid a new outline.

## Verification and formats

€8m less €0.4m costs is €7.6m. The authority and cash-trigger issues above are confirmed internal inconsistencies or missing assumptions, not conclusions about a real shareholders' agreement. The summary and comic panel 6 now carry the hiring outcome; the summary inherits the unexplained five-hire consent claim. The comic's existing scene can support its caption conceptually, but this review does not certify its embedded artwork or speech bubbles.
