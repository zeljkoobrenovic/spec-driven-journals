**Artificial intelligence (AI)** is software that classifies, predicts or generates content from learned patterns. When an investor asks for an “AI strategy”, the request hides **three investment questions**: a product customers will pay for, internal work that improves enough to change spending, and a substitute for the product customers already buy. Each needs its own evidence, cost and decision, and using a tool establishes none of them. In the fictional Larkspur scenario the growth investor’s thesis assumes all three at once: a paid AI feature before the next round, lower cost per customer, and no displacement by a general-purpose assistant.

Settle **who coordinates** before answering. At Larkspur, Priya is accountable for holding the three questions together, Alex for the shared platform, evaluation method and data permissions, and Sam for the expenditure tests. Only the two or three functions with the largest routine workflows join at first: implementation, support and finance. Sales and legal join when a question reaches them. A function can name a champion with protected time and authority where the workflow justifies it. This is a proportionate proposal, not a requirement that every function has a seat.

For the **product question**, compare the complete customer workflow with the current alternative and estimate the cost per successful outcome, including review, data preparation and monitoring. Check the rights to use data for the proposed purpose; the US National Institute of Standards and Technology’s generative AI profile gives a useful risk structure. [S18: NIST generative AI profile](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf) Keep a funding demonstration separate from a product commitment. Larkspur runs a paid three-customer document-classification pilot (€45,000, six engineer-weeks) with a quality threshold agreed in advance; general release is built only if the threshold and the price are confirmed.

For the **internal-work question**, run an experiment that can change a decision: defined tasks, a comparison group, recorded tool versions and dates, review and correction counted, and stopping rules agreed first. Saved time is capacity; it becomes cash only through a spending decision such as an avoided hire. **The coding evidence is dated and mixed.** A 2022 GitHub Copilot experiment found 55.8% shorter completion times on a bounded JavaScript task; METR’s early-2025 study found experienced open-source developers 19% slower with AI allowed; METR’s February 2026 update described selection and measurement problems that make later estimates a weak guide. [S19: Peng et al., Copilot experiment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06590) [S20: METR early-2025 experiment](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/) [S21: METR 2026 update](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/) Larkspur’s eight-week support pilot (€6,000) defers a planned hire only if complete handling time falls at least 20% with the reopen rate held.

For the **substitution question**, name one customer task a substitute could perform, the switching constraint, and the evidence that would justify defending, partnering, repricing or stopping. A general assistant can draft Larkspur’s weekly maintenance schedule but cannot dispatch it or keep the regulator’s records; a five-customer test (€15,000) decides whether a planned suggestions feature resumes or stops.

Together the three decisions commit €66,000 and nine engineer-weeks from the team that also carries the roadmap. Record tools and dates so later readers know which evidence still applies.
