# Spec: The Rise of the Technology Operating Partner

## Intent

Close Part IV with a chapter explaining why technology operating partners and the functions around them are gaining importance in private equity. Connect the investor's need for technical judgment to the company leader's need for useful support, while distinguishing a growing remit from an unmeasured increase in headcount or investment returns. Extend Part IV's practical treatment of advisers without repeating its engagement charter.

## Audience

Product and engineering leaders working under investors; secondarily investment professionals building an operating capability and technology executives considering the role. Assume no finance or AI background: define portfolio, private equity, investment firm and fund, diligence, investment thesis, operating partner, artificial intelligence (AI) and pilot before relying on them, and expand role abbreviations (CIO, CTO, CPO, CEO, VP, IT, HR) at first use in every reading format. Private equity is the evidence base; other ownership models are comparisons, not an assumed equivalent.

## Success criteria

- Link the portfolio-learning responsibility to [[learn-through-investors-network]], including maintaining access and shared resources; preserve the wider operating-function argument.

- Explain the pressures behind the function: technology-dependent operating plans, continuity after diligence, acquisitions and shared learning, and AI as both an opportunity and a risk to an investment thesis.
- Define technology operating partner (including the technical operating partner wording) and distinguish the function from a job title, company CTO/CIO, investment partner and temporary consultant. Treat organizational models as choices, not a universal maturity ladder.
- Review all seven supplied sources: six directly accessible web pages and the author-supplied text copy of David Mackey’s LinkedIn post. Identify the copy as the consulted version and distinguish the recruiter’s experience-based route into the role from evidence about hiring frequency or career outcomes. Supplement with direct descriptions from KKR and Blackstone.
- Attribute industry observations and date examples. Do not invent hiring growth, average holding periods, performance premiums or a universal need for a separate AI partner.
- Map responsibilities across investment assessment, the funded early plan, delivery, leadership, acquisitions, portfolio learning and exit. Define useful outputs and the boundaries of company responsibility.
- Compare the operating partner with CIO, CTO, CPO, Chief Architect and VP/Head of Engineering by scope, accountability, delegated authority, practical collaboration and evidence of success; explain overlapping and combined roles.
- Explain the operating partner’s contribution to hiring at portfolio companies: assess the capability gap, define roles and funding, support sourcing and candidate assessment, clarify appointment and compensation authority, and help with onboarding, development and succession. Distinguish the operating partner from the company hiring manager, HR and recruiters; assess internal development and interim help alongside permanent hiring, including AI appointments.
- Explain AI as a change to investment assumptions, a company capability and a tool for the operating function itself; address evaluation, data boundaries, running costs and the case for a dedicated AI specialist.
- Explain internal, specialist-team and external models; CIO/CTO experience and AI specialization; availability, costs and supplier conflicts. Keep formal authority tied to the actual assignment and agreements.
- Include one explicitly fictional worked comparison and practical ways for a company leader to assess the function. Introduce the three-company AI support-tool example right after the role definition, say what the tool does (drafts customer-service replies from past support records for staff to check) and return to it for authority, staffing and evaluation. Keep cash, released capacity and attribution distinct: a cash saving is a fall in spending against an agreed comparison after counting the tool's own setup and running costs; unchanged pay with more work handled is capacity. Keep career advice for prospective partners as a signposted aside after the company-facing argument.
- Use the book's opening learning objective and three KEY POINTS. Aim for 3,800–5,400 words, with readable prose and at most two substantial comparison tables. Link relevant existing chapters and the bibliography.
- Add a chapter hero logo, a matching navigation icon and three explanatory figures in the book’s ivory, navy, teal and ochre visual style. Illustrate the boundary between portfolio support and company leadership, alternatives in capability building and hiring, and the fictional companies’ different AI decisions (labelling the second company's step as confirming permitted data use, not technical access). Use descriptive alt text and captions; retain generation prompts and provenance.
- Place the article last in Part IV, immediately after `investors-adviser`, in source folder `22-tech-operating-partner`. Connect the preceding adviser chapter to the wider operating function and hand off to Part V. Retain all permalinks and align navigation and counts with 34 main chapters.
- Add a 300–500-word TL;DR and a five-page illustrated comic. Preserve the operating function's continuity across the investment, company leadership and appointment authority, alternatives to hiring, and AI's effects on investment assumptions, company work and the operating function. Follow the article's independent fictional three-company AI comparison; retain the distinction between released capacity, cash savings and attribution. Reuse the journal cast as presenters, give each page three strips with dialogue and labels in the artwork, and include alt text, short captions and rendered transcripts. Preserve scripts and image provenance and inspect every page against its script.

## Non-goals

A comprehensive career guide, compensation survey, financial forecast, endorsement of a supplier, or claim that an operating partner replaces company management. Do not restore the discontinued Productscapes appendix or rewrite the existing Larkspur scenario.

## Modalities

- Article: the existing illustrated article, chapter hero logo, navigation icon and three explanatory figures.
- TL;DR: `summary.md`, a self-contained 300–500-word summary in the journal's concise prose style, reusing the portfolio-support overview after the opening paragraph; it states that the evidence comes mainly from private equity (explained in plain words), explains the investment thesis before that figure and says what the AI support tool does.
- Comic: `comics.md`, five illustrated pages of three strips each. The familiar fictional cast presents the three-company AI example alongside the operating function's role, capability choices and review evidence; the page scripts drive artwork, captions and transcripts. The comic says what the support tool does, and shows the partner asking whether AI changes what customers will buy or pay for as a question separate from whether the tool works.
