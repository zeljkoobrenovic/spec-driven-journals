An **operating model** describes how teams, responsibilities and decisions are arranged. It helps determine whether a company can turn a plan into repeated useful decisions. An organization chart and a headcount target are incomplete descriptions of that capability. Investment plans fund what can be counted, such as tools and headcount in a cheaper location, and rarely fund the slower change in how decisions are made, while the investor's timetable leaves little room for it.

**Figure 1:** *Design teams around the work, knowledge and decisions customers depend on.*

**Follow an actual piece of work** from customer need to outcome. Identify where it waits, who resolves disagreement, where knowledge is concentrated, and how quality becomes visible. A delivery problem may arise from unclear priorities, decision delays, or overloaded specialists rather than insufficient effort.

**Assess leaders in their system.** A **chief technology officer’s (CTO’s)** choices matter, but so do the authority, resources, information, and commercial commitments surrounding the role. Leadership change can be justified; it still needs a diagnosis and a transition plan. Replacing a person without changing the conditions can reproduce the failure while losing valuable knowledge.

**Offshoring** locates work abroad; **nearshoring** does so in a nearby country. Both need a complete operating case. The fictional example compares salary savings with coordination and transition costs. Include management attention, recruitment, retention, knowledge transfer, security, travel where needed, and the interfaces through which teams make decisions. A lower pay rate does not establish a lower cost of a successful customer outcome.

Capacity is also **more than paid hours**. Teams need skills, context, clear priorities, and recovery from interruptions. Cutting an apparently underutilized specialist can make several other teams slower. A small service buffer can support resilience. These claims should be tested in the actual flow of work rather than used as a blanket defense of every existing role.

The supplied role description includes staffing, leadership development, and delivery-location questions, making this work part of the Technology Principal perspective in this manuscript. [P01: supplied role brief](bibliography.html) It does not establish a universal mandate to restructure company teams.

Investor expectations need a **funded people plan**. Distinguish roles already authorized from hiring conditional on another round, allowing for recruitment, onboarding and management time. A corporate reporting change or cost-reduction target also needs a test of who will hold the knowledge and authority to serve customers. Explain the same conditions to investors and teams. Fund the transition and review the capability it creates or removes, rather than treating a headcount target as an operating model.

Use organizational health measures as diagnostic evidence alongside delivery and customer outcomes. Employee voice can reveal problems that a monthly dashboard misses. The goal is a system that can perform now, learn, and retain the knowledge required for future commitments. Explain who bears the transition burden and whether the company has funded the work needed to make the change succeed.
