**Visma**, a group of business-software companies, offers a setting for examining repeated investor entries and exits behind one continuing manager, acquisitions, local decision-making and shared support. The case uses operating evidence through 2024 and a later annual report that reconciles that year’s earnings. It matters because a change of fund, even under the same manager, can change the money and timeline behind long-running platform work.

The manager’s name, **Hg**, is constant from 2006 to 2023. The money behind it is not. Hg’s own 2017 announcement names two different funds for its 2006 and 2014 investments, records KKR’s announced complete exit in 2017, with completion stated as subject to regulatory approval, and states that Hg would represent 41% of the equity after that buyout. [S62: Hg 2017 Visma announcement](https://hgcapital.com/insights/hg-leads-usd5-3bn-buyout-of-visma) The December 2023 announcement describes a secondary sale valuing the business at €19 billion, about 20 new investors with over €1 billion, about €3 billion of new investment from existing shareholders, and Hg continuing with a majority stake. [S30: Visma 2023 transaction](https://www.visma.com/newsroom/visma-attracts-new-investors-for-further-international-expansion-in-a-transaction-valuing-the-company-at-eur-19-billion-59703d9f) Neither document says who sold in 2023, which vehicles hold Hg’s stake now, or how much money, if any, reached the company rather than departing shareholders. A secondary sale transfers existing ownership; it does not automatically finance product development.

Visma describes local entrepreneurship alongside shared infrastructure, knowledge, product investment and 33 acquisitions in 2024. [S32: Visma annual-report announcement](https://www.visma.com/newsroom/visma-releases-annual-and-sustainability-reports-for-2024-7f5c9f37) The sources contain no documented intervention with a cost and an observed customer outcome, so the operating model is a question the case raises, not a demonstrated mechanism. What buying nearly three companies a month does establish is a reason to read the earnings definitions closely.

**Continuity needs checking in the numbers as well as the ownership.** The same year appears under a different earnings definition. EBITDA means earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization; adjusted EBITDA makes further specified exclusions. The 2024 annual report shows EBITDA of €892.646 million; the 2025 annual report’s reconciliation adds €11.665 million of acquisition-related expenses to reach €904.311 million of adjusted EBITDA for the same year, the rounded €893 million and €904 million presentations. [S43: Visma 2024 annual report](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/69787181d8720ea08c1f22fe/69787181d8720ea08c1f460b_Visma-Annual-Report-2024.pdf) [S44: Visma 2025 annual report, p. 96](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/69787181d8720ea08c1f22fe/69bb9fc407e856cb8d6f7726_Visma%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf) This is a changed measure, not additional operating improvement or new cash.

The adjustment cannot resolve the budget decision. An adjusted measure is one basis for comparing operating businesses before deal costs, alongside the full cost of the strategy. A group that keeps acquiring must still plan the cash and engineer-weeks for the next transactions and for integrating the last ones, know which measure its lenders apply, and name who may approve the next purchase. **Costs can be exceptional for one transaction while recurring across a continuing program.**

After a transaction like these, recheck three things: which vehicles hold the interests, how their rights are exercised and which horizons affect platform work, without assuming one fund holds the majority; whether the shared-service budget and integration capacity are still funded, and by whom; and which earnings definition the board, the lenders and the operating plan each use. Independent customer, employee and acquired-company evidence remains a material gap, as does the contribution of shared technology support.
