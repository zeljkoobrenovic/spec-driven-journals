**Incentives are rewards and consequences that influence choices.** An investment may add new equity terms, exit expectations and fund-level incentives to the rewards and risks already shaping company decisions. Shared ownership creates some common interests, but executives, the fund, employees and customers still face different exposure, rights and time horizons.

**Management equity** gives company leaders ownership or economic rights under an agreed plan: a founder’s **rollover**, shares a manager buys, or **options** to buy shares on stated terms. **Vesting**, **preferences**, **dilution** and departure terms decide what an award can pay. Fictional examples: an executive with 5% of proceeds above a €100 million threshold receives €2 million at €140 million, not 5% of the whole; options pay only what exceeds their exercise cost. The company’s payout waterfall governs these; the fund’s **carried interest**, its manager’s share of fund profit, follows the fund’s own waterfall ([[fund-economics]]), and fund investors’ alignment principles do not cover every employee or customer concern. [S02: ILPA principles](https://ilpa.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/ILPA-Principles-3.0_2019.pdf)

**A target needs the measure it could damage beside it.** In a fictional plan, a bonus on 25% revenue growth counts only contracts with positive first-year contribution, paired with **gross revenue retention**, this year’s revenue from last year’s customers before new sales to them, at or above 90%, plus the onboarding waiting-time target. Net retention, which adds expansion, is reported beside it; growth from some customers can hide losing others. Reviewed quarterly, a failed guardrail withholds the growth component; a commission rule set before the year starts defers half the commission on loss-making contracts to first renewal. Measurement alone cannot fix a payout rule that rewards the damaging choice.

**One consolidation shows the four bets.** Closing an acquired product’s second support location saves about €600,000 a year. If the saving survives to a sale two to four years away at an assumed ten-times multiple, equity value rises by about €6 million: about €300,000 for the executive above the threshold, about €4 million to the fund at its 70% holding, and for the fund’s manager about €800,000 at an illustrative 20% carry rate, only if the whole fund has cleared its hurdle. Diversification spreads the fund’s exposure without shrinking a loss on Larkspur. Eleven of fourteen employees lose their jobs now; thirty add-on customers renew in the next two quarters, when their specialists may be gone. Larkspur chooses a staged twelve-month consolidation, three specialists retained, about €550,000 approved by the board. Either plan saves only about €150,000 in year one, so staging costs €200,000 more than the nine-month closure and delays the full saving from month ten to thirteen; the next stage pauses if renewals fall or waiting time is breached for two months. The eleven’s burden remains unresolved, and the record says so.

Review a decision by separating its quality, execution and result, and reward early reports of a failed assumption.
