# Editorial review: Management Equity, Fund Carry and Employee Jobs Are Different Bets

Reviewed 14 September 2026. Read the full article, summary and reader-facing comic text.

## Overall assessment

The argument is that apparent alignment through shares or targets conceals different payouts, risks and time horizons. It serves leaders interpreting their own incentives and setting incentives for teams. The €100m threshold example, option exercise-cost example and attention to employees and customers are useful, concrete and ethically serious without becoming a sermon.

The chapter's title has a strong point of view, but its middle broadens into leadership replacement and reporting culture. Those sections diffuse the comparison of “different bets” and duplicate the organization and governance chapters. The employee-job perspective is announced strongly but receives less development than executive compensation.

## Detailed feedback

- **Introduction and tone:** “We are all shareholders now” is an effective opening because it names a familiar claim worth examining. The added contrast with “ordinary product and engineering work” is less convincing: nonfinancial incentives, career risk and equity can exist before an outside investment. Focus on the actual new instruments and pressures.
- **Organization and flow:** Rollover, purchased shares, options and fund carry build logically. “Measures Can Reward the Wrong Work” is the right bridge from mechanics to behavior. “A Leadership Change Needs a Hypothesis” then leaves the incentive question and starts a separate diagnosis of executive effectiveness. Move that section to “Fix Decisions Before Hiring.”
- **Clarity and terminology:** The payout examples make percentages intelligible. The first equity section introduces preferences, dilution, vesting, leaver provisions and waterfalls close together; a compact worked comparison would help the reader retain the differences. Distinguish a company payout waterfall from the fund waterfall taught earlier.
- **Examples and numerical accuracy:** Five percent of the €40m excess is €2m; the option example correctly subtracts the €20,000 exercise cost from €30,000 proceeds. Both state assumptions and avoid implying a tax result. No arithmetic correction is needed. Cash requirements before a sale and departure terms remain document- and jurisdiction-specific; the article already says so.
- **Pacing and evidence:** The ILPA discussion appropriately limits LP alignment to its constituency. The wider claims about metric distortion are plausible mechanisms, but the chapter could demonstrate one actual trade-off rather than listing revenue, releases and retention in succession. Research annotations about stronger incentives and “little sign” of operating improvement need sample/date context before a reader applies them to all ownership settings; they are verification concerns here.
- **Conclusion and stakeholder coverage:** “Who Is Missing From the Alignment Story?” is the real moral and practical destination. It should have more space, ideally a decision where an executive's upside and employees' continuing risk pull apart. The final questions are useful but currently more developed than that example.
- **Other formats:** The summary and comic preserve the payout distinctions. Their introductions repeat the overly narrow account of incentives before outside ownership. The comic's employee-transition panel is a strong seed for a fuller stakeholder example in the article.

## Recommended changes

- **High impact — Complete the comparison promised by the title.** Use one proposed consolidation to compare executive proceeds, fund timing, employee job/career exposure and customer continuity. State what the company decides and which burden remains unresolved.
- **High impact — Move leadership replacement to the organization chapter.** Retain only the point that compensation or exit expectations can distort assessments of a leader. This preserves the useful diagnosis while giving it a better home.
- **Medium impact — Turn the guardrail list into one worked incentive choice.** For a revenue target, show the paired retention/service measure, who reviews it and what changes when the guardrail fails. Clarify that measurement alone cannot correct a payout rule that rewards the damaging choice.
- **Medium impact — Replace the opening generalization.** Suggested rewrite: “An investment may add new equity terms, exit expectations and fund-level incentives to the rewards and risks already shaping company decisions.” Apply it across all formats.
- **Low impact — Shorten the title only if navigation needs it.** “Equity, Carry and Jobs Are Different Bets” is an optional stylistic simplification; the existing title is distinctive and substantively justified.

## Proposed structure

Shared-shareholder claim → actual payout terms and examples → company equity versus fund carry → one incentive-driven company decision → consequences for employees and customers → how to review the decision. Keep the stakeholder ending and precise arithmetic; remove the detour into general leadership diagnosis.
