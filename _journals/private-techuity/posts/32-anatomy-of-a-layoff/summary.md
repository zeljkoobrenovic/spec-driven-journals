On 1 October the fictional Larkspur board meets the decision that [[the-financing-slipped]] left open. The €600,000 bridge commitment has arrived with a condition: the existing investors fund it only if, by 31 October, the board adopts a plan that holds the €400,000 reserve until 30 June without the delayed round. **A reduction request has to be traced to its source before it is answered.** This scenario is separate from the shared Larkspur ledger, and every figure is fictional.

The baseline comes first: forty employees, €324,000 of monthly payroll, €50,000 of contractors ending with Reduction R1, €76,000 of other costs, €250,000 of collected revenue and a €200,000 burn. Then the pressure is separated. The bridge’s condition is a **funding condition**: cash to a date, which Sam translates into a saving of at least €40,000 a month from February. The investor director’s vote under the reserved matter is a **formal approval right**, answered with a decision the board can take. The remark that other companies cut a fifth of engineering is **influence**: about €28,000 a month, chosen by ratio, which would not meet the condition.

The alternatives are costed on one basis. The hiring freeze is already in the burn, R1 removes €50,000 from November, and a pay deferral is a loan, not a saving. **R2** stops the field-worker mobile app and narrows the second country to its two signed customers: eight roles, €72,000 a month recurring, €120,000 of one-off cost. **R3**, running on collected revenue alone, would remove about sixteen further roles and the revenue base itself; it is costed as the floor, not chosen.

Reconciled by date, R2’s savings ramp from €20,000 in December to the full €72,000 in February, while severance, outplacement and accrued leave are paid first. With the bridge drawn on 15 November, cash is €505,000 on 30 June and the reserve is reached in August: the condition is met. Without R2 it is €200,000. Without the bridge the reserve is breached in January whatever R2 does. **A reduction makes money that arrives last longer; it cannot replace money that is late.**

The board adopts R2 on 15 October with the investor director’s consent; Ines gives the notices on 31 October within the plan and the law; roles are chosen by the work that stops. Afterwards Larkspur has twelve engineers on two teams, the app archived with a state-of-work record, both second-country go-lives renegotiated to September, and a transfer test the remaining engineer must pass before the specialists leave. The eight receive paid notice, severance on a stated formula, good-leaver option terms and outplacement, and hear it individually first. The thirty-two who remain get a published stopped list, an on-call rota of eight instead of ten, and the evidence that would restore the plan. Thresholds in the EU directive and the US statute decide whether a collective procedure applies and therefore the calendar; Larkspur checks its own jurisdiction before fixing the date. [S77: Council Directive 98/59/EC](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/1998/59) [S78: US WARN Act](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/29/2101) A margin-driven reduction and a post-acquisition consolidation follow the same method with different clocks.
