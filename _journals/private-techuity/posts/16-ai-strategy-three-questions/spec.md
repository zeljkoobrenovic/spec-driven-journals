# Spec: An AI Strategy Hides Three Investment Questions

## Intent

Separate the three investment questions inside an investor’s request for an AI strategy: a product customers will pay for, internal work that changes spending, and a substitute for the product customers already buy. Give each a decision, the evidence required and one completed Larkspur example, and settle who coordinates the three answers in proportion to the company’s size.

## Audience

Product and engineering leaders inside companies working under investors, including leaders who inherit an ownership arrangement. Assume no specialist finance background; explain necessary terms before use. Investor-side readers are secondary.

## Success criteria

- Link unfamiliar AI demonstrations and study visits to [[learn-through-investors-network]] for contextual learning before turning an encounter into an investment decision.

- Add one whole-post overview visual to the TL;DR after its opening paragraph, generated with the Nano Banana article illustrator. Keep the 300–500-word summary prose, bold emphasis and citations; provide alt text and a concise numbered caption.
- Provide a distinctive article header logo and a simple navigation icon with unique asset paths. Keep both consistent with the journal’s visual style.
- Include two explanatory article figures with accurate short labels, alt text and numbered captions. Use restrained bold emphasis for key claims; preserve the words, citations and historical data. The comic modality is a sequence of comic pages: each page is one image of three stacked strips that carries its dialogue, labels and amounts in the artwork, with consistent fictional characters and a short caption under each page. Every string drawn in a page comes from the page script, which traces to the article, and generated artwork is inspected against the script (spelling, cast identity, which speaker each bubble points to, counts and sizes), not assumed.
- Distinguish the date an experiment ran from its publication date, and describe percentage changes using the measured quantity and relevant participant group.
- Make the company leader’s decision, authority and funding assumptions explicit. Distinguish the stated ownership arrangement from a claim about every investor.
- Keep historical findings within their source scope and label new comparative scenarios as fictional.
- Introduce necessary terms in ordinary language before using them in explanations, examples, tables, or diagrams; keep opening key points readable without prior study.
- Move from a concrete question through explanation and example to a practical conclusion; connect the conclusion to the next chapter in the configured reading order.
- Make summaries and comic storyboards understandable on their own; preserve source limits and label fictional scenarios, including changes in example assumptions.
- Explain the title question and its implications for a concrete company decision.
- Use the three investment questions as the visible structure, each with a decision, evidence required and one Larkspur example that ends with the chosen option, alternatives rejected, funding, scarce capacity, who is authorized, a dated gate and the evidence that would change it. State recurring costs and give the net figure before a capacity result is called a saving. Keep the dated coding-study comparison as a compact evidence box inside the internal-work question.
- Make every decision rule complete: each gate states what happens when a condition is met, when it is missed, and in the space between the stated thresholds. Quality is a prerequisite for every continuation branch, and both quality measures used in a pilot continue after rollout.
- Make a deferred hire feasible in time as well as in money: state when recruitment must begin, the earliest start a later trigger allows, and who covers the interim. Every stated start date must allow the stated recruitment lead time from an explicit opening or confirmation date, with funded coverage for any gap the arithmetic leaves. Distinguish a check that fails before the deferral from deterioration after it, and give each its own financial result.
- Separate additional cash payments from existing employees' time and one-time from recurring costs wherever a figure is given. Label a figure built from internal time as a cost-based estimate rather than cash, and state the period each figure belongs to. Give the calendar-year cost of each alternative start date alongside the months-avoided figure, and state a saving net of the costs actually incurred to get it, including interim cover when it is spent.
- Trigger the staffing fallback on capacity, not on volume alone: state what happens to staffing and service when post-rollout quality fails or the time saving is lost, size the funded cover against the resulting capacity gap, and give an escalation when the allowance is not enough.
- Keep the precautionary hiring threshold distinct from a service-capacity deficit. Demand rising on its own does not remove the tool's productivity benefit, so state the usable capacity that applies when the tool runs, when it is restricted to some categories and when it is withdrawn entirely, and compute any deficit against that usable figure. Where the added person does not close the deficit, say so and state what covers the remainder.
- State the post-rollout reference for each continuing quality measure, given that rollout removes the untreated comparison group, and say that each monthly check draws a fresh sample compared within like request categories.
- Describe a dated deferral benefit as a benefit of that period, not as a recurring one, and state what a recurring benefit would additionally require.
- Give each customer-trial outcome a mutually exclusive branch with an unambiguous rule at every boundary value, an explicit end date for any extension, and a distinction between approval to continue a limited trial and approval to build a general release. Any promise made to customers before the trial must be bounded by the conditions that actually govern continuation — a customer who elects to keep paying, quality still holding, and an explicit approval — and must not contradict the stop and redesign branches.
- Keep the summary's trial attached to the concrete work it performs, explain effort-based units as effort rather than elapsed time, and describe each competitive action rather than naming it. Attribute a bounded finding about a tested configuration to that configuration in every format.
- Keep a representative accuracy sample separate from a deliberately enriched safety sample, and report the two results separately so adding safety cases cannot move the ordinary-work estimate.
- Describe each cited study's design in the terms its own methodology uses, including the unit that was randomized and the outcome actually measured.
- Explain a capacity or price threshold as a chosen margin when it is stricter than the arithmetic break-even, and show the break-even alongside it.
- Introduce each named decision-maker by role at first mention, and expand or explain finance, fundraising and technical terms in plain English at first use in every format, including the independently readable summary and comic.
- Present company-wide coordination as a proportionate proposal integrated early: who coordinates, which functions participate in this company, which decisions are shared. Champions are an option with time and authority, not a requirement for every function; state the two-or-three-functions starting point in every format.
- Use the specific investor expectation in the scenario rather than prevalence claims about investors or companies.
- Distinguish sourced observations, the manuscript's analysis, and explicitly fictional examples.
- Include a meaningful trade-off or counterargument and identify the evidence that would change the judgment.
- Link related chapters and cite substantive external factual claims close to the text.
- Keep the TL;DR and the comic page script consistent with the full article.

## Non-goals

Universal prescriptions, invented evidence, promises of investment performance, or disclosure of confidential inputs. The chapter is not a legal or tax opinion.

## Modalities

- Full Article: index.md, the substantive argument.
- TL;DR: summary.md, the practical implications in concise prose.
- Comic: comics.md, eight illustrated fictional comic pages of three strips each, with the dialogue, labels and amounts in the artwork, alt text, short visible captions, dialogue transcripts and machine-readable page scripts.
