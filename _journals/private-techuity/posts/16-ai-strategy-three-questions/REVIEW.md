# Editorial review: An AI Strategy Hides Three Investment Questions

Reassessed 15 September 2026 from the complete current article, summary and comic text where present, the previous review where present, revision log and Git changes. See the [collection review](../REVIEW.md).

## Current assessment

The three questions finally organize the chapter rather than merely introduce it. Each has a bounded decision: a paid customer pilot, an internal support experiment and a test of possible product substitution. The €66,000/nine-engineer-week portfolio makes organizational coordination proportionate and concrete. Priya's accountability, Alex's shared technical responsibilities and Sam's financial role are an effective alternative to adding a separate coordination chapter.

The article is strongest when it treats AI effects as something to measure in a particular workflow. Remaining work concerns the conversion from task speed to hiring capacity, one inconsistent pilot threshold, and the comic's coverage. Keep the current outline.

## Active recommendations

1. **Medium impact — connect the support experiment to the deferred hire.** A 20% reduction in handling time for three agents does not automatically establish capacity equivalent to a seventh full-time agent. On a simplified time-only model, three people working at 80% of the previous time per ticket supply the previous capacity of 3.75 people; with the other three unchanged, that is 6.75, before demand growth or non-ticket duties. A rollout to all six would imply 7.5 on the same restrictive assumptions. State which rollout, demand and whole-job measurements justify deferring the hire. Keep €32,000 as a gross two-quarter salary avoidance and subtract ongoing tool, review and rollout costs before calling it a net saving.

2. **Medium impact — use a consistent customer-pilot gate.** The paid pilot requires review time below 1.5 hours a week, but the later failure condition refers to review not falling below the existing four hours. Those are different thresholds. Explain whether one stops the experiment and the other authorizes wider release, or use the agreed 1.5-hour gate throughout. Because every safety-relevant case is meant to reach a human, the 95% overall classification score also needs a separate check for missed safety-relevant cases. An overall average alone does not establish that routing requirement.

3. **Medium impact — show all three questions in the comic.** The reader-facing panels remain dominated by coding-productivity studies, review and permissions. The new substitution test and paused €120,000 feature are largely absent. Replace one generic AI panel with the customer alternative and the decision it changes; use another to show the internal experiment's hire condition. The earlier request for visible coordination is addressed in text, but the current comic still does not represent the balanced three-question article.

4. **Low impact — keep the substitution example explicitly tied to an assumed configuration.** The general assistant's lack of integrations and records is plausible for the fictional test, but it is not a durable statement about all current assistants. Name it as the configuration tested by the five customers, with the date or version the chapter itself asks teams to record. The decision to pause a feature is more persuasive than a universal claim about what AI cannot do.

5. **Low impact — shorten the evidence box's reading burden.** The studies are relevant and appropriately bounded, but the dense box interrupts the internal-pilot decision. Two shorter paragraphs separating the earlier experiments from the later methodological update would preserve the evidence without competing with the three decisions. No additional generic AI introduction is needed.

## Verification and formats

The portfolio adds correctly: €45,000 + €6,000 + €15,000 = €66,000 and six + one + two = nine engineer-weeks. €64,000 annual salary gives €32,000 for two quarters before other costs. These are fictional choices, not realized savings.

[METR's February 2026 update](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/) does support the manuscript's important qualification: selection and measurement problems limit interpretation of the later productivity estimate. The researchers' belief that newer tools probably help is not a cleanly established effect size. Preserve that distinction and the task-specific scope of the earlier studies. The summary reflects the new three-part structure better than the comic. This review verifies text and selected evidence, not model performance or artwork.
