A **company investment** commits resources now for an expected future benefit. Choosing investments requires a view of both cash and **team capacity**, the time and capability available to do the work. Several attractive projects can fit one limit and exceed the other. Investor ownership changes the conditions of that choice: establish the actual funding, authority and deadline before committing, and name the result the funding was agreed on.

In a fictional Larkspur exercise, the board authorizes one envelope for the first hundred days after closing, up to €500,000 of additional cash and 24 engineer-weeks, the same plan [[first-hundred-days]] follows. Ines, the CEO, proposes the combination; the board approves it. Routine operating work is already budgeted; the change budget must also fund the agreed recovery requirement. Four proposals compete: service restoration, REC-1 (€80,000, four weeks); a simpler customer-setup step, ONB-1 (€180,000, twelve weeks); a full customer portal (€220,000, twelve weeks); and portal research (€20,000, two weeks). A fifth, KNW-1, needs no cash and four protected specialist-weeks outside the 24. The four total €500,000 and 30 engineer-weeks. **The cash fits; the engineering time does not.**

Sort the rows by what they rest on. Restoration and KNW-1 meet agreed obligations, though the company still chooses how to meet them. The setup change rests on customer evidence and on the growth plan’s assumption that onboarding can grow without a proportional increase in the implementation team, the one investor condition that matters here. The portal rests on an assumption; the research row exists to test it. **Opportunity cost** is the benefit of the best alternative a choice gives up; a shared specialist makes it binding even when both projects have funding.

The approved plan funds restoration, the setup change and the research, with KNW-1 alongside: €280,000 and 18 engineer-weeks, leaving €220,000 and six weeks in reserve. The full portal is deferred; spare money does not make twelve weeks fit into six, and its benefit is untested. Inside the plan Ines authorizes spending; any reserve draw or change to what the envelope funds stays with the board. The record names who is accountable and what evidence would change the choice.

Then review the combination, not each project. By the day-45 restoration test the €80,000 and four weeks had been spent on the restore environment, backup access, fifteen-minute backups and the test itself. The service took eleven hours against an agreed four: a departed engineer’s credential and an unavailable database version. That spending is **sunk cost**; the obligation remains. Correction and a retest by day 85 need about €20,000 and two weeks from the reserve, so Ines asks the board, which approves: restoration now €100,000 and six weeks, €300,000 and 20 engineer-weeks committed, €200,000 and four weeks left, the portal still deferred. The retest passed; the day-100 review drew the last €40,000 and four weeks for a data-quality step, leaving €340,000 committed, all 24 weeks used and €160,000 in reserve.
