**Comic.** Mira, Northline’s product leader, and Tomas, its engineering leader, choose a learning visit arranged by their investor and examine what they can bring home. An investor puts money into a business expecting a financial return, and it often knows people across the businesses it has funded. Northline is fictional: it makes inventory software, which tracks the goods a business holds in stock, for small wholesalers, businesses that buy goods in quantity and sell them on to shops. The team is considering reorder suggestions from artificial intelligence (AI), software that learns from past sales and current stock and recommends what to buy again. Five pages follow one chronology: the choice of format, the approval of time and money, the visit, the return discussion with the reframed question, and the learning review a month later. Each page is three strips; the amounts and labels are drawn into the artwork, and a transcript follows every page.

Northline’s example is independent of the book’s Larkspur budget and calendar; its cast is its own, and every figure is illustrative. The host, the support lead and the chief executive appear as unnamed supporting figures. The Prosus, Balderton and Insight Partners activities in the article are described from their organizers’ own accounts; the scenes here do not reenact them.

The team is considering reorder suggestions generated by artificial intelligence (AI): software that learns from past sales and current stock and recommends what to buy again. Their investor — a business that puts money into companies expecting a financial return, and that often knows people across the businesses it has funded — offers three kinds of access: a broad five-day international tour, an online seminar, or introductions for a two-day regional visit to businesses whose staff use similar systems every day. Mira and Tomas want to understand how those people decide whether to accept a suggestion during a busy working day. They choose the regional visit, because the hosts agree to show their everyday work steps and discuss exceptions; the seminar would offer less access to the people doing the work, and the wider tour would give less time to this question.
