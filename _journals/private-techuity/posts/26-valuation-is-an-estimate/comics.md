- *Strip 3.* **Sam:** “Cash is money actually received or paid, on its own dates.” **Priya:** “So we can show a profit and still be short of cash.”

**Page 4: Why EBITDA, and what it leaves out.** EBITDA leaves out interest (the charge for borrowing), income taxes, and depreciation and amortization, so that operating earnings can be compared across businesses with different financing, tax circumstances and asset histories. The excluded costs still affect value: interest must be paid, and assets may need replacing. EBITDA cannot establish how much cash the business can spend. An adjusted EBITDA excludes further specified costs; some improve comparability, others remove costs the business will keep incurring. Ask for a reconciliation, a line-by-line calculation showing how one reported number becomes another. Morgan is a technology adviser at an investment firm.
