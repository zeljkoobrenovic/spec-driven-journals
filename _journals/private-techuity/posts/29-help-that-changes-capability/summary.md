A **capability** is something the company can reliably do. Support is useful when it develops a missing capability, resolves a constraint or improves a decision, so begin with the company need, not with an expert, a tool or an introduction. An investor may give Larkspur faster access to relevant people and experience. Compare that access with the help the company could obtain elsewhere, including the time and obligations each route brings. Investors offer support for mixed reasons: a stronger holding, a shared operating service’s economics, portfolio relationships and reputation. The offer is real and not free of interest; neither makes it the best route.

Describe the gap through the work that fails. In fictional Larkspur, three of five sampled implementations needed one specialist’s manual configuration, about 80 hours each. Distinguish four requests: help understanding the problem, help deciding, people to do work and help developing the company’s skills.

An investor’s **operating team**, where it has one, helps companies improve their work; its **portfolio companies** are the businesses its funds invest in; **peer networks** connect leaders who exchange experience. KKR’s Capstone description shows a firm advertising such support, not its availability or effectiveness for any company. [S22: KKR Capstone description](https://www.kkr.com/approach/capstone) Peer sessions earn their time when they start from decisions participants actually face, keep information within agreed boundaries and record a practice’s context. A corporate investor’s shared service brings conditions: who sees the data, what leaving costs, what continues after an ownership change. The best route may be outside the investor’s network.

**Compare credible alternatives for the same need.** In the week after closing, Priya and Alex weigh a peer conversation (an hour within two weeks, no cost), the investor’s specialist introduced by Morgan (ten days over six weeks from day 21 after closing, capped at €1,500 a day, so €15,000 at most from the €180,000 ONB-1 budget), an independent specialist (procurement and references take seven to nine weeks, so a six-week assignment would end after the day-90 cohort measurement; rate unverified) and a permanent hire (three to four months, about €150,000 a year recurring). They choose the investor’s specialist for a bounded assignment ending about day 63, alongside a Larkspur engineer, after the peer conversation and provisional on references. The independent specialist is the fallback; hiring stays deferred pending pilot evidence. Priya authorizes it within the pilot budget, Alex releases six engineer-days from portal research, and the decision would change if the start slipped past day 21 or the specialist’s experience turned out to be with much larger customers.

**Borrow experience with its context attached.** Introduced customers may differ from the intended market; a reused tool may need administration the team lacks. A company may sensibly keep buying a scarce specialist service if the continuing cost and responsibility are explicit.

The search ends with a written request: “Help our customer team make one setup path repeatable, using a specialist who can work alongside our engineer for six weeks; Priya will assess customer outcomes.” The next chapter turns it into a charter.
