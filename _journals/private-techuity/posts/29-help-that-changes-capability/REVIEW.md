## Active recommendations

1. **Medium impact — put the provider comparison on one calendar.** Priya wants the path repeatable “within the pilot's first six weeks,” but the chosen specialist starts in three weeks and works for six weeks. Those statements imply completion around pilot week nine. The next chapter's week-three/day-20 baseline introduces another clock. Define pilot day zero, engagement start, baseline and handover dates once. Also clarify whether the independent route's four-to-six-week procurement period is its total lead time or additional to the investor specialist's three weeks; that determines whether a six-week assignment really misses the day-90 review.

2. **Medium impact — show a price or cap for the selected engagement.** “Charged to the company,” “likely similar” and “Sam confirms the fit” establish a cost category, not an inspectable cost comparison. Give a fictional day rate or fixed cap, state what the ten days cost, and identify the portion of ONB-1 it uses. Keep the independent provider's price and relevant experience explicitly unverified until checked. The choice can be provisional on availability and references; it should not appear fully evidenced simply because the investor's contact has been assigned favorable assumptions.

3. **Low impact — remove the sentence explaining what a sourcing chapter should contain.** It is editorial scaffolding. Start the comparison with Priya and Alex's decision. The concrete written request is a strong conclusion; preserve it rather than adding another generic capability test afterwards.

4. **Low impact — keep the opening callout scoped to the investor described.** “Operating support is part of what investors sell to their own investors” describes some fund managers, not all investors introduced in the collection. “Where a fund manager promises operating support…” would fit the body, which already allows no operating team and alternative sources.
