# Editorial review: Find the Help That Changes What Your Team Can Do

Reviewed 14 September 2026. Read the full article, summary and reader-facing comic text.

## Overall assessment

The post argues for selecting support by a missing company capability, checking fit and availability, and planning how the result will continue. It serves leaders who are offered networks, specialists or shared services. Its distinction between advice and delivery, warning about introduced customers outside the target market and acceptance of sensible continuing specialist services are strong.

The article has two overlapping catalogues of help and repeats much of the engagement chapter. Its opening also claims an exclusivity the body later undermines: useful support is said to exist only because of the investor, although readers are rightly encouraged to consider outside providers.

## Detailed feedback

- **Introduction and confirmed inconsistency:** “This kind of help exists only because of the investor” and “a single company couldn't reach” are unjustified. Companies can develop peer, specialist and customer networks independently; investor access may extend or accelerate them. “The best route may be outside the investor's network” later recognizes exactly that. Correct this framing in all formats.
- **Organization and flow:** The onboarding capability gap is a good start. The company-need table is useful; the following investor-type table repeats many of its fields and revisits a comparison used throughout the book. The chapter then moves through introductions, capability transfer, hiring and availability, largely restating both tables.
- **Clarity and terminology:** The four request types—diagnosis, decision help, delivery and skills development—are especially clear and should be retained. The billing-code example is precise but duplicates earlier chapters and changes the opening problem unnecessarily. “Operating team … where it has one” is appropriately qualified in the article; the summary should retain that condition.
- **Examples and pacing:** “Start Small and Transfer the Work” provides a plausible bounded assignment, but the team never compares actual providers. It is a sourcing chapter without a sourcing decision. The passage allowing a useful continuing service is important: do not edit it into a blanket independence requirement.
- **Evidence and unsupported claims:** The Capstone citation establishes an advertised offering, and the text correctly says so. Statements about investors' reasons for offering support should allow mixed motives, including service economics and relationship goals, rather than assume every offer is explained by increasing the holding's value. Research annotations should keep study design and sample limits when making selection-versus-help claims; those estimates cannot identify the impact of this fictional engagement.
- **Tone and conclusion:** “Borrow experience with its context attached” is a concise and useful expression of the author's voice. The closing “concrete request” is the right handoff, but it would be stronger if the request were actually written.
- **Other formats:** Summary and comic repeat the exclusivity claim. Their capability-transfer scenes are more concrete than the general catalogues and could anchor the revised article.

## Recommended changes

- **High impact — Correct the opening.** Suggested rewrite: “An investor may give Larkspur faster access to relevant people and experience. Compare that access with the help the company could obtain elsewhere, including the time and obligations each route brings.”
- **High impact — Show a selection among credible alternatives.** Compare a peer conversation, investor specialist, independent specialist and permanent hire for the same onboarding need. State availability, local effort, cost category, relevant experience and the reason for the chosen first step.
- **Medium impact — Keep one support map.** Retain the company-need table and fold only genuinely different corporate-policy or commercial conditions into it. Remove the second investor-category catalogue.
- **Medium impact — End with the actual request.** For example: “Help our customer team make one setup path repeatable, using a specialist who can work alongside our engineer for six weeks; Priya will assess customer outcomes.” Let the next chapter negotiate its detailed commitments.
- **Low impact — Move repeated charter and coaching detail to the adjacent chapters.** Keep one paragraph on continuing dependence and a link to the engagement review.

## Proposed structure

Missing onboarding capability → four kinds of request → comparison of actual sources, including outside the network → provider selection and uncertainties → concrete request handed to the engagement chapter. If a shorter collection is the priority, combine this sourcing sequence with “Useful Engagement” as one article on selecting and using help; retain the adviser-role chapter separately.
