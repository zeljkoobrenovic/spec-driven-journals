Larkspur is a fictional software company. New customers cannot use its product until it is set up for them — slow work that leans on one person. An investor has bought an ownership share and offers help. A **capability** is something a company can reliably do; support earns its cost when it builds a missing one. Begin with the need, not an expert.

![A concrete company need is matched to available support, full costs and an accountable path to repeatable capability.](assets/images/11-help-that-changes-capability/summary-at-a-glance.jpeg)
**Figure 1:** *Request the help that closes a specific gap in company work.*

Investors reach relevant people faster, and offer support for mixed reasons: a stronger company raises the value of their ownership share, and a shared advisory team needs work to justify its cost. The offer may be sincere and also serve the investor’s own interests; neither fact tells you whether it is the best fit.

**Describe the gap through the work that fails.** The investigation before the investment sampled five setups. In three, **configuration** — adjusting the software to suit a customer — needed one specialist's manual attention. Across all five, average total effort was about 80 hours per customer, including data cleaning, customer sessions and rework.

**Compare credible alternatives for the same need.** Day 0 is the board meeting that adopted the plan, days after the investment completed; dates count from it. It set aside €180,000 for ONB-1, the **onboarding pilot** — a limited trial of a better customer setup. Priya, accountable for the pilot, and Alex weigh four routes:

- **A peer conversation** with a leader at another company the investor owns. An hour within two weeks; no fee, half a day of preparation.
- **The investor's specialist.** Ten days over six weeks from day 21, capped at €1,500 a day: €15,000 at most from the pilot budget.
- **An independent specialist.** Finding and contracting one takes seven to nine weeks, so it would finish after day 90, when the eight pilot customers are measured. Rate unverified.
- **A permanent hire.** Three to four months to start, then about €150,000 a year in pay and other employment costs.

They choose the investor's specialist, ending about day 63, provisional on **reference checks** — conversations with people who have seen the specialist’s previous work. The independent route is the fallback, used only if this one falls through; hiring waits for evidence. The **board** — the company's directors — lets Ines, the chief executive, approve spending inside the plan; she passed this to Priya. Alex releases six engineer-days from other research. The choice would change if the start slipped past day 21, or the checks showed only much larger customers.

**Borrow experience with its context attached.** An introduced customer may not resemble your market; a reused tool may need administration you lack. Deliberately keeping a specialist service is fine if its cost and responsibilities are explicit.


The search ends with a request: "Help our customer team make one setup path repeatable, using a specialist who can work alongside our engineer for six weeks; Priya will assess customer outcomes." The next chapter turns it into a **charter** — the written agreement fixing the work, its cost and who directs it.
