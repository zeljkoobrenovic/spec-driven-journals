**Comic.** Larkspur is a fictional software company. Before a new customer can use its product, Larkspur has to set the product up for them, and that setup takes too long and depends too much on one person. An investor has bought an ownership share in the business and offers help. Seven pages start from what the company actually needs, compare the investor’s offer with help available elsewhere, and end with the written request that the next chapter turns into a **charter** — the short written agreement that fixes what a helper will do, for how long, at what cost and under whose direction. Each page is three strips; the amounts, dates and labels are drawn into the artwork, and a transcript follows every page.

Morgan advises the investor on technology; Alex leads technology at Larkspur, Priya leads product and is accountable for the trial, Sam leads finance and Ines is the chief executive. ONB-1 is the name of that trial: the wider customer-setup pilot, with €180,000 set aside for it, a starting measurement taken at day 20, the eight pilot customers measured at day 90 and the **board** — the directors who oversee the company and approve its major decisions — reading the result at day 100. Inside that pilot sits one six-week assignment by an outside specialist, whose fee is capped at €15,000. Days are counted from day 0, the board meeting that adopted the operating plan; day 21 matters because that is the earliest the investor’s specialist could start. All are fictional. Comparative scenarios use separate assumptions; the scenes do not reenact real events.
