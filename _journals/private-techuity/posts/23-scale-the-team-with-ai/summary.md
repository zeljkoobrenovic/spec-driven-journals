**Artificial intelligence (AI)**, software that predicts or generates content from data, reaches Larkspur, a fictional company, as a reason to buy tools and hire fewer people. Ines, the chief executive, tests it as a capacity claim at a **gate**, a fixed day with prewritten rules.

**Capacity is work that completes**, not **headcount** (people employed); active **seats** (licensed accounts) and AI-suggested code developers kept are activity. Evidence is **throughput**, every change and customer setup delivered monthly, beside the error rate (items per hundred causing an error within thirty days). More throughput counts only while that rate holds.

**Attach the claim to a stage.** **Traced** (each stage and wait recorded), an approved type of pricing change (say, a phone-booking surcharge) takes ten working days: seven waiting for one of two specialists who can safely change the **invoicing module**, which prepares customer bills, two building, one releasing it to customers. Halving building saves one day in ten if the queue holds, which the pilot measures.

**Two pilots, one method.** In two ninety-day **pilots** (limited trials), fourteen engineers get a coding tool and three specialists one drafting each new customer’s **configuration** (settings such as currency and invoice day). Each is compared with the prior three months under old checks: monthly **invoice reconciliation** (matching invoices to orders and prices) or customer corrections.

**Count the cost first.** About €10,400 of expected cash over four months, €12,800 at most, for **licences** (paid rights to use tools), usage charges and legal review, plus about €23,500 of estimated staff time (not cash), excluding extra review hours the pilot measures.

**“Fewer people” is a claim to test.** Morgan, the investor’s adviser, suggests dropping the deferred second team. No **funding condition** in Larkspur’s **term sheet** (proposed terms, not guaranteed money) ties money to AI-related staffing or productivity. The investor’s **director** on the overseeing **board** votes only when the roles reach it. A **benchmark**, other companies’ results for comparison, is influence, not authority. The team stays deferred on demand, committed money (investors signed to pay) and board approval.

**The gate, day 120.** Work delivered by day 90 is judged after thirty days’ evidence; gain is the percentage change in monthly deliveries. Missing evidence, a higher error rate or a longer **lead time** (request to delivery) withdraws the tool. 20% or more continues only if Ines funds the added review hours, naming work they displace; 0% or less withdraws. In between earns one sixty-day extension, judged on day 210 after follow-up: 20% or more continues on the same conditions; less withdraws. Both dates assume no pause: any tool-use month above the baseline error rate pauses the tool a month, delaying the dates; even a passing result then awaits Ines’s written restart.

A billing engineer is learning the two specialists’ work. An error-free change the engineer releases without either specialist’s help shows someone else can now do it, not that AI sped the learning or made engineers unnecessary.
