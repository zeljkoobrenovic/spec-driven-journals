**Artificial intelligence (AI)**, software that predicts or generates content from data, reaches Larkspur, a fictional scheduling-software company, as a reason to buy tools and as a reason not to hire. Ines, the chief executive, treats both as one capacity claim: two ninety-day pilots with a dated gate, and the deferred second team’s conditions unchanged.

**Capacity is work that completes.** Capacity is the work a team finishes, not the people it employs. Lines accepted, drafts produced, seats active and **tokens** (the units AI services meter) are activity. Evidence is changes and setups completed per month, checked as before the tool, with review time counted as cost.

**Attach the claim to a stage of the trace.** After the hiring chapter’s delegation, a pricing change has a **lead time**, request to the live system, of about ten working days: up to three waiting for a rule decision, seven in the two specialists’ review queue, two building, one release. No tool changes who is authorized to decide. The queue moves only if someone else can change the invoicing module safely, a knowledge-transfer test. Halving building saves one day in ten and feeds the same reviewers.

**Two pilots, one method.** A **pilot** is a limited trial before committing. Fourteen engineers get a coding tool; three implementation specialists get one that drafts customer configurations. Each measures completed work against the three months before, with the old quality check on fresh samples: invoice reconciliation for code, customer corrections within thirty days for setups. Reviewers record their own review time. Published coding studies disagree and measure others’ work. [S19: Peng et al., Copilot experiment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06590) [S20: METR early-2025 experiment](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/)

**Count the cost first.** About €10,000 of cash: **licences**, the paid right to use the tools, usage charges and legal review of the data terms. About €23,500 of employees’ time as a cost-based estimate, not cash, for training, security review and pilot management, before review time, unknown until measured. That time comes from scheduled maintenance, not the protected transfer day.

**“AI means fewer people” is a claim to test.** Morgan, the investor’s adviser, writes that the deferred second team may not be needed. A **funding condition** ties money to an event; Larkspur’s term sheet has none. A director’s vote carries authority when board approval is sought; none has been cast. A benchmark from other companies is influence, answered with Larkspur’s own evidence. The team was deferred on demand, committed money and board approval; a tool changes none of them, only the roles’ size and shape once they are met.

**The gate, day ninety.** Quality first: more reconciliation errors or setup corrections withdraws the tool regardless of throughput. A gain of at least 20% with quality holding continues the tool and enters the staffing arithmetic; between the baseline and 20%, sixty more days, no new seats, once; at or below the baseline, withdrawn. If the billing engineer releases a country-rule change unreviewed and reconciliation is clean, the transfer has shortened: the third specialist arrives sooner; no engineer becomes unnecessary.
