# Spec: You Cannot Fund Every Good Project at Once

## Intent

Translate investor expectations into a feasible combination of product and engineering work under two separate limits, cash and team capacity. Lead with the worked Larkspur exercise, state the funding boundary, complete the selection as a full decision record, and show one review that revises the combination. Keep only the one investor condition relevant to the exercise and link to the financing comparisons for the rest.

## Audience

Product and engineering leaders inside companies working under investors, including leaders who inherit an ownership arrangement. Assume no specialist finance background; explain necessary terms before use. Investor-side readers are secondary.

## Success criteria

- Add one whole-post overview visual to the TL;DR after its opening paragraph, generated with the Nano Banana article illustrator. Keep the 300–500-word summary prose, bold emphasis and citations; provide alt text and a concise numbered caption.
- Provide a distinctive article header logo and a simple navigation icon with unique asset paths. Keep both consistent with the journal’s visual style.
- Include two explanatory article figures with accurate short labels, alt text and numbered captions. Use restrained bold emphasis for key claims; preserve the words, citations and historical data. The comic modality is a sequence of comic pages: each page is one image of three stacked strips that carries its dialogue, labels and amounts in the artwork, with consistent fictional characters and a short caption under each page. Every string drawn in a page comes from the page script, which traces to the article, and generated artwork is inspected against the script (spelling, cast identity, which speaker each bubble points to, counts and sizes), not assumed.
- Make the company leader’s decision, authority and funding assumptions explicit. Distinguish the stated ownership arrangement from a claim about every investor.
- Keep historical findings within their source scope and label new comparative scenarios as fictional.
- Define necessary terms before using them, and begin with a recognizable company decision.
- Develop one connected explanation from the question through options and example to an actionable conclusion.
- Label fictional scenarios and proposed working methods; do not imply validated performance or guaranteed access to resources.
- Include a useful trade-off and conditions for changing the decision, and show one review in which new evidence changes the selected combination.
- State the envelope as a limit on commitments made in the first hundred days, and keep committed, incurred and finished apart: say “allocated” or “committed” where work or payments run past day 100.
- Account for scarce people, not only cash: KNW-1 states the time of both the specialist and the second engineer, which budget carries it, and what ordinary work gives way, without counting any week twice against the 24. Every numerical summary of the proposals (the article table’s training row with the note beneath the table, the decision record, the summary and the comic’s two-limits page) shows the same figure: about eight protected person-weeks outside the 24, four from the specialist and about four from the second engineer. The table’s effort cells stay short enough to read beside the project names at desktop width: the training cell gives the figure in brackets, the total row compares 30 with the 24, and the trainer and learner breakdown sits in the note directly beneath the table.
- State the recovery requirement in full (dispatch back within four hours, no more than fifteen minutes of schedule updates lost) before the selection; describe the day-45 failure against both parts; qualify the day-85 retest as passing for loss of the application environment in one region, with a regional outage untested.
- Record the continuing cost a selection creates: ONB-1’s €30,000 a year of upkeep from the next financial year, paid from the operating budget and outside the €500,000 envelope. The article, the summary and the comic (in the artwork and caption of its combination page) each state it.
- The formats describe the table’s rows the same way: four proposals that need additional cash (the portal research and the portal build being two stages of one idea) and a fifth training item on separately protected time. The article’s section heading does not count the requests.
- Dense decision passages are split so that one sentence carries one decision or comparison: the day-0 funding-and-capacity bullet, and the comic gives the failure, the corrective approval and the retest’s scope a page each, with the €20,000 and two weeks approved kept apart from the revised totals.
- Each reading format stands alone for a lay reader: the summary and the comic define an engineer-week, name the projects descriptively rather than by record code, introduce Ines as chief executive officer and the board, and explain sunk cost and reserve where they use them. Each format also says what the customer portal is (an online workspace customers use themselves) and what a cloud region is (one geographic group of the cloud provider’s computing facilities), so the retest’s limit reads as: systems rebuilt inside a region that kept working, not recovery when that whole location is unavailable. The article describes where the backup credentials go by function (one centrally controlled store for access credentials), not as a “managed service”.
- Comic artwork carries the worked example: the first page shows the four Larkspur requests by name, the two-limits page shows 24 weeks available against 30 needed with 6 over and the about eight protected training weeks kept apart, and the revision page shows the original plan beside the plan after the failed test. Visible labels, captions and alt text agree.
- Connect the preceding and following chapters in the configured reading order.
- Provide an independently readable summary of 300–500 words, counted as rendered (prose, table headings and cells, and expanded cross-link titles; the image line and its caption excluded), and eight fictional comic pages of three strips each, with consistent visible and structured text; generate the page artwork from the recorded page scripts.

## Non-goals

Investment advice, universal legal rights, provider endorsements, new claims about historical company returns, or confidential input disclosure.

## Open questions

The methods would benefit from future observed company engagements. Current examples illustrate reasoning and are not evidence of effectiveness.
