A **company investment** commits resources now for an expected future benefit. It needs both cash and **team capacity**, the time and skills available to do the work.

![Investment options are constrained by both cash and team capacity, with staged choices revised as evidence changes.](assets/images/17-cannot-fund-everything/summary-at-a-glance.jpeg)
**Figure 1:** *Choose a feasible combination and preserve room to learn.*

Larkspur, a fictional scheduling-software company, has a new investor. Its board, the directors who oversee the company, limits new commitments in the first hundred days after the investment to €500,000 of additional cash and 24 **engineer-weeks**. One engineer-week is one person’s work for one week. Routine work is already budgeted. Ines, the chief executive officer, proposes the plan; the board approves. The customer portal would be an online workspace customers use themselves.

| Proposal | Additional cash | Engineer-weeks |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Improve service restoration | €80,000 | 4 |
| Simplify one customer-setup step | €180,000 | 12 |
| Build a full customer portal | €220,000 | 12 |
| Research whether customers need the portal | €20,000 | 2 |
| **Total** | **€500,000** | **30, against 24** |

**The cash fits; the engineering time does not.** A fifth item needs no additional cash: a specialist trains a second engineer to run and recover critical scheduling software. It takes about eight weeks, four from each person, set aside before the 24 were counted.

Sort the proposals by what they rest on. Restoration and the training meet agreed obligations: Larkspur has promised its service back within four hours of a failure, losing at most fifteen minutes of updates. The setup change rests on customer evidence and the condition the investor funded: many more customers set up without the setup team growing as fast. The portal rests on an assumption the research tests. Each choice has an **opportunity cost**, the best alternative given up: two funded projects that need the same specialist cannot both proceed.

The board approves restoration, the setup change and the research, with the training alongside. The uncommitted rest is the **reserve**; only the board may draw on it. The full portal is deferred: spare money does not make its twelve weeks fit into the six left. The setup step’s upkeep, €30,000 a year, comes later from the operating budget, outside the limit.

Then review the whole combination. At day 45 the restoration test failed: eleven hours, and a night of updates lost. The €80,000 and four weeks already spent are **sunk cost**: spending that cannot be recovered, and so cannot by itself justify more. The promise still stands, so the board draws on the reserve for a correction and a retest.

| Decision | Cash committed | Weeks allocated | Reserve left |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Day 0: plan approved | €280,000 | 18 | €220,000, 6 weeks |
| About day 47: correction, retest | €300,000 | 20 | €200,000, 4 weeks |
| Day 100: customer-data checks before setup | €340,000 | 24 | €160,000, 0 weeks |

The day-85 retest passed, for one kind of failure. A **cloud region** is one geographic group of the cloud provider’s computing facilities. Larkspur rebuilt its own systems inside a region that kept working; losing the whole region was not tested. Committed is not spent, and allocated is not finished: some work and payments run past day 100.

Record each decision, and update the record after each review.
