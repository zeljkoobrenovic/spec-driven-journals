**Each new owner starts from a new price, but the product work carries on.** TeamSystem passed through four owners in sixteen years, and its accounts show what the next one actually inherits.

Palamon invested in TeamSystem, an Italian maker of software for accountants and smaller companies, in 2000. Bain Capital bought it in 2004, Hg took control in 2010, and Hellman & Friedman followed in March 2016, with Hg keeping a smaller stake. Hg reported eleven acquisitions during its ownership. Each buyer paid, in its price, for progress earlier owners had made.

The first full year after the 2016 sale shows why the numbers need care. Because the group changed hands in March, its 2016 accounts covered only ten months, so dividing 2017 revenue by that short year suggests 37.7% growth. Management's rebuilt full-year comparison, which also counts businesses bought during 2016 from January, gives 8.9%, and even that includes companies bought in 2017. **Compare the same months before you compare performance.** The same trap catches technology measures such as revenue per engineer.

Then profit. The 2017 report turns adjusted EBITDA, an earnings measure before borrowing costs, tax, asset charges and costs management classes as non-core, of €113.0 million into a loss of €56.8 million. Two lines carry most of that gap, and neither is a cash payment of its size. The €72.5 million of amortization and depreciation spreads the cost of software and customer relationships valued in the 2016 purchase. The €72.0 million finance charge includes estimates and fees; the finance costs actually paid were €52.1 million. [S49: TeamSystem 2017 report](https://www.teamsystem.com/media/files/865_Consolidated%20Financial%20Statements%20as%20at%20and%20for%20the%20year%20ended%2031%20December%202017%20of%20TeamSystem%20Group.pdf)

So neither headline is the cash available for the next product plan. The adjusted figure sits above those payments, about €25 million of capital spending and €20 million of non-core costs. The loss, mostly the price of the purchase and the debt that funded it, does not prove the software got worse.

Development shows the same split. The company recorded €13.4 million of 2017 development as an asset, charged against profit over later years. That changes when the cost reaches the accounts, **not whether the cash is paid**. It also recorded €1.9 million for connecting and changing IT systems: left out of the adjusted measure, but still work a team must do.

For the leader inheriting that work, a new owner's starting point is not the start of the product's obligations. Customer migrations, development commitments and dependence on particular people's knowledge span ownership periods. Carry a record of commitments, costs and results across the handover, ask the incoming owner which parts of its plan rely on work already under way and whether it has funded the rest, and agree which evidence would change the plan.

TeamSystem shows what a group accumulates across its owners: useful products and knowledge, but also obligations and continuing work. The final chapter asks who received the gains and who carried the costs: [[success-for-whom]].
