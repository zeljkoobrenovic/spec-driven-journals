**TeamSystem**, an Italian business-software group, passed from Palamon's 2000 investment to Bain Capital in 2004, Hg in 2010 and Hellman & Friedman in March 2016. Hg reported eleven acquisitions during its ownership. The 2017 accounts, after the sale, recorded development as an asset and costs of connecting and changing systems. The sequence makes the handover question concrete: which product work and financial obligations come with the business the next owner buys?

Hg's announced 2010 purchase put **enterprise value** at €565 million. That is a price for the whole business, shareholders' and lenders' claims together; the shares alone are worth roughly that minus debt plus cash. Each owner started from its own purchase price.

**Lesson one: compare equivalent periods.** The company changed hands in March 2016, so its 2016 accounts covered ten months; dividing the two reported totals shows 37.7% growth in **revenue** (sales recorded before costs). Against a 2016 rebuilt as a full year that also counts businesses bought during 2016 from January, growth is 8.9%, and even that includes businesses bought in 2017. [S49: TeamSystem 2017 consolidated report](https://www.teamsystem.com/media/files/865_Consolidated%20Financial%20Statements%20as%20at%20and%20for%20the%20year%20ended%2031%20December%202017%20of%20TeamSystem%20Group.pdf)

**Lesson two: separate accounting earnings from payments.** The 2017 report lists the steps that turn adjusted **EBITDA** of €113.0 million into a loss of €56.8 million. EBITDA is earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization: profit before borrowing costs, tax and the yearly charges that spread asset and acquisition costs over their years of use. The largest charge, €72.5 million of depreciation and amortization, spreads the cost of earlier purchases, largely software and customer relationships valued in the 2016 purchase; recording it moved no cash; check related payments separately.

The €72.0 million net finance cost is the accounting cost of borrowing, not the bill: €52.1 million was paid as finance costs, and other financing payments (loan fees and amounts owed to sellers of acquired companies) are reported separately. Recording €13.4 million of development as an asset deferred its cost in the accounts but **did not remove its cash cost**; plan cash by payment dates.

**Lesson three: carry unfinished obligations across ownership changes.** HgCapital, the investment firm now called Hg, sold part of its TeamSystem investment in 2016 and kept the rest, a **partial exit**. HgCapital Trust, whose shares trade on the London stock exchange and which invests through Hg's **funds** (investors' pooled money Hg manages), received £39 million in cash and kept a stake valued at £6.1 million. [S48: HgCapital Trust 2015 results, p. 14](https://www.hgcapitaltrust.com/~/media/Files/H/Hgcapital-Trust-V2/documents/investors/financial-calendar/hgcapitaltrust-dec-2015-general.pdf) That stake was sold in 2021, when the Trust reinvested through a later Hg fund, and none of the sources examined states the **net cash** reaching its shareholders, the money left after fees and deductions. A new owner's financial starting point **does not erase earlier product obligations**: carry costs, commitments and results into the next ownership discussion and ask who funds the remaining burden, a proposed practice, not a result the history establishes.
