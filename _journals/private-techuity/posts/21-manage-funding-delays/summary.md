**When expected money arrives late, the plan that spends it has to change before job offers and signed contracts change it for you.** Put cash and commitments on a calendar, find the last date a decision still matters, and have the board authorize one fallback.

![A four-step ladder shows how far expected money has got, from expression of interest through approval pending and a signed commitment to cash received; a cash chart falls from €1.6 million toward a €400,000 reserve, which proceeding as planned breaches in late November, deferring reaches on 31 December and a bridge loan pushes to 31 March; the decision is due by 15 August, deferral is chosen, and a locked gate holds deferred spending until cash is received.](assets/images/21-manage-funding-delays/summary-at-a-glance.jpeg)
**Figure 1:** *Price the alternatives by when each reaches the reserve, choose one before the deadline, and restart deferred spending only on cash received.*

Take Larkspur, the fictional scheduling-software company this book follows. It expected €4 million of new investment on 15 September; by July, the money looks likely in mid-December instead. The work is on schedule. **The funding slipped, not the work**, and treating it as an execution problem would produce the wrong fix.

The first step is to be honest about where the money stands. An investor saying it wants to invest is not a signed commitment, and a signed commitment is still not cash in the bank. In July, Larkspur’s round is only an expression of interest.

Then put it on a calendar. Larkspur holds €1.6 million, spends €200,000 a month more than it collects, and the board wants at least €400,000 kept as a reserve. That reserve is reached on 31 December. Two hires and a €120,000 customer portal are lined up, with offers and the contract due on 16 August. So **15 August is the latest useful decision date**.

Sam, the finance chief, prices three alternatives. Proceeding as planned breaches the reserve in late November, before the round could close. Deferring the hires and the portal holds until 31 December. Adding a €600,000 bridge loan from the existing investors would reach 31 March, but only if its money arrives by 30 November, and in July the bridge is itself just an expression of interest.

On 10 August the board chooses to defer, and Sam must secure a signed bridge by 1 September. The board also decides now to stop renewing outside contract engineers, saving €50,000 a month from November, with notice going out on 1 October unless cash already received makes it unnecessary. Customers get no promise that depends on the hires, and the team is told plainly which plan is running.

The hardest rule is about restarting. **Deferred spending restarts only when cash already received keeps the company above the reserve** until 30 June, with the new cost included. At Larkspur’s numbers even the bridge doesn’t pass that test, so the offers and the portal wait for the round’s cash itself.

The fallback has limits, and saying so is part of it: the deferral and the contractor saving carry Larkspur only to about 20 January if no money comes. Part V turns to changing the team itself, starting with adding people in [[scale-the-team-up]].
