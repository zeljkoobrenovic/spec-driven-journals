# Editorial review: TeamSystem: Each New Owner Inherits Progress and Unfinished Work

Reassessed 15 September 2026 from the complete current article, summary and comic text where present, the previous review where present, revision log and Git changes. See the [collection review](../REVIEW.md).

## Current assessment

The revised title, ownership timeline and completed earnings reconciliation make this a more useful case for leaders inheriting acquisition work. The comparison of statutory and pro forma periods remains especially strong. The July 2024 announcement is no longer described as cash received, and the 2021 reinvestment prevents a simplistic continuation of the 2016 stake.

The principal unresolved issue is now the cash interpretation of the new bridge. The source contains more information than the revision acknowledges, and some finance charges are noncash. A later primary report also permits a more precise update of the 2024 realization status. Preserve the current structure and the separation between reported history and proposed leadership practice.

## Active recommendations

1. **High impact — correct the finance-cost/cash explanation and the claimed evidence gap.** The article calls €72.0m net finance cost an obligation consuming cash, while saying interest paid is not isolated. Notes 7–8 include vendor-loan remeasurement and discounting effects; note 10 reports €52.1m of finance costs paid, including €49.8m of note interest. Thus the earnings charge is not the cash payment, and material payment evidence is available in the same report. Use the notes to distinguish charges, cash payments and financing movements; do not substitute €72m for cash interest or infer that the disclosed note interest is every financing cash outflow. [2017 report, notes 7–10, printed pp. 44–45](https://www.teamsystem.com/media/files/865_Consolidated%20Financial%20Statements%20as%20at%20and%20for%20the%20year%20ended%2031%20December%202017%20of%20TeamSystem%20Group.pdf)

2. **Medium impact — update the 2024 verification limit using the later report, without merging unlike figures.** HgCapital Trust's 2024 annual report lists TeamSystem among full realizations during the year, with £34.189m of gross proceeds; the adjacent account says £34.2m returned. Its footnote warns that gross realizations precede performance-fee/facility deductions and can differ in timing from fund distributions. This is stronger evidence than the July announcement, but does not make £24.3m the final cash receipt or close the 2016-to-2024 holding history. Cite the later status and reconcile the differing scopes before replacing the announcement figure. [HgCapital Trust 2024 report, printed pp. 46–47](https://www.hgcapitaltrust.com/~/media/Files/H/Hgcapital-Trust-V2/documents/investors/publications/2024/hgt-fy-2024-report-and-accounts.pdf)

3. **Medium impact — keep announced and completed events distinct in the prose too.** The table labels the 2021 and 2023 events as completion unverified, but the partial-exit narrative says the earlier fund sold and the post-2021 holding was different. Either verify completion from subsequent primary reporting or write “the announced transaction would replace the earlier exposure with a later-fund investment.” The reasoning against assuming stake continuity is valid even before every closing date is established.

4. **Medium impact — remove universal payment timing from the accounting explanation.** “The cash still has to be paid in the year the engineers work” is not guaranteed: payment terms and accruals can move cash between periods. Say “Capitalization does not eliminate the underlying cash cost; use payment dates in the cash plan.” Likewise, amortization does not prove an asset's purchase consideration has already been fully paid. The relevant point is that the amortization charge itself is noncash.

5. **Low impact — improve the bridge's readability and heading.** “Profitable and Loss-Making in the Same Year” repeats the ambiguity corrected in Toys R Us. “From Adjusted EBITDA to the Statutory Loss” is more precise. Keep the non-core total in the bridge and move its long component list immediately below, so the principal deductions are easier to follow. This is a presentation repair, not a request to omit the reported reconciliation.

## Verification and formats

The bridge reproduces the €169.798m gap within the stated €1–2 thousand component rounding; the tax credit reaches the reported loss. The capitalized-development components sum to €13.385m. The arithmetic improvement is real; the cash interpretation needs the correction above. The summary should retain that distinction and the updated transaction-status limits. The comic text is coherent but its revised speech has not been certified against the existing images.
