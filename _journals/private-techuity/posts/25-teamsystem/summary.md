**TeamSystem**, a business-software group, helps explain how a software group can expand, invest in products, report positive adjusted earnings and report a **statutory loss**, the final loss reported under the applicable accounting rules, at the same time. This case examines selected evidence from 2000 through 2017; it is not a current company valuation or a verdict on every later ownership period. The case matters because a buy-and-build strategy is set by investors and financed largely with debt, while the integration and platform work it creates is inherited by the operating team and carried across successive owners.

Palamon’s retrospective describes a regional, founder-led Italian business developing through national expansion, management recruitment and investment. Later owners entered at different prices and stages of development. These are distinct investment episodes, even though the company’s history continues. The sources do not support treating all owners as one actor with a single plan.

HgCapital’s account describes work on product strategy, pricing, reporting, cash collection and acquisitions. Those activities can affect different outcomes. Product work may improve adoption; collection work can release cash; acquisitions can add capabilities while creating integration demands. The public accounts identify interventions more securely than their separate causal contributions.

The March 2016 results presentation reported a completed partial exit, including cash proceeds and a retained interest for HgCapital Trust. [S48: HgCapital Trust 2015 results, p. 14](https://www.hgcapitaltrust.com/~/media/Files/H/Hgcapital-Trust-V2/documents/investors/financial-calendar/hgcapitaltrust-dec-2015-general.pdf) The estimated value of the stake still held remains exposed to future results. A headline investment multiple does not reconstruct an ultimate investor’s net cash return.

**Financial comparisons need a consistent business and period.** The 2017 company report compares a full year with a constructed full-year 2016 presentation because the statutory prior period began at the acquisition date. It also distinguishes adjusted EBITDA from the statutory result. EBITDA means earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization; further adjustments exclude additional specified costs. These measures answer different questions and must be reconciled. [S49: TeamSystem 2017 consolidated report](https://www.teamsystem.com/media/files/865_Consolidated%20Financial%20Statements%20as%20at%20and%20for%20the%20year%20ended%2031%20December%202017%20of%20TeamSystem%20Group.pdf)

Development recorded as an asset **still consumes cash**. An integration expense excluded from an earnings measure can still be necessary to a strategy that keeps buying companies. Ask what recurring investment the operating model requires before treating adjusted earnings as money available for distribution.

A new owner’s financial starting point **does not erase earlier product obligations**. Customer migrations, integration work and knowledge dependencies can span transactions. Carry the costs, commitments and observed results into the next ownership discussion, and identify who funds the remaining burden. This is a proposed leadership application; the disclosed TeamSystem history does not establish its effectiveness across funding rounds or corporate ownership.

For company leaders, the case suggests a disciplined conversation about which improvements the entry price already assumes, what each acquisition adds, and how **integration capacity will be funded** and measured. Customer migration, reliability and employee experience need evidence beyond group growth. The strongest lesson concerns what the group accumulates: useful products and knowledge, but also obligations, dependencies and continuing work.
