**TeamSystem**, a business-software group, shows how a company can expand, invest in products, report positive adjusted earnings and report a **statutory loss**, a loss under the accounting rules it must follow, in the same year. Each ownership change creates a new financial starting point, but migrations, integrations and dependencies continue, so each new owner inherits both progress and unfinished work. The case examines selected evidence from 2000 through 2017.

The documented episodes run from Palamon's 2000 investment in a founder-led regional business through Bain Capital from 2004, HgCapital's 2010 purchase at an announced €565 million enterprise value, and the 2015 agreed sale to Hellman & Friedman, completed in March 2016: distinct episodes at different prices, not one plan.

The 2016 sale illustrates a partial exit: £39 million of cash proceeds to HgCapital Trust plus a retained stake valued at £6.1 million. [S48: HgCapital Trust 2015 results, p. 14](https://www.hgcapitaltrust.com/~/media/Files/H/Hgcapital-Trust-V2/documents/investors/financial-calendar/hgcapitaltrust-dec-2015-general.pdf) Money received and **retained investment value**, the estimated worth of the interest still held, differ. Later documents differ: the Trust reinvested through a later Hg fund in 2021, so its later holding was not the 2016 stake; a July 2024 agreed sale valued that holding at £24.3 million; the Trust's 2024 report lists £34.2 million of gross proceeds. None is the net cash an ultimate investor received.

**Financial comparisons need a consistent business and period.** The 2017 report compares a full year with a constructed full-year 2016 presentation because the statutory prior period began at the March 2016 acquisition date. Dividing the two statutory totals would show 37.7% revenue growth; against the pro forma base, growth was 8.9%, and even that includes newly consolidated businesses. [S49: TeamSystem 2017 consolidated report](https://www.teamsystem.com/media/files/865_Consolidated%20Financial%20Statements%20as%20at%20and%20for%20the%20year%20ended%2031%20December%202017%20of%20TeamSystem%20Group.pdf)

The same report reconciles adjusted EBITDA of €113.0 million to a consolidated loss of €56.8 million. The €169.8 million gap comprises €20.3 million of costs management classes as non-core, €11.1 million of bad-debt allowance, provisions and impairment, €72.5 million of depreciation and amortization, and €72.0 million of net finance cost, offset by a €6.1 million tax credit. Neither large line is a cash payment of the amount shown. Amortization is a non-cash charge spreading the cost of earlier-acquired assets, largely intangibles recognized when the 2016 purchase price was allocated. The finance charge includes non-cash items, such as remeasured amounts owed to sellers of acquired companies; the notes show €52.1 million of finance costs actually paid, €49.8 million of it note interest.

Capitalizing development **does not remove its cash cost**: about €13.4 million of development was recorded as an asset in 2017, and the cash plan should follow payment dates. An integration expense excluded from an earnings measure is still work the operating team must do; [[visma]] examines whether such costs recur.

A new owner's financial starting point **does not erase earlier product obligations**. Carry costs, commitments and observed results into the next ownership discussion and ask who funds the remaining burden; this is a proposed practice, not a result the history establishes. What the group accumulates across its owners is the lesson: useful products and knowledge, but also obligations, dependencies and continuing work.
