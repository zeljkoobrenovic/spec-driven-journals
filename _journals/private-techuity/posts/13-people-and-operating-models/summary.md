An **operating model** describes how teams, responsibilities and decisions are arranged. It helps determine whether a company can turn a plan into repeated useful decisions. An organization chart and a headcount target are incomplete descriptions of that capability.

Follow an actual piece of work from customer need to outcome. Identify where it waits, who resolves disagreement, where knowledge is concentrated, and how quality becomes visible. A delivery problem may arise from unclear priorities, decision delays, or overloaded specialists rather than insufficient effort.

**Assess leaders in their system.** A **chief technology officer’s (CTO’s)** choices matter, but so do the authority, resources, information, and commercial commitments surrounding the role. Leadership change can be justified; it still needs a diagnosis and a transition plan. Replacing a person without changing the conditions can reproduce the failure while losing valuable knowledge.

**Nearshoring** and **offshoring**, locating work in relatively nearby or more distant countries, need a complete operating case. The fictional example compares salary savings with coordination and transition costs. Include management attention, recruitment, retention, knowledge transfer, security, travel where needed, and the interfaces through which teams make decisions. A lower pay rate does not establish a lower cost of a successful customer outcome.

Capacity is also more than paid hours. Teams need skills, context, clear priorities, and recovery from interruptions. Cutting an apparently underutilized specialist can make several other teams slower. A small service buffer can support resilience. These claims should be tested in the actual flow of work rather than used as a blanket defense of every existing role.

Use organizational health measures as diagnostic evidence alongside delivery and customer outcomes. Employee voice can reveal problems that a monthly dashboard misses. The goal is a system that can perform now, learn, and retain the knowledge required for future commitments. Explain who bears the transition burden and whether the company has funded the work needed to make the change succeed.
