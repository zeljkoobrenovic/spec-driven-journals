An **engagement** is an agreed piece of support with a purpose, participants and boundaries. An **engagement charter** records that agreement. The specialist also reports to the investor. Before work starts, agree how that responsibility relates to the company’s priorities and who can direct the assignment.

![A support charter connects the agreed result, company and adviser resources, boundaries, evidence review and handover.](assets/images/20-useful-engagement/summary-at-a-glance.jpeg)
**Figure 1:** *Make support feasible, reviewable and sustainable after the engagement.*

The input is the previous chapter’s request: one setup path made repeatable, a specialist alongside a Larkspur engineer for six weeks, Priya assessing outcomes. The fictional charter fills in the fields; days count from closing, engagement weeks from day 21, when the specialist starts. The need is diligence finding D-3: onboarding depends on one specialist’s manual configuration, about 80 hours per implementation. The result is a path the engineer and customer team can run without them. Priya is the **accountable company leader**; the **investor-side sponsor** is the firm’s partner responsible for the holding, who secures the specialist’s days. Resources are ten specialist days over six weeks (day 21 to about day 63), six engineer-days from portal research, two customer-team sessions and Priya’s time. Specialist days are capped at €1,500 a day, €15,000 at most, paid from the €180,000 ONB-1 budget and counted inside it. The specialist recommends, Priya decides, Alex approves code changes; scope changes need Priya and the sponsor, budget changes the board. A baseline from customer records is due by day 20; the handover test is an engineer and a customer-team member completing a setup with the specialist observing only.

**External help consumes internal time**: without the six engineer-days, the engagement is not feasible.

**In engagement week three, about day 40, the evidence changes the problem.** The first cohort setups confirm what the day-20 baseline suggested: for customers with imperfect data, cleaning their records, not the setup step, decides when a setup completes. Priya proposes it, the sponsor agrees, Alex confirms the effort. Templating less common customer types stops; the remaining five specialist days define a data-intake check and the engineer builds a validation for the three commonest defects. Ten days, six engineer-days, the €15,000 cap and the review date are unchanged; no cash is added. Extending and hiring are rejected; a funded data-quality step is a board decision at day 100, on the day-90 cohort.

Three boundaries apply: the charter names who directs the work, so no parallel reporting line forms; interviews are pilot evidence, not assessments, and though the firm may form a view under its existing rights, a company-sponsored assessment needs new access, agreed and announced; and hours released are not cash saved, so waiting time and errors are recorded too.

**At week six, about day 63,** the team runs two new customers’ setups without the specialist, effort is falling and waiting time has not moved. The engagement ends on schedule with no continuing service; the ten days cost €15,000, within the cap and inside the roughly €90,000 the pilot incurs by day 100. An engagement may end with a capability, a better decision or an understood continuing service. None should leave a dependency nobody planned to fund.
