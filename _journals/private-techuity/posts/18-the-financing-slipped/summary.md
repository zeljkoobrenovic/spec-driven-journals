The team is on schedule, but the €4m round expected on 15 September now looks likely in mid-December. **The roadmap did not slip; a funding assumption did**, and that assumption has to change before it becomes a promise the company cannot keep. Revising the plan needs the board’s authority, and a bridge from the existing investors is an investment decision on their side. This is a separate fictional Larkspur scenario, with its own cash, burn, hires and dates.

Start by naming the stage the money has reached: expression of interest, approval pending, contractual commitment with conditions, or cash received. Only the last is money. In July the round is at the first stage; “likely in December” forecasts a conversation, not a date cash arrives. Naming the stage also keeps blame out of it: this delay is an unfunded dependency, not a failed hypothesis or weak execution.

Then put cash on a calendar. Larkspur holds €1.6m on 1 July, burns €200,000 a month and has agreed a €400,000 reserve, so without new money that reserve is reached on 31 December. Sort commitments into protected obligations (customer contracts, the recovery requirement), work already committed, and spending still reversible: two hires from September at €30,000 a month and a €120,000 portal contract due for signature in August. Those make **15 August the latest useful decision date**.

Three alternatives follow. A, proceed as planned, breaches the reserve in late November, before any December closing. B, defer the hires and the portal, holds to 31 December with no margin. C, B plus a conditional €600,000 bridge from the existing investors, reaches 31 March — but only if proceeds arrive by 30 November with no fee or cash interest before March. In July that bridge is only an expression of interest, and even a written commitment would carry conditions rather than be cash.

The escalation states the decision required, the latest useful date and the consequence of delay. On 10 August the board chooses **B now**, with a written bridge commitment due by 1 September and a review on 1 October. One reduction, R1, is priced now: contract engineering agreements ending 31 October are not renewed, saving €50,000 a month from 1 November. That moves the reserve date only to about 20 January without the bridge, or to mid-May with it. Reductions lower the bridge needed but do not replace it, and the level at which Larkspur could run on revenue alone is decided in [[anatomy-of-a-layoff]].

Deferred spending restarts on one rule: **cash received, or a conditional commitment the board has specifically authorized counting** against a dated cash forecast and a funded downside. A term sheet meets neither gate; a signed subscription agreement meets the second only with that authorization, and spending is then timed to the closing date its conditions imply, not to the signature. No customer gets a promise that depends on the hires, and the team is told it is executing B. The same method covers a refinancing or a parent withdrawing budget.
