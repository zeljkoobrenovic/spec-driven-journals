The team is on schedule, but the €4m round expected on 15 September now looks likely in mid-December. **The roadmap did not slip; a funding assumption did**, and the company has to change that assumption before it becomes a delivery promise it cannot keep. The plan was the basis on which the existing investors committed money, so the revision needs the board’s authority, and a bridge from those investors is an investment decision on their side. This is a fictional Larkspur scenario with its own assumptions.

Start by naming the stage the money has reached: expression of interest, approval pending, contractual commitment with conditions, or cash received. In July the round is at the first stage. “Likely in December” is a forecast of a conversation, not a date on which cash arrives. Naming the stage also keeps blame out of the response: a delayed round is an unfunded dependency, not a failed hypothesis or weak execution, and it needs a different fix.

Then put cash on a calendar. Larkspur holds €1.6m on 1 July, burns €200,000 a month and has agreed a €400,000 reserve with the board, so without new money the reserve is reached on 31 December. Sort commitments into protected obligations (customer contracts, the recovery requirement), work already committed (the onboarding pilot, the recovery work) and spending that is planned but still reversible: two hires from September at €30,000 a month and a €120,000 portal contract to be signed in August. The offers and the signature make **15 August the latest useful decision date**.

Three alternatives follow. A, proceed as planned, breaches the reserve in late November, before any mid-December closing. B, defer the hires and the portal and keep the pilot and the recovery work, reaches the reserve on 31 December with no margin. C, B plus a conditional €600,000 bridge from the existing investors, covers a slip to March, but the bridge is pending approval and is not cash.

The escalation to the board states the decision required, the latest useful date and the consequence of delay. On 10 August the board chooses **B now**: Sam obtains a written bridge commitment by 1 September, the plan is reviewed on 1 October, and Alex and Priya stage the deferred work and prepare the next reduction list. No customer receives a promise that depends on the hires. The team is told it is executing B and what would restore the original plan.

The original plan returns only on a **signed subscription agreement with understood conditions**, not at term-sheet stage, and spending restarts against the cash date those conditions imply. The same method covers a refinancing that leaves less cash and a corporate parent withdrawing budget: dated cash, protected obligations, reversible commitments, a latest useful date, priced alternatives and one authorized fallback. In every case continued ownership is a real scenario, and the record of the decision is what the next decision-maker inherits.
