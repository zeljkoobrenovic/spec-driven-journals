## Current assessment

The main earlier accuracy problems are resolved. The new title names the earnings measure, the table includes both net-loss scopes, and the opening no longer declares the technology plan adequate or financing the sole cause. The supplier loop now has a clear sequence and attribution to management's declaration. Preserving the employee and Hasbro evidence gives the case a perspective missing from several successful-exit accounts.

This is now one of the stronger case chapters. The remaining edits are local: keep program duration and cash terminology consistent, and shorten repeated explanations without losing the necessary-versus-sufficient distinction.

## Active recommendations

1. **Medium impact — say “negative operating cash flow” in comic panel 3.** “Positive operating earnings, negative cash” can mean a negative cash balance. The fiscal 2016 release reports $1 million used in operating activities, but $566 million of cash and equivalents at year end. The caption is more precise than its dialogue. Replace the bubble with “Positive operating earnings; almost no operating cash flow.” Check the existing image for the changed words, rather than assuming a caption update changed a speech bubble. [Fiscal 2016 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1005414/000100541417000010/truq4-16earningsreleaseexh.htm)

2. **Medium impact — use one duration for the proposed technology program.** The opening calls it a “three-year technology program”; the later table correctly describes 2018–2021 as a four-year total. Use “multi-year” in the preview or repeat the four-year period. The later generic example of benefits over three years should be clearly hypothetical if retained.

3. **Low impact — keep the debt-service observation tied to its historical period.** “The debt-service line recurs every year” can imply the same $400 million throughout the ownership period. The evidence is management's description in September 2017, not a reconstructed annual schedule. Prefer: “Debt service is an annual cash demand in this account; the technology figure is the total of one proposed program.” The chapter's explicit 2005–2017 evidence gap should remain.

4. **Low impact — compress repeated transfer cautions near the ending.** The supplier sequence, interacting-pressures section and final practices all explain that a plausible technology intervention is not a sufficient rescue. Keep the cash mechanism and one clear common limit, then let the customer, employee and supplier consequences carry the conclusion. This is a pacing preference; no substantial restructuring remains beneficial.

## Verification and formats

The newly added financial lines agree with the [company's release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1005414/000100541417000010/truq4-16earningsreleaseexh.htm): $460m operating earnings − $457m interest expense + $2m interest income − $34m tax = $29m consolidated loss; subtracting $7m attributable to noncontrolling interests gives the $36m attributable loss. Operating cash flow of −$1m and capital expenditure of $252m are separate; their simple difference is −$253m, before other investing and financing flows. Interest already included in operating cash flow must not be deducted again.

The summary now preserves the financial and causal qualifications. The supplier figures remain explicitly management's account, and the Hasbro charge is one supplier's reported consequence rather than all supplier losses. This review does not reconstruct the complete financing history or re-estimate a counterfactual rescue.
