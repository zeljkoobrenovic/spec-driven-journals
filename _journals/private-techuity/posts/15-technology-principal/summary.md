The Technology Principal described in this manuscript helps investment and company teams make better technology-dependent decisions. The role spans an investment lifecycle, but its formal authority depends on the firm and the particular engagement.

The supplied private role description includes pipeline support, technical diligence, advice to investment professionals, practical operating projects, leadership and staffing, portfolio learning, AI strategy, and exit preparation. [P01: supplied role brief](pt-bibliography.html) This grounds the book's perspective. It is one brief, not evidence that every firm organizes the role in the same way.

**Responsibility for useful advice is different from authority to direct a company.** The Principal can assess a risk, recommend a response, support execution, and track evidence without becoming the CEO, CTO, or board. Clarify who decides, funds, implements, and receives confidential information. Explicit authority becomes particularly important when a recommendation comes from someone associated with the owner.

Credibility requires fluency in both investment and operating work. Investors need to understand how a technology finding changes a thesis, timing, price, or required capital. Company teams need help that respects customer commitments and the practical difficulty of implementation. Neither audience is well served by a generic maturity score or an unqualified assurance that a platform will scale.

The role also needs a capacity model. Deal work can be urgent; operating work depends on continuity. If every day is preallocated, the advertised support may disappear when a transaction arrives. Make trade-offs and escalation visible, and use specialists where the work exceeds the Principal's competence or available time.

Judge contribution through decisions improved, findings accepted into action, interventions that achieve useful outcomes, and capability left with the company. Avoid counting meetings or claiming the full increase in enterprise value as the role's personal result.

A practical engagement charter names the question, sponsor, company owner, scope, information boundaries, output, time commitment, and handoff. The strongest version of the role leaves executives better able to make and execute decisions, while giving investors a more accurate understanding of what the business can accomplish and what it will cost.
