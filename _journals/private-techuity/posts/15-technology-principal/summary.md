In this book, a **Technology Principal** is an adviser working for an investment firm who assesses technology and helps companies improve their operations. A **chief technology officer (CTO)** leads technology inside the company. The adviser may help assess investment assumptions, improve decisions or deliver a defined piece of support, while company leaders normally carry the continuing operating responsibility.

The description draws on a supplied private role brief. It is one arrangement, not a universal definition or an automatic grant of authority over company staff and budgets. [P01: supplied role brief](bibliography.html)

Begin by understanding **which job the adviser is doing**. **Due diligence** investigates a business before an investment decision. **Coaching** develops a person’s judgment and practice. Operating support helps improve company work. Formal oversight or a temporary executive assignment creates further responsibilities that need to be explicit.

Those roles can overlap. Company leaders and employees should understand the purpose of each conversation, what information will be shared and whether the adviser is recommending, deciding or delivering. Proximity to the investor can give a suggestion influence even when no formal instruction was intended.

Use the company’s decision map to agree who approves spending, directs work and resolves disagreement. If the role changes, revisit the authority and resources. Do not let a temporary assignment become an unexplained second route for setting team priorities.

Build a shared account of the business through customer commitments, operating evidence and feasible options. In fictional Larkspur, pricing changes taking three weeks because only two people understand the billing code gives the adviser a concrete constraint to investigate. A generic request for a better platform gives much less direction.

Company and investor conversations should preserve the **same facts**, even when the detail differs. Both sides need room to revise a conclusion when the evidence changes. A useful challenge explains the assumption, supporting observations and what would justify a different decision.

**Distinguish advice from delivery capacity**. A recommendation may still require company engineering time, product decisions and funding. Check the adviser’s availability across other commitments before relying on continued involvement; consider another specialist or a revised scope where needed.

Establish whether this role exists in your arrangement. A venture investor may offer a partner or an introduction; a growth investor may supply expansion expertise; a corporate owner may provide specialists who also enforce group policy. Some investors offer no operating team. Ask who employs the person, what assignment and time they have, and which decisions they may make. Apply those questions before transferring the supplied fund-specific role to your company.

Judge the relationship through decisions improved, constraints resolved and capabilities the company can sustain. Include the effort on both sides and make continuing dependence explicit. The adviser is one possible connection into a wider set of investor people and resources, whose usefulness still depends on the company’s specific needs.
