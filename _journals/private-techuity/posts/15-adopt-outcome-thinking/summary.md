**A delivered feature is a cost until something changes for customers.** Before choosing work, write an outcome record: one change you want for customers, one business result it should feed, the measures between them, who answers for each, and when each can be read.

A new investment makes more activity possible, but being busy is not getting results. Investors often ask: “What is your north star?” A feature list tells them nothing; a single number tells the team nothing. The outcome record answers with a pair.

At Rotaline, the book’s fictional scheduling-software company, the investment has just closed. Priya, who leads product, has three requests: a customer portal, simpler setup for new customers, and recovery work so the service can be restored faster after a failure. First, someone must say what the work is for.

**Priya starts with the customer**, in the customers’ own words, after asking six of them: a new customer is scheduling real work within a few weeks of signing, without Rotaline’s specialist setting the account up by hand. Today the median wait from contract to first real schedule is about ten weeks, and each setup takes about 80 hours of staff time. Three of the six said the wait had delayed their own rollout. That shows the outcome matters, not yet that fixing it earns anything.

Then Ines, the chief executive, gets her executives and the investor’s director to agree one business outcome. They settle on **keeping paying customers and growing what they pay**. The investor reads that as net revenue retention: if last year’s customers paid 100, then bought 20 more, cut 10 and left with 5, they now pay 105, a retention of 105%.

The outcomes join at one shared measure: the share of customers with a first real schedule within eight weeks. That a fast start makes customers renew is a hypothesis, tested at their first anniversaries. Responsibility attaches to measures: product answers for setup hours and first-schedule weeks, customer success and the people who set prices for renewal, sales and customer success for expansion.

**Every measure also gets an honest clock.** Setup hours show within weeks, first-schedule weeks within a quarter, renewals only after a year. The investor, expecting to own the company for about four years, cannot wait a year to learn. So the first eight customers report hours and weeks at day 90, ninety days after the board adopts the plan. If neither moves, the leadership reopens the outcome pair itself, not just the plan.

Finally, balance becomes a budget. Rotaline splits the plan into new capabilities, improvements and running the product, and fixes the split before ranking anything. An item enters only if it moves a measure, protects an outcome or obligation, or tests an assumption. A loud customer’s request then competes for its slice, not for the whole plan.
