> **Part III in one line:** choose where to invest money and team capacity, then connect the work to customer outcomes, business results and lasting capability.

<br>
An investor may expect growth, stronger earnings or a business that can operate more reliably. Customers have their own needs, and the company has limited money and team capacity. Product and technology leaders must turn those demands into a set of choices that can work together.

An **investment thesis** explains why an investor expects an investment to succeed. A **company investment** commits resources to a future benefit, such as improving a product or developing a team. The two are connected, but the investor’s expectations still need examination against customer evidence and the company’s ability to deliver.

This part begins with choosing what to fund. It then follows selected work through the product, technology and operating decisions needed to create a useful result.

## The Learning Path

- [[pt-product-value]] traces a chosen change through customer behavior to business results.
- [[pt-engineering-and-architecture]] examines the software and engineering capabilities the plan needs.
- [[pt-valuation-and-design]] connects the financial expectations from Part I to specific design choices.
- [[pt-cloud-economics]] evaluates spending against the useful service delivered.
- [[pt-security-and-resilience]] considers protection and recovery when future losses are uncertain.
- [[pt-data-and-ai]] separates product opportunities, internal improvement and competitive threats.
- [[pt-people-and-operating-models]] examines the people, skills and decision processes needed to sustain the work.
- [[pt-acquisitions-and-carveouts]] brings these choices together when businesses are combined or separated.

By the end, you should be able to explain why one combination of work deserves resources, how benefits would arise and what evidence would change the plan. Measuring value includes customer outcomes and continued capability as well as financial results.

Begin with [[pt-choosing-investments]]. Part IV then considers how the investor’s people and connections can help close the gaps the plan exposes.
