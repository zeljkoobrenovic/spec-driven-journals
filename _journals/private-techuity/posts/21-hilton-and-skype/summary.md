Hotel group **Hilton** and internet-calling product **Skype** illustrate successful investor outcomes with different ownership and technology contexts. They also show why an attractive sale, or **exit**, is insufficient evidence that a particular intervention caused the return. Exits matter to product and engineering leaders because much of what investors ask for during ownership is justified by the sale they expect to make.

**Figure 1:** *Reconstruct each case and keep financial outcomes separate from causal claims.*

Hilton's ownership period included the financial crisis, changes to borrowing terms and amounts owed, changes in the business, public listing, and staged realization. Its 2013 registration statement describes commercial systems supporting reservations, distribution, and property operations, and reports a material debt reduction through the 2010 restructuring. [S23: Hilton registration filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1585689/000119312513364703/d593452ds1.htm) Those systems provide plausible business mechanisms; the filing does not isolate their contribution to sponsor returns.

Blackstone later reported a 3.1× investment multiple and $14 billion of profit on Hilton. [S24: Blackstone 2018 investor call](https://ir.blackstone.com/files/doc_events/BLACKSTONE-Second-Quarter-2018-Earnings-Investor-Call.pdf) These are results reported by the **sponsor**, the investment firm, not a demonstrated net LP (limited partner, an investor in a fund) outcome or an independently identified technology effect. The case also includes uneven operating recovery: some owned and leased property results remained below earlier levels in the registration statement's comparison.

Skype involved a shorter sequence. eBay completed the sale of a controlling stake in 2009 while retaining a minority interest. The public filing describes **intellectual-property** issues, concerning legal rights to essential technology, and their settlement, operating investments, and financial results. [S26: eBay Skype sale announcement](https://investors.ebayinc.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2009/EBay-Inc-Completes-Sale-of-Skype/default.aspx) [S27: Skype registration filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1498209/000119312511096544/ds1a.htm) Microsoft announced an $8.5 billion acquisition in 2011 and completed it that October. [S28: Microsoft Skype completion](https://news.microsoft.com/source/2011/10/13/microsoft-officially-welcomes-skype/)

**Transaction valuations are not enough to calculate a fund return.** Ownership percentages, debt, additional funding, transaction costs, distributions, and timing matter. Microsoft's strategic willingness to pay is also an alternative explanation for the sale price. It is not evidence that every operating change produced an equivalent increase in standalone value.

Skype's retirement in May 2025 belongs to the **later product history**. [S29: Microsoft Skype retirement](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/skype/22ccebb6-0cf9-4e1d-916f-aaf978e1b129) It does not retroactively erase the 2011 investor outcome or establish that earlier decisions caused the eventual retirement.

For company leaders, Skype’s documented transitions prompt a fresh map of authority, **technology rights and intended product purpose** at each change of owner. Hilton prompts a financing and time-horizon question. These questions can help in other arrangements, but the cases do not establish venture-investor outcomes or a universal strategic premium. Transfer the question and gather the evidence for the current company.

The transferable lesson is to reconstruct the chain from initial condition through financing and interventions to **distinct outcomes**. Both cases contain evidence gaps concerning stakeholder experience and the incremental impact of technology. They justify better questions about distribution, intellectual property, resilience, and strategic buyers; they do not supply a universal private equity technology playbook.
