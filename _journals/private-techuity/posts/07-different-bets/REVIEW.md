# Editorial review: Management Equity, Fund Carry and Employee Jobs Are Different Bets

Reassessed 15 September 2026 from the complete current article, summary and comic text, previous review, revision log and Git changes. See the [collection review](../REVIEW.md).

## Current assessment

The new consolidation case makes employee and customer consequences part of a decision rather than a closing acknowledgment. Separating company equity from fund carry, adding an actual payout guardrail and moving leadership diagnosis to the organization chapter all strengthen the title's argument. Preserve the precise threshold/option examples and the refusal to present eleven lost jobs as offset by an investor gain.

The structure now serves company leaders well: payout mechanics lead to behavior, a choice and the unresolved burden. The new example also introduces misleading risk language, an unsupported cost comparison and inconsistent comic chronology. Those corrections matter more than shortening the distinctive title or redesigning the outline.

## Active recommendations

- **High — Correct the fund-risk row in “One Consolidation, Four Different Bets.”** “Little at company level; the fund holds several investments” confuses diversification with little exposure to this company's loss. The fund's stake, the manager's carry and employees' livelihood are different exposures, not an exposed workforce versus an effectively risk-free fund. State the capital and prospective carry that can be lost; explain diversification as spreading exposure. Also identify the €6m as incremental company equity value under the stated assumption, not the amount of carried interest. The actual carry share depends on the fund waterfall.
- **Medium — Reconcile transition costs with the claimed first-year saving.** €350,000 severance + €120,000 retention + €80,000 contractors = €550,000, about 92% of the €600,000 annual run-rate saving. The incremental retention/contractor cost alone is €200,000, one third. Neither supports “roughly half of the first year's saving” without a separate timing calculation. Show when savings begin under the twelve-month staging and distinguish annual run rate from cash saved in year one.
- **Medium — Define net revenue retention and design the guardrail for the harm.** “Whether last year's customers are still paying” does not define a revenue retention ratio: expansion can offset lost customers. Define the same-customer revenue cohort and expansion, contraction and churn; pair it with gross or customer retention if the concern is customer loss. A public issuer's definition illustrates the distinction. [Primary example: SEC-filed shareholder letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1517413/000151741321000007/ex991-shareholderletter123.htm). Explain why an executive-bonus correction also changes sales commissions, and establish that rule prospectively rather than implying an automatic retrospective withholding.
- **Medium — Repair comic panels 4–5.** Panel 4 says customer renewals run on a longer clock than the fund's sale; the article puts thirty renewals in the next two quarters and exit two to four years away. Panel 5 turns a modeled higher sale price into a certainty. Say renewals are the near-term constraint and the higher exit value is conditional. The summary should likewise retain the timing and valuation assumptions.
- **Low — Separate options from share awards in the comparison table.** “Nothing until exercise” and proceeds above an exercise price describe the illustrative option, not every share award. Keep the existing instrument-specific qualification close to the table. Remove “The title's comparison needs a decision to bite on,” which narrates the editorial task rather than the company problem.
