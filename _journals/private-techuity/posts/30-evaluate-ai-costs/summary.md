**An AI feature has no single return on investment; it has a return for a stated period, under stated conditions.** Measure the cost of each useful task, separate why the bill moved, and commit only at the low end of the demand the company can fund.

Many AI features are sold for a fixed monthly fee but paid for by the amount of text the model reads and writes. The price stays put; the bill moves.

Rotaline, the fictional company this book follows, sells a feature that sorts customers’ inspection documents for €300 a month. By September 2027 forty customers pay for it, and the vendor’s bill is growing faster than the feature’s revenue. The investor’s adviser asks for “the AI ROI”.

Start with the company’s unit, not the vendor’s: **a document sorted and accepted** without correction. In August, 40 customers brought €12,000 and suppliers were paid €7,424. That looks healthy until the engineers’ and support staff’s time is counted: at salary cost, the month lost €1,124. Supplier cost per customer had risen from €130 to about €186 against the €300 price.

Next, **separate why the bill changed**. Model charges rose from €1,400 in April to €5,824 in August. More usage added €2,644. Construction, meaning how the feature is built (longer instructions, extra material sent with each document, retries on a larger model), added €3,236. A 20% vendor price cut took €1,456 off, hiding almost half the construction increase. Construction is Rotaline’s to fix: two engineer-weeks of work are expected to cut model cost per document by 35%, turning August’s loss into a projected surplus of about €914 a month.

From April to September, €57,000 of revenue less about €30,100 of supplier cost leaves about €26,900: roughly 90%, provided accuracy and review effort held, which the monthly samples show they did. **That is a return before staff costs.** Charge the staff time and the six months are about €800 short, before the trial and launch costs still unrecovered.

The vendor then offers a 2028 commitment: a fixed monthly payment, owed even if usage falls short, for 25% off the usage it covers. Rotaline commits €2,400 a month, sized at the low end of the board’s forecast range. That saves €800 a month anywhere in the range and costs €400 more only in a harsh stress case. Ines, the chief executive, signs in November, once October’s bill shows the 35% saving, so Rotaline does not commit to usage it is about to cut.

Finally, **write the stop rules before the review**: reprice if supplier cost per customer tops €150 for two months; switch to planner review, or suspend sorting, if accuracy falls below 95% or a safety document is missed; decide within set windows if the vendor raises prices or retires the model.
