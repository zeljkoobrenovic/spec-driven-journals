An investor puts money into businesses expecting a financial return, and often knows people across the businesses it has funded. It can help leaders reach people, experience and settings that their everyday work would leave undiscovered. **Learning can answer a known question, reveal a new one or build a relationship that becomes useful later.**

Prosus, a global technology investor, reports taking 20 founders of technology companies to China for five days to explore its ecosystem for artificial intelligence (AI): the companies, people and services around software that learns from data to make predictions or suggestions. The account reports reflections, not subsequent company results. [S101: Prosus](https://www.prosus.com/news-insights/2026/a-window-on-china-a-mirror-on-ourselves)

**Choose a format for its purpose.** Knowledge sharing preserves practical cases. Seminars explain topics; workshops let people practise. Summits and conferences connect people. Recurring peer communities let participants return to difficult questions. Study visits show work in unfamiliar settings. Relevant access and time to reflect matter more than an impressive destination.

A useful community needs continuity and reciprocity: members give and receive help. Agree who attends, what may be quoted with a person’s name and what reaches the investor’s investment staff. The author’s [CTO Starter Kit](https://ctostarter.com/start/index.html) collects guidance for chief technology officers (CTOs), the executives responsible for a company’s technology. Its [public repository](https://github.com/zeljkoobrenovic/cto-starter-kit) of project files shows one way to organize shared guidance and routes to peers. Using it in an investor community is a proposed application.

In an **independent fictional example**, Northline makes inventory software, which tracks the goods a business holds in stock, for small wholesalers: businesses that buy goods in quantity and sell them on to shops. Product leader Mira and engineering leader Tomas choose a two-day visit to businesses whose staff use similar systems, to learn how customers would use AI reorder suggestions: recommendations of which products to buy again and how many. Their chief executive approves up to €1,200 from the existing learning budget and six person-days (one person’s working day each): two travel and visit days plus one preparation and follow-through day per leader. A review of a potential supplier moves back one week. The investor charges no fee; if the access disappears, a seminar and a follow-up conversation replace it.

The visitors see people reviewing suggestions inside the screen they already use to manage stock. A host credits reliable product records, such as correct product codes and stock counts, and easy corrections. Northline’s support lead then notes that its customers’ product records vary widely. The team takes a better question into customer interviews: what information and control would users need? It has not authorized a feature or demonstrated a saving.

**Keep observation, explanation and local relevance distinct.** Review what changed in the company’s understanding, which relationships continue and what needs investigating.
