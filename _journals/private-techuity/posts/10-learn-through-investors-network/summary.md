**An investor’s network can help you learn what your everyday work would never show you.** Some learning answers a question you already have, some reveals one you had missed, and some builds a relationship that becomes useful later. Each deserves room.

In April 2026, Prosus, a global technology investor, took 20 founders of technology companies on a five-day visit to Shenzhen, Shanghai and Beijing to explore how artificial intelligence (AI) reaches everyday use there. Its account reports founders reconsidering their assumptions, not later results, and that is the point: the organizer provides access, and the participants work out what it means for them.

So **choose the format for the learning you need**. A case note or an hour with a practitioner can settle a focused question. A seminar explains a topic; a workshop lets people practise. Summits and conferences connect people across companies. A recurring peer community lets members return to hard problems with people who know their circumstances, and it lasts only if members give as well as take. A visit shows unfamiliar work where it happens. Distance and prestige are weak guides; relevant access and time to reflect matter more.

In a fictional example, Northline makes inventory software for small wholesalers and is considering AI suggestions for which products to reorder, and how many. Mira, who leads product, and Tomas, who leads engineering, want to see how busy staff decide whether to accept a suggestion. Their investor offers a five-day international tour, an online seminar or introductions for a two-day regional visit. They pick the visit, because its hosts will show their everyday work. The chief executive approves up to €1,200 from the existing learning budget and six person-days, and a supplier review moves back a week.

At one host, suggested orders are reviewed inside the screen staff already use to manage stock. Mira first credits the AI, but the person placing the orders says reliable product records and an easy way to change a suggestion matter as much. **Keep what you saw, what you were told and what you conclude apart**: that explanation is the host’s, and the visitors have not tested it. Back home, Northline’s support lead points out that its own customers’ records vary widely. So the team brings home a better question, not a feature: what information and control would customers need to trust a reorder suggestion? Mira adds it to customer interviews already scheduled, and Tomas asks the host for another conversation. Nothing has been authorized and no saving has been shown.

**Attendance authorizes learning; implementation follows the company’s usual decision process.** Judge the value by what changed: an assumption reconsidered, a practice adapted, a peer contacted months later. Agree beforehand what may be quoted with a person’s name and what reaches the investor’s investment staff, so people can speak candidly.
