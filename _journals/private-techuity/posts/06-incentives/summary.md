Shared equity creates some common interests but does not make everyone's risks, rights, and time horizons identical. Management, founders, fund investors, the manager, employees, and customers can experience the same transaction differently.

Understand the actual management-equity instrument. Rollover shares, purchased shares, options, and incentive pools can have different thresholds, preferences, dilution, vesting, and departure consequences. Ask for worked scenarios rather than relying on a headline percentage. Legal and tax interpretation belongs with qualified advisers using the actual documents.

Fund carry operates at another level. It does not automatically track the payout of executives in one company. LP (limited partner, an investor in a fund)-oriented alignment practices are useful, but they do not represent every employee or customer interest. [S02: ILPA principles](https://ilpa.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/ILPA-Principles-3.0_2019.pdf)

**Examine the behavior a measure rewards.** An EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization)-only target can discourage investment with later benefits. Revenue growth can conceal weak contribution. Release frequency can reward trivial output. Use connected outcome measures and guardrails that make the most likely distortions visible, while retaining management judgment.

Leadership changes also need a hypothesis. Define what capability is missing and examine the system around the individual. A CTO can be constrained by weak product leadership, unstable commercial commitments, or an impossible plan. Replacement can be necessary, but the expected difference should be stated and tested.

A healthy review separates decision quality, execution quality, and results. Good decisions can have poor outcomes; weak decisions can get lucky. Rewarding only the result can teach people to protect the story rather than reveal a failed assumption early.

For a Technology Principal, credibility requires the same factual account with investors and company teams. Adapt the detail to the audience without presenting incompatible claims about feasibility or value.

Finally, identify who is absent from the alignment story. Employees may bear restructuring costs without equity upside. Customers may face reduced service or difficult migration. Record these burdens and the justification for accepting them. A gain in enterprise value does not demonstrate that every affected group is better off. Alignment should remain an explicit design and review task throughout ownership.
