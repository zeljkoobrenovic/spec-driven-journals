# Spec: Customers, Lenders and Investors: What Each Expects in Return

## Intent

Explain the funding behind a company leader's commitments by following one fictional need, Larkspur's €100,000, through a customer prepayment, a loan and a new share issue, and completing the choice. Introduce investor ownership across private funding arrangements, with public markets and founder funding as brief comparisons; distinguish new company cash from payments to existing owners.

## Audience

Product and engineering leaders inside companies working under investors, including leaders who inherit an ownership arrangement. Assume no specialist finance background; explain necessary terms before use. Investor-side readers are secondary.

## Success criteria

- Add one whole-post overview visual to the TL;DR after its opening paragraph, generated with the Nano Banana article illustrator. Keep the 300–500-word summary prose, bold emphasis and citations; provide alt text and a concise numbered caption.
- Provide a distinctive article header logo and a simple navigation icon with unique asset paths. Keep both consistent with the journal's visual style.
- Include two explanatory article figures with accurate short labels, alt text and numbered captions. Use restrained bold emphasis for key claims; preserve the words, citations and historical data. The comic modality is generated from its six-panel storyboard with consistent fictional characters and preserved captions.
- Open with Larkspur's €100,000 need and close it: show what the same amount permits under a customer prepayment, a loan and a new share issue, with one obligation per route that changes the delivery promise; state the chosen option, the alternatives rejected, the funding, the scarce capacity, who is authorized and the evidence that would reopen the decision.
- State that a budget gives the amount and the funding terms give timing, approvals and obligations; do not rank the amount below the terms.
- Explain four common sources of business money in terms a reader can hold: customers, lenders, owners, asset sales.
- Define share, stock exchange, public company and private company in passing; send IPO, audit and reporting-category detail to the glossary.
- Present venture, growth, buyout and corporate labels as orientation in one compact table and hand the consequences to the funding chapter.
- Keep historical findings and reading annotations within their source scope; label the Larkspur figures as fictional.
- Keep the summary and comic on the same argument as the article, including the completed decision; end with 3–4 questions and a curated reading list.

## Non-goals

Detailed investor-fit comparisons belong in [[raise-what-you-need]]. Keep this first chapter focused on cash sources, basic ownership and the obligations accompanying each source. Leave valuation and returns to their dedicated chapters and the map of transaction-scale cash recipients to [[announcement-is-not-a-budget]].
