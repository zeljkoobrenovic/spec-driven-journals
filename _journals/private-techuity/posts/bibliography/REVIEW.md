# Editorial review: Bibliography and Evidence Guide

Reviewed 14 September 2026. Read the complete bibliography, all chapter source annotations and the collection's evidence framing. External verification was selective; this review does not claim to have read every linked source in full.

## Overall assessment

The bibliography provides unusually candid evidence provenance: stable identifiers, version dates, interested perspectives, partial access and the distinction between consulted pages and complete documents. It is valuable to readers assessing the historical cases and researchers following the claims. Preserve that transparency, particularly the limits on the Toys R Us 10-K and the separation of private inputs from public evidence.

Its weakness is maintenance as a reader-facing reference. Revision history interrupts the opening, numerical identifiers provide little thematic navigation, and the chapter-end “To Probe Further” additions sit outside the otherwise careful source-register method. Several of those annotations are more assertive than the main text.

## Detailed feedback

- **Introduction and tone:** The opening clearly explains what inclusion means. References to the “beginner-reading revision,” “structural revision,” “Owned revision” and “research pass two” tell the editor's history rather than helping readers assess the current book. Preserve dates and scope while moving revision chronology into an editorial log.
- **Organization and retrieval:** Stable S/P identifiers are useful and should not be renumbered to close gaps. Add a thematic index and chapter backlinks so readers can find valuation, delivery, AI or a particular case without knowing an identifier.
- **Clarity and consistency:** The repeated “Evidence type / Consulted scope” format is readable. S58–S61 use different bolding and wording from preceding entries; normalize those minor presentation differences. A long PDF's acquisition is correctly distinguished from actually reading selected sections.
- **Collection-level evidence gap:** Chapter-end reading lists introduce sources and specific claims beyond this numbered register. Either register sources used substantively, after verifying their consultation scope, or distinguish optional recommendations from evidence relied upon. Do not retroactively say the author read a source merely because it appears in a list.
- **Confirmed issues to reconcile in the linked chapters:** TeamSystem's 2024 note converts an announced transaction value into received cash; the cited [announcement](https://www.hgcapitaltrust.com/news-insights/news/2024/08-07-2024) does not establish receipt. The valuation chapter's unicorn-study annotation broadens the population to private companies generally. Visma's ESMA note overgeneralizes formal applicability. The finale's supplementary annotations blur study provenance and chronological coverage. The individual reviews provide locations, sources and the distinction between confirmed wording problems and further verification needed.
- **Private inputs and audience:** P01 and P02 responsibly identify the limits of private material. The current core book uses little of the Productscapes terminology and has no configured optional appendix. Keep P02 if it documents an actual intellectual input, but explain that role briefly rather than presenting an undeveloped model as something readers need to understand.
- **Ending and effectiveness:** “How to use the evidence” repeats much of “How to Read an Entry” and the opening. Combine the general rules in one place. The source-specific limits should stay attached to entries, where they are most useful.

## Recommended changes

- **High impact — Bring the further-reading claims under the same evidence discipline.** Check claim, population, period, transaction status and source version; then update the relevant chapter annotation and the register together. Treat this as a substantive source-scope review, not merely a broken-link check.
- **Medium impact — Add a topic index and “used in” chapter links.** Readers should be able to navigate from a question to evidence and back. Preserve stable identifiers even where the sequence has gaps.
- **Medium impact — Replace process history with present-tense scope.** Example: “Sources were consulted on 12–13 September 2026. Each entry states the version and material consulted; access to a document does not imply every page was reviewed.” Retain individual dates where they affect interpretation.
- **Medium impact — Separate proposed methods from empirical validation once, clearly.** Keep the private-input provenance, but avoid repeating the same unvalidated-model warning at several levels.
- **Low impact — Harmonize entry formatting and remove the duplicate closing reading instructions.** These changes improve scanability without reducing useful qualifications.

## Proposed structure

Purpose and evidence categories → topic/chapter index → public source register → private-input provenance → brief remaining research limits. Keep the detailed consultation notes. Move editorial revision chronology to an internal record, and distinguish sources used as evidence from optional further reading without inventing consultation history.
