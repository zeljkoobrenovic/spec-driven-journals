# Spec: Fund Economics: Fees, Distributions and Performance Reports

## Intent

Provide an optional reference for the fund-side mechanics that the Part I chapters mention but should not teach in full: management fee, carried interest, the distribution waterfall with preferred return, catch-up and clawback, and the DPI/RVPI/TVPI, gross/net and subscription-line vocabulary of a fund performance report. Close with what those mechanics mean for a company leader's requests and plans.

## Audience

Product and engineering leaders inside companies working under investors who want the depth behind [[announcement-is-not-a-budget]] and [[three-different-returns]], and secondary investor-side readers. Assume no specialist finance background; explain necessary terms before use.

## Success criteria

- Open with a three-bullet KEY POINTS block and a short paragraph stating that the page is optional depth for the fund-side mechanics behind Part I.
- Preserve the €160-on-€100 waterfall arithmetic and definitions moved from the announcement chapter, with the SEC citation, and show in words and simple arithmetic what a preferred return, catch-up and clawback change.
- Preserve the €100 / €60 / €90 DPI, RVPI and TVPI example, the gross-versus-net distinction and the subscription-line caution moved from the returns chapter, with the ILPA citation.
- End with one short section on what the mechanics mean for the company leader: incentives can shape the timing and kind of requests; a valuation increase pays nobody until realized; received versus estimated value.
- Label every rate as an illustrative assumption; keep the body under about 1,400 words; link back to [[announcement-is-not-a-budget]], [[three-different-returns]], [[different-bets]] and [[glossary]].
- Make the company leader's decision, authority and funding assumptions explicit. Distinguish the stated ownership arrangement from a claim about every investor.

Readers can find, interpret, and use the material without confusing evidence with proposals or fictional examples. Navigation is stable; confidential provenance remains outside site content.

## Non-goals

A full treatment of fund structuring, tax, legal terms or performance benchmarking; investment advice; a claim that any fee level or hurdle is typical. No historical case material.

## Modalities

This reference page uses the Article modality only. Reference material is not padded with a TL;DR or comic.

## Open questions

Revise when the announcement or returns chapters change their examples, or if the bibliography's numbering of S01 and S05 changes.
