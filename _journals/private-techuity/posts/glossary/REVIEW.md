# Editorial review: Glossary

Reassessed 15 September 2026 from every current definition and index entry, the previous review, revision log and chapter usage. See the [collection review](../REVIEW.md).

## Current assessment

The alphabetical index, separate authority section and split entries make this a substantially better reference. The AI definition now includes knowledge-based approaches, the obsolete Productscapes entry is gone, and sponsor labels, DORA lead time, profit measures and capacity versus cash are usefully distinguished. The final author-defined terms remain appropriately labelled as proposals.

The most important regression is overcorrection: the glossary says that only cash already in the company's account can fund a budget, and that a term sheet is not a contractual commitment regardless of its provisions. Because the introduction declares this page the terminology standard, those sentences can propagate into every format. A teaching definition should explain the distinction without overruling an arrangement it has not examined.

## Active recommendations

- **High — Replace “A budget can rely only on the last state” and “Only this state funds a budget.”** Received cash is different from an expected round, but budgets and authorized commitments can include forecast operating receipts and committed facilities drawable when needed. Conversely, cash received can be restricted or already required for obligations. Suggested wording: “Record received cash separately from forecast receipts and contractual funding. Before an irreversible commitment, establish when usable funds will be available, what conditions remain and the authorized fallback.” The SBA's description of [working-capital credit lines](https://www.sba.gov/sba-lenders/) supplies a concrete counterexample to the cash-already-in-the-account rule; it is not a financing recommendation for fictional Larkspur. Reconcile the [financing-delay review](../23-the-financing-slipped/REVIEW.md) at the same time.
- **Medium — Repair the term-sheet and transaction-status definitions.** “The legal effect … depends on the document” is followed by “it is not a contractual commitment.” Prefer “It does not by itself establish an unconditional commitment to fund; distinguish any binding provisions.” Similarly, a value can be announced after completion. “An announcement alone does not establish receipt or realization” is accurate; “an announced … value is neither received nor realized” is not a reliable universal rule. Keep full, partial and non-cash realization distinct from cash received by a particular holder.
- **Medium — Present accountability and approval as related, separate roles.** “Accountable company leader” is useful, but the current definition requires an executive authorized to decide, whereas Priya is accountable for work whose expansion the board approves. Say “the person answerable for delivery or results within a stated remit,” then identify the approver separately. An investment committee's consent can also be relevant under particular reserved rights; avoid implying that a company label alone settles every approval path.
- **Medium — Apply the improved definitions across formats before claiming the chapters follow them.** The glossary correctly allows debt investors, separates capacity from cash and distinguishes DORA's commit-to-production interval. The funding callout, loan case and organizational trace still contradict those definitions. Replace the introduction's certification with an editorial objective, and use the linked reviews to correct the contradictions. A glossary rule cannot make a worked example accurate by declaration.
- **Low — Finish retrieval and keep short definitions proportionate.** “Audited financial statements,” “Terminal value” and “Term sheet” have definitions but no alphabetical entries. Add those links and check paired terms systematically. Section-level anchors are an acceptable implementation of the previous recommendation; individual row anchors are optional. Retain the informal asset/liability qualification and the more careful microservices entry rather than expanding this into an accounting or architecture textbook.

## Verification and changes since the previous review

The [OECD memorandum](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2024/03/explanatory-memorandum-on-the-updated-oecd-definition-of-an-ai-system_3c815e51/623da898-en.pdf) explicitly includes machine-learning and knowledge-based approaches; the revised scope is correct. [DORA's current guide](https://dora.dev/guides/dora-metrics/) supports five metrics and the commit-to-production definition. Do not restore the historical four-metric formulation. Visma's report defines ARR as annualised repeatable revenue, which the glossary correctly distinguishes from the usual recurring-revenue label.
