**“Cut a fifth of engineering” is a number, not a plan.** A reduction is decided by the work that stops, and judged by when its savings actually arrive in the bank.

**Figure 1:** *Find the source of the pressure, cut by work, reconcile cash by date, and look after both sides of the door.*

Take Larkspur, the fictional scheduling-software company this book follows, with forty employees and a delayed €4 million investment. Its existing investors will lend a €600,000 bridge, but only if the board adopts a plan by 31 October that keeps cash above its €400,000 reserve until 30 June, without counting the delayed money.

The request to cut arrives in three forms that sound alike. The loan condition is a cash date with money attached; it says nothing about headcount. The investor’s director holds a formal vote on the plan. And the remark that other companies cut a fifth of engineering is only **information, not an instruction**; at Larkspur it would save €28,000 a month, when Sam, the finance chief, calculates that at least €40,000 is needed.

So the board compares options on one basis and chooses to **stop whole pieces of work rather than slice every team**. The mobile app, with no revenue yet, stops. The second-country expansion narrows to the two customers already signed. That removes eight roles and saves €72,000 a month. A deeper cut to what customer payments alone could cover was rejected: it would remove the support staff that revenue depends on and the pilot the new investors want to see.

Then comes the part plans often skip. **Savings arrive after costs.** Leavers are paid through one to three months’ notice and receive severance, €120,000 in all, so the full monthly saving only arrives in April. With the bridge, cash on 30 June is €488,000, meeting the condition. Without the bridge, cash still falls below the reserve by January. At these terms the reduction makes the loan last longer; it cannot replace it.

Customers are handled before notices go out. One second-country customer agrees to start in September; the other keeps its March date, so one specialist stays until a colleague proves, on real work, that they can carry the rules alone.

The people matter on both sides of the door. The eight are told individually and first, and all receive the same written terms: paid notice, severance, job-search help, a reference and a year to use share options they have already earned. The thirty-two who stay get a published list of work no longer expected, rather than silently absorbing eight people’s jobs. Local employment law decides the calendar, so advisers confirm it before any date is fixed.

Even so, the reduction only buys time until August. The next chapter, [[scale-the-team-with-ai]], asks the same questions of a claim that AI changes what the team can do.
