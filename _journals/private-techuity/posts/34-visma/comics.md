**Comic.** A change of fund, even under the same manager, can change the money and timeline behind long-running work on the systems several products share, such as sign-in, billing and hosting. A **fund** is money pooled from investors and used to buy **shares**, the units of ownership in a company. The **investment manager** is the firm that runs the fund, and one firm can run several funds over the years, each with its own investors and its own holding period, the time those investors expect to keep the investment before it is sold. The fund, or a holding company it sets up, owns the shares; the manager’s name on the door tells you little about whose money is behind it.

Seven pages follow that pattern through Visma’s documents: the ownership chain in plain words, the December 2023 secondary sale, the funds and shareholders entering and leaving named in Hg’s and Visma’s announcements, the pace of acquisitions, the two earnings definitions under which the same year is reported, the bridge between them and what an add-back does, and the budget and authority a fictional team would recheck. Each page is three strips; the reported figures and definitions are drawn into the artwork, and a transcript follows every page. **EBITDA** is a profit measure taken before borrowing costs, tax and the accounting charges that spread asset costs over years; **adjusted EBITDA** leaves out further items the company specifies.

Morgan is an investor’s adviser; Alex leads technology, Priya leads product and Sam leads finance. All are fictional people reading Visma’s and Hg’s public announcements and reports; the scenes do not reenact real events.
