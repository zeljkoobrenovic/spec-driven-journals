**Visma**, a group of business-software companies, has kept the same investment firm, **Hg**, involved since 2006 while the funds and shareholders behind it changed. A **fund** is money pooled from investors and used to buy **shares**, units of ownership in a company. The **investment manager** runs the fund; one firm can run several funds, each with its own investors, rights and holding period (how long its investors expect to keep it before selling); the fund, not the manager, owns the shares. A change of fund, even under the same manager, can change the money and timeline behind long-running work on systems several products share.

Hg’s 2017 announcement names two different Hg funds for its 2006 and 2014 investments. It also announces a **buyout**, a purchase giving a group of buyers control. Investment firms KKR and Cinven were to sell, KKR all its shares and Cinven 40% of its own holding, subject to regulatory approval. Hg led the buying group and would hold 41% of the shares. [S62: Hg 2017 Visma announcement](https://hgcapital.com/insights/hg-leads-usd5-3bn-buyout-of-visma)

Visma’s December 2023 announcement describes a **secondary sale**: existing shareholders sold shares to other investors, so the money went to sellers, not the company. The deal values the business at €19 billion; about 20 new investors put in over €1 billion and existing ones about €3 billion, and Hg stays **majority** holder (more than half the shares). [S30: Visma 2023 transaction](https://www.visma.com/newsroom/visma-attracts-new-investors-for-further-international-expansion-in-a-transaction-valuing-the-company-at-eur-19-billion-59703d9f) Neither document says who sold in 2023, which funds now hold Hg’s shares, or how much money, if any, reached Visma.

Visma describes local decision-making, shared infrastructure (technical foundations such as hosting and security), product investment and 33 acquisitions in 2024, nearly three a month. [S32: Visma annual-report announcement](https://www.visma.com/newsroom/visma-releases-annual-and-sustainability-reports-for-2024-7f5c9f37) The sources document no intervention with its cost and customer outcome; the model is a question, not proof.

**Check the numbers too.** **EBITDA** is profit before interest (borrowing costs), taxes, and depreciation and amortization (charges spreading asset costs over years of use). Visma’s 2024 annual report shows EBITDA of €892.646 million; its 2025 report adds back €11.665 million of “M&A expenses” (mergers and acquisitions: fees for carrying out deals, not the companies’ purchase prices), to reach **adjusted EBITDA** of €904.311 million for the same year. [S43: Visma 2024 annual report](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/69787181d8720ea08c1f22fe/69787181d8720ea08c1f460b_Visma-Annual-Report-2024.pdf) [S44: Visma 2025 annual report, p. 96](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/69787181d8720ea08c1f22fe/69bb9fc407e856cb8d6f7726_Visma%20Annual%20Report%202025.pdf) An **add-back** removes a recorded expense from the comparison; no cash comes back; the later figure is a changed measure, not extra performance.

The adjustment cannot settle the budget. **Costs can be exceptional for one transaction while recurring across a continuing program.** A group that keeps acquiring must plan cash and engineer-weeks (one engineer for one week) to buy the next companies and integrate the last ones, know which earnings measure its lenders apply, and name who approves the next purchase.

After such a transaction, recheck: which funds or holding companies hold the shares and what they expect, without assuming one fund holds the majority; whether the shared-service budget and integration capacity (people and time to connect acquired systems and move customers) are still funded, and by whom; and which earnings definition the board, lenders and plan each use. Independent customer, employee and acquired-company evidence is missing.
