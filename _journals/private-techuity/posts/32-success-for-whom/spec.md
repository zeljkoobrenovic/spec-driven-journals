# Spec: Success for Whom, and for How Long?

## Intent

Close the book by putting its five cases side by side (investor result, company result, stakeholder evidence, what is not established), stating durable success as the author’s proposed standard, and applying it to one final fictional Larkspur decision with a real trade-off. Keep buyout and healthcare research bounded and in service of that judgment; treat an exit as a decisive reporting event for some investors, not the only measurement horizon.

## Audience

Product and engineering leaders inside companies working under investors, including leaders who inherit an ownership arrangement. Assume no specialist finance background; explain necessary terms before use. Investor-side readers are secondary.

## Success criteria

- Add one whole-post overview visual to the TL;DR after its opening paragraph, generated with the Nano Banana article illustrator. Keep the 300–500-word summary prose, bold emphasis and citations; provide alt text and a concise numbered caption.
- Provide a distinctive article header logo and a simple navigation icon with unique asset paths. Keep both consistent with the journal’s visual style.
- Include two explanatory article figures with accurate short labels, alt text and numbered captions. Use restrained bold emphasis for key claims; preserve the words, citations and historical data. The comic modality is a set of comic pages — each image one page of three stacked strips with the dialogue, labels, amounts and conditions lettered in the artwork — generated from the `comic-page` blocks in `comics.md`, with consistent fictional characters, a caption and a transcript per page.
- Open with a compact case matrix drawn only from the four case chapters; leave unknown cells explicitly unknown.
- Complete one Larkspur decision in which a financially attractive option damages customer continuity or transfers cost to employees: chosen option, alternatives rejected, funding, scarce capacity, who is authorized, evidence that would change it, and the recorded disagreement with its review.
- Give suppliers their own row in the success-for-whom table; allow a deliberately funded continuing specialist service as durable capability; link the handover chapter where handover is mentioned.
- Make the company leader’s decision, authority and funding assumptions explicit. Distinguish the stated ownership arrangement from a claim about every investor.
- Keep historical findings within their source scope and label new comparative scenarios as fictional.
- Introduce necessary terms in ordinary language before using them in explanations, examples, tables, or diagrams; keep opening key points readable without prior study.
- Move from a concrete question through explanation and example to a practical conclusion; connect the conclusion to the next chapter in the configured reading order.
- Make summaries and comic storyboards understandable on their own; preserve source limits and label fictional scenarios, including changes in example assumptions.
- Explain the title question and its implications for a concrete company decision.
- Distinguish sourced observations, the manuscript's analysis, and explicitly fictional examples.
- Include a meaningful trade-off or counterargument and identify the evidence that would change the judgment.
- Link related chapters and cite substantive external factual claims close to the text.
- Keep the TL;DR and the comic page scripts consistent with the full article; inspect every generated page against its script.
- Price the final decision on one stated cost basis: the €75-an-hour fully loaded planning rate (pay plus the other costs of employing someone) from the onboarding chapter, 2,000 hours a year for a full-time role and 1,000 for the half-time data-preparation role, so the half-time role is €75,000 and the year-two cash reduction €75,000; never present a fully loaded rate as pay alone, and keep released staff time on a separate line from payments avoided.
- Fix one calendar counted in months from day zero and walk every milestone through it (day-100 review early in month four; board meeting late in month four; Option A’s 90-day cut-off at the close of month seven; second-cohort review at about month six; migration months seven to twelve; month-nine review; year two from month thirteen), and compute retained staffing from that calendar (five months, €62,500).
- Make every saving conditional on the obligation that ends it: A′’s saving starts only when the last manual-path customer has moved; B’s month twelve is a target backed by the contract check, with named staffing and a funding decision for any account still on the manual path afterwards; the migration starts only if the second cohort passes both month-six tests (average effort of 50 hours or less counting every hour of data work, and a median wait of eight weeks or less), which permit a request for the next stage and approve nothing.
- Present the next-sale callout as a possible consequence of unresolved commitments in a sale process, not as a legal duty; the chapter is not a legal opinion.
- Price every fallback against Option B’s own plan, not against the full-time baseline: full-time coverage costs €12,500 a month in total and the planned half-time service €6,250, so each month after month twelve in which full-time coverage replaces the half-time service costs €6,250 more than planned and cuts the €75,000 year-two saving by €6,250 (twelve such months remove it); a delay before month thirteen changes no saving, because none was scheduled. Apply the capacity condition to every fallback branch (the continuing workload must fit the 1,000 half-time hours, otherwise the board funds extra coverage), name who supplies the coverage if the specialist declines (the outside data-preparation provider) and charge any provider premium against the saving.
- Wherever a short format refers to the month-six tests, state them (average setup effort of 50 hours or less including all data work; median wait of eight weeks or less, counted from contract signing to the customer first scheduling real work in the product), say what migration moves customers onto (the newer setup process), say that unfinished results postpone the decision and that a failed test stops the planned start; label the comic’s €75,000 as a planned, conditional saving in the artwork and the caption, and keep the decision page’s caption ordered as tests, funding, review.
- In the case matrix keep one decisive investor/company contrast and the main evidence gap per case and leave transaction chains, including HgCapital Trust’s 2021 sale and reinvestment and its 2024 sale, to the case chapters; if the 2024 sale is mentioned at all, never join the Trust’s 2016 retained stake to it without naming the 2021 sale and reinvestment through a later fund; say that the Trust’s figures are its own share, not the whole price; name the currencies the matrix uses once.
- Keep the decision table’s reopening evidence in one row per trigger (month-six tests, month-nine count, early finish) and each healthcare study in its own short paragraph.
- Report the 2023 hospital study’s 4.6 conditions per 10,000 stays as an adjusted difference against the comparison hospitals (25.4% of the acquired hospitals’ pre-acquisition rate of about 18 per 10,000), never as the observed before-and-after change at the acquired hospitals; in every format that cites the 2025 hospital-sale letter, name the comparison group of 18 hospitals sold to other for-profit owners.
- Figure 2’s alt text or caption names the cycle’s direction and its three questions (who benefits, who bears costs, what could change).
- Explain revenue, operating earnings, net loss (costs exceeding income), fiscal year, cohort, median, retained stake and private equity where each short format first needs them; the TL;DR defines a fund as pooled investor money, explains operating profit before operating margin, includes remaining value in the investor result and stays at or under 500 prose words; the TL;DR overview figure labels its last station AFTER THE OWNERSHIP CHANGE and shows no currency symbol.

## Non-goals

Universal prescriptions, invented evidence, promises of investment performance, or disclosure of confidential inputs. The chapter is not a legal or tax opinion.

## Modalities

- Full Article: index.md, the substantive argument.
- TL;DR: summary.md, the practical implications in concise prose.
- Comic: comics.md, seven illustrated pages of three strips each, with alt text, visible captions and per-strip transcripts rendered from the page blocks that are also the artwork scripts; the five cases appear as reported figures on boards, never reenacted.

Evidence gaps and chapter-specific next research are tracked in the separate editorial backlog. The book uses the author’s accepted working title; evidence and role questions remain open.
