# Editorial review: Match the Funding to the Work

Reassessed 15 September 2026 from the complete current article, summary and comic text, previous review, revision log and Git changes. See the [collection review](../REVIEW.md).

## Current assessment

The new title accurately names the chapter's task, and the €610,000 requirement gives the company leader a much better model than the former category tour. Build, transition, maintenance, temporary staffing and uncertainty now have explicit places. The distinction between Alex/Priya's recommendation and CEO/CFO/board financing authority is clear. Preserve those improvements and the conclusion that continuing under current ownership can be a legitimate choice.

However, the central choice is not financially demonstrated: it treats freed capacity as repayment cash and specifies an incomplete loan schedule. The chapter has improved structurally while introducing a high-impact error in the decision it now asks readers to emulate. Repair that example before further polishing. The order from work to alternatives to authority and reassessment is sound; no revised outline is needed.

## Active recommendations

- **High — Remove capacity value from the cash available for debt service.** In “The choice,” €350,000 operating cash is added to €135,000 of freed implementation capacity to produce €485,000. The latter is 18 hours × 100 implementations × €75, an assigned value of staff time. It is not extra receipts or a reduction in cash payroll. The linked roadmap chapter distinguishes these. Show an actual cash mechanism and its dates, or assess debt service from the €350,000 alone. For example: “Released staff time is a capacity benefit. Finance excludes it from repayment cash until cancelled contractor spending or additional collected contribution is evidenced.” €350,000 already exceeds the stated €310,000 year-two payment by €40,000; whether that margin is sufficient needs a dated downside forecast, not the €135,000 addition. Correct the summary's identical shortcut and reassessment language about repaying a loan “from capacity.”
- **High — Complete the three-year loan terms.** €250,000 principal annually beginning in year two repays only €500,000 by the end of year three. State a €250,000 balloon, a longer term or a different amortization schedule, then recalculate interest on the outstanding principal and test each payment date. Flat €60,000 annual interest also needs a stated basis if principal is falling. These are confirmed gaps in the fictional agreement, not questions about current market lending terms.
- **Medium — Make the funding decision genuinely reviewable.** The paragraph says the leaders brought “the review date,” but supplies no date; triggers alternate between effort above 70 hours and failure to reach 62. Set a cohort, review date and distinct warning/stop thresholds. Explain whether a reserve includes financing costs and avoid treating €2.5m as removing all future funding risk.
- **Medium — Qualify the new callout.** A small raise does not inevitably force a rescue round; a large raise need not fund unjustified work or dilute every investor's economic return. Use “can” and identify the trade-off between ownership cost, reserve and future financing risk. Preserve the balanced body rather than reopening the old universal prescription through a highlight.
- **Low — Round transparently and let the comic show the comparison sooner.** 25% of €270,000 is €67,500, reasonably rounded to €70,000; label the rounding. Several comic panels still cover general categories before the final caption announces the choice. That is a pacing improvement for a later pass, after the choice is corrected.
