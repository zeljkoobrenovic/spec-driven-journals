# Spec: Match the Funding to the Work

## Intent

Size the work a company must do next (base work, transition, uncertainty allowance, decision runway), then compare which funding arrangement can support it. Show one fictional Larkspur comparison of two feasible arrangements for the same need and the choice made under stated assumptions, and give leaders who inherit the arrangement an action within their authority. Separate investor type, control, financing and transaction context; a carve-out and a turnaround are not categories of investor. Detailed control and partnership questions belong to Part II and are linked, not repeated.

## Audience

Product and engineering leaders inside companies working under investors, including leaders who inherit an ownership arrangement. Assume no specialist finance background; explain necessary terms before use. Investor-side readers are secondary.

## Success criteria

- Provide a distinctive article header logo and a simple navigation icon with unique asset paths. Keep both consistent with the journal’s visual style.

The comic modality is generated from its six-panel storyboard with consistent fictional characters and preserved captions.
- Complete one fictional sizing comparison: the cash need with its components, two feasible arrangements, the chosen option, alternatives rejected, funding, scarce capacity, who is authorized, and the evidence that would change the decision; include the counterarguments to raising less (a reserve for uncertainty; a future round may be unavailable).
- Make the financing test checkable: state the opening cash and the board’s minimum balance, show a compact year-by-year cash-balance forecast (loan received, operating cash, work paid for, loan payments, closing cash) for the base and downside cases, and distinguish an annual operating-cash deficit covered from retained cash from a missed payment, and consuming the reserve from underfunding the committed work. Say whether maintenance after the funded period sits inside the operating forecast.
- Give the fallback a feasible staffing sequence: who performs the twelve engineer-weeks, what the amber data-quality step buys with its €40,000 and who supplies its four engineer-weeks (and what work moves to free them), and how that schedule reaches the month-six measurement without double-booking the implementation specialist.
- State whether Arrangement A’s eighteen-month expansion is a contractual condition or the plan the money is raised on (enforced in practice through the budget approval right), and describe later equity after a disappointing pilot as potentially more expensive or unavailable rather than as certain worse terms; keep that distinction in the summary and comic.
- Replace category determinism with the conditional formulation (terms and expectations affect pace, tolerated losses and time) in all three formats; keep the category comparison to a compact recap with links.
- Explain when changes in demand, funding or timing require the ownership and financing fit to be reassessed; keep the principle that declining outside ownership can be a successful decision.
- Make the company leader’s decision, authority and funding assumptions explicit. Distinguish the stated ownership arrangement from a claim about every investor.
- Keep historical findings within their source scope and label new comparative scenarios as fictional.
- Introduce necessary terms in ordinary language before using them in explanations, examples, tables, or diagrams; keep opening key points readable without prior study.
- Move from a concrete question through explanation and example to a practical conclusion; connect the conclusion to the next chapter in the configured reading order.
- Make summaries and comic storyboards understandable on their own; preserve source limits and label fictional scenarios, including changes in example assumptions.
- The summary states the pilot test in full (eight customers, from 80 staff hours per customer, 50 the target, measured at day 90, board decision at day 100) and keeps the after-tax, after-existing-obligations qualification on operating cash. The comic’s closing panels carry both offers (€610,000 need, €750,000 loan, €2.5m ownership investment), the decisive trade-off and the leaders’ recommendation as distinct from board approval; image-only labels in the comic panels and Figure 1 have a short visible text equivalent.
- Explain the title question and its implications for a concrete company decision.
- Distinguish sourced observations, the manuscript's analysis, and explicitly fictional examples.
- Include a meaningful trade-off or counterargument and identify the evidence that would change the judgment.
- Link related chapters and cite substantive external factual claims close to the text.
- Keep the TL;DR and six-panel comic script consistent with the full article.

## Non-goals

Universal prescriptions, invented evidence, promises of investment performance, or disclosure of confidential inputs. The chapter is not a legal or tax opinion.
