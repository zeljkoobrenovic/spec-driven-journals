# Spec: Match the Funding to the Work

## Intent

Size the work a company must do next (base work, transition, uncertainty allowance, decision runway), then compare which funding arrangement can support it. Show one fictional Larkspur comparison of two feasible arrangements for the same need and the choice made under stated assumptions, and give leaders who inherit the arrangement an action within their authority. Separate investor type, control, financing and transaction context; a carve-out and a turnaround are not categories of investor. Detailed control and partnership questions belong to Part II and are linked, not repeated.

## Audience

Product and engineering leaders inside companies working under investors, including leaders who inherit an ownership arrangement. Assume no specialist finance background; explain necessary terms before use. Investor-side readers are secondary.

- Complete one fictional sizing comparison: the cash need with its components, two feasible arrangements, the chosen option, alternatives rejected, funding, scarce capacity, who is authorized, and the evidence that would change the decision; include the counterarguments to raising less (a reserve for uncertainty; a future round may be unavailable).
- Make the financing test checkable: state the opening cash and the board’s minimum balance, show a compact year-by-year cash-balance forecast (loan received, operating cash, work paid for, loan payments, closing cash) for the base and downside cases, and distinguish an annual operating-cash deficit covered from retained cash from a missed payment, and consuming the reserve from underfunding the committed work. Say whether maintenance after the funded period sits inside the operating forecast. State which pilot outcome the forecast assumes and include the spending that outcome triggers (the €40,000 amber step in the 62-hour case); list the earmarked money (allowance, runway, first-year maintenance, reserve) separately with its spending and release dates, so that the earmarked amount matches the year-end it is reported at and the month-18 release is a relabelling, not a receipt. Map maintenance, the contract specialist and the runway to months so that no month is paid for twice. State the repayment-risk conclusion only as far as the forecasts test it: both illustrated cases cover every payment, and the remaining risk is cash falling short on a repayment date in any year because receipts fall or spending rises, not a risk confined to years one and two.
- Give the fallback a feasible staffing sequence: who performs the twelve engineer-weeks, what the amber data-quality step buys with its €40,000 and who supplies its four engineer-weeks (and what work moves to free them), and how that schedule reaches the month-six measurement without double-booking the implementation specialist. - Replace category determinism with the conditional formulation (terms and expectations affect pace, tolerated losses and time) in all three formats; keep the category comparison to a compact recap with links.
- Explain when changes in demand, funding or timing require the ownership and financing fit to be reassessed; keep the principle that declining outside ownership can be a successful decision.
- Make the company leader’s decision, authority and funding assumptions explicit. Distinguish the stated ownership arrangement from a claim about every investor.
- Keep historical findings within their source scope and label new comparative scenarios as fictional.
- Introduce necessary terms in ordinary language before using them in explanations, examples, tables, or diagrams; keep opening key points readable without prior study. Figure labels that use jargon (Figure 2’s “adjust scope”) get a plain equivalent in the visible caption and the alt text; the summary states the loan’s 8% as annual interest, the charge for borrowing, on the amount still owed.

The comic’s closing panels carry both offers (€610,000 need, €750,000 loan, €2.5m ownership investment), the decisive trade-off and the leaders’ recommendation as distinct from board approval; image-only labels in the comic panels, the summary overview and Figure 1 have a short visible text equivalent. The summary stays within 300–500 prose words.

## Non-goals

Universal prescriptions, invented evidence, promises of investment performance, or disclosure of confidential inputs. The chapter is not a legal or tax opinion.
