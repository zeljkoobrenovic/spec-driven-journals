**Match the funding to the work.** Personal cash for a selling founder, money for customer learning, funding for expansion and a change of control are different needs, and the same software can require different arrangements under different circumstances. Product and engineering leaders may not choose the investor but they inherit the terms. The terms of the funding and the investor’s expectations affect the pace, the tolerated losses and the time available for the plan; the investor’s category on its own does not.

Separate **four dimensions**: who the investor is, what rights it holds, how the company is financed and what event it faces. A carve-out is a separation and a turnaround is a problem to solve; neither identifies the owner. [[customers-lenders-investors]] introduces the arrangements; this chapter keeps only the question each one raises about the work.

**Size the need before naming the investor.** In the fictional Larkspur case, onboarding depends on manual configuration and the queue is growing. Alex and Priya size the fix at about **€610,000**: €180,000 to build a reusable setup step, €30,000 of first-year maintenance, €90,000 of transition, €150,000 for a contract implementation specialist during the build, a €70,000 uncertainty allowance and €90,000 of decision runway in case the cohort evidence arrives late. Asking for the €180,000 build alone would fund the software and nothing that makes it work.

Two arrangements could fund it. **€2.5m of growth equity** brings a reserve and removes the risk that a later round is unavailable, but carries approval rights and an expectation of a second country within eighteen months, a pace the pilot has not earned. **A €750,000 term loan** at 8% keeps control and funds the staged work, with fixed repayments whatever the pilot shows. Under the stated assumptions, about €350,000 of annual operating cash and customer evidence for the setup constraint but none for the second country, Ines and Sam recommend the loan: the year-two payment fits operating cash plus freed capacity even if effort falls only to 62 hours. Signed second-country customers would reverse the choice; a cohort still above 70 hours would send them back to the equity conversation while the loan still has headroom.

Alex and Priya do not sign the financing. They bring the revised scope, the cash requirement with its components, the alternative of hiring two specialists at about €300,000 a year and the review date. The CEO, the CFO and the board renegotiate terms; the shareholders’ agreement says when shareholder consent is needed.

**Control and partnership are separate questions.** A minority stake can carry material approval rights; a founder can keep shares and lose a role. [[decide-who-decides]] and [[investor-under-pressure]] develop them. Revisit the fit when demand, funding or time changes. Changing the transaction, changing the plan or staying under current ownership can each be right: a business that decides against outside ownership has answered the question, not failed a test.
