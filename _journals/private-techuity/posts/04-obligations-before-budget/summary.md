**Positive EBITDA does not establish cash available for a new initiative.** Growing earnings and an unfunded migration can coexist. Reconcile the earnings figure with investment, taxes, financing payments, working-capital needs and the cash already on hand, then check its dates and restrictions. An ownership arrangement changes the conditions of that reconciliation rather than creating the gap: borrowing adds contractual dates, a shareholders’ agreement may add distributions or approval thresholds, a parent may set an allocation.

**EBITDA** is earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization. **Capital expenditure** is spending on assets such as equipment or qualifying development. **Working capital** here is money tied up in unpaid customer invoices and inventory, less amounts owed to suppliers; growth can absorb cash when the business spends before collecting.

The fictional Larkspur planning model starts with €10 million of EBITDA. After €4 million of cash interest, €1 million of cash tax, €2 million of capital expenditure, €1 million of additional working capital and €1.5 million of required principal repayment, €0.5 million remains. The bridge is a **planning calculation, not a universal payment priority**. Interest and principal are contractual and move only under the loan’s terms or an agreed change; tax follows statute; capital expenditure is a management choice, partly contracted; distributions are a proposal unless an agreement requires them. Engineering salaries are already inside EBITDA.

That residual, plus €0.3 million of opening cash above the board’s reserve, gives about €0.8 million of discretionary capacity against a proposed €1 million onboarding automation. Three priced options: **minimum continuity**, a contract specialist for two peak quarters, €75,000, decided by Ines and Sam at mid-year; a **staged pilot** of the reusable setup step, €180,000 to build plus €30,000 of first-year maintenance, approved by Ines within her delegated limit, reviewed on the first cohort at day 90 with the board deciding at day 100 and a month-six second-stage gate of 50 hours or less; or the **full €1 million request**, which exceeds the residual and needs the board, the lender under the covenant or new equity. A first cohort below 60 hours brings the second-stage decision forward; a fallback to a one-quarter specialist adds €37,500 to a build already spent rather than replacing it. A separate Larkspur scenario has no acquisition debt: €3m of cash and monthly spending exceeding receipts by €250,000, so simple **runway** is 12 months. Hires adding €100,000 a month cut it to about 8.6 months, before a round expected in month nine. [[the-financing-slipped]] works through the dated version of that decision.

**Covenants** can give lenders rights after one weak quarter, and floating rates raise cash costs while sales weaken: €60 million of debt costs €1.8 million more a year at 9% than at 6%. **Capitalization** changes when development costs enter profit; later amortization is excluded from EBITDA, and the cash still leaves.
