**Positive EBITDA does not establish cash available for a new initiative.** A company can report growing operating earnings while an essential migration stays unfunded. Reconcile the earnings figure with investment, taxes, financing payments, working-capital needs and the cash already on hand, then check the dates and restrictions on that cash. An ownership arrangement changes the conditions of that reconciliation rather than creating the gap: acquisition borrowing adds contractual interest and repayment dates, a shareholders’ agreement may add distributions or approval thresholds, a corporate parent may set an allocation.

**EBITDA** is earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization. It leaves out financing payments, income tax and investment spending. **Capital expenditure** is spending on assets such as equipment or qualifying development. **Working capital**, in this example, is money tied up in unpaid customer invoices and inventory, less amounts owed to suppliers; growth can absorb cash when the business spends before collecting.

The fictional Larkspur planning model starts with €10 million of EBITDA. After €4 million of cash interest, €1 million of cash tax, €2 million of capital expenditure, €1 million of additional working capital and €1.5 million of required principal repayment, €0.5 million remains. The bridge is a **planning calculation, not a universal payment priority**. Interest and principal are contractual and move only under the loan agreement; tax follows statute; capital expenditure is a management choice, partly contracted; distributions are a proposal unless an agreement requires them. Engineering salaries are already inside EBITDA.

That residual, plus €0.3 million of opening cash above the board’s reserve, gives about €0.8 million of discretionary capacity against a proposed €1 million onboarding automation. Three priced options: **minimum continuity**, a contract specialist and defect fixes for €150,000, decided by Ines and Sam at the mid-year review; a **staged pilot** of the reusable setup step for €200,000 and twelve engineer-weeks, approved by Ines within her delegated limit, the board deciding the second stage on the month-six cohort review; or the **full €1 million request**, which exceeds the residual and needs the board, the lender under the covenant or new equity. Ines funds the pilot; Priya is accountable; a cohort below 60 hours per implementation would bring the second stage forward. [[cannot-fund-everything]] applies the method across several projects.

A separate Larkspur scenario has no acquisition debt: €3m of cash and monthly spending exceeding receipts by €250,000, so simple **runway** is 12 months. Hires adding €100,000 a month cut it to about 8.6 months, before a round expected in month nine. [[the-financing-slipped]] works through the dated version of that decision.

**Covenants** can give lenders rights after one weak quarter, and floating rates raise cash costs while sales weaken: €60 million of debt costs €1.8 million more a year at 9% than at 6%. **Capitalization** changes when development costs enter profit; later amortization is excluded from EBITDA, and the cash still leaves.
