Security and resilience protect the company's ability to operate, keep commitments, and recover. Their value cannot be judged solely from whether an incident happened during the reporting period.

Start with critical business services and credible failure scenarios. Identify what customers would lose, how long the company could tolerate disruption, and which dependencies matter. Then connect controls, response, and recovery to those scenarios. A compliance checklist can support the work but does not replace evidence that the service can withstand or recover from failure.

NIST's Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 includes governance alongside identification, protection, detection, response, and recovery. [S17: NIST CSF 2.0](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/CSWP/NIST.CSWP.29.pdf) The implication for ownership is practical: executives and boards must understand the risk decisions, responsibilities, and resources. Purchasing a security tool does not transfer that accountability.

**Risk reduction is different from booked profit.** The fictional example in the full article changes an assumed annual loss probability and calculates a reduction in expected loss. Those probabilities are uncertain, the distribution may contain severe tail outcomes, and the result is not an EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) saving. Use scenarios and sensitivity to support judgment rather than presenting false precision.

Recovery needs operational evidence. A backup exists; a restore test demonstrates something about usability. Even a successful technical restore may not establish that customer service, communications, data reconciliation, and supplier dependencies can recover within the required time. Test the business process as well as the component.

A Technology Principal can help make risk material to the investment and operating plan, commission focused expertise, and ensure findings receive an owner and a decision. Company management retains its responsibilities. Information sharing must respect the engagement's confidentiality, purpose, and authority.

A useful scorecard therefore combines exposure, remediation progress, tested recovery, and consequential exceptions. Avoid rewarding teams for suppressing incident reports or closing findings without verifying the result. The objective is a company that understands and manages its exposure, including the uncomfortable decisions that require money, time, or an explicit acceptance of residual risk.
