The early period after funding or an ownership change should establish a **funded operating plan**: who is accountable, what cash and capacity are approved, when decisions fall due and what is deferred. A hundred days is a planning convention, not proof that every business changes at that speed. Confirm which transaction assumptions and review dates change the company’s plan.

The chapter follows one fictional scenario: a growth investment in Larkspur whose thesis assumes onboarding volume can double without more implementation staff. The board authorized one envelope of **€500,000 additional cash and 24 engineer-weeks**; Ines, the CEO, spends inside the plan, and any reserve draw returns to the board.

Confirm inherited findings with the people who will do the work. **D-3** arrives by identifier: onboarding depends on one specialist’s manual configuration, about 80 hours each in a five-of-twelve sample. Management keeps the dependency but not the sampled figure, measuring a baseline by day 20 from all twelve implementations of the previous two quarters: 80 hours again, now with population, period and source stated, and configuration under half the hours. Alex’s claim that 40% of that effort is poor customer data stays on the record for the pilot to resolve.

**The plan.** ONB-1, the onboarding pilot: Priya, €180,000 committed, 12 engineer-weeks, day-90 cohort review. KNW-1, a second engineer able to release and recover the scheduling engine: Alex, no cash, 4 weeks of the specialist’s protected time outside the 24, day-60 demonstration. REC-1, a restore test: Alex with the operations lead, €80,000, 4 weeks, day-45 test. Portal research: Priya, €20,000, 2 weeks. Total **€280,000 and 18 engineer-weeks**, leaving €220,000 and 6 weeks in reserve. Commitments are not spending: about €90,000 of ONB-1 is incurred within the period and the rest over two quarters, with €30,000 a year of maintenance in the operating budget from the next financial year. The portal build, second-country expansion and two hires are deferred, each decision recorded.

Dependencies decide the order: the restore environment before day 45, the baseline before the cohort, the specialist’s time never spent twice. Keep outside support inside the plan’s cash ([[useful-engagement]]).

**Review the plan, not its completion rate.** REC-1 failed at day 45 on a departed engineer’s credential and an unavailable database version; its €80,000 was sunk. The board approved **€20,000 and 2 weeks from the reserve** for the correction, and the retest passed at day 85. KNW-1 passed at day 60. ONB-1’s cohort of eight cut effort from 80 to 62 hours, not the 50 assumed; about 40% of the remaining hours traced to data quality, and waiting time was unchanged. On the agreed rule, 62 funds the next learning step: the board approved a **€40,000, 4-week data-quality step** from the reserve, using the last engineer-weeks (committed €340,000 and 24 weeks; €160,000 left), dated the expansion decision to the next quarterly review around day 190, no expansion before month 13, and kept hiring deferred. That revised commitment is the payoff. [[the-financing-slipped]] asks what changes when money arrives late; [[handover-of-obligations]] carries the obligations forward.
