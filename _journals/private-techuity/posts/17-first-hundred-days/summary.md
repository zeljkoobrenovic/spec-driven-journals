The first 100 days should establish the conditions for useful execution: shared understanding, clear ownership, reliable baselines, feasible priorities, and funded decisions. The number is a planning convention, not evidence that every business changes at the same speed.

Start by checking what has changed since diligence and what company leaders already know. Some commitments are urgent; some findings were based on incomplete access; some initiatives already work. Separate immediate continuity and material risk obligations from the improvement portfolio, while keeping both visible to the people responsible.

**Choose a small set of connected priorities that fits actual capacity.** The chapter uses three priorities for fictional Larkspur: improve onboarding, reduce a knowledge dependency, and verify recovery. The example is a method demonstration. It is not a claim that three priorities, or any fixed count, is empirically optimal.

Each initiative needs a company owner, a business mechanism, a baseline, resources, dependencies, and a review condition. State what must stop or wait to make room. An outcome target without an implementation path is an ambition; an activity list without an outcome is a work program of uncertain value.

Sequence dependencies deliberately. A growth target may require simpler implementation before more sales demand. A data initiative may require clear ownership and lawful access before a new tool. A recovery exercise may reveal a need for supplier changes. The portfolio should express these connections rather than assuming every initiative can proceed independently.

The Productscapes material proposes continuity from assessment into action and learning. [P02: Productscapes hypothesis](pt-bibliography.html) This chapter turns that hypothesis into a practical operating discipline while leaving room to test whether the additional structure earns its coordination cost.

At the end of the period, review decisions and evidence rather than celebrating completion of a presentation. Which assumptions survived? Which outcomes moved? What remains uncertain? Which initiatives should stop, expand, or change? The Principal's task is to help make this learning usable by executives and investors. The result should be a company-owned plan that can adapt through the rest of ownership without losing sight of its economic purpose or operational limits.
