The early period after funding or an ownership change should establish a **funded operating plan**: what the business will do, who is accountable, what cash and capacity are approved, when each decision falls due and what has been deferred. A hundred days is a planning convention, not evidence that every business changes at the same speed. The transaction introduces new assumptions, review dates and expectations formed during diligence. Confirm which of them change the company’s existing plan.

The chapter follows one fictional scenario: a growth investment in Larkspur whose thesis assumes onboarding volume can double without proportional implementation staff. The board authorized one envelope of up to €500,000 additional cash and 24 engineer-weeks; Ines, the CEO, spends inside the plan, and any reserve draw returns to the board.

Start by confirming the inherited findings with the people who will do the work. Finding **D-3** arrives from diligence with its identifier: onboarding depends on one specialist’s manual configuration, about 80 hours per implementation in a five-of-twelve sample. Management confirms the dependency, rejects the sampled figure as a baseline and measures one from all twelve implementations of the previous two quarters by day 20: 80 hours again, now with population, period and source stated, and configuration under half of the hours. Alex’s explanation, that roughly 40% of the effort is poor customer data, stays recorded as the disagreement the pilot must resolve.

**The plan is worked through.** ONB-1, the onboarding pilot from D-3: Priya accountable, €180,000 committed, 12 engineer-weeks, cohort review at day 90. KNW-1, a second engineer able to release and recover the scheduling engine, from D-5: Alex, no cash, 4 weeks of the specialist’s protected time outside the 24, demonstration at day 60. REC-1, a restore test against the agreed recovery objective, from D-6: Alex with the operations lead, €80,000, 4 weeks, test at day 45. Portal research: Priya, €20,000, 2 weeks. Total €280,000 and 18 engineer-weeks, leaving a reserve of €220,000 and 6 weeks. Commitments are not spending: about €90,000 of ONB-1 is incurred within the period, the rest over two quarters, and €30,000 a year of maintenance sits in the operating budget from the next financial year. Deferred with a decision recorded: the portal build, second-country expansion and two implementation hires.

**Sequence dependencies and accept imperfect baselines.** The restore environment must exist before day 45; the baseline before the cohort; the scheduling specialist’s time cannot be spent twice. Measure internal effort and customer waiting time separately. Outside support for ONB-1 sits inside the plan’s capacity and cash through the engagement charter in [[useful-engagement]].

**Review the plan, not its completion rate.** REC-1 failed at day 45 on a departed engineer’s credential and an unavailable database version; the €80,000 was spent. The board approved €20,000 and 2 weeks from the reserve for the correction, and the retest passed at day 85. KNW-1 passed at day 60. ONB-1’s cohort of eight cut effort from 80 to 62 hours, not the 50 assumed; about 40% of the remaining hours traced to data quality; waiting time was unchanged. On the agreed rule, 62 is the band that funds the next learning step: the board approved a €40,000, 4-week data-quality step from the reserve, using the last engineer-weeks (committed €340,000 and 24 weeks; €160,000 of cash left), set the expansion decision for the next quarterly review around day 190, with expansion no earlier than month 13, and kept hiring deferred. That revised commitment, not a completed task list, is the payoff.
