Hotel group **Hilton** and internet-calling product **Skype** illustrate successful investor outcomes with different ownership and technology contexts. They also show why an attractive sale, or **exit**, is insufficient evidence that a particular intervention caused the return. The two sales need different explanations: Hilton a financing explanation, Skype an explanation of technology rights and of what one particular buyer valued. Exits matter to product and engineering leaders because much of what investors ask for during ownership is justified by the sale they expect to make.

Hilton's ownership ran about eleven years, from the 2007 leveraged purchase through the financial crisis, a 2010 restructuring that the company's 2013 registration statement reports as a $4.0 billion debt reduction, a 2013 public listing and a final share sale in 2018. The same filing describes a reservation system, a guest-profile system and the proprietary OnQ property-management system as interlinked core elements. [S23: Hilton registration filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1585689/000119312513364703/d593452ds1.htm) Earlier annual filings date OnQ: introduced in 2003 and installed at virtually all hotels by end-2005, before the buyout. [S64: Hilton 2003 annual filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/47580/000104746904007535/a2128996z10-k.htm) [S65: Hilton 2005 annual filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/47580/000110465906016388/a06-2283_110k.htm) **The owner inherited the platform; what its later work added to the return is not measured.**

Blackstone later reported a 3.1× investment multiple and $14 billion of profit on Hilton. [S24: Blackstone 2018 investor call](https://ir.blackstone.com/files/doc_events/BLACKSTONE-Second-Quarter-2018-Earnings-Investor-Call.pdf) These are results reported by the **sponsor**, the investment firm, not a demonstrated net LP (limited partner, an investor in a fund) outcome or an identified technology effect. Recovery was also uneven: the owned and leased portfolio's adjusted EBITDA was still below 2008 levels at the point the filing discusses.

Skype's sequence was about two years. eBay completed the sale of a controlling stake in 2009 while retaining a minority interest and receiving a note from the buyer. The first dated technology change is the November 2009 settlement that brought rights to the core **peer-to-peer** technology into the company; the product had depended on rights held by another party and in litigation. The filing also dates the January 2011 Qik acquisition and group video calling launch without isolating what either added to the price. [S26: eBay Skype sale announcement](https://investors.ebayinc.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2009/EBay-Inc-Completes-Sale-of-Skype/default.aspx) [S27: Skype registration filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1498209/000119312511096544/ds1a.htm) Microsoft announced an $8.5 billion acquisition in 2011 and completed it that October. [S28: Microsoft Skype completion](https://news.microsoft.com/source/2011/10/13/microsoft-officially-welcomes-skype/)

**Transaction valuations are not enough to calculate a fund return.** Dividing $8.5 billion by $2.75 billion ignores ownership percentages, debt, additional funding, transaction costs, distributions and timing. Microsoft's strategic willingness to pay is also an alternative explanation for the sale price, not evidence that every operating change produced an equivalent increase in standalone value.

Skype's retirement in May 2025 belongs to the **later product history**. [S29: Microsoft Skype retirement](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/skype/22ccebb6-0cf9-4e1d-916f-aaf978e1b129) It does not erase the 2011 investor outcome or establish that earlier decisions caused the eventual retirement.

The shared evidence limit is that neither case supplies complete fund cash flows, an independent allocation of the gain, or employee and customer outcomes. The usable result is two operating questions. **Hilton asks whether the company has financing and time for the operating plan. Skype asks whether it controls the rights the product depends on and what a particular buyer values.** Neither answer can be read from the exit price alone.
