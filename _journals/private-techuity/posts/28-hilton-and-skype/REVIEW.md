# Editorial review: Hilton and Skype: A Successful Exit Still Needs Explaining

Reassessed 15 September 2026 from the complete current article, summary and comic text where present, the previous review where present, revision log and Git changes. See the [collection review](../REVIEW.md).

## Current assessment

The early comparison and two operating questions make the paired case easier to follow. Hilton now clearly raises financing and time; Skype raises rights and buyer-specific value. The revised eBay charge annotation is accurate, and the final figure no longer interrupts the handoff. Preserve the refusal to calculate a fund return by dividing transaction headlines, and the careful separation of Skype's later retirement from the earlier investment outcome.

The technology chronology is only partially repaired. The new tables appropriately resist attribution, but one labels dated evidence as undated and another leaves an inherited capability unknown despite an accessible earlier primary filing. The answer should be more affirmative about what is known while remaining cautious about causation.

## Active recommendations

1. **High impact — correct the new Skype “not dated” claim.** The cited registration filing explicitly dates the Qik acquisition to January 2011, group video calling's premium launch to January 2011, and a Citrix partnership announcement to March 2011. Those are documented events within the ownership window. Replace “Described but not dated in the consulted material” with the dates actually supplied. Dating an acquisition or launch does not isolate its contribution to the sale price. [Skype registration filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1498209/000119312511096544/ds1a.htm)

2. **Medium impact — establish OnQ's inherited baseline from an earlier filing.** Hilton's 2003 annual filing says it introduced OnQ in 2003; its 2005 filing says it was installed at virtually all hotels in its system by year-end 2005. Both predate the 2007 Blackstone transaction. This resolves the narrow inherited-capability question, while subsequent improvements and contribution to returns remain unmeasured. Suggested table entry: “Inherited: OnQ introduced in 2003, before Blackstone's acquisition; the cited 2013 description does not isolate later system improvements or their financial effect.” [Hilton 2003 filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/47580/000104746904007535/a2128996z10-k.htm), [Hilton 2005 filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/47580/000110465906016388/a06-2283_110k.htm)

3. **Medium impact — separate missing causal attribution from missing chronology throughout.** The opening comparison, summary and comic panel 2 currently emphasize that the filing cannot say what was inherited. Update them to carry the established baseline and preserve only the actual unknown: how much subsequent work contributed to the return. The previous review asked for dates, not proof that ownership caused every change. More disclaimers are not a substitute for using available evidence.

4. **Low impact — consolidate the remaining closing qualifications.** “Two Operating Questions” still restates several earlier cautions before the common limitation paragraph and bold final answer. Keep the most useful case-specific questions and one common limit. The two-company structure is worth preserving; a split would duplicate the return caveats and weaken the contrast.

5. **Low impact — keep the new investor callout proportionate.** Investors can use exits as evidence of a strategy's performance, but “proof of their model” is an unnecessarily sweeping account of motives. The body already has the stronger, fairer formulation: examine the sponsor's reported result and explanation.

## Verification and limits

The [eBay 2007 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1065088/000089161807000579/f34172exv99w1.htm) supports a €375 million earn-out payment plus €630 million additional impairment, totaling €1.005 billion. The previous review was right to distinguish components and wrong to imply any currency correction was established; the present annotation is resolved.

The newly checked filings establish technology dates, not a counterfactual or a return decomposition. Complete fund cash flows and independent stakeholder comparisons remain unavailable in the material reviewed. The [Blackstone-hosted PEI retrospective](https://www.blackstone.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2019/03/pei_blackstone_hilton.pdf) was retrieved: it supports its short sponsor-hosted ownership narrative, not an independent return reconstruction. Textual comic changes do not establish that the baked-in dialogue matches the revision.


Comic panel 6 has revised dialogue but an unchanged image and no regeneration flag. Its visible speech needs **verification** against the new text; the metadata alone cannot establish agreement.
