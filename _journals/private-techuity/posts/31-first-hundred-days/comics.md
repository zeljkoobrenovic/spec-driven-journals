**Comic.** Larkspur, a fictional company, sells scheduling software. Every new customer needs **onboarding**: Larkspur’s staff set the software up with that customer’s settings and data. An investor has just funded Larkspur’s growth. Its **investment thesis** is its reason for expecting the investment to succeed. At first the thesis was that Larkspur could onboard twice as many customers without more onboarding staff. Before investing, the investor ran **due diligence**, an investigation of the company, and cut that expectation to 50% more customers in the first year. Eight pages follow the first hundred days, in which one finding from that investigation becomes a funded plan, a restore test fails and is corrected in time, the pilot reports, and the day-100 review changes the plan by a rule agreed in advance. Each page is three strips; the amounts, dates and thresholds are drawn into the artwork, and a transcript follows every page. Every figure is fictional.

Ines is the chief executive; Priya leads product; Alex leads technology; Sam leads finance; Morgan is the investor’s technology adviser. All are fictional. The **board**, the directors who approve major spending, decides the plan. An **engineer-week** is one engineer’s working time for one week. Day numbers count from day 0, the board meeting that adopts the plan.
