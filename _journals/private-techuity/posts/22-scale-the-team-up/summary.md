**Headcount is not capacity.** Before adding people, follow one piece of work through the company and find out what it is actually waiting for. New hires who join the same queue only make it longer.

![One pricing change's sixteen-day path runs through a three-day rule wait, a seven-day specialist queue, two days of building, a three-day sign-off wait and one day of release; the two decision waits are delegated, forecast to cut the path to ten days, while the specialist queue is a knowledge gap closed by one billing engineer, and a team of five is deferred.](assets/images/22-scale-the-team-up/summary-at-a-glance.jpeg)
**Figure 1:** *Trace the work, remove the decision waits first, then hire for the gap that remains.*

Investors often push for new money to turn quickly into people, because headcount is easy to see and report. Rotaline’s board, in this fictional example, wants a second product team of five. But when Alex, who leads technology, and Priya, who leads product, trace one pricing change from request to the live system, it takes sixteen working days. **Only two of them are building.** One is release. The other thirteen are waiting: three for Priya to confirm the pricing rule, seven in a queue for the only two specialists who can safely change the invoicing module, and three for Ines, the chief executive, to sign off.

That trace separates two kinds of problem. Some waits exist because one person holds the authority; others exist because only two people hold the knowledge. **Change the decisions first.** Priya agrees a catalogue of pricing rules the team may implement without asking her, and Ines hands invoice sign-off to the finance team. That is forecast to take about six days out, bringing the change down to ten, a forecast Alex will check on the next three changes. It needs no hire, though it isn’t free: finance and Ines now spend time on the review.

The seven-day queue doesn’t move, because delegation can’t fix a knowledge gap. So the gap the plan really has is a third person able to change the module, not five new engineers who would take months to learn it and add more changes to review. Rotaline fills one approved position with a billing engineer, about €90,000 a year, and protects a day a week of each specialist’s time for teaching. The five-person team waits until second-country demand is shown, the next funding round is committed and the board has approved the roles. **Transfer is proven by performance**: if the engineer can’t release a rule change without the specialists after six months, the transfer has failed.

The same discipline applies to leaders. Before replacing one, state what new leadership must enable and what evidence shows it is missing. When the investor’s director later proposes a chief product officer above Priya, Ines tests the idea the same way, finds no evidence yet, and adds a senior product manager and coaching instead, with a review in six months.

Cheaper locations deserve the same scrutiny. Replacing €1 million of supplier spending with a €650,000 team sounds like a big saving, but coordination and a €250,000 transition make the first year cost more than before.
