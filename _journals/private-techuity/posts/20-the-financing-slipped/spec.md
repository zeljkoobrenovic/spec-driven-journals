# Spec: Planning on Money That Hasn't Arrived

## Intent

Show a company leader how to respond when an expected round, refinancing or parent budget arrives late while delivery is on track: put cash and commitments on a calendar, find the latest useful decision date, price the alternatives, obtain one authorized fallback, tell customers and teams which plan is running, and restart deferred spending only under an explicit rule that separates counting promised money in the forecast from spending against it. Handover through ownership changes is a separate chapter, [[handover-of-obligations]]; the deeper workforce reduction that the 1 October review has to decide is a separate chapter, [[anatomy-of-a-layoff]], which continues this scenario’s figures.

## Audience

Product and engineering leaders inside companies working under investors, including leaders who inherit an ownership arrangement. Assume no specialist finance background; explain necessary terms before use. Investor-side readers are secondary.

## Success criteria

- Open with the actual scenario (September round now likely in December) rather than a general account of ownership, and avoid contrasts with “companies without investors”; name the specific condition the arrangement changes. Keep the opening callouts and key points short (under 250 words together): the delayed investment and the three practical actions (name the money’s stage, work the decision by date, authorize one fallback). Committee mechanics, the bridge and the detailed restart rule belong in their own sections, not the opening.
- Work one fictional Larkspur scenario with this chapter’s own figures (€1.6m on 1 July, €200,000 monthly burn, €400,000 reserve reached 31 December, hires at €30,000 a month, €120,000 portal contract), stated in the text as deliberately separate from the shared Larkspur ledger (no ONB-1 identifier or shared ledger figures), through to a dated board decision: chosen option, alternatives rejected, funding, scarce capacity, who is authorized, review date and the evidence that would change it.
- State one restart rule for deferred spending that separates two permissions: **counting** a signed-but-conditional commitment in the dated cash forecast (only when the board specifically authorizes it) and **spending** against money not yet received (only if a forecast that counts cash already received, and nothing promised, still holds the reserve **until 30 June** with the new obligation included). Define that forecast in every format: it starts from cash in the bank, leaves out investment and loan money not yet received, and keeps the plan’s stated assumptions about future customer payments and operating costs (the net monthly burn). The hires and the portal are the deferred spending the test governs; whether the bridge may be counted is a separate permission, because it is financing being pursued, not spending. Give the horizon’s reason (two further slips of the three-month size already seen: December to March, March to June) so a reader can apply the test without inventing a period. Show that at Larkspur’s figures the test fails before anything is added (B with R1 and no new money reaches the reserve about 20 January; with the bridge’s cash received, about 20 May, still short of 30 June), and that new spending only makes it worse. Name the financing assumption of every such calculation: without the bridge, a portal paid in mid-December leaves €380,000 on 31 December (below the reserve before year-end) and the hires from 1 November leave €440,000 (reserve about 7 January); with the bridge’s cash received, the same portal payment leaves €980,000 (reserve about late April, still short of 30 June). So offers and the portal signature wait for the round’s cash, confirmed by Sam. Give the same action for each state (cash received; signed with conditions outstanding; money fails to arrive) in the article, summary and comic, and in the short formats state the spending test in full: cash already received, with the new hires or contract payments included, keeps the company at or above the €400,000 reserve until 30 June.
- Make the failed-money response honest and dated. The board decides on 10 August that R1’s notice is served on 1 October unless cash already received by then passes the same test without it; uncertain money (anything short of cash received) does not stop the notice, and serving it on time is what secures November’s saving. Trace three cases, each with notices already served, approval authority, cash position and the next decision before the reserve is breached: (1) nothing signed on 1 October; (2) a signed conditional agreement (the bridge) failing after 1 October; (3) the bridge’s cash received, then the round failing in December (€600,000 plus interest owed at maturity). Wherever a format mentions case 3, it ends in a December board decision on the spending level Larkspur can continue at and on repaying or renegotiating the loan, not in a spending cut already made. The deeper reduction needs its own board approval and pays its transition costs before it saves, so it is not a fallback for missing money. Say that it cannot replace the bridge only as a result of Larkspur’s later-costed terms (see [[anatomy-of-a-layoff]]), not as a general rule about reductions, in the summary as well as the article; what Larkspur does if neither the bridge nor the round arrives is stated as unresolved in this chapter. Never write “if closing fails, R1 and the deeper cut run”.
- State the bridge’s payer, amount, arrival date and loan terms behind the March date: a convertible loan, no arrangement fee, interest added to the loan and converted with it, no principal, interest or other charge payable in cash before maturity twelve months after receipt (after every date in the chapter, including the R1 extension to about 20 May), and what is owed if the round never closes. Cost at least one post-December reduction with its latest useful date (R1: about 20 January without the bridge, about 20 May with it) and hand the deeper continuation level to [[anatomy-of-a-layoff]]; do not describe R1 as a complete fallback.
- Derive the 15 August latest useful decision date from stated fictional events: offers and the portal contract are both due on 16 August; the portal’s €120,000 is committed at signature and paid on 15 September. Do not present sending an offer as a universal legal point of no return.
- Explain every finance term and role abbreviation at first meaningful use in each format, including the standalone summary and comic (round, shares as ownership stakes, bridge, interest, closing, burn, reserve, expression of interest, investment committee, convertible loan, term sheet, subscription agreement, collected revenue, Reduction 1 (R1), CFO/CTO/CEO), and say briefly what Larkspur sells and what the pilot, portal and recovery work are, in the comic as well as the article. The excerpt uses plain words (no unexplained “round”, “refinancing” or “parent budget”). Reading-list annotations define fund, buyout fund (including a controlling stake: enough ownership and votes to decide major company matters), portfolio company and preferred stock (with one concrete priority, such as being paid from a sale before ordinary shareholders). The article explains the dispatch service (the software that sends field staff to their jobs) and avoids the unexplained “roadmap”; the summary states the recovery requirement concretely (restoring, within four hours of a failure, the software that assigns field staff to jobs) rather than as “four-hour service recovery”. Short formats say a term sheet “does not count as incoming cash in Larkspur’s forecast”, never that it counts for nothing. Every document drawn in a comic panel is one the story has established (panel 6: the operating record and a customer contract).
- Make the latest useful decision date (15 August) central, and distinguish the stages of a commitment: expression of interest, approval pending, signed commitment with conditions, cash received.
- Retain only the escalation step this decision needs, with a link to the governance chapter; keep the distinction between a failed hypothesis, poor execution and an unfunded dependency.
- Include two short variations (a refinancing that leaves less cash; a corporate parent withdrawing budget) without developing a second full case.
- Keep Figure 1 (ownership paths from a review junction) with a caption that matches the revised text; keep the article header logo and navigation icon.
- Keep the TL;DR (300–500 words) and the comic page scripts consistent with the article; inspect every generated page against its script and regenerate pages whose artwork cannot carry the financing story rather than re-captioning them misleadingly. The comic must show an on-schedule delivery plan and a funding date moving from September to December; compare reserve dates with C marked as B plus a conditional €600,000 receipt, not a lower burn; and carry the decision dates (10 August, 15 August, 1 September, 1 October) as lettered timelines, not decorative calendars.
- Keep historical and research references within their source scope, with current publication status and subgroup qualifications; say that a term sheet may state an expected closing date but does not guarantee closing or cash; label fictional figures as fictional.
- Close with the next-chapter handoff to the handover chapter, and trim Questions to Consider to three.

## Non-goals

The deeper reduction decision and its employee consequences (owned by [[anatomy-of-a-layoff]]); handover, exit readiness, exit-period distortions and transaction forms (moved to [[handover-of-obligations]]); regular investor cadence and peer networks (governance and Part IV chapters); universal prescriptions, invented evidence, promises of investment performance, or disclosure of confidential inputs. The chapter is not a legal or tax opinion.

## Modalities

- Full Article: index.md, the substantive argument.

- Comic: comics.md, eight illustrated pages of three strips each — each image one page with the dialogue, labels, amounts and dates lettered in the artwork — with alt text, visible captions and per-strip transcripts rendered from the page blocks that are also the artwork scripts.
