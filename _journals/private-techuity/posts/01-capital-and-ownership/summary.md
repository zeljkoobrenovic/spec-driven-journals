**An investment announcement does not tell you the company’s spending budget.** Money may go to selling shareholders, existing lenders or transaction costs. Identify the recipient before assuming a product or hiring plan is funded.

Compare the **actual arrangement** first. A fictional minority round can put €8m into Larkspur while leaving the founder in control. A fund’s €40m purchase of existing shares pays the seller. A corporate acquisition needs a separate agreement about group funding and local authority. These announcements support different operating promises. The detailed fund map below applies where that structure exists; not every investor uses a fund or a holding company.

A fund-backed buyout map separates **four types of organization**. The **investment firm**, also called the manager or sponsor, employs investment professionals. A **fund** pools money from investors under agreed terms. A **holding vehicle** is a company used to hold an investment and possibly borrowing. A **portfolio company** is the operating business owned through that investment. Real arrangements may contain additional layers.

Each can have its own obligations. A firm raising a new fund does not automatically make that money available to a company held by an older one. Borrowing can sit in several places, while its payments may depend on cash from the operating business. Ask which entity holds the cash and which must make the payment.

A **limited partner (LP)** is an investor in the fund. In a common arrangement, it makes a **commitment**, a promise to supply money under agreed conditions. A **capital call** requests a payment against that promise. The **general partner (GP)** has management responsibilities and powers under the fund arrangements. An increase in an investment’s estimated value is a separate event: it does not itself pay cash to the LP.

The manager may receive a **management fee** for its work and **carried interest**, a contractual share of investment profits. A **distribution waterfall** states how payments are allocated. In the chapter’s simplified example, €160 comes back from €100 invested. The LPs receive their €100 first; a 20% share of the €60 profit gives the GP €12 and leaves another €48 for the LPs. The LPs receive €148 in total. Actual agreements can use different conditions and payment sequences. [S01: SEC private equity guide](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/investment-products/private-investment-funds/private-equity)

Owning and running a company involve different decisions. The fund’s investment committee approves investments within its authority; the company’s board and executives have their own responsibilities. Ownership alone does not give every shareholder authority to direct staff or sign contracts.
