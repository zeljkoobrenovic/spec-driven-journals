**Choose investor support from the work and the capability missing from your company.** An operating model arranges people, responsibilities, decisions and information; a blueprint makes the investor's contribution and the company's commitments specific enough to test.

![Four equal panels connect a decision route to board oversight, experienced judgment to an adviser, specialist expertise to shared experts, and execution capacity to a dedicated company assignment.](assets/images/operating-model-blueprints/four-needs-four-contributions.png)

Start with the business priority: growth, profitability, acquisitions or scaling beyond founder dependence. Translate the investment thesis—the explanation of why the investment should succeed—into company work. The same priority can require different support, depending on what prevents progress.

The chapter proposes **four blueprints that can be combined**. **Management delivery with board oversight** fits a capable team with a funded plan and clear approval routes. **An operating adviser** brings experience and challenge when management can implement the decision. **Shared specialists** supply scarce expertise through named assignments, dates, costs and company counterparts. **Dedicated execution inside the company** adds sustained leadership or coordination, with an explicit reporting line, delegated decisions and a handover condition. These are adaptable arrangements, with no universal ranking.

Choose **company organization separately**. Local delivery, common standards and shared services can coexist. Sharing expertise across an investor's portfolio—the businesses it invests in—differs from integrating functions inside a corporate group. Establish the benefit, service owner, funding and information access for each shared activity. Common ownership alone does not establish permission to pool customer data.

Write one agreement covering purpose, accountable company leaders, decision rights, people, funding, displaced work, evidence and review. Advice, assessment, approval and executive direction need distinct remits. A specialist's availability becomes useful only when company employees have time to work with them. Include implementation and continuing service costs alongside any adviser fees.

In the **independent fictional example**, three software businesses need coordinated access and support for two products. They retain local roadmaps and test shared identity under common security and reporting requirements. A specialist commits six weeks; a program leader commits twelve. Two company engineers each reserve one day weekly during the specialist assignment, with other work deferred and funding confirmed.

At six weeks, the pilot supports the access requirement, but wider rollout threatens a customer release. The group chief executive approves a smaller step within the agreed authority and reports the revised forecast to the board. At twelve weeks, management reviews whether to end, narrow or make the assignment permanent. **Technical progress informs the next commitment; it does not establish revenue or investment returns.**
