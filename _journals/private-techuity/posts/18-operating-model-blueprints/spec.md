# Spec: Working With Investors: Operating Model Blueprints

## Intent

Open Part IV with a chapter based on `_drafts/operating-models/INPUT-1.md` and `INPUT-2.md`. Help a product or technology leader choose and agree a workable relationship between investor involvement and company delivery. Combine business priorities and forms of investor involvement in a practical selection guide. Make blueprints usable through their decisions, people, funding, review practices and conditions for change.

## Audience

Product and engineering leaders inside investor-backed companies; CEOs, finance leaders and operating partners are secondary readers. Explain operating model, investment thesis, value creation plan, shared service and federated arrangements in plain language. Use private-equity evidence with brief, qualified venture and growth comparisons.

## Success criteria

- Distinguish the investor’s own internal operations, its relationship with portfolio companies, and the company’s internal organization. Concentrate on the latter two.
- Present four proposed blueprints: management delivery with board oversight, an operating adviser, shared specialists, and dedicated execution inside the company. They are combinations to adapt, with no universal ranking or maturity ladder.
- Provide a selection guide covering growth, profitability, acquisitions and scaling. For each priority, connect the missing capability to possible investor contributions and the company's internal design; explain why the same priority can need different combinations.
- For each blueprint, explain suitable conditions, decision rights, delivery capacity, funding, review evidence, failure signs and a condition for changing or ending the arrangement. Include concrete product or technology consequences.
- Treat local autonomy, common standards, shared services and integration as a separate design choice. Distinguish sharing expertise across a fund’s portfolio from integrating functions inside an acquired corporate group.
- Tie formal authority to the actual appointment, delegation and governing arrangements. Separate advice, assessment, approval and executive direction; make company and investor commitments visible.
- Include a reusable blueprint record, a suggested review rhythm and one explicitly fictional acquisition example independent of Larkspur’s existing scenarios. State what is funded, what company capacity is used and what changes at review.
- Explain where AI affects capability, data access, implementation and accountability without making a common tool or specialist team a universal prescription. Link the technology operating partner chapter and existing AI, hiring, governance and acquisition chapters.
- Review the supplied links directly, identify the sections actually consulted, and separate source observations from the author’s blueprint designs. Date the 2018 survey and current firm examples; do not infer causal performance, universal board behavior, promised savings or current market prevalence from them.
- Preserve the book’s learning objective, three KEY POINTS, questions and further reading. Aim for 3,000–4,000 words of article prose, readable captions, and no more than two substantial tables.
- Add a chapter logo, navigation icon and seven explanatory figures in the book’s ivory, navy, muted teal and ochre style. Retain the connected-model, support-choice and pilot-review figures. Add one distinct visual within each of the four blueprint sections: board oversight with company-led delivery; an adviser supporting a company decision; a booked specialist assignment with a company owner; and a dedicated company assignment with agreed reporting and handover. Depict advice and shared expertise without implying additional executive authority. Use accurate labels, descriptive alt text and numbered captions; preserve generation prompts and provenance.
- Place the article immediately after the Part IV introduction, before `help-that-changes-capability`. End with a transition to sourcing useful help. Retain the permalink and artwork, number its source folder `18-operating-model-blueprints`, and align the reading guide, contents and manuscript with 33 main chapters.

## Non-goals

A fund-administration manual, company-wide reorganization instructions, a jurisdiction-specific governance document, an investment-return model or a replacement for the existing engagement charter. Do not assume that all portfolio companies form a corporate group or can share customer data. Do not repeat the main chapters’ financial calculations or restore the discontinued Productscapes appendix.

## Modalities

Illustrated draft article and companion specification, with a chapter logo, navigation icon and seven explanatory figures. Retain the illustrated Article modality without a separate summary or comic.

None for this draft. The author confirmed connecting the investor–company relationship and the company’s internal operating model from a product and technology leader’s perspective; combining business priorities and investor involvement in a practical selection guide; and using private equity as the main evidence base with brief venture and growth comparisons.
