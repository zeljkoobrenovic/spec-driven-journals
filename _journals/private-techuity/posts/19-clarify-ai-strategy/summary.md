**A request for an “AI strategy” hides three different investment questions.** Will customers pay for an AI product? Will internal work improve enough to change spending? Could a substitute replace what customers already buy? Each needs its own evidence, cost and decision date.

**Figure 1:** *One request, three questions, each with its own test, and the rule that decides what counts as a saving.*

Take Larkspur, a fictional company selling maintenance-scheduling software. A growth investor asks for an AI strategy, and each leader hears something different: Priya, who leads product, hears a new feature; Sam, in finance, expects lower costs; Ines, the chief executive, worries about a competitor. So Ines makes Priya accountable for holding the three answers together, and the departments with the most routine work join first.

**Will customers pay?** Customers spend hours sorting incoming inspection documents, so Priya proposes an AI step that does the sorting. Three customers agree to pay €300 a month for a ten-week trial costing €45,000 and six engineer-weeks. The test is the whole job, not the model: review time, corrections and trust. On 11 December the tool must sort 95% of ordinary documents correctly, miss no safety-relevant document, cut review below 1.5 hours a week, and keep at least two customers paying. Passing buys **a funding request, not a launch date**.

**Will internal work improve?** The evidence is mixed. One 2022 experiment found a small coding task done 55.8% faster; a 2025 study of experienced developers on their own projects found tasks took 19% longer with AI. So Larkspur tests locally: three support agents use an assistant while three work as before, and quality must hold whatever the speed. Even then, **saved time is capacity, not cash**. It becomes cash only if the planned seventh hire moves from April to October 2027, which after €8,000 of licenses and upkeep is about €24,000 in 2027. If demand forces an earlier start or quality fails, the gain shrinks or disappears.

**Could a substitute replace us?** In Larkspur’s test, a general assistant fed a hand-uploaded spreadsheet drafts a plausible schedule but cannot send jobs to technicians, re-plan or keep the inspection records regulators require. A €15,000 test with five customers decides, on 15 January 2027, whether to defend, reprice, partner, or stop a planned €120,000 feature.

The three tests cost €66,000, and all rest on one rule: **a saving is real only when an actual payment changes**, on a date, by someone authorized to change it. Whether the team can deliver any of this is the question [[assess-capability]] takes up next.
