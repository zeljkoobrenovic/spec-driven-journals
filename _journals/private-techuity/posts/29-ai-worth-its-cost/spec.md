# Spec: Critically Evaluate AI Costs: Measure the Return per Task and per Period

## Intent

Show a leader under investors how to read the running cost and the return of AI the company already runs, in the product it sells and in the tools its staff use, the way [[cheaper-cloud-bill]] reads a cloud bill: choose a unit of useful customer work, put every figure on one comparable basis, separate why the cost changed (usage, rates, the way the feature is built), decide how much to commit under a stated demand range, and record the exposure that remains. The reader leaves able to compute the return on an AI feature or tool per period from figures they actually have, to say what would make it stop paying, and to answer an investor who asks for "the AI ROI" with a number that has a unit, a period and a condition attached.

The chapter is the third chapter of Part VI, after cloud costs and resilience. It continues [[ai-strategy-three-questions]], whose product trial and support pilot it takes as having passed, and it complements [[scale-the-team-with-ai]], which measures capacity; this chapter measures money.

## Context

Larkspur, the book's fictional scheduling-software company, is in 2027. The document-sorting feature that three customers trialled at €300 a month each (running cost about €60 a month per customer at trial volume) passed its release gate on 11 December 2026 and is in general release to paying customers. The support assistant from the same chapter is rolled out to six agents (licences about €3,600 a year, evaluation upkeep about €4,400 a year). The coding and setup pilots of [[scale-the-team-with-ai]] have ended and their gate results are stated as assumptions, not re-argued. The people are the same (Alex technology, Priya product, Ines the company, Sam finance, Morgan the investor's adviser). Every new figure is fictional; the shared Larkspur ledger is not touched.

## Audience

Product and engineering leaders inside companies working under investors, including leaders who inherit an ownership arrangement. Assume no specialist finance background; explain necessary terms before use. Investor-side readers are secondary.

## Success criteria

- **A unit that explains the business.** For the product feature, cost and return are expressed per useful customer task (a document sorted and accepted without correction), never per token or per model call alone; for the internal tools, per ticket resolved and per change completed, inheriting the definitions of [[scale-the-team-with-ai]]. Explain token, model call, inference and context at first use, and show why the same feature's model cost per task rises as prompts, retrieved context and retries grow, even when the price per token falls. Construction effects (the 80% rise, the 35% proposed saving) are stated as changes in model cost per document at unchanged vendor prices, so that model mix (retries to a costlier model, easy documents routed to a cheaper one) and cached context billed at a reduced rate are inside the measure; a raw token count is never equated with spending.
- **One comparable basis.** A table puts the feature's monthly figures on one basis: price paid by the customer, model and hosting charges, evaluation and monitoring upkeep, the share of engineering time that keeps it working, and support load, for at least two dated months, so that a margin per customer and per task can be read off and compared.
- **Separate why the cost changed.** Decompose a cost change into usage (more customers, more documents per customer), rates (vendor price per token, up or down) and construction (longer prompts, more context, retries, a larger model chosen for quality), as the cloud chapter separates usage, rates and architecture. Each cause has a different owner and a different remedy, stated.
- **Return with a unit, a period and a condition.** Define return on investment (ROI) in ordinary words with a plain example at its first meaningful use, compute it for the feature and for one internal tool for a stated period, separate customer value (the estimated value of time freed, not money, derived from the trial's review-effort limit rather than presented as a measured result) from revenue from contribution before existing payroll (supplier costs counted in the months they concern, with the money actually collected and paid reconciled against them so that no period cost is called cash), and state the condition under which it holds (quality above the release threshold on both samples, review effort below its per-customer limit). Every ROI figure in every format carries its period and its condition; none is presented as a permanent property of the feature, and a figure that includes an estimated month or a year not yet closed is labelled a projection wherever it is quoted (artwork included: Figure 2's period figures carry "projected" and the €540 its per-customer unit), with the assistant's €64,000 named as employment cost and its €8,000 as licences and upkeep. The assistant's 300% is labelled a return on those recurring payments, excluding the €2,000 of rollout and training time, with the result including that time (€22,000 on €10,000, 220%) beside it in every format; the seventh agent's licence is accounted for (the agent works unassisted during induction in 2027, as the pilot chapter's six-licence figure assumes; seven licences, €4,200, plus €4,400 of upkeep make the 2028 cost about €8,600, labelled as licences plus upkeep in the artwork and alt text, and the pilot chapter's "about €8,000 from 2028" explained as its six-licence figure). The feature's running return is a return on supplier running cost before staff costs, named as such in the definition, the artwork and the comic, with the result after allocated staff time (about €800 short over the six months) stated beside it. A compact before-and-after calculation shows what the proposed construction saving does to the monthly operating result and to the cost-per-customer rule, with the one-off engineering effort kept separate; €914.40 is the projected monthly surplus and €2,038.40 the improvement on August, and a better month reduces the unrecovered balances only gradually, never establishing payback by itself. The −€18,098 and −€79,398 are unrecovered balances, not spending totals. Period totals reconcile to monthly inputs, and any cumulative balance names every one-off and operating cost it includes, cash and allocated staff time separately, so that no cost is counted twice or dropped.
- **A commitment decision under a demand range.** The vendor offers a committed-spend discount for a year on the usage the fixed payment covers, with excess usage at list price. Derive the demand range explicitly from customers × documents per customer × post-change cost per document, show the cost at the low, expected and high ends with and without the commitment, state the crossover points between options, treat a committed minimum as an obligation like the cloud chapter's minimum spend, and check what an ownership change, a vendor price change (in the correct direction: a higher price means the fixed payment covers fewer tokens) or a model retirement does to it, with a timeline of notice, successor test access, decision and payments still owed, written as explicit branches for timely, late and absent successor access (for each: the notice action, the last fixed payment, the pricing and processing mode from the retirement date and the suspension path), with the access deadline (sixty days before the retirement date) named separately from the sixty-day testing window after access, missing usable access by that deadline stated as a negotiated termination trigger in its own right, and a successor that passes in time staying under the commitment, where ending the commitment is a right, not a delivery guarantee: sorting continues from the retirement date only on a model measured on the release-gate samples, with each customer's planner capacity confirmed for the review-aid mode, and is otherwise suspended with the service change agreed with the customer. The funded usage period (2028) is distinguished in every format from the payment tail that 2028 usage produces in early 2029, which is reserved, not a further year of usage; the payment dates follow from stated invoice terms (overage invoiced on the first day of the following month, due 45 days later, so November and December usage is paid by 15 January and 15 February 2029). Ordinary non-cancellation is stated separately from any negotiated termination right. The chronology is one line: the September review approves the option conditionally, the release-gate samples and October's measured bill supply the evidence, and the signature follows that evidence in a named month; no format shows a signature before the evidence exists. Record the decision with chosen option, alternatives rejected, funding, who is authorized, a dated review and the evidence that would reopen it.
- **The stop and reprice rules.** State, before the review, what would make the feature stop paying: a token cost per task above a named fraction of the price, quality below the threshold (ordinary and safety samples kept separate, as in the trial), review effort at or above its per-customer limit, a vendor price rise above a named level, or customers declining the price at renewal, and what the response to each is (reprice, change the construction, change model, review-aid mode, suspend, withdraw), including who performs any fallback, that continued processing depends on the customer confirming planner capacity for it (otherwise processing is suspended and the service change agreed with the customer), and that a review-effort failure is answered by remeasuring the time saved against that customer's manual baseline and agreeing revised scope or price or withdrawing the feature for that customer, never by a higher fee alone. Tie the customer promise to the trial's conditions, not to open-ended continuation. Any retention exception is a provisional continuation test with comparable groups, a window and a minimum sample, not proof of causation (the TL;DR and comic included), and its loss cap and expiry are unconditional.
- **Investor questions answered as they are asked.** Give the leader answers to "what is our AI ROI", "why is the AI bill growing faster than revenue" and "should we commit to a bigger contract" that each point at the unit, the decomposition and the commitment decision above rather than at a single number.
- **House rules.** Status highlight and key points readable without prior study and in ordinary words (essential terms such as token introduced where first used, including in the highlights); long explanations split so that each paragraph carries one point (calculation, projection and consequence for the assistant; eligibility, evidence, loss cap and expiry for the retention exception); quantitative figures drawn from numeric coordinates on one consistent scale, or labelled schematic; the opening leads with the concrete problem and the three questions before the history; terms explained in each format at first meaningful use (AI, ROI, tokens, margin, loaded cost, customer-months, overage, arrears, percentage points, source-title abbreviations), independently in the TL;DR and the comic, with plain substitutes preferred over jargon in the TL;DR (reused background text billed more cheaply rather than cached context; document samples used to check release quality rather than release-gate samples; a replacement model that fails those checks rather than a failed successor; a minimum payment owed even when usage falls rather than a floor); the comic's stated calculation order matches the order applied (usage, construction, rates); the final comic's return figures carry their periods (April to September 2027; 2027) in the artwork; each comic caption paragraph carries one point (contract rights, deadline, fallback; one paragraph per investor answer); figure labels readable at phone width without relying on colour; claims about billing models scoped to this feature, not to AI or licences in general; the company leader's decision, authority and funding assumptions explicit; historical findings within source scope, fictional scenarios labelled; a meaningful counterargument (a feature can be worth keeping at a loss for a period if it wins renewals, but only with a dated limit and the loss stated) with the evidence that would change the judgment; hand-off to Part VII; TL;DR and comic consistent with the article.

## Non-goals

- Not a repeat of the three AI investment questions, the trial design or the support pilot; those are taken as done and linked.
- Not a capacity or headcount argument; that is [[scale-the-team-with-ai]].
- Not a vendor or model comparison, a price list, or a forecast of token prices; figures illustrate the method and are labelled fictional. No prevalence claims about AI margins in general.
- Not a data-protection or security treatment beyond what the commitment decision needs; the NIST profile is linked, not taught.
