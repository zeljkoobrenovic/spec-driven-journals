# Editorial review: Success for Whom, and for How Long?

Reassessed 15 September 2026 from the complete current article, summary and comic text where present, the previous review where present, revision log and Git changes. See the [collection review](../REVIEW.md).

## Current assessment

The finale now concludes this collection rather than starting a separate research essay. The case matrix, supplier row and final Larkspur decision connect its evaluative standard to the preceding chapters. Research is shorter without dismissing patient harms, and deliberately funded continuing services are accepted as a legitimate capability. The closing sentence has a clear subject and purpose.

The final decision still needs substantial local repair. One alternative violates stated customer commitments, its savings may mix cash and released time, and the transition is called funded without its full cost or dated schedule. Those weaknesses matter because the finale presents this as the culmination of the book's method. Retain the new outline; improve the worked judgment.

## Active recommendations

1. **High impact — distinguish a genuine tradeoff from an option that fails the stated constraint.** Option A withdraws service before some contracts end, yet Sam and Morgan's position is described as one of two positions where “neither … is wrong.” Earlier chapters treat those obligations as protected. Acknowledge that A as written is inadmissible under the scenario's own rule, or make it a feasible alternative with customer consent, compensation and transition costs included. Then compare it with B on remaining effects. Suggested rewrite: “The board rejects the unmodified cutoff because it breaches the protected commitments. It compares a consent-based accelerated migration with the longer supported transition, including the cost and burden of each.” Evidence need not erase disagreement, but a financial preference does not erase an obligation.

2. **High impact — establish which part of the €220,000 is an actual saving.** The specialist's role and “part of a support engineer's time leave the plan.” Released employee time does not automatically reduce expenditure; this is the same distinction taught in the onboarding chapter. If the support engineer remains employed, separate capacity released from salary or supplier payments avoided. Only then can €220,000 − €70,000 = €150,000 be called a net recurring cash saving. State whether the continuing half-time role and the earlier €150,000 specialist-cost assumption refer to the same person and cost basis.

3. **Medium impact — fund and date the transition, not just the eventual service.** Option B starts after the data-quality step and lasts two quarters, but the disagreement describes only a one-quarter delay against a 90-day cutoff. Show the starting and ending dates, retained full-time cost, engineering capacity and any direct migration expenditure. An extra quarter of the stated €220,000 annual cost is €55,000 before other transition costs; the actual comparison depends on the timing chosen. “From the following year” and “saving brought forward” need that calendar. Name what gives way in next quarter's eight-week allocation. The €70,000 continuing service alone does not demonstrate that mitigation during migration is funded.

4. **Medium impact — temper the claim that investor results are best documented.** The matrix contains transaction valuations and sponsor-reported multiples, several explicitly incomplete investor cash-flow histories, and detailed company financial statements. It supports saying transaction headlines are prominent and consistent stakeholder outcome series scarce. It does not establish that realized investor returns are the best-documented or “narrowest” outcome across these five companies. Carry the corrected TeamSystem verification and Hilton technology baseline from their reviews into any later synthesis revision.

5. **Medium impact — bring the comic into the new finale.** Its panels still mainly cover research, handover and general questions; the thirty-customer decision and recorded conflict are absent. The previous structure survives in the shortest format. Use at least one panel for the competing feasible choices and one for the chosen support, cost and review. Keep the fictional document-reading frame for clinical evidence and avoid implying these settings predict software-company effects.

6. **Low impact — finish the terminology and pacing pass.** The first question asks about “five columns,” but the case matrix and six-perspective table give that phrase different possible meanings. Name the categories intended. The case matrix can also be shortened to the facts the final decision uses; reproducing so many earlier figures slows the opening. The new investor callout's “increasingly” and “easier to sell” need evidence or conditional wording. These are secondary to repairing the example.

## Verification and limits

The corrected research attributions are supported by primary sources. [Davis et al.'s April 2024 revision](https://www.nber.org/papers/w26371) supplies the −12%/+15% transaction-type comparison. The [BMJ review](https://www.bmj.com/content/382/bmj-2023-075244) searched through April 2023 and cannot synthesize the later papers. The [Cohn–Hotchkiss–Towery abstract](https://academic.oup.com/rof/article-abstract/26/2/257/6516584) supports the new “limited role” qualification.

The [2023 JAMA study](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2813379) supports 4.6 additional conditions per 10,000 eligible hospitalizations and 25.4% relative to the pre-acquisition rate; 4.6/18.1 ≈ 25.4%. The [2025 research letter](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama-health-forum/fullarticle/2837043) compares 18 hospitals with 18 and addresses financial measures, not patient outcomes. These checks verify scope and transcription, not the studies' causal identification through a fresh data analysis.
