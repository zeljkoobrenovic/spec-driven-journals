# Research brief: Product information, pricing, promotions and search

Prepared 7 November 2026 for chapter writers of *ORDERED: Architecting Commerce Platforms*. Feeds chapters 06 (product information), 07 (pricing architecture), 08 (promotions and loyalty) and 09 (search and discovery). The brief is platform-neutral: Shopify, commercetools, Adobe Commerce and SAP Commerce get the most space because their documentation is public and dated, not because the book endorses them.

## How to read this brief

Every substantive claim carries one tag:

- **[VERIFIED: url, published YYYY-MM(-DD)]**: the page was fetched in this session (7 November 2026) and supports the claim. Paraphrases stay close to the source; quoted text is quoted. The date is the source's publication date; undated product and documentation pages are marked "n.d., accessed 2026-11".
- **[WIDELY REPORTED: url, YYYY]**: from a reputable secondary source (law-firm alert, trade press, encyclopaedia, a search-engine snippet of a primary page that could not be fetched), not from a primary document fetched in this session.
- **[ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]**: industry knowledge or the analyst's inference. Every statement about how a proprietary engine works internally falls here unless the vendor has described it.

**Evidence age matters.** Commerce platforms re-model their catalogs every few years (Shopify's product model changed in 2024–25; commercetools shipped a second catalog model in 2026). The SAP Commerce evidence below is a 2018 Javadoc **(evidence age: 8 years)**; the Ravelin promotion-abuse article is from 2020 **(evidence age: 6 years)**. Sources older than about three years are flagged.

**Research limitation.** The session's shared web-search quota ran out two-thirds of the way through; the rest was done by fetching known URLs. Several primary sites blocked automated fetching: **gs1.org and gs1uk.org** (HTTP 403 / Incapsula), so GS1 definitions rest on a GS1 support article, the GS1 reference site for Digital Link, and encyclopaedia pages; **help.sap.com** conceptual pages (only the 2018 Javadoc and a knowledge-base preview were readable); **Amazon Seller Central** policy pages (login wall); the **IFRS Foundation** standard text (only the standard's landing page); **Bundeskartellamt** 2025–26 press releases (the 2013 releases and the case-overview page were readable). No official **SAP Commerce** or **Salesforce Commerce Cloud** pricing documentation was verified. No independent (non-vendor) measurement of promotion incrementality or coupon abuse was found; those sections rely on academic papers and vendor surveys and say so.

**Volatility flags.** ⚠ marks claims likely to change within 12 months: limits, product names, plan eligibility, beta status, pending litigation.

## PART 1: Product modeling

### 1.1 Product, variant, SKU, GTIN: four different things

| Term | What it identifies | Who assigns it | Stable? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Product | The sellable concept the shopper browses ("Kestrel shell jacket") | Merchant | Title and copy change freely |
| Variant | One purchasable combination of option values ("Red / Small") | Merchant, from options | Must stay stable once sold |
| SKU | Merchant's own stock-keeping code for a variant | Merchant / ERP / WMS | Must never change |
| GTIN | The global trade-item number under the barcode | Licensed from a GS1 member organisation | Global, never reused for a different item |

- Shopify's model is three-tiered: "The container for all product information (title, description, vendor)" is the product; options are customer choices such as colour or size; "Each variant represents a specific combination of option values, like 'Red / Small'" [VERIFIED: https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/build/graphql/migrate/new-product-model, n.d., accessed 2026-11]. The help centre says the same in merchant words: "each combination of option values for a product can be a variant for that product" [VERIFIED: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/variants, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- commercetools tells integrators to "Treat the sku values as immutable identifiers" and to plan SKU naming before creating products; a product has staged and current (published) data with Publish / Unpublish / Revert Staged Changes actions, and "You can only delete unpublished Products" [VERIFIED: https://docs.commercetools.com/api/projects/products, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- schema.org (version 30.1, 2026-09-16) models the same split: `Product` carries `sku` ("a merchant-specific identifier"), `gtin`, `gtin13`, `mpn`, `brand`, `offers`; a `ProductGroup` is a family that differs "only along defined dimensions, such as size or color", linked by `isVariantOf` / `hasVariant`, and "the variant inherits all product features from the base model, unless defined locally" [VERIFIED: https://schema.org/Product, published 2026-09-16].
- A GTIN is "unique, global and verifiable within the GS1 system"; "Only a GS1 Member Organisation can provide you with a GS1 GTIN" [VERIFIED: https://support.gs1.org/support/solutions/articles/43000734120-what-is-a-gs1-gtin-, last modified 2024-08-30]. The four formats are GTIN-8 (EAN-8 barcode, small items), GTIN-12 (UPC-A, North America), GTIN-13 (EAN-13, the European retail barcode) and GTIN-14 (ITF-14 / GS1-128, trade units for wholesale, not point of sale); each is a company prefix, item reference and modulo-10 check digit, and "each type of trade item gets its own GTIN", including each packaging configuration [WIDELY REPORTED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Trade_Item_Number, 2026].
- A GLN (Global Location Number) is a 13-digit GS1 key, compliant with ISO/IEC 6523, that identifies "a physical location such as a warehouse or a legal entity such as a company or customer"; in EDIFACT and X12 it sits in the party segments (NAD, N1) while GTINs sit in line items, and because a GLN and a GTIN-13 can share the same digit pattern, only the segment context distinguishes them [WIDELY REPORTED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Location_Number, 2026].
- GS1 has "120 local member organizations covering 150 countries" and "over 2 million user companies"; the first barcode was scanned in a shop on 26 June 1974; "The most influential GS1 standard is the Global Trade Item Number (GTIN)" [WIDELY REPORTED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GS1, 2026].
- GS1 Digital Link encodes GS1 keys in web URIs so a code on the pack can resolve to online information; the standard was "first ratified in August 2018" and has since been split, with the URI Syntax standard as the foundation; the legacy combined document is at version 1.1.4 (January 2026) and is now an archive [VERIFIED: https://ref.gs1.org/standards/digital-link/, published 2026-01]. The Sunrise 2027 programme, launched in 2022, "sets a global target date of 2027" for retail point-of-sale to scan 2D codes (QR with Digital Link, GS1 DataMatrix) alongside 1D barcodes [WIDELY REPORTED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GS1, 2026] ⚠.

**For newcomers.** A *variant* is one buyable version of a product (one size in one colour). A *SKU* is the code the merchant's own systems use for that variant. A *GTIN* is the global barcode number licensed from GS1 that identifies the same item in every company's systems.

**Architecture implications** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]. The merchant must decide, early, which identifier is the join key between catalog, ERP, WMS, marketplaces and search; the GTIN is the only one every external party understands, the SKU is the only one the merchant controls, so most systems carry both and treat both as immutable. Starting: one platform, SKUs typed by hand, GTINs bought in blocks. Growing: the first ERP or marketplace integration fails on products without GTINs or with reused SKUs. Omnichannel: identifier discipline (one SKU per variant across all systems, GLNs for every warehouse, store and wholesale account) becomes a prerequisite for EDI and marketplace feeds. Group: a master-data function owns identifier allocation across brands.

### 1.2 Variant limits: a concrete constraint that shapes catalog design

- Shopify's historic limit was 100 variants per product. New GraphQL product APIs supporting up to 2,048 variants shipped in API version 2024-04 ("updated the GraphQL Admin API in April 2024"); "The sheer amount of data for high-variant products was no longer compatible with the design of the REST Admin API" [VERIFIED: https://www.shopify.com/blog/2048-variants, published 2025-10-15]. On 15 October 2025 the limit opened to all merchants: "up to 2,048 variants" per product, up from the "historical variant limit of 100", with a warning that apps not on the supported GraphQL APIs "may have a downgraded or broken experience" [VERIFIED: https://shopify.dev/changelog/the-product-variant-limit-is-now-2048-for-all-merchants, published 2025-10-15]. Bulk mutations create, update or reorder up to 2,048 variants per call; `productSet` is the declarative mutation for the case where "an external system is the source of truth" and replaces options and variants in one operation [VERIFIED: https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/build/graphql/migrate/new-product-model, n.d., accessed 2026-11] ⚠.
- commercetools now has two catalog models. The Classic model keeps "Product Variants ... embedded within the Product with a 100-variant limit"; the Modular model "introduces Variants as first-class resources" that can be published or unpublished individually and "Removes the 100-variant limit to enable catalogs with up to 10,000 Variants per Product" (public beta 26 June 2026) [VERIFIED: https://docs.commercetools.com/api/releases/2026-06-26-introduced-modular-catalog-in-public-beta, published 2026-06-26]. Modular Catalog went generally available on 29 September 2026; it "uses Standalone Prices and supports up to 10,000 Variants per Product", and Product Search for Modular projects is still in public beta [VERIFIED: https://docs.commercetools.com/api/releases/2026-09-29-modular-catalog-is-now-generally-available-and-product-search-for-modular-catalog-is-available-in-public-beta, published 2026-09-29] ⚠. The limits page confirms 100 embedded variants per product, 10,000 standalone variants in Modular, 1,000 product types and 10,000 categories per project [VERIFIED: https://docs.commercetools.com/api/limits, n.d., accessed 2026-11] ⚠.
- Adobe Commerce models variation as product *types* rather than a variant count: a simple product "is a physical item with a single SKU"; a configurable product presents variations where "each option represents a separate, simple product with a distinct SKU"; grouped products show several standalone products together; a bundle lets shoppers assemble their own product from options; a virtual product "is not a tangible product" (services, memberships); downloadable products deliver files; gift cards (Adobe Commerce only) come as virtual, physical or combined, each with a unique redemption code [VERIFIED: https://experienceleague.adobe.com/en/docs/commerce-admin/catalog/products/product-create, last update 2026-10-08].
- SAP Commerce (hybris) models variants as a hierarchy: a base product with `VariantProduct` subtypes (for example style then size) where only the last level is purchasable, and a `GenericVariantProduct` type for multidimensional variants that share one type and carry their variant attributes as values [WIDELY REPORTED: https://learning.sap.com/courses/exploring-the-technical-essentials-of-sap-commerce-cloud-br/supported-product-variants_ec011fbe-be2c-4bef-b306-1975333dec74, n.d., search snippet; page not fetchable in this session]. The `VariantsService` API in the 1811 Javadoc exposes variant attributes per base product [WIDELY REPORTED: https://help.sap.com/doc/a4265d5ea8314eb2929e6cf6fb8e35a5/1811/en-US/de/hybris/platform/product/VariantsService.html, 2018] **(evidence age: 8 years)**.

| Platform | Variation model | Hard limit (2026) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shopify | product → options → variants | 2,048 variants/product (all plans since 2025-10-15) |
| commercetools Classic | product → embedded variants | 100 variants/product |
| commercetools Modular | product → standalone Variants | 10,000 variants/product |
| Adobe Commerce | configurable parent → simple children (own SKUs); bundle, grouped | no documented count on the pages read |
| SAP Commerce | base product → VariantProduct levels / GenericVariantProduct | no documented count on the pages read |

**For newcomers.** An *option* is a dimension of choice (size); an *option value* is one choice (M). A *configurable product* (Adobe) or *base product* (SAP) is the parent that groups the purchasable children. A *bundle* or *kit* is a product sold as a set of other products; the components keep their own identity and stock.

**Architecture implications** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]. Variant limits are not trivia: apparel and footwear catalogs (sizes × colours × lengths) routinely exceed 100 combinations, and a platform limit decides whether the merchant models one product per colour, splits products artificially, or needs a different platform. Bundles and kits force a decision on where stock lives (component level, almost always) and how a kit price is allocated back to components on the invoice (see Part 4). Starting: hosted platform defaults suffice. Growing: the first limit hit is usually variants or option count, and the workaround (split products) poisons search, feeds and analytics later. Omnichannel: the catalog model must match the marketplaces' parent/child expectations (`item_group_id`, bol.com's EAN-per-variant). Group: catalog structure becomes a shared standard across brands.

### 1.3 Categories, taxonomies and attribute sets

- Google's product taxonomy is "a categorization tree that automatically sorts your products for Google Shopping"; a feed may submit either the numeric id or the full path ("Apparel & Accessories > Clothing > Dresses"), `google_product_category` is optional and overrides Google's automatic classification, and categories published "after August 2011" are accepted and mapped to the latest version [VERIFIED: https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/6324436, n.d., accessed 2026-11]. The current English taxonomy file is headed "Google_Product_Taxonomy_Version: 2021-09-21" and lists 5,595 categories [VERIFIED: https://www.google.com/basepages/producttype/taxonomy-with-ids.en-US.txt, published 2021-09-21] **(evidence age: 5 years, but still the live file)**.
- Shopify's Standard Product Taxonomy is open source (MIT), described as "a universal language for product classification" covering "25+ essential verticals" with categories, attributes and values; versions follow CalVer "in sync with Shopify's API release schedule" (current 2026-10); mappings to "another taxonomy" live under `data/integrations`; the committed `dist/` directory is deprecated and scheduled for removal on 31 October 2026 [VERIFIED: https://github.com/Shopify/product-taxonomy, n.d., accessed 2026-11] ⚠. Releases run from 2024-07 to 2026-10 [VERIFIED: https://shopify.github.io/product-taxonomy/, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- commercetools limits each product to 50 product-level and 50 variant-level attributes indexed for search and 100 attribute groups per project [VERIFIED: https://docs.commercetools.com/api/limits, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- bol.com's content model is "a JSON file of 4,000+ product classifications ('chunks')", each defining its attributes, allowed values and which are mandatory; a new product needs a GPC code (the chunk id) [VERIFIED: https://api.bol.com/retailer/public/Retailer-API/v10/functional/retailer-api/product-content-api.html, n.d., accessed 2026-11].

**For newcomers.** A *taxonomy* is a fixed tree of categories a channel understands. An *attribute set* is the list of fields a product of a given kind must carry (a jacket has waterproof rating; a boot has sole material). *Mapping* is the table that translates the merchant's categories and attributes into each channel's.

**Architecture implications** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]. Every channel has its own taxonomy; the merchant's internal taxonomy should be the one it merchandises by, with mappings to Google, Shopify, bol.com and Amazon kept as data, not code. Attribute sets are the contract between PIM, storefront filters and search facets: a facet can only exist for an attribute that is filled consistently. Starting: use the platform's category tree. Growing: the first marketplace forces a mapping table. Omnichannel: a PIM (1.5) owns attribute sets and mappings. Group: a group-level taxonomy with brand extensions.

### 1.4 Digital goods, gift cards, lifecycle and localization

- Adobe treats downloadable, virtual and gift-card products as distinct types (1.2) [VERIFIED: https://experienceleague.adobe.com/en/docs/commerce-admin/catalog/products/product-create, last update 2026-10-08]; in its inventory logic, virtual and downloadable products "Always use the SSA recommendation" because nothing ships [VERIFIED: https://experienceleague.adobe.com/en/docs/commerce-admin/inventory/basics/product-types, last update 2026-10-08].
- Shopify lets merchants "activate gift cards on your store to sell gift card products" and create cards from the admin; use is subject to Shopify's gift-card terms [VERIFIED: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/gift-card-products, n.d., accessed 2026-11]; market price rounding "doesn't apply to gift cards" [VERIFIED: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/international/pricing/rounding, n.d., accessed 2026-11]. Gift cards and store credit as *liabilities* are in Part 4.
- Lifecycle: commercetools separates staged from published data and forbids deleting published products (1.1); Google Merchant Center's `availability` takes `in_stock`, `out_of_stock`, `preorder` or `backorder` (the last two require `availability_date`, "up to one year in the future"), and an `expiration_date` must be "less than 30 days in the future" [VERIFIED: https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/7052112, n.d., accessed 2026-11]. GS1 issues a new GTIN for every distinct trade item and packaging configuration (1.1) [WIDELY REPORTED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Trade_Item_Number, 2026].
- Localization: in Shopify "Each published language gets its own URLs, and your store displays the translated version when translations exist"; translations come from the Translate & Adapt app (manual or AI), CSV import or third-party apps, and checkout and notifications follow the customer's language [VERIFIED: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/markets/languages, n.d., accessed 2026-11]. bol.com accepts attribute values in `nl`, `nl-BE`, `fr` or `fr-BE` while attribute ids are English [VERIFIED: https://api.bol.com/retailer/public/Retailer-API/v10/functional/retailer-api/product-content-api.html, n.d., accessed 2026-11]. Akeneo lists "localizing content for each market" among enrichment functions [VERIFIED: https://www.akeneo.com/what-is-a-pim/, n.d., accessed 2026-11].

**For newcomers.** A *digital good* is a product with no shipment (download, licence, service). A *gift card product* is sold like a product but creates a prepaid balance, not a delivery. *Product lifecycle* is the set of states a product passes through (draft, active, end of season, discontinued) and the rules about which systems may still reference it.

**Architecture implications** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]. Products are never deleted once sold: orders, returns, invoices and analytics reference them for years, so "discontinued" is a state, not a deletion. Digital goods and gift cards need their own fulfilment path (no WMS, no carrier) and their own tax and accounting treatment, which is why every platform models them as types. Translations are per product, per language, per channel; a PIM is the natural owner once there are more than two languages.

### 1.5 PIM: what the vendors say it does, and when it is needed

- Akeneo: a PIM is "a system or solution that helps businesses manage, centralize, and distribute product information", a "central source of truth"; it collects "data from your ERP, MDM, PLM, and other systems", enriches "product descriptions with technical and usage data", links media from DAM systems, and lets teams "Manage catalogs for every sales channel in one system of record" with API connectors and marketplace syndication; the page frames the need as "reducing the complexity of managing multiple, disconnected databases or spreadsheets" [VERIFIED: https://www.akeneo.com/what-is-a-pim/, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- Pimcore is "an open core platform for Product Experience Management" combining a core framework with PIM, MDM, DAM, CDP, DXP/CMS and digital-commerce modules; data is stored separately from output channels and delivered via REST and GraphQL; the stack is PHP/Symfony with MySQL/MariaDB and OpenSearch or Elasticsearch; licensing is the Pimcore Open Core License (POCL) with Community, Professional, Enterprise and PaaS editions; platform version 2026.1 [VERIFIED: https://github.com/pimcore/pimcore, n.d., accessed 2026-11] ⚠.
- Salsify calls itself a "Product Experience Management (PXM)" platform: "Manage all product content in one central system of record", "Easily syndicate product content to every consumer touch point", a "direct-to-retailer network", and a GDSN data pool to "Synchronize standard supply chain, marketing, and ecommerce attributes globally"; the page announces an acquisition by Cinven [VERIFIED: https://www.salsify.com/, n.d., accessed 2026-11] ⚠.
- inriver positions PIM as "Product information orchestration for the AI era" and lists syndication ("Syndicate Advance"), digital shelf analytics and content onboarding as modules [VERIFIED: https://www.inriver.com/pim/, n.d., accessed 2026-11].

**Practitioner caveat** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]. All four descriptions are vendor pages; none gives a testable threshold for when a PIM pays off. The analyst's synthesis, consistent with the vendor pages: a PIM becomes necessary when product data has more than one *producer* (buying, marketing, suppliers, translators) and more than one *consumer* (web, marketplaces, print, wholesale, stores) and the commerce platform's product record can no longer hold the workflow (enrichment status, approvals, per-channel variants of the same copy). Below that, the commerce platform or a spreadsheet with discipline is cheaper; above it, the absence of a PIM shows up as channel-specific copies that drift.

**For newcomers.** A *PIM* (product information management system) is the editorial system where product data is created, enriched, translated and approved before it is published to channels. *Enrichment* is adding the marketing, technical and media content a channel needs beyond what the ERP holds. *Syndication* is publishing product content to each channel in that channel's format and rules.

**Architecture implications** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]. The ERP remains the system of record for the SKU's existence, cost and logistics attributes; the PIM becomes the system of record for descriptive content; the commerce platform holds a published copy. The hard design question is the direction of each attribute's flow (who may edit what), not the tool. Starting / Growing: no PIM; the platform is the catalog. Omnichannel: a PIM, with the catalog in the platform demoted to a channel copy. Group: the PIM is shared infrastructure with brand-level workflows.

### 1.6 Syndication: Google Merchant Center, schema.org, marketplaces

- Google Merchant Center's product data specification requires `id` (max 50 characters, "Keep it stable"), `title` (max 150), `description` (max 5,000), `link`, `image_link`, `price`, `availability`; `brand` is required for new products except movies, books and music; `gtin` is strongly recommended and "If you submit a product with an incorrect GTIN value, your product will be disapproved"; `mpn` is required only when there is no manufacturer GTIN; `identifier_exists` defaults to `yes`; variants need `item_group_id` (required in Brazil, France, Germany, Japan, the UK and the US, and for all free listings); a new 500 × 500 pixel image minimum will be enforced from 31 January 2027 [VERIFIED: https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/7052112, n.d., accessed 2026-11] ⚠.
- Tax display in the same specification: for the US and Canada prices "shouldn't include tax"; "For all other countries: Include value added tax (VAT) or Goods and Services Tax (GST) in the price"; "If an item is out of stock, the price must still be clearly visible on the landing page" [VERIFIED: https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/7052112, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- Google's merchant-listing structured data requires `name`, `image` and an `Offer` (not `AggregateOffer`) with `price` (or `priceSpecification.price`) and `priceCurrency` (ISO 4217); "merchant listing experiences require a price greater than zero"; variants use `isVariantOf` / `inProductGroupWithID` and each variant should have a distinct URL; a past `priceValidUntil` can stop the listing from displaying; shipping and return policies set in Merchant Center take precedence over markup [VERIFIED: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/merchant-listing, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- bol.com (the largest Dutch/Belgian marketplace): "The EAN is a unique product identifier that you have to supply for each product"; content without an EAN is DECLINED; enrichment levels 0 and 1 are mandatory for publishing; labelled images are mandatory at level 1; "Absence of a valid CE MARKING will result in the product going offline" where a chunk requires it; descriptions accept only a fixed set of HTML tags; upload reports are asynchronous (wait at least one hour) and kept for 28 days; the data model "is updated daily" [VERIFIED: https://api.bol.com/retailer/public/Retailer-API/v10/functional/retailer-api/product-content-api.html, n.d., accessed 2026-11] ⚠.

**For newcomers.** A *feed* is a file or API stream of product records in a channel's format. *Structured data* (schema.org JSON-LD) is product information embedded in the web page itself so search engines can read it. A *channel's content rules* decide whether a product is listed at all.

**Architecture implications** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]. Each channel validates product data against its own rules and rejects silently or asynchronously; syndication therefore needs a per-channel outbox with validation, a report of rejections, and a feedback loop into the PIM. Price and availability belong in the same feed as content but change at a different cadence, which argues for separate update paths (content daily, price and stock near-real-time). Starting: the platform's built-in Google feed. Growing: a feed tool (Channable, Productsup and the like) or the marketplace's connector. Omnichannel: syndication from the PIM, with the feed tool as transport. Group: shared syndication service with brand-level channel accounts.

## PART 2: Pricing

### 2.1 Price lists, price books and price selection

- Shopify B2B: "You can use B2B catalogs to choose which products and prices to offer your B2B customers"; a catalog can exclude or include products, apply "percentage-based price adjustments" or "set fixed prices"; it is assigned to a B2B market or (on Plus) directly to company locations; quantity rules limit how many items a buyer may order and volume pricing "offers price breaks when a customer buys a set quantity of a product in the same order"; non-Plus plans allow "up to 3 active catalog assignments across active B2B markets" [VERIFIED: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/b2b/catalogs, n.d., accessed 2026-11] ⚠. In the developer model a company "contains locations and contacts", catalogs "can be assigned only to a company location", and payment terms are "pre-negotiated" per location [VERIFIED: https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/build/b2b, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- Shopify Markets: "By default, prices are converted automatically using the current market exchange rate"; merchants can set manual rates, "control the prices in different markets using price percentage adjustments", or set fixed per-market prices through catalogs; local currencies require Shopify Payments [VERIFIED: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/markets/pricing, n.d., accessed 2026-11]. Rounding: prices are "automatically rounded to the most common denominator for each currency" and merchants "can't customize your rounding rules to anything different from these defaults" [VERIFIED: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/international/pricing/rounding, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- commercetools: a price is defined per SKU and per *scope* (currency, country, customer group, channel), and "only one Price can exist for a price scope"; selection walks an eight-step fallback from most to least specific (customer group + channel + country ... down to none), time-bound prices beat non-time-bound ones within a step, and when several match "the cheapest is selected, before Cart Discounts"; a variant may hold up to 100 embedded prices or 50,000 standalone prices; tiered prices apply to the full quantity once the minimum is reached [VERIFIED: https://docs.commercetools.com/api/pricing-and-discounts-overview, n.d., accessed 2026-11]. Standalone Prices are "a flexible way to store your product prices, especially with a large number of prices", with validity, tiers and staged changes as update actions [VERIFIED: https://docs.commercetools.com/api/projects/standalone-prices, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- Adobe Commerce: "Tier pricing lets you offer a quantity discount from a product listing or product page"; tiers are scoped per website and per customer group (or shared catalog in B2B), fixed or percentage, and "The prices in the storefront take precedence from the highest to the lowest quantity" [VERIFIED: https://experienceleague.adobe.com/en/docs/commerce-admin/catalog/products/pricing/product-price-tier, n.d., accessed 2026-11]. B2B shared catalogs give "gated shared catalogs with custom pricing for different companies"; "The system supports only one public shared catalog at a time" and custom catalogs hold "pricing that is visible only to logged-in associates of the assigned company accounts" [VERIFIED: https://experienceleague.adobe.com/en/docs/commerce-admin/b2b/shared-catalogs/catalog-shared, last update 2026-10-08]. Catalog price rules are "triggered before a product is placed into the shopping cart" and "do not use coupon codes" [VERIFIED: https://experienceleague.adobe.com/en/docs/commerce-admin/marketing/promotions/catalog-rules/price-rules-catalog, last update 2026-10-08].
- SAP Commerce (Europe1 pricing): a price row "represents an assignment of a price (or tax, or discount) to a product, or user or both in combination"; the factory "matches price rows by product or product group, user or user group, min quantity, currency and daterange" and "Removes price rows from set of matching rows if they will never be applicable" [VERIFIED: https://help.sap.com/doc/a4265d5ea8314eb2929e6cf6fb8e35a5/1811/en-US/de/hybris/platform/europe1/jalo/Europe1PriceFactory.html, published 2018 (hybris Commerce Suite 18.11)] **(evidence age: 8 years; the Europe1 model is long-lived but check current docs)**. The `PriceRow` type carries `product`/`pg` (product price group), `user`/`ug` (user price group), `currency`, `unit`, `minqtd`, `price`, `net`, `startTime`, `endTime`, `channel`, `giveAwayPrice` [VERIFIED: https://help.sap.com/doc/a4265d5ea8314eb2929e6cf6fb8e35a5/1811/en-US/de/hybris/platform/europe1/model/PriceRowModel.html, published 2018]. A knowledge-base article shows a classic failure: a price with a validity period "does NOT expire after the validity end date has passed" because "the validity range date is compared with the cart creation date" [VERIFIED: https://userapps.support.sap.com/sap/support/knowledge/en/2267117, n.d., accessed 2026-11].

| Platform | Unit of pricing | Dimensions of a price | Customer-specific prices |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shopify | Catalog (price list + product set) | market, company location, currency, quantity break | fixed or % per catalog, per company location |
| commercetools | Price (embedded or standalone) per SKU | currency, country, customer group, channel, validity, tiers | customer group scope; standalone prices for scale |
| Adobe Commerce | Product price + tier prices + shared catalogs | website, customer group / shared catalog, quantity | custom shared catalog per company |
| SAP Commerce | Price row | product or product group, user or user group, currency, unit, min quantity, validity, channel, net/gross | user price groups and per-user rows |

**For newcomers.** A *price list* (Shopify "catalog", SAP "price rows", commercetools "standalone prices") is a named set of prices for a group of customers or a market. *Price selection* is the rule that picks one price when several could apply. A *contract price* is a price agreed with one wholesale account, which outranks the list price.

**Architecture implications** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]. The number of prices grows multiplicatively (SKUs × currencies × countries × customer groups × validity windows), which is why every platform moves from embedded prices to a separate price store at scale and why price selection must be deterministic and documented. The pricing *engine* (the thing that selects) and the price *data* (the lists) are different concerns; channels that cannot call the engine (marketplaces, EDI, POS offline) receive copies, and every copy is a promise that can drift. The SAP cart-creation-date bug is the generic lesson: a price must be selected at a defined moment (cart creation, checkout, order confirmation) and that moment must be the same across systems. Starting: one list in the platform. Growing: market prices and the first wholesale price list; copies in the marketplace. Omnichannel: a single price source (platform or PIM/ERP-fed pricing service) that every channel reads, with validity windows for scheduled changes and effective-dated history for invoices and the 30-day rule (2.3). Group: a pricing service shared across brands, per-country legal rules as configuration.

### 2.2 Tax-inclusive display: EU vs US

- Directive 98/6/EC (16 February 1998, OJ L 80/27 of 18 March 1998) defines the selling price as "the final price for a unit of the product, or a given quantity of the product, including VAT and all other taxes" and the unit price as "the final price, including VAT and all other taxes, for one kilogramme, one litre, one metre, one square metre or one cubic metre"; "The selling price and the unit price shall be indicated for all products" and "must be unambiguous, easily identifiable and clearly legible" [VERIFIED: http://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/31998L0006, published 1998-03-18].
- The US contrast has no federal equivalent; the clearest operational statement is Google's: US and Canada prices "shouldn't include tax", everywhere else VAT/GST is included [VERIFIED: https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/7052112, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- Shopify implements the split as "dynamic tax-inclusive pricing": storefront prices include local tax for a fixed list of countries and regions (the UK, Germany, France, Japan, Australia among them) and exclude it elsewhere "such as the US"; the home tax rate is stripped from the base price and the destination rate applied, so a $100 product shows as $90.91 in untaxed regions and $109.09 in the UK at 20 % VAT; shipping rates are not adjusted and fixed international prices are not adjusted [VERIFIED: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/international/pricing/dynamic-tax-inclusive-pricing, n.d., accessed 2026-11].

**For newcomers.** *Tax-inclusive* means the shown price is what the consumer pays, tax already inside; *tax-exclusive* means tax is added at checkout. In the EU consumer prices must be shown tax-inclusive; in the US sales tax is normally added at checkout because it depends on the delivery address.

**Architecture implications** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]. Store the price with an explicit "includes tax: yes/no" flag and the tax basis it was computed on; never let a storefront infer it. A European merchant selling to the US needs net prices as the base and gross prices derived, or two price lists; a US merchant selling to the EU needs the reverse. Rounding after conversion and after tax changes the gross price by cents, which matters for the 30-day lowest-price rule and for marketplace price matching.

### 2.3 The Omnibus "prior price" rule (Directive 2019/2161)

- Directive (EU) 2019/2161 of 27 November 2019 (OJ L 328/7, 18 December 2019) inserts Article 6a into Directive 98/6/EC: "Any announcement of a price reduction shall indicate the prior price applied by the trader for a determined period of time prior to the application of the price reduction"; "The prior price means the lowest price applied by the trader during a period of time not shorter than 30 days prior to the application of the price reduction"; member states may set different rules for goods that "deteriorate or expire rapidly", a shorter period for products on the market less than 30 days, and for progressive reductions may let the prior price be "the price without the price reduction before the first application". Member states had to adopt measures by 28 November 2021 and "shall apply those measures from 28 May 2022"; fines must be possible with a maximum "of at least EUR 2 million" [VERIFIED: http://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32019L2161, published 2019-12-18].
- Germany transposed it as § 11 PAngV: whoever announces a price reduction to consumers must state "the lowest total price applied to consumers within the last 30 days before the reduction"; for stepwise, uninterrupted increasing reductions the lowest price before the first step may be shown for the duration; exemptions for individual reductions and for perishable goods when the reason is made clear (translated from German) [VERIFIED: https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/pangv_2022/__11.html, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- The Netherlands: ACM announced on 6 December 2022 that from 1 January 2023 it supervises the tightened "van-voor" rules; the discount must be calculated on the lowest price of the preceding 30 days ("A discount must be a real discount on the lowest price that applied before", translated from Dutch); exceptions for perishables, products on the market under 30 days, and progressive discounts for at most three months; the worked example: a price raised from €50 to €70 and cut 35 % after 25 days is really a 9 % discount on the €50 reference [VERIFIED: https://www.acm.nl/nl/publicaties/acm-wil-einde-aan-nepkortingen-na-komst-strengere-regels, published 2022-12-06]. On 11 June 2024 ACM published fines (decisions of 2 May 2024) against Day Traders (Koopjedeal), Leen Bakker, G-Star Raw eStore, JYSK and PVH Brands Netherlands (Tommy Hilfiger) for discounts off a misleading reference price: "Consumers were therefore shown a fake discount" (translated) [VERIFIED: https://www.acm.nl/nl/publicaties/acm-publiceert-boetebesluiten-webwinkels-voor-nepkortingen, published 2024-06-11].
- The same directive defines ranking for the search chapter: "Ranking refers to the relative prominence of the offers of traders or the relevance given to search results as presented, organised or communicated by providers of online search functionality, including resulting from the use of algorithmic sequencing, rating or review mechanisms, visual highlights, or other saliency tools" (recital 19) [VERIFIED: http://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32019L2161, published 2019-12-18].

**For newcomers.** The *prior price* (reference price, "van-prijs", "Streichpreis") is the price a discount is announced against. The *30-day rule* says it must be the lowest price charged in the 30 days before the reduction, not the last list price.

**Architecture implications** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]. The rule converts price history into a legal record: the pricing service must keep an effective-dated history per SKU per country per channel and be able to answer "lowest price in the last 30 days" at the moment a promotion is published. Promotions that lower prices progressively need the first reference preserved. Marketplaces and feeds show the strike-through price too, so the reference price is itself a syndicated field. A merchant with price copies in several channels (2.1) cannot comply reliably; this is the strongest regulatory argument for a single price source in Europe.

### 2.4 Price parity and price control on marketplaces

- 2013, Germany and the UK: Amazon's price parity clause meant "sellers are prohibited from selling products they offer on Amazon cheaper through any other sales channel"; on 27 August 2013 Amazon told the Bundeskartellamt it would stop enforcing it, and the authority required that "the company gives up its price parity clause for good" [VERIFIED: https://www.bundeskartellamt.de/SharedDocs/Meldung/EN/Pressemitteilungen/2013/27_08_2013_Amazon-Preisparit%C3%A4t.html, published 2013-08-27]. On 26 November 2013 the authority closed the case after the "legally binding deletion of the price parity clauses from Amazon's contractual conditions with all the sellers"; its president: "Price specifications for one's own competitors can never be justified" [VERIFIED: https://www.bundeskartellamt.de/SharedDocs/Meldung/EN/Pressemitteilungen/2013/26_11_2013_Amazon-Verfahrenseinstellung.html, published 2013-11-26]. Amazon ended the policy on its EU marketplaces from 31 August 2013 and the UK OFT closed its parallel case; the policy was kept outside the EU [WIDELY REPORTED: https://www.insideglobaltech.com/2013/09/02/amazon-informs-german-and-uk-competition-authorities-of-its-plan-to-end-price-parity-policy/, 2013] **(evidence age: 13 years; historical)**.
- 2022, EU: on 20 December 2022 the Commission made Amazon's commitments binding: not to use non-public seller data for its retail business; to "treat all sellers equally when ranking the offers for the purposes of the selection of the Buy Box winner"; to "display a second competing offer to the Buy Box winner" when sufficiently differentiated on price and/or delivery; non-discriminatory Prime qualification; seven years for the Prime and second-Buy-Box commitments, five years for the rest, under a monitoring trustee [VERIFIED: https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_22_7777, published 2022-12-20].
- 2023, US: the FTC and 17 states sued Amazon on 26 September 2023 alleging "Anti-discounting measures that punish sellers" who offer lower prices elsewhere, including that Amazon can "bury discounting sellers so far down in Amazon's search results that they become effectively invisible" [VERIFIED: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/09/ftc-sues-amazon-illegally-maintaining-monopoly-power, published 2023-09-26] ⚠ (litigation pending).
- 2022–2026, Germany: in July 2022 the Bundeskartellamt found "Amazon is a company of paramount significance for competition across markets" (Section 19a GWB), upheld by the Federal Court of Justice in April 2024; in June 2025 it sent Amazon "its preliminary legal assessment" of its influence on seller prices and its Fair Pricing Policy; "On 5 February 2026 the Bundeskartellamt prohibited Amazon from applying so-called price control mechanisms" and ordered disgorgement of an initial amount of about 59 million euros "because the violation is still ongoing" [VERIFIED: https://www.bundeskartellamt.de/EN/Digital_economy/proceedings_against_large_digital_companies/Amazon/Amazon.html, n.d., accessed 2026-11] ⚠. The mechanisms flagged offers priced "too high"; Amazon "either removes the offer or displays it in a manner that makes it difficult for customers to find"; influence is "permissible only in cases involving 'excessive' pricing"; the decision rests on Section 19a(2) and Section 19 GWB and Article 102 TFEU, "is not yet final" and can be appealed directly to the Federal Court of Justice [WIDELY REPORTED: https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/02/german-competition-authority-fines-amazon-for-allegedly-influencing-prices-of-thirdparty-sellers-on-amazon-marketplace, 2026-02-11] ⚠.

**For newcomers.** A *price parity clause* forbids a seller from selling cheaper anywhere else. A *price control mechanism* is the opposite direction: the marketplace suppresses offers it judges too expensive. The *Buy Box* is the default "add to cart" offer on an Amazon product page; losing it is commercially close to being delisted.

**Architecture implications** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]. Marketplaces compare the merchant's marketplace price against its own webshop and against other sellers, continuously and algorithmically; a merchant therefore needs per-channel price rules (marketplace price = webshop price + fee uplift, within tolerance) and monitoring of Buy Box status and suppressed offers as operational signals. Because the legal boundaries move (2013 parity ban, 2022 commitments, 2026 German prohibition under appeal), channel price rules must be configuration, not code. Growing: the first marketplace shows the merchant how fast a price copy drifts. Omnichannel: channel pricing policies in the pricing service, with a feed of marketplace price-state events back into it.

## PART 3: Promotions

### 3.1 Discount types and stacking rules in three platforms

- Shopify has three discount classes: product discounts (specific items or collections), order discounts (cart subtotal) and shipping discounts. All merchants can combine order with free-shipping, product with free-shipping, and product with product on separate items; product with order and order with order need an eligible checkout; multiple product discounts on the same line need Shopify Plus; "Multiple shipping discounts can't apply to the same order". Limits: "You can have a maximum of 25 active automatic discounts" (apps included) and "Customers can use a maximum of 5 product or order discount codes and 1 shipping discount code on the same order". Order of application: product discounts first, then order discounts on the revised subtotal, then shipping; combined percentage order discounts are "calculated on the original subtotal" (10 % + 20 % on $100 = $70); when discounts cannot combine, the best discount for the cart applies; manually added custom discounts always apply [VERIFIED: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/discounts/discount-combinations, n.d., accessed 2026-11] ⚠. Discounts apply to "the subtotal of the order before taxes, and then any applicable tax is applied afterwards"; rollouts schedule activation and let merchants "test a discount against the discounts that your store offers already" [VERIFIED: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/discounts, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- commercetools separates Product Discounts (lower the displayed price before the cart; "Only one Product Discount applies to any Price of a Product Variant at any given time") from Cart Discounts (line items, custom line items, shipping or total; several apply in sort order). Cart discounts have a unique `sortOrder` across all cart discounts and discount groups, a stacking mode that can stop further discounts after a given one, and Discount Groups where only the best saving applies; under "best deal" mode the platform applies whichever of product or cart discount gives the lower total per line item; discount codes unlock a cart discount and inherit its sort order [VERIFIED: https://docs.commercetools.com/api/pricing-and-discounts-overview, n.d., accessed 2026-11]. Value types are relative, absolute (distributed proportionately, evenly or individually), fixed price, and "Adds one free Line Item to the Cart as a gift"; multi-buy targets implement "buy X, get Y at a discounted rate" (trigger 6, discounted 2 applies twice on 12 items); pattern targets express bundles such as 2 jeans + 1 shirt for $100 off [VERIFIED: https://docs.commercetools.com/api/projects/cartDiscounts, n.d., accessed 2026-11]. Limits: 100 active code-less cart discounts per project plus 100 per store; "A Cart can contain up to 10 DiscountCodes"; a code can map to up to 10 cart discounts [VERIFIED: https://docs.commercetools.com/api/limits, n.d., accessed 2026-11] ⚠.
- Adobe Commerce cart price rules: actions are "Percent of product price discount", fixed amount per item, fixed amount for the whole cart (subtotal, shipping only if enabled) and "Buy X get Y free"; "The rule with the highest priority (lowest number) is applied first"; rules with equal priority "do not combine; they apply separately based on rule ID"; "Discard subsequent rules" set to Yes "Prevents any other pricing rules that may apply to a product from being applied"; coupons are none, specific, or auto-generated in bulk with "Uses per Coupon" and "Uses per Customer" (the latter only for logged-in customers); free shipping "For matching items only" or "For shipment with matching items" [VERIFIED: https://experienceleague.adobe.com/en/docs/commerce-admin/marketing/promotions/cart-rules/price-rules-cart-create, n.d., accessed 2026-11]. Conditions on shipping or payment method "are met only in checkout after the specific shipping/payment methods are selected", so such coupons may only apply at the last step [VERIFIED: https://experienceleague.adobe.com/en/docs/commerce-admin/marketing/promotions/cart-rules/price-rules-cart, n.d., accessed 2026-11].

| Concern | Shopify | commercetools | Adobe Commerce |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Where a promotion acts | product / order / shipping class | product price vs cart (line, shipping, total) | catalog rule (before cart) vs cart rule |
| Ordering | fixed: product → order → shipping | explicit unique `sortOrder` | priority number, lowest first |
| Stop stacking | class-combination settings | stacking mode; discount groups (best only) | "Discard subsequent rules" |
| Codes per order | 5 + 1 shipping | 10 | per-rule coupon with usage caps |
| Active automatic promotions | 25 | 100 + 100 per store | not stated on pages read |

**For newcomers.** *Stacking* is applying more than one promotion to the same cart or line. A *rules engine* evaluates promotion conditions (who, what, how much, when) at cart time instead of hard-coding them. *Discount allocation* is spreading an order-level discount over the lines so each line, and later each invoice line and return, carries its share.

**Architecture implications** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]. All three platforms converge on the same design: promotions are data with an explicit order of evaluation, a stop rule, and a per-code usage ledger; the storefront shows an *estimate* and the cart engine is the only place the final discount is computed. Order-level discounts must be allocated to lines at order time (commercetools' proportionate / even / individual modes make this explicit) because returns, partial refunds and invoices work per line. Rollouts and "test against existing discounts" exist because promotion interaction is the most common source of margin bugs. Starting: platform discounts. Growing: automatic discounts plus codes, first stacking surprises on Black Friday. Omnichannel: one promotion engine that store POS and marketplaces can at least mirror; marketplace promotions remain separate systems. Group: brand-level promotion calendars with shared engine and allocation rules.

### 3.2 Coupon and promotion abuse

- Ravelin defines promo abuse as "a type of policy fraud that involves customers taking advantage of a merchant's promotional offers"; its types are multi-accounting ("the creation of multiple accounts for fraudulent purposes" to claim sign-up offers repeatedly), referral-bonus abuse (referring oneself), and voucher abuse "when businesses send out discount codes that are too simple and become predictable" [VERIFIED: https://www.ravelin.com/blog/promotion-abuse-ecommerce, published 2020-10-23] **(evidence age: 6 years; the article links a 2024 survey)**. The same page reports "53% of merchants experienced increased promo abuse activity in the past year" (highest in digital goods, 56.5 %) and 51.1 % expecting an increase, from "Ravelin's latest Fraud & Payments Survey" with no sample size shown [VERIFIED: same URL; vendor survey, treat as directional].
- Secondary sources add a consumer-side figure (a 2024 Riskified survey of 1,000 US consumers in which 58 % admitted using multiple email addresses to reuse coupons) and list promo abuse among the main merchant fraud types for 2025 [WIDELY REPORTED: https://thepaypers.com/fraud-and-fincrime/reports/fraud-prevention-in-ecommerce-report-2024-2025, 2024; vendor surveys].

**For newcomers.** *Promotion abuse* is using an offer in a way the merchant did not intend (new-customer discounts on a tenth account, guessed codes, self-referrals). It is a fraud-and-identity problem that shows up as a promotion cost.

**Architecture implications** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]. Single-use codes generated in bulk (Adobe's auto-generation, Shopify's per-customer limits) and per-customer usage ledgers are the baseline; identity signals (device, payment instrument, address) rather than email are needed to make "once per customer" mean anything, which links promotions to the identity chapter. Measure abuse as a share of redemptions per campaign, not as a global fraud rate.

### 3.3 Gift cards, store credit and loyalty points as liabilities

- Starbucks (FY2025 10-K, fiscal year ended 28 September 2025): "Amounts loaded onto stored value cards are initially recorded as deferred revenue and recognized as revenue upon redemption"; "Based on historical redemption rates, a portion of stored value cards is not expected to be redeemed and will be recognized as breakage over time in proportion to stored value card redemptions", net of "remittance to government agencies under unclaimed property laws, if applicable"; breakage revenue was $200.4 million (FY2025), $187.6 million (FY2024) and $196.1 million (FY2023) in company-operated stores plus $22.0 / $20.0 / $18.9 million in licensed stores; the stored value card and loyalty liability stood at $1,751.7 million at 28 September 2025 after $15,245.8 million deferred and $15,199.5 million recognised in the year; loyalty: "We defer revenue associated with the estimated selling price of Stars earned ... as each Star is earned and a corresponding liability is established in deferred revenue", net of estimated unredeemed Stars; "Stars generally expire after six to twelve months, depending on the market" [VERIFIED: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/829224/000082922425000114/sbux-20250928.htm, published 2025-11 (10-K for FY ended 2025-09-28)].
- US GAAP (ASC 606) guidance: "Gift cards are often not redeemed for products or services in their full amount"; "A reporting entity should recognize estimated breakage as revenue in proportion to the pattern of exercised rights"; breakage not expected to occur "should be recognized as revenue when the likelihood of the customer exercising its remaining rights becomes remote"; for amounts that must be remitted under unclaimed-property law the entity "should not recognize estimated breakage as revenue"; "The revenue standard requires a reporting entity to estimate the expected breakage amount" rather than wait for it [VERIFIED: https://viewpoint.pwc.com/dt/us/en/pwc/accounting_guides/revenue_from_contrac/revenue_from_contrac_US/chapter_7_options_to_US/74unexercised_rights_US.html, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- IFRS 15 was issued in May 2014 together with the FASB's Topic 606, is "effective for annual reporting periods beginning on or after 1 January 2018", follows the five-step model, and replaced IFRIC 13 *Customer Loyalty Programmes*, which "addressed the accounting by the entity that grants award credits to its customers" [VERIFIED: https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ifrs-15-revenue-from-contracts-with-customers/, n.d., accessed 2026-11]. IFRS 15 paragraphs B44–B47 contain the matching breakage rule (recognise expected breakage in proportion to the pattern of rights exercised; amounts due to a government are a liability) and loyalty points are a separate performance obligation when they give a "material right", with revenue allocated on relative stand-alone selling prices [WIDELY REPORTED: https://ifrscommunity.com/knowledge-base/revenue-from-customers-unexercised-rights-breakage/, 2024; the standard's text could not be fetched].
- Shopify store credit is a store-funded balance on a customer account usable as a payment method on the online store, POS and Shop; "Store credit expiration is set per issuance" ("Unlike gift cards" there is no store-wide default expiry), a single account may hold under $15,000 USD, it can be issued manually or as a refund method, and stores created on or after 12 May 2025 pay third-party transaction fees on the store-credit portion of an order [VERIFIED: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/customers/store-credit, n.d., accessed 2026-11] ⚠.

**For newcomers.** A *liability* here is money the merchant has received (or value it has promised) for goods not yet delivered: unredeemed gift cards, store credit, loyalty points. *Breakage* is the part of that value that will never be redeemed; accounting rules say when it may be counted as revenue. A *material right* is a loyalty benefit valuable enough that it must be treated as a separate thing the customer paid for.

**Architecture implications** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]. Gift cards, store credit and points are ledgers, not promotions: every issuance, redemption, expiry and breakage estimate must be auditable per instrument, with balances reconcilable to the general ledger monthly. The commerce platform holds the balance; the finance system holds the liability; they must agree. Because expiry and unclaimed-property rules vary by country and US state, expiry must be a per-issuance attribute (as Shopify models it), not a global setting. Starting: platform gift cards, finance reconciles by export. Growing: store credit for refunds; the first breakage estimate. Omnichannel: one balance usable online, in store and at the marketplace-returns desk, which needs a central balance service. Group: a loyalty platform shared across brands with a single liability ledger.

### 3.4 Loyalty programs and measuring incrementality

- Starbucks Rewards is "primarily a spend-based loyalty program" in which members earn Stars per purchase and redeem a reward "for free products"; members are enrolled when they register a stored value card [VERIFIED: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/829224/000082922425000114/sbux-20250928.htm, published 2025-11].
- Academic evidence on measuring promotion lift: Gubela, Lessmann and Jaroszewicz argue that "revenue maximization is a more plausible business objective compared to maximizing conversions" when customers differ in spending, that "The distribution of campaign revenues is typically zero-inflated because of many non-buyers", and show on real e-commerce data and "a digital coupon targeting campaign" that revenue uplift modelling improves campaign profit over conversion-based targeting [VERIFIED: https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.08729, published 2019-11-20 (EJOR 2019)]. Gubela and Lessmann extend uplift modelling to multiple treatments (several coupon types) and continuous revenue outcomes with causal forests, noting that "Prior work examines models for single treatments and binary customer responses" [VERIFIED: https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.03336, published 2021-01-09 (AMCIS 2020)]; the paper's experiment found only one of several coupon types (a €15 coupon) with positive incremental cumulative revenue, with a 12 % coupon positive on individual effects yet below control on revenue [WIDELY REPORTED: search snippet of the same paper's PDF, 2021; not read in full].
- Practitioner guides agree on the method: a randomised or geo *holdout* that does not receive the promotion is the counterfactual; lift is the difference in conversion or revenue between exposed and holdout groups; redemptions alone mislead because "a promotion can redeem heavily and still be close to zero incremental if most redeemers were already at checkout" [WIDELY REPORTED: https://bluealpha.ai/articles/direct-mail-attribution-promo-codes and https://www.uniqodo.com/glossary/what-is-incrementality, 2024–2025; vendor content, no independent benchmark found].

**For newcomers.** *Incrementality* is the sales a promotion caused that would not have happened without it. A *holdout group* is a set of customers deliberately not given the promotion so the difference can be measured. *Redemption rate* (how many used the code) is not incrementality.

**Architecture implications** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]. Measuring incrementality needs three things the platform rarely provides by default: a stable customer identity across channels, the ability to assign and withhold promotions per customer (holdout flags in the promotion engine), and order data that records which promotions were *offered*, not only which were *redeemed*. This is an analytics-chapter dependency; the promotion engine's job is to emit the events.

## PART 4: Search and discovery

### 4.1 The index as a copy of the catalog

- commercetools' Product Search indexes product fields, variant data, "Variant attributes that are declared searchable", prices and availability; "The `variants.*` fields contain the data for all Product Variants (including the Master Variant) for a Product"; search returns product ids and the client fetches full data separately; price filters work on `variants.prices.currencyCode`, `.country`, `.customerGroup`, `.channel` and on `variants.prices.currentCentAmount` when product discounts apply; facets are distinct, ranges, count and stats, with `postFilter` for multi-select navigation [VERIFIED: https://docs.commercetools.com/api/projects/product-search, n.d., accessed 2026-11]. The older Product Projection Search is deprecated: projects created after 31 August 2026 "cannot be activated" for it, it deactivates after 30 consecutive days without calls, and it did not support standalone prices or product-level attributes [VERIFIED: https://docs.commercetools.com/api/projects/products-search, n.d., accessed 2026-11] ⚠. Search indexing limits: up to 10,000 standalone prices per product in Classic; in Modular, indexed variants and standalone prices "share a combined limit of 10,000 per Product" with variants indexed first [VERIFIED: https://docs.commercetools.com/api/limits, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- Algolia's Shopify B2B integration re-syncs catalog data "once a day", some changes "can take up to two days to reach all B2B customers", and "Product reindexing is never automatic", so price changes reach records only after a manual reindex [VERIFIED: https://www.algolia.com/doc/integration/shopify/sending-and-managing-data/b2b, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- Shopify's search filters compute values and counts "using only the first 1,000 results"; collections with more than 5,000 products show no filters; a store has at most 25 filters, each showing up to 100 values [VERIFIED: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/search-and-discovery/filters, n.d., accessed 2026-11] ⚠.
- Elasticsearch: query vectors "must have the same dimension and be created with the same model as the document vectors" [VERIFIED: https://www.elastic.co/docs/solutions/search/vector/knn, n.d., accessed 2026-11]; search-time synonyms are "recommended because you can update your synonym sets without reindexing" while index-time synonyms require reindexing [VERIFIED: https://www.elastic.co/docs/solutions/search/full-text/search-with-synonyms, n.d., accessed 2026-11].

**For newcomers.** The *search index* is a separate, denormalised copy of the catalog built for fast matching; it is not the catalog. *Indexing* is the job that copies changes into it; anything that happens between a catalog change and the next indexing run is *staleness*. A *facet* is a filter (size, colour, price band) with counts, computed from indexed attributes.

**Architecture implications** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]. The index is downstream of catalog, pricing and availability, each changing at a different pace; the design decision is which of them is *in* the index (filterable, sortable) and which is *joined at query time* (fresh but not filterable). Price and stock in the index are the two most common stale promises ("in stock" in search, sold out on the page). Index refresh needs to be event-driven from the catalog and stock systems, with a full rebuild path for mapping changes. Starting: the platform's built-in search. Growing: a hosted search service fed by an app, with daily sync and visible staleness. Omnichannel: event-driven indexing from the catalog/PIM, stock and pricing services; separate indexes per market or language. Group: a shared search platform with brand-specific relevance configuration.

### 4.2 Engines and services

- Elasticsearch: kNN search is "the primary way to query `dense_vector` fields after you store embeddings"; approximate kNN is "best for most production workloads where low latency and scale matter more than perfect recall"; exact kNN "scores every matching document, which guarantees accurate results but does not scale well for large datasets"; the `lookup` query-vector builder is "Generally available since 9.4" [VERIFIED: https://www.elastic.co/docs/solutions/search/vector/knn, n.d., accessed 2026-11]. "Hybrid search runs full-text search and vector search in one request" and reciprocal rank fusion is the recommended way to merge rankings [VERIFIED: https://www.elastic.co/docs/solutions/search/hybrid-search, n.d., accessed 2026-11]. Synonyms are managed as synonym sets through the synonyms API ("the most flexible approach"), the `synonym_graph` filter "is designed for search-time use only", and a circuit breaker for large synonym sets is GA since 9.4 [VERIFIED: https://www.elastic.co/docs/solutions/search/full-text/search-with-synonyms, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- OpenSearch: "OpenSearch vector search provides a complete vector database solution for building efficient AI applications"; an index with `index.knn: true` and a `knn_vector` field supports raw `knn` queries and `neural` queries that embed text with a registered model at query time [VERIFIED: https://docs.opensearch.org/latest/vector-search/, n.d., accessed 2026-11]. Hybrid search "was introduced in OpenSearch 2.11": a `hybrid` query wraps a keyword clause and a `neural` clause, a normalization processor "converts the scores from each query clause to a common scale and then combines them" (min-max with arithmetic mean and per-clause weights in the example), and a score-ranker processor "uses reciprocal rank fusion (RRF)" [VERIFIED: https://docs.opensearch.org/latest/vector-search/ai-search/hybrid-search/index/, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- Algolia launched NeuralSearch on 2 May 2023 as "a next-generation vector and keyword search in a single API", with "Neural Hashing" that "compresses these search vectors from 2,000 decimal long numbers into static length expressions"; Frasers Group reported in a four-week test "above ~65 percent drop in zero search results and up to 17 percent uplift in conversion rates" [VERIFIED: https://www.algolia.com/about/news/algolia-launches-ai-powered-algolia-neuralsearchtm-the-world-s-fastest-hyper-scalable-and-cost-effective-vector-and-keyword-search-api/, published 2023-05-02; vendor-reported customer results].
- Shopify's Search & Discovery app: product boosts ("A maximum of 10 search terms can be added to a product boost", not applied when the query uses search syntax), result-type selection, out-of-stock handling, and "Semantic understanding is included in online store search", using "product title, description, type, category, and tags, but not images" [VERIFIED: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/search-and-discovery/search, n.d., accessed 2026-11]. Semantic search reached the Shopify and Advanced plans on 24 June 2024: it "goes beyond keyword matching and better understands buyer's intent", for example "something to wear in the summer" instead of "shorts" [VERIFIED: https://changelog.shopify.com/posts/semantic-search-is-now-available-on-more-plans, published 2024-06-24] ⚠.
- Constructor describes a "Commerce Reasoning Engine" behind search, browse, recommendations, quizzes and an "AI Shopping Agent" that "Helps shoppers find what they want to buy using natural language"; it claims more than 400 billion requests per year, a Petco "13% lift in ecommerce conversions", and Leader placement in the Gartner Magic Quadrant for Search & Product Discovery 2026 [VERIFIED: https://constructor.com/, n.d., accessed 2026-11; vendor claims]. Bloomreach Discovery, "powered by Loomi", claims "Ecommerce Search Boosts RPV by 20% or More" and Canadian Tire conversions up "Over 20%", with Forrester Wave 2025 and Gartner 2025 placements; the page gives no methodology and does not describe its ranking or merchandising tools [VERIFIED: https://www.bloomreach.com/en/products/discovery, n.d., accessed 2026-11; marketing page].
- The research lineage of semantic product search is Amazon's KDD 2019 paper: "Pure lexical matching via an inverted index falls short" on hypernyms, synonyms, antonyms, morphological variants and spelling errors; a neural model trained on customer behaviour with a loss that separates random negatives, impressed-but-not-purchased and purchased items reported "at least 4.7% improvement in Recall@100 and 14.5% improvement in mean average precision (MAP)" over semantic baselines, plus online A/B results [VERIFIED: https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00937, published 2019-07-01].

| Elasticsearch / OpenSearch (self-run or managed) | your own index, analysers, synonyms, ranking | built by your team | kNN + hybrid (RRF) in Elastic; hybrid since OpenSearch 2.11 |
| Algolia | hosted index, API | Rules (pin, hide, boost, bury, filter) | NeuralSearch, May 2023 |

**For newcomers.** *Lexical search* matches words (with stemming, synonyms, typo tolerance). *Vector* or *semantic search* matches meaning by comparing numeric embeddings of the query and the product. *Hybrid search* runs both and merges the two ranked lists, commonly with reciprocal rank fusion, which rewards products that rank well in either.

**Architecture implications** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]. The hosted-versus-self-run decision is about who owns relevance tuning and who owns the data pipeline, not about features; all options now offer hybrid retrieval. Embeddings add a model dependency: the index and the query must use the same model, so a model change is a full reindex. Vendor conversion claims are customer anecdotes without controls and should be presented as such.

### 4.3 Relevance, merchandising rules and synonyms

- Algolia Rules "let you make precise and (if desired) temporary modifications to your search results" on top of relevance, with the caveat that "Rules are no substitute for a well-configured index"; consequences include pinning, hiding, boosting or burying categories, filtering, rewriting the query and returning custom data; a rule may have up to 25 conditions on query pattern, filters or context, and a validity period "so that promotions end automatically" [VERIFIED: https://www.algolia.com/doc/guides/managing-results/rules/rules-overview/, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- Shopify filters: standard filters "for Availability, Category, Price, Product type, Tags, and Vendor are available to all stores"; option and metafield filters are built from product data; values within one filter are "or", across filters "and"; "The price filter doesn't display for currencies other than your shop's default currency" [VERIFIED: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-store/search-and-discovery/filters, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- Elasticsearch synonym formats: explicit rules use `=>` for one-way replacements and "Equivalent mappings use commas to group interchangeable terms" [VERIFIED: https://www.elastic.co/docs/solutions/search/full-text/search-with-synonyms, n.d., accessed 2026-11].

**For newcomers.** *Relevance* is the engine's ordering of results for a query. A *merchandising rule* is a human override (pin, boost, bury, hide, redirect) for a query or category, usually time-boxed. A *synonym set* tells the engine that "jacket" and "coat" (or "waterproof" and "rain") should match each other.

**Architecture implications** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]. Merchandising rules are data with validity windows and owners, like promotions; they need the same calendar, the same review, and a count (Algolia's per-plan rule limits, Shopify's 25 filters) that forces pruning. Facets depend on attribute completeness (1.3): a "waterproof" facet with half the jackets unfilled hides the other half. Synonyms are language- and market-specific, so one set per language index.

### 4.4 Usability evidence and metrics

- Baymard (article published 12 September 2024, updated 29 April 2026; "10,000+ usability scores" across five search topics, "170+ benchmarked sites and apps"): "56% of sites fail to adequately support users' search needs"; "46% of desktop, 58% of mobile, and 64% of app ecommerce sites have an overall 'mediocre or worse' performance"; roughly half of test participants prefer search to navigation; failure rates by query type: exact 12 %, product type 20 %, symptom 37 %, feature 39 %, use case 43 %, compatibility 44 %, abbreviation and symbol 54 %, non-product 66 % [VERIFIED: https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-search-query-types, published 2024-09-12, updated 2026-04-29]. Its search collection adds: autocomplete offered by 80 % of sites but "only 19% achieve the highest performance"; 69 % give no relevant suggestions for closely misspelled queries; 54 % do not update thumbnails to the variant searched for; site-wide Baymard cites "344 benchmarked top sites" and "700+ UX guidelines" [VERIFIED: https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-search-report-and-benchmark, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- **Methodology caveat** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]: Baymard's numbers are expert heuristic reviews of large, mostly US and UK retailers against its own guidelines, informed by moderated usability tests; they measure how many sites *break a guideline*, not conversion impact, and are not a random sample of European mid-market shops. Use them to name failure modes, not to size business cases.
- Metrics as defined by Algolia's analytics: "The No Results Rate chart shows the percentage of searches with no results"; click-through rate is "the percentage of tracked searches with at least one click event"; conversion rate "the percentage of tracked searches with at least one conversion event"; click position averages the rank of clicked results and "Smaller values are better"; keystrokes within 30 seconds aggregate into one search [VERIFIED: https://www.algolia.com/doc/guides/search-analytics/concepts/metrics/, n.d., accessed 2026-11].

**For newcomers.** *Zero-results rate* is the share of searches that return nothing; it is the first metric to watch because every such search is a shopper the catalog could not answer. *Search conversion* is the share of searches followed by a purchase. *Click position* says whether the right product is near the top.

**Architecture implications** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]. Zero-result queries are catalog-quality signals as much as search-engine signals (missing synonyms, missing attributes, products not indexed); the loop from search analytics back to PIM and merchandising is the organisational design, not a feature. Track metrics per language and per channel (app, mobile web, desktop differ in Baymard's data).

### 4.5 B2B search with contract prices and assortments

- Algolia's guidance: with fewer than 100 pricing levels per product, store "one price per tier or customer group" as a nested attribute and sort by segment price through virtual replicas (20 per index by default); with more levels, keep per-customer prices out of the index and merge them at query time, accepting that "the per-customer prices aren't in the Algolia index, you can't use them for sorting or filtering"; hybrid designs index segment prices and fetch customer prices; "Having many attributes increases your index size and can slow down your search" [VERIFIED: https://www.algolia.com/doc/guides/solutions/ecommerce/b2b-catalog-management/tutorials/personalized-pricing, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- Algolia's Shopify B2B integration stores per-company-location prices in a `_bp` attribute keyed by location id; "logged-in B2B customers can only retrieve the `_bp` entry for their own company location" and "retail visitors can't retrieve B2B data at all"; a hidden `clids` attribute lists which locations may see each product (assortment); "stores with many locations can exceed the Algolia record size limit", so about 50 company locations is the recommended ceiling; records carry only the highest volume-pricing tier [VERIFIED: https://www.algolia.com/doc/integration/shopify/sending-and-managing-data/b2b, n.d., accessed 2026-11] ⚠.
- commercetools exposes customer-group and channel price scopes as search filters (4.1) [VERIFIED: https://docs.commercetools.com/api/projects/product-search, n.d., accessed 2026-11].

**For newcomers.** An *assortment* is the subset of the catalog a wholesale account is allowed to see and buy. A *contract price* is that account's negotiated price. B2B search must answer "what can *this* buyer see, at *their* price" for every result.

**Architecture implications** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]. Three designs recur: price-per-segment in the index (filterable, bounded by segment count), price-per-account joined at query time (unbounded, not filterable), or both. Assortment visibility must be enforced in the search service (token-scoped filters), not in the storefront, because search results are an information leak. The same choice applies to availability per warehouse or store. Growing: segment prices in the index. Omnichannel: hybrid, with the pricing service as the authority at query time and in the cart.
