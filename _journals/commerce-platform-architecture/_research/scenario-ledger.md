# Kestrel & Pine — Canonical Scenario Ledger

Kestrel & Pine is **fictional**. Every figure, system, name and event below is invented for teaching. Chapters must agree with this ledger. If a chapter needs a new fact, append it under "Additions by chapter" rather than inventing a conflicting one. Never present Kestrel & Pine as a real company, and never give it real product or vendor names: its systems are named by role ("the platform", "the ERP", "the WMS"), which also keeps the book platform-neutral.

Kestrel & Pine is a composite of patterns common among growing omnichannel brands. It is **not** a disguised version of any real brand or retailer.

## The company

- **Kestrel & Pine B.V.** — an outdoor apparel and equipment brand headquartered in **Arnhem, the Netherlands**. Founded **2016** by **Mara Lindqvist** and **Teun Baas**. Privately held; the founders hold a majority, a Dutch growth investor (**Veluwe Capital**, fictional) holds a minority stake since **2022**.
- Sells its own brand (about **75%** of revenue: jackets, fleeces, trail trousers, merino base layers, socks, packs) and selected third-party equipment brands (about **25%**: footwear, tents, stoves, accessories).
- **2026 figures (fiscal year = calendar year):** revenue **€48.0 million**; gross margin **54%**; EBITDA about **€3.4 million**; **310 employees** (stores 96, warehouse 88, head office 126), of whom **14** work in technology.
- Markets: the Netherlands (home), Belgium (since 2020), Germany (since 2022). Currency: euro throughout.

## Warehouse and logistics

- **Duiven warehouse** (near Arnhem), **8,500 m²**, opened **2023**, run on a standalone **cloud WMS** since 2023. Before 2023, a 3PL in Tiel fulfilled all online orders.
- 2026 outbound: **336,000 parcels** (D2C and marketplace orders fulfilled by Kestrel & Pine) and **9,400 wholesale shipments** (cartons and pallets); **1.9 million units** shipped.
- Carriers: **PostNL** (NL), **bpost** (BE), **DHL** (DE), **DPD** (backup, all countries), booked through a **multi-carrier shipping service** integrated in the WMS flow. Cut-off for next-day NL delivery: **22:00**.
- About **40%** of marketplace units for Germany are fulfilled by the marketplace's own fulfillment program from stock Kestrel & Pine sends there in advance; that stock is a **location outside Kestrel & Pine's control**.
- Returns: **72,000 return parcels** in 2026; item return rate **24%** and order return rate **19%** on D2C; **61%** of returned items resold as new; **28%** of returns are exchanges. A **returns portal** (SaaS, since 2025) issues labels and triggers refunds through the platform's API.

## Peak (Black Friday week 2026, 23–29 November)

- **31,000 online D2C orders** in the week (**10.6%** of the year's D2C orders); peak hour Friday 27 November **20:00–21:00**: **2,300 orders** (**38 per minute**); storefront traffic in that hour about **41×** an average hour.
- The platform held. The **stock sync chain** did not: WMS → ERP every 15 minutes, ERP → platform hourly, platform → marketplace connector hourly, so a sale took up to **two hours** to reduce the stock shown elsewhere; on Friday evening the WMS connector queue backed up for **four hours** (orders reached the warehouse late).

## The 2027 situation — problems Kestrel & Pine starts with

1. **The Black Friday Oversell (Friday 27 November 2026).** The Ridgewalker jacket sold out in the warehouse at 19:40; the storefronts and marketplaces kept selling it for up to two hours. **1,340 units** oversold; **1,120 orders** canceled or delayed; cost about **€96,000** including goodwill vouchers and marketplace penalties (illustrative). Pattern: The Phantom Stock, The First Snowfall.
2. **The Three-Status Order (December 2026).** Order **#1052877** showed *Delivered* in the platform, *In picking* in the WMS and *Not invoiced* in the ERP for six days; **11 support tickets** from one customer. Pattern: Schrödinger's Order.
3. **The Double Refund (January 2027).** Over three weeks, **212 returns** were refunded twice: once by the returns portal through the platform's API and once manually by support in the PSP console; **€19,400** refunded twice, most of it recovered. Pattern: The Boomerang, The Message in a Bottle.
4. **The Price Drift (October 2026).** The German storefront sold the Ridgewalker jacket at **€179** for **nine days** while the marketplace listing and the stores charged **€199**; a price-list sync had failed silently. **410 orders**; margin lost about **€8,200**; a price-parity warning from a marketplace. Pattern: The Price Nobody Can Explain.
5. **The ASN Chargeback (Q3 2026).** Bergzicht Outdoor deducted **€14,200** for despatch advices (DESADV) whose SSCC pallet labels did not match what arrived. Pattern: The Pulled Rug (contract), The Lynchpin (one EDI consultant).
6. **The Store Stock Mirage (December 2026).** Click-and-collect at Utrecht promised **37** items that the store had already sold, because store stock reached the ERP only nightly. Pattern: The Phantom Stock.
7. **The App That Wrote Stock (March 2026).** A pre-order app adjusted inventory in the platform while the WMS feed also wrote it; two weeks of phantom reservations on **600 variants** (illustrative). Pattern: The Secret Agent (unowned writer), The Spaghetti Junction.
8. **The Card-Testing Night (August 2026).** **48,000** bot attempts with stolen card numbers in one night against the checkout; **€2,900** in PSP fees for declined authorizations; the PSP paused the account for 40 minutes. Pattern: a Trust failure (Part VIII).
9. **The Familiar Stranger.** Eva Janssen (below) exists as an online account, a store customer in the POS loyalty list, a newsletter subscriber and a returns-portal contact, with no shared identifier.
10. **The Deprecated Endpoint (February 2027).** The marketplace connector retired an API version on a published date; the agency script that read it had not been updated; stock to bol was stale for **two days**. Pattern: The Pulled Rug.

## The Waypoint program (2027)

Launched **March 2027** by the CEO on the Head of Engineering's proposal. Budget **€1.1 million** over 2027–2028 on top of the running technology spend. Canonical decisions (chapters elaborate; do not contradict):

- **W1 — Stock ownership.** The WMS is the **system of record for physical stock** at Duiven; the POS is the system of record for store stock; the marketplace's fulfillment program stock is a tracked external location. An **availability service** (bought as a module of the OMS below, not built) computes available-to-promise per channel from stock events; the hourly sync chain is retired by **Q4 2027**.
- **W2 — One order record.** An **OMS** (order management system, bought) becomes the order hub by **Q4 2027**: every channel's order (platform, marketplaces, EDI, manual wholesale, later stores) is created there, with one state model and one order number family. The platform keeps the checkout and the storefront order history.
- **W3 — Product information.** A **PIM** is introduced in **Q3 2027** as the system of record for product content and attributes; the ERP keeps costs and purchasing; the platform and the connector become consumers.
- **W4 — Stay and surround.** Kestrel & Pine **stays on its SaaS platform**; no replatforming. Capabilities the platform does not own well (OMS, PIM, availability) are added beside it; the platform's own POS is evaluated in **2028**. The decision is revisited at a **2029 review**.
- **W5 — Integration standards.** Every integration has a named owner, a contract (fields, direction, frequency, system of record), idempotent handling of repeated messages, and a reconciliation job; the integration inventory is kept current. Stock, orders and prices move by **events**; products by scheduled publication; finance by **daily batch**.
- **W6 — EDI compliance.** SSCC labels generated by the WMS at pack time; DESADV sent from the WMS confirmation, not from the ERP; one EDI partner card per trading partner.
- **W7 — One definition of an order.** The data warehouse adopts one metric definition for order, revenue and return, owned by the Data Lead.
- **W8 — Agent readiness, not agents.** Accurate product data (via the PIM), availability and delivery promise through APIs, and order status by API are prerequisites; no shopper-facing AI agent is built in 2027.

## Scale stages (used in every "Across Scale Stages" section)

| Stage | Typical size | Shape | Kestrel & Pine |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1 — Starting** | under about €2M revenue | One channel, a hosted platform, founder-run operations, a 3PL or a back room | 2016–2018 |
| **2 — Growing** | about €2M–€20M | Two or three channels, first store or wholesale, first integrations (ERP or accounting, 3PL, marketplace), a small team and an agency | 2019–2022 |
| **3 — Omnichannel** | about €20M–€200M | Stores, wholesale, marketplaces and several countries; own warehouse; a dozen or more systems; the joins become the problem; OMS, PIM and integration standards become necessary | 2023– (now) |
| **4 — Group** | over about €200M, or several brands or business units | Multiple brands, countries or business units; platform teams; shared services vs autonomy; acquisitions; vendor-scale concerns (tenancy, extensibility) | not yet |

The revenue bands are illustrative markers, not thresholds; the shape of the business decides the stage.
