# Research brief: Marketplaces and channel integration, physical stores and point of sale

Prepared 7 November 2026 for chapter writers of *ORDERED: Architecting Commerce Platforms*. The brief feeds chapter 20 (marketplaces and channel integration), chapter 17 (stores and omnichannel) and chapter 7 (pricing: price parity on marketplaces). It is platform-neutral: Amazon, bol, Zalando, Kaufland, Otto, ChannelEngine, EffectConnect, Shopify, Square and the others appear because they publish dated, public material, not because the book recommends them.

## How to read this brief

Every substantive claim carries one tag:

- **[VERIFIED: url, published YYYY-MM(-DD)]**: the page was fetched in this session (7 November 2026) and supports the claim. Paraphrases stay close to the source; quoted text is quoted. The date is the source's publication date. Undated product and help pages are marked "n.d., accessed 2026-11". Dutch, German, Italian and Flemish official pages were read in the original and are quoted in English translation, marked "(translated)".
- **[WIDELY REPORTED: url, YYYY]**: from a reputable secondary source (trade press, competition press, an encyclopaedia, a vendor release relayed by press) or from a search-engine summary of a primary page that could not be fetched directly.
- **[ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]**: industry knowledge or the analyst's inference. Every statement about how a marketplace, connector or POS works *internally* falls here unless the vendor has described it.

**Evidence age matters.** Marketplace fee tables, API versions, performance thresholds and programme names change every year; Amazon publishes fee changes with effective dates, bol retires API versions on a 12-month schedule, and Zalando is closing a programme that trade press still describes as current. ## PART 1: Selling on marketplaces

### 1.1 Two selling models: seller-fulfilled and marketplace-fulfilled

Every large marketplace offers the same two logistics models under its own names:

| Model | Who holds the stock | Who ships | Who handles returns | Amazon name | bol name | Zalando name |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Seller-fulfilled | Seller | Seller (own carrier contracts or the marketplace's labels) | Seller, under marketplace rules | FBM / MFN ("Merchant Fulfilled Network") | Own shipping, or "Shipping via bol" (bol labels, seller stock) | "Own Logistics" (partner fulfilment) |
| Marketplace-fulfilled | Marketplace warehouse, stock sent in advance | Marketplace | Marketplace | FBA | "Logistics via bol" (LvB) | ZFS (Zalando Fulfilment Solutions); ZEOS Fulfilment for multi-channel |

- Zalando's partner-university page describes the options: under ZFS "you will retain **full ownership and control** over your stock" after shipping it to a Zalando warehouse, and "We manage your returns"; under Own Logistics the partner must "manage your stock and shipments from your warehouse", include a return note and a pre-paid return label in each parcel, and have a valid EU return address; ZEOS Fulfilment serves "one stock pool" across channels including the partner's own website, with returns "processed in our nearest return centre" and resold [VERIFIED: https://partner.zalando.com/university/article/zalando-logistics-solutions-overview, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- bol describes Logistics via bol as "We handle storage, shipping, and returns" [VERIFIED: https://partnerplatform.bol.com/en/l2op/logistics-via-bol, n.d., accessed 2026-11]. Its conditions page says base rates "include pick and pack, shipping, customer service, and returns"; stock is "counted daily and charged"; a +40% peak surcharge on stock costs applies in November and December; a +100% long-term-storage surcharge applies to stock held over 180 days (clothing and shoes exempt); returning or destroying unsellable stock costs €0.45 per item excl. VAT [VERIFIED: https://partnerplatform.bol.com/en/idp/conditions-and-rates-lvb, n.d., accessed 2026-11] ⚠.
- Amazon's performance policy distinguishes the models operationally: the cancellation rate, late shipment rate, valid tracking rate and on-time delivery rate "only apply to seller-fulfilled orders" [VERIFIED: https://m.media-amazon.com/images/G/03/B2B/MFN/Changes_to_Order_Performance_Program_Policy_-_DE.pdf, PDF metadata dated 2026-05-21].

**For newcomers.** *Seller-fulfilled* means the merchant ships marketplace orders from its own stock and carries the delivery promise. *Marketplace-fulfilled* (FBA, LvB, ZFS) means the merchant sends stock to the marketplace's warehouse in advance; that stock becomes a location the merchant does not control. *Commission* (referral fee) is the percentage of the sale price the marketplace keeps; fulfilment fees are charged on top in the marketplace-fulfilled model.

**Architecture implications** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]. Marketplace-fulfilled stock must be modelled as an *external location* with its own quantity feed (Amazon's `FBA_INVENTORY_AVAILABILITY_CHANGES` notification exists for exactly this), not as a copy of warehouse stock. Seller-fulfilled orders must enter the merchant's order record and the warehouse flow with the marketplace's deadline attached, because the performance metrics below are computed from ship confirmations and tracking that the merchant must send back. Starting-stage merchants usually pick one model per marketplace; Omnichannel-stage merchants run both and need the OMS to route by channel and location.

### 1.2 Amazon Selling Partner API (SP-API)

The SP-API is the only programmatic interface to Amazon's seller systems since the retirement of the older MWS.

- **MWS retirement.** Amazon's SP-API changelog says MWS "will no longer be available" from **1 April 2024** (an earlier date of 31 December 2023 was pushed back) and that "All private developers using any MWS API sections for selling on Amazon must fully migrate to Selling Partner API (SP-API) by March 31, 2024"; public applications had to migrate Orders, Reports and MFN sections by 31 July 2022, Products by 30 September 2022 and all other sections by 31 December 2022 [WIDELY REPORTED: https://developer-docs.amazon.com/sp-api/changelog/important-amazon-marketplace-web-service-mws-removal-dates and https://developer-docs.amazon.com/sp-api/changelog/updates-to-mws-migration-dates, 2023–2024; the pages now redirect to the `developer-docs.amazon` domain and returned 404 in this session].
- **Documentation site.** The SP-API documentation moved to `https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/` (the `.amazon.com` URLs redirect with HTTP 301). The landing page points to API references, models ("Index of all SP-API models with links to API references and GitHub Swagger models"), a deprecation schedule and an "API Health Dashboard"; page metadata shows an update on 2026-09-09 [VERIFIED: https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/welcome, updated 2026-09-09] ⚠.
- **Rate limiting.** SP-API uses "the token bucket algorithm": each operation has a usage plan with a *rate* (tokens added per second) and a *burst* (bucket size); a throttled request returns HTTP 429 and "can be retried"; most limits apply per selling-partner-and-application pair; the `x-amzn-RateLimit-Limit` header shows the limit "when available" and only on 20x, 400 and 404 responses; *dynamic* usage plans "adjust to each selling partner based on business metrics" and "Rates do not dynamically increase because an application makes API requests more frequently". Amazon's guidance: "Don't hardcode timers" and "Respond to events rather than polling on a loop, for example, update prices on price notifications" [VERIFIED: https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/usage-plans-and-rate-limits, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- **Orders API limits.** Orders API v0: `getOrders` rate **0.0167 requests/second** (one call per minute) with burst 20; `getOrder`, `getOrderItems`, `getOrderAddress`, `getOrderBuyerInfo` 0.5/s with burst 30; `confirmShipment` 2/s burst 10; `updateShipmentStatus` 5/s burst 15. The newer Orders API v2026-01-01 lists `searchOrders` at 0.0056/s (about one call every three minutes) with burst 20 and `getOrder` at 0.5/s burst 30 [VERIFIED: https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/orders-api-rate-limits, n.d., accessed 2026-11] ⚠.
- **Orders API versions.** "The Orders API v0 is deprecated"; the current version is v2026-01-01 (sellers only) with `searchOrders` returning "orders created or updated during the time frame or other filtering criteria"; v0 operations include `getOrders`, `getOrder`, `getOrderItems`, `getOrderAddress`, `getOrderBuyerInfo`, `confirmShipment`, `updateShipmentStatus`; orders older than two years are excluded from responses [VERIFIED: https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/orders-api, n.d., accessed 2026-11] ⚠.
- **Personal data needs a separate token.** "Restricted operations return customers' Personally Identifiable Information (PII)"; calling them needs a Restricted Data Token from `createRestrictedDataToken` (Tokens API v2021-03-01). Restricted operations include `getOrderBuyerInfo`, `getOrderAddress`, `getOrderItemsBuyerInfo` and `getReportDocument` for PII-bearing reports [VERIFIED: https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/tokens-api-use-case-guide, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- **Feeds.** The Feeds API (v2021-06-30) "lets you upload data to Amazon to manage your selling business" with operations `createFeedDocument`, `createFeed`, `getFeed`, `getFeedDocument`, `getFeeds`, `cancelFeed`; "Feeds API support for XML and flat file product listings feeds is deprecated" in favour of JSON; feed types include `JSON_LISTINGS_FEED`, `POST_ORDER_ACKNOWLEDGEMENT_DATA` and `POST_ORDER_FULFILLMENT_DATA`; feed result documents are retained for 28 days [VERIFIED: https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/feeds-api and https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/feed-type-values, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- **Listings.** The Listings Items API (v2021-08-01) gives "programmatic access to selling partner listings on Amazon" and must be used with the Product Type Definitions API, which supplies the JSON schema per product type [VERIFIED: https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/listings-items-api-v2021-08-01-reference, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- **Notifications instead of polling.** "Instead of polling for information, your application can receive information directly from Amazon"; Amazon also recommends that you "have a means to retrieve needed information" in case of outages or delayed delivery; destinations are Amazon SQS or Amazon EventBridge; subscriptions can carry CEL filter expressions [VERIFIED: https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/notifications-api, n.d., accessed 2026-11]. Notification types include `ORDER_CHANGE` (order status changes and buyer-requested cancellations), `ANY_OFFER_CHANGED` (top offers, external prices, Buy Box winners), `PRICING_HEALTH` (an offer "ineligible for the Featured Offer (Buy Box) because its price is uncompetitive"), `LISTINGS_ITEM_STATUS_CHANGE`, `LISTINGS_ITEM_ISSUES_CHANGE`, `LISTINGS_ITEM_MFN_QUANTITY_CHANGE`, `FBA_INVENTORY_AVAILABILITY_CHANGES`, `FEED_PROCESSING_FINISHED`, `REPORT_PROCESSING_FINISHED`, `ACCOUNT_STATUS_CHANGED` (normal, at-risk, deactivated) [VERIFIED: https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/notification-type-values, n.d., accessed 2026-11].

**For newcomers.** An *API usage plan* is the marketplace's budget for how often you may call each operation; exceeding it returns HTTP 429 and your integration must back off. A *feed* is an asynchronous bulk upload (listings, prices, stock, shipment confirmations) whose result you fetch later. A *notification* is a push message the marketplace sends to a queue you own when something changes, so you do not poll.

**Architecture implications** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]. The Orders API's one-call-per-minute budget for `getOrders` is the clearest evidence in this brief that a marketplace integration cannot be designed as a tight polling loop: a Growing-stage merchant with one seller account is fine, but a connector serving thousands of sellers must either subscribe to `ORDER_CHANGE` or schedule polls per account. The split between order data and PII (the RDT) means the merchant's order record should store the marketplace order id and only the PII it needs for fulfilment, with retention aligned to Amazon's data-protection policy (not fetched in this session). Asynchronous feeds mean price and stock updates are *eventually* applied; the connector must track feed status and reconcile.

### 1.3 Amazon seller performance metrics and thresholds

Amazon's Order Performance policy (the European seller-facing PDF) states the thresholds and windows; the figures match Amazon's own seller blog.

| Metric | Definition (quoted) | Threshold | Window | Applies to |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Order Defect Rate (ODR) | "all orders with one or more defects … represented as a percentage of total orders"; defects are negative feedback, an A-to-z Guarantee claim, a credit card chargeback | "under 1%"; "above 1% may result in loss or restriction of selling privileges" | 60 days | all orders |
| Cancellation Rate (pre-fulfilment) | "all seller-canceled orders … as a percentage of total orders"; customer-initiated cancellations excluded | "under 2.5%" | 7 days | seller-fulfilled |
| Late Shipment Rate | orders "with a shipping confirmation that is completed after the expected ship date" | "under 4%" | 10 or 30 days | seller-fulfilled |
| Valid Tracking Rate | shipments with a valid tracking number | "greater than 95%"; below 95% in a category "may result in restrictions on your ability to sell non-FBA items within that category" | 30 days | seller-fulfilled |
| On-Time Delivery Rate | tracked units delivered on or before the seller-promised date, "before any promise extensions" | "90% or higher" expected on Amazon.de; consistently below 80% for 30 days after a warning may lead to deactivation of listings or FBM restrictions; sellers with fewer than 20 shipped units in the window are exempt | rolling 14 days (units promised in the last 21 days minus the most recent 7) | seller-fulfilled |

[VERIFIED: https://m.media-amazon.com/images/G/03/B2B/MFN/Changes_to_Order_Performance_Program_Policy_-_DE.pdf, PDF dated 2026-05-21] ⚠. Amazon's seller blog states the same three headline numbers: "keep your ODR under 1%", LSR "ideally under 4%" for Fulfilled by Merchant offers, and "Keep your cancellation rate below 2.5%" [VERIFIED: https://sell.amazon.com/blog/selling-policies, published 2024-10-16]. Third-party guides agree and add that the 90% OTDR minimum for Amazon.com took effect 25 September 2024 [WIDELY REPORTED: https://www.ecomengine.com/blog/amazon-seller-performance-metrics, updated 2025-06-16].

**For newcomers.** *ODR* is the share of orders that went wrong from the customer's point of view. *Pre-fulfilment cancellation* is the seller cancelling an order it could not fulfil (an oversell shows up here). *Late shipment* is confirming shipment after the promised ship date, which is why the ship confirmation must flow back to the marketplace on time.

**Architecture implications** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]. These metrics turn integration latency into a commercial risk: a stock sync that lags by an hour produces oversells that become seller cancellations (2.5% over seven days is a small number for a merchant with a few hundred marketplace orders a week), and a warehouse connector that confirms shipments a day late produces late-shipment rate breaches. The architecture consequence is that channel allocation and ship confirmation should be event-driven from the system of record for stock and from the WMS, not batch jobs. This is the mechanism behind the ledger's Black Friday Oversell "marketplace penalties".

### 1.4 Amazon fees (US and UK schedules as dated examples)

- US: Individual plan $0.99 per item sold; Professional "$39.99/month + selling fees"; referral fees are "a percentage of the total price or a minimum amount, whichever is greater", mostly 8–15% with a $0.30 minimum; Clothing and Accessories 5% up to $15, 10% from $15 to $20, 17% above $20; Media 15% plus a $1.80 closing fee [VERIFIED: https://sell.amazon.com/pricing, n.d., accessed 2026-11] ⚠.
- UK: Individual £0.75 per item; Professional £25 per month; Clothing and Accessories 5% up to £15, 10% from £15 to £20, 15% above £20 (cut from 8%/15% effective 15 December 2025), minimum £0.25; most referral fees 8–15%; FBA storage £0.62–£0.76 per cubic foot per month January–September rising to £0.82–£1.51 October–December for standard-size goods; parcel fulfilment fees fell by an average £0.26 on 15 December 2025; a fuel and logistics surcharge rises from 1.5% to 2% on 2 November 2026 [VERIFIED: https://sell.amazon.co.uk/pricing, n.d., accessed 2026-11] ⚠.

**Architecture implications** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]. Fees vary by category, price band and fulfilment model and change on published dates; margin reporting per channel needs the fee schedule as dated reference data, not a constant in a spreadsheet. This belongs in the analytics layer (chapter 28), not in the connector.

### 1.5 bol (Netherlands and Belgium)

bol describes itself as "the number 1 marketplace in the Netherlands and Belgium" and runs a partner platform with policy pages on service standards, the quality score, the strike process, the buy box and "Growth Reward" [VERIFIED: https://partnerplatform.bol.com/en/, n.d., accessed 2026-11].

**API.**
- The bol Partner API (Retailer API **v10** current; Offers API **v11** current, v10 deprecated; Retailer information API v11 in beta) covers orders and shipments, returns, shipping labels, inventory and replenishments (Logistics via bol), invoices, commissions, product content, promotions, offers, insights and a shared Process Status API; "All integrators authenticate with OAuth2 against login.bol.com"; "most calls are asynchronous; confirm the result afterwards"; a demo environment has "hardcoded examples for every endpoint, with the same models and validation as production"; bol "does not offer a plug-and-play solution" but names third-party integration partners [VERIFIED: https://api.bol.com/retailer/public/Retailer-API/, n.d., accessed 2026-11] ⚠.
- Lifecycle: semantic versioning; when a new major version is released the previous one is marked deprecated and "scheduled for removal in 12 months"; deprecated versions "will only receive maintenance for critical issues"; releases are "dynamically adapted" rather than on a fixed cadence [VERIFIED: https://api.bol.com/retailer/public/Retailer-API/api-lifecycle.html, n.d., accessed 2026-11]. Release planning adds that major versions are announced at least 14 days ahead and that deprecated resources have a minimum support period of 12 months (3 months for the Advertising API); v10 planned GA 2023-10-01 [VERIFIED: https://api.bol.com/retailer/public/Retailer-API/release-planning.html, n.d., accessed 2026-11]. The partner platform news feed announced on 3 November 2026 that "Offers API v10 stops on 1 February 2027" [VERIFIED: https://partnerplatform.bol.com/en/, published 2026-11-03] ⚠.
- Rate limiting: bol "makes active use of rate limiting", returns 429 when a limit is hit, includes remaining-budget and replenishment information in every response (header capitalisation varies by endpoint), publishes active limits at `https://api.bol.com/retailer/public/ratelimits`, and advises sending "only changes that need synchronizing" and "events as they happen instead of large batches" [VERIFIED: https://api.bol.com/retailer/public/Retailer-API/ratelimits.html, n.d., accessed 2026-11].

**Commission.** "You only pay when you sell something, which is called a commission": a fixed amount per item plus a percentage of the selling price incl. VAT, both by category; example for Kitchen Merchandise: variable 5%–12.5%, fixed €0.05–€0.95; a pasta machine sold at €206.50 carries 12.4% (€25.60) plus €0.95 fixed, €26.55 excl. VAT [VERIFIED: https://partnerplatform.bol.com/en/idp/commission, n.d., accessed 2026-11] ⚠.

**Service standards and strikes.** Standards listed: items on time, cancellations, track & trace, returns, customer questions, phone availability, review score, response time, NPS after customer contact [VERIFIED: https://partnerplatform.bol.com/en/idp/service-standards, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- *Items on time*: minimum 90%; target 93% "Delivered on time" for own shipping and 95% "On time handover" for Shipping via bol (handover measured at the carrier's scan, before the carrier's collection time); from 27 July 2026 the two are assessed separately; "The customer's order time is the determining factor" for the promise [VERIFIED: https://partnerplatform.bol.com/en/idp/items-on-time, n.d., accessed 2026-11] ⚠.
- *Cancellations*: "you may not cancel more than 2% (from 3 or more items cancelled per week)"; counted are seller-initiated cancellations, customer cancellations after the promised delivery date, and automatic cancellation "on the third day after the promised delivery date" when an order is not confirmed in time [VERIFIED: https://partnerplatform.bol.com/en/idp/cancellations, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- *Customer questions*: a per-seller "dynamic standard" by category (examples 3%, 5%, 6% of orders) rather than a fixed limit [VERIFIED: https://partnerplatform.bol.com/en/idp/customer-questions, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- *Strikes*: a strike is "a warning" for missing Items on time, Cancellations or Response time in a week; strikes count over the last 22 weeks; 1–4 strikes: an e-mail; 5: the shop is temporarily closed pending an improvement plan; 7: the account is closed for at least one year. A strike expires early after two consecutive weeks with at least 98.5% delivered on time, no more than 0.5% cancellations and 90% of questions answered within 24 hours [VERIFIED: https://partnerplatform.bol.com/en/idp/strike-process, n.d., accessed 2026-11] ⚠.

**For newcomers.** A *buy box* is the single "add to basket" button on a product page that many sellers compete for; performance and price decide who wins it. A *strike* is bol's formal warning for a week below standard. A *quality score* is bol's composite seller rating.

**Architecture implications** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]. bol's rules make two integration behaviours mandatory rather than nice-to-have: confirm or cancel orders fast (auto-cancellation on day three after the promised delivery date counts against the seller) and send track-and-trace on handover. The 12-month deprecation schedule is the concrete form of the ledger's "Deprecated Endpoint" incident: a connector that nobody owns will break on a published date. Growing-stage merchants should buy the connector; Omnichannel-stage merchants should still own the deprecation calendar.

### 1.6 Zalando: Partner Program, Connected Retail and ZEOS

- **Partner Program share (evidence age).** In 2021 "the Partner Program and Connected Retail share now accounts for 30% of Zalando's Fashion Store GMV", up from 24%, with "more than 5,800 brand partners", "almost 7,000 stores" and GMV of €14.3 billion [VERIFIED: https://corporate.zalando.com/en/node/9354, published 2022-03-01] **(evidence age: 2021 data)**. The 2025 annual report reports partner-business GMV growth of 7.5% in 2025 against group GMV of €17,560.2 million, without a share figure in the excerpts seen [WIDELY REPORTED: https://corporate.zalando.com/sites/default/files/media-download/zalando-se_annual-report-2025_eng_261003_s.pdf, 2026; the PDF exceeded the fetch limit] ⚠.
- **Integration surface.** The partner site lists three models (Wholesale, Partner Program, Connected Retail); onboarding runs through **zDirect**, where partners "provide the legal details needed to start selling on Zalando" and set contact, carrier and VAT information per market; logistics options listed are Own logistics, ZEOS, ZFS, ZRS (returns) and ZSS (shipping). No commission figures are published on the page [VERIFIED: https://partner.zalando.com/, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- **ZEOS.** Announced 17 October 2023 as "Zalando E-commerce Operating System": a multi-channel fulfilment solution piloted since October 2022, "one stock pool, one carrier integration and one user interface", 23 European markets, 12 warehouses, over 40 carriers, about 30 brands at launch, with Zalando Logistics Solutions having handled "almost 380 million partner items" since 2017 for over 1,000 partners [VERIFIED: https://corporate.zalando.com/en/node/10491, published 2023-10-17]. By December 2025 ZEOS was "available in 24 markets", served "18 sales channels" including other marketplaces and brands' own webshops, had a ZEOS Shopify application and "over 60" live merchants [VERIFIED: https://corporate.zalando.com/en/node/10922, published 2025-12-10] ⚠.
- **Connected Retail is ending.** Under the programme, "stationary retailers sold branch stock directly through the platform and shipped orders from their stores"; a Zalando spokesperson confirmed the programme ends by the end of June 2027, to "bundle all partners in one central model" under its "platform and efficiency strategy" (translated) [VERIFIED: https://www.textilwirtschaft.de/tech/zalando-stellt-connected-retail-ein-158921, published 2026-04-24] ⚠. Trade press reports it is being folded into zDirect with the old tool withdrawn in June 2027; in 2021 the network had "more than 3,400 active stores in nine European markets" (translated) and later over 4,000 [WIDELY REPORTED: https://www.textilwirtschaft.de/tech/zalando-stellt-connected-retail-ein-158921 and https://fashionunited.de/nachrichten/einzelhandel/zalando-baut-connected-retail-aus-c-a-als-partner-expansion-nach-oesterreich/2021021539302, 2026 and 2021].

**For newcomers.** *Connected Retail* was ship-from-store on someone else's marketplace: a physical shop's stock was listed on Zalando and the shop shipped the parcel. *ZEOS* is a marketplace operator selling its logistics and software to brands as a service, including for orders that never touch Zalando.

**Architecture implications** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]. Zalando's trajectory shows the marketplace moving from channel to infrastructure: the same warehouses now fulfil the brand's own webshop. For a merchant that means a third kind of stock location (marketplace-held but multi-channel) and a dependency on the marketplace's order and stock APIs for its *own* storefront. Connected Retail's closure is also a reminder that marketplace programmes retire; store-stock exposure to a marketplace should be built on the merchant's own store-stock feed (Part 5), so that a programme change is a connector change, not a re-architecture.

### 2.2 Order import, acknowledgement and status back-flow

The order lifecycle on every marketplace is the same shape, and the connector's job is to keep two order records in step:

1. The marketplace creates the order and exposes it (Amazon `ORDER_CHANGE` notification or `getOrders`; bol order endpoints; ChannelEngine `GET /v2/orders/new`).
2. The merchant acknowledges, binding its own order number to the marketplace's (ChannelEngine: "links the ChannelEngine order to your internal system"; Amazon `POST_ORDER_ACKNOWLEDGEMENT_DATA` feed). bol auto-cancels unconfirmed orders on the third day after the promised delivery date (section 1.5).
3. The merchant confirms shipment with carrier and tracking (Amazon `confirmShipment` or `POST_ORDER_FULFILLMENT_DATA`; bol shipments; ChannelEngine tracking back-flow). Late or missing confirmation is what the late-shipment and valid-tracking metrics measure.
4. Cancellations flow both ways: marketplace- or customer-initiated (Amazon `ORDER_CHANGE` carries "buyer-requested cancellations") and seller-initiated (counted in the cancellation rate).
5. Returns are announced by the marketplace and acknowledged by the merchant (ChannelEngine `POST /v2/returns/merchant/acknowledge`; EffectConnect `/returns/*` with `/return/reasons` and `/return/solutions`; bol Returns API).

[ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT] The five-step shape is the analyst's synthesis of the APIs cited in sections 1.2, 1.5 and 2.1; marketplace order states differ in name and are not all documented on the pages fetched.

**Sync intervals.** Vendors describe stock and order sync as "real-time" or "instantly" (ChannelEngine) or "every few minutes" (Linnworks) and do not publish intervals [VERIFIED: as cited in 2.1]. Amazon's rate limits (one `getOrders` call per minute per seller-app pair) and bol's preference for "a steady flow of updates" over batches bound what any connector can do by polling [VERIFIED: sections 1.2 and 1.5]. [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT] In practice connectors poll each marketplace on a schedule of minutes and push stock changes as they receive them; the merchant-side schedule (how often the ERP or WMS tells the connector) is usually the slow link. The ledger's hourly platform-to-connector sync is the illustrative case.

**Architecture implications** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]. Decide early whether marketplace orders are *created in the OMS by the connector* or *created in the connector and mirrored*. The former gives one order record and one state model (the ledger's W2); the latter is cheaper at the Growing stage but produces the "three-status order" once stores and EDI arrive. Idempotent acknowledgement keyed on the marketplace order id is the minimum: both Amazon notifications and connector polling can deliver the same order twice.

### 2.3 Stock allocation per channel

[ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT] Connectors let merchants publish a fraction, a fixed quantity, or a buffer-adjusted quantity per channel (EffectConnect's "warehouse management with multiple storage locations" and ChannelEngine's per-channel offers are the visible ends of this). The stock number sent to a marketplace is a *promise*, and Amazon's `LISTINGS_ITEM_MFN_QUANTITY_CHANGE` notification exists so that the connector can see what Amazon currently believes [VERIFIED: section 1.2]. The architectural rule the brief supports: the available-to-promise figure per channel must be computed once, from stock events, and published to every channel from that one computation; a connector that holds its own stock copy is a fourth system of record (the ledger's "fourth copy for listings").

### 2.4 Price parity and repricing

- **The EU price-parity decision (evidence age).** On 26 November 2013 the Bundeskartellamt closed proceedings against Amazon after Amazon deleted its price-parity clauses from contracts with all Marketplace sellers and informed sellers; the authority's view: "Price specifications for one's own competitors can never be justified"; the cooperation with the UK Office of Fair Trading in the European Competition Network ensured the clauses were abandoned "EU-wide" [VERIFIED: https://bundeskartellamt.de/SharedDocs/Meldung/EN/Pressemitteilungen/2013/26_11_2013_Amazon-Verfahrenseinstellung.html, published 2013-11-26] **(evidence age: 13 years; the decision is the origin of today's rules, not a description of them)**.
- **Price competitiveness still decides visibility.** Amazon's `PRICING_HEALTH` notification tells a seller that an offer is "ineligible for the Featured Offer (Buy Box) because its price is uncompetitive", and `ANY_OFFER_CHANGED` reports changes in "top offers, external prices, or Buy Box winners" [VERIFIED: https://developer-docs.amazon/sp-api/docs/notification-type-values, n.d., accessed 2026-11]. bol's partner pages say good performance "improves your chance of appearing in the buy box" [VERIFIED: section 1.5].
- **Repricing** is a standard connector feature: ChannelEngine offers "dynamic pricing, margin guardrails, and cost-based insights to adjust prices per channel"; EffectConnect adjusts prices "automatically to changing costs and competition" (translated) [VERIFIED: section 2.1].

**For newcomers.** *Price parity* (a "most-favoured-nation" clause) is a contractual requirement that the seller's marketplace price be no higher than its price elsewhere; in the EU Amazon dropped the clause in 2013, but marketplaces still compare prices to decide who gets the buy box. *Repricing* is an automated rule that moves a listing's price within bounds in reaction to competitors' prices.

**Architecture implications** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]. Repricing must run on the *channel price*, not on the merchant's base price list, and must be bounded by a floor computed from cost and fees (section 1.8) — otherwise a connector rule can undercut the merchant's own storefront, which is one way the ledger's Price Drift happens. Chapter 7 should treat the channel price as a derived price with a published source, effective date and floor.

### 2.5 Marketplace as channel and competitor

- The Bundeskartellamt's 2013 release states the structural fact: Amazon "as the largest online retailer, competes directly with" the sellers on its Marketplace [VERIFIED: https://bundeskartellamt.de/SharedDocs/Meldung/EN/Pressemitteilungen/2013/26_11_2013_Amazon-Verfahrenseinstellung.html, published 2013-11-26].
- Zalando's own reporting shows the mixed model: a first-party retailer whose partner business was 30% of Fashion Store GMV in 2021 and which now sells fulfilment and software (ZEOS) to brands that may not sell on Zalando at all [VERIFIED: sections 1.6].
- OTTO's seller page says the marketplace "does not admit every seller" and reviews each application individually (translated) [VERIFIED: https://www.otto.market/, n.d., accessed 2026-11].

**Architecture implications** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]. The data the merchant sends to a marketplace (content, prices, sell-through via stock updates) is also market intelligence for a competitor; the architecture decision is which attributes and which stock granularity leave the building. At Group stage, brands typically keep the richest content in the PIM and syndicate a reduced, channel-specific view.

### 3.2 Digital Services Act: trader traceability (Article 30)

- The DSA (Regulation (EU) 2022/2065) applies from **17 February 2024** (with some provisions from 16 November 2022) [VERIFIED: http://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32022R2065 (Article 93), published 2022-10-27].

**For newcomers.** The *DSA* is the EU regulation on online intermediaries; for marketplaces its practical core is "know your trader": verify who the seller is before they can sell, keep the data, and be able to suspend them.

**Architecture implications** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]. For a merchant, Article 30 is why every marketplace onboarding now asks for identity documents, bank details and register numbers, and why a seller account can be suspended for missing data. ### 4.2 Offline operation

Store sales cannot stop when the internet does; vendors document what works offline and who carries the risk.

- Shopify: POS needs internet to "sync with your Shopify admin", e-mail receipts and capture card payments; "offline checkout" (cash and manual payments) is available to all users; "offline payments" for credit and debit cards must be enabled in the admin (POS app 9.14.0 or later), work with specific readers, exclude swipe, Tap to Pay and manual entry, are "captured after your device reconnects to the internet", carry a merchant-set device daily limit and per-transaction limit, and Shopify advises reconnecting "as soon as possible, ideally within 24 hours"; declined payments surface only after reconnection; offline payments are not available for retail locations in France [VERIFIED: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/sell-in-person/shopify-pos/selling-offline and https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/sell-in-person/shopify-pos/selling-offline/offline-payments, n.d., accessed 2026-11] ⚠.
- Square: offline sessions on Readers and Stands end after 24 hours; Register, Terminal and Handheld must upload within 72 hours of session start; "Pending offline payments will expire after 72 hours if you don't reconnect to the internet and upload them"; "You're responsible for any expired, declined, or disputed payments accepted while taking offline payments"; sellers set a per-transaction cap between $1 and $50,000; from April 2026 offline payments are enabled by default unless the seller opts out [VERIFIED: https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/5095, n.d., accessed 2026-11] ⚠.

**Architecture implications** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]. Offline mode means the store is, for hours, an *independent system of record* for sales and store stock, reconciled later. The implications are: store transactions need locally generated identifiers that never collide (a signature counter and transaction number in Germany, Part 4.3), stock reservations made online against store stock during an outage are unreliable (click-and-collect promises need a buffer or a confirmation step), and payment capture is deferred with the merchant bearing decline risk. A platform's POS and a separate POS differ most here: the platform's POS reconciles into the same order and stock model; a separate POS reconciles through an integration that must be designed for replay.

VAT (drinks with meals excluded), assessed per establishment; businesses below the threshold may use it voluntarily under the same rules; "An invoice can never replace the kasticket" (translated); tickets required from 1 July 2016; records kept 7 years [VERIFIED: https://www.geregistreerdkassasysteem.be/nl/ondernemer/voor-wie/richtlijnen and https://www.geregistreerdkassasysteem.be/nl/klant/algemeen/wie-moet-het-gks-gebruiken, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- GKS 2.0: data are passed to the FPS "almost in real time"; both register and FDM must be certified as GKS 2.0; mandatory for new hospitality businesses from 1 July 2026; existing systems must migrate by 1 July 2027 (register bought before 2018), 1 July 2028 (2018–2021) or 1 January 2029 (2022 or later) (translated) [VERIFIED: https://www.geregistreerdkassasysteem.be/nl/nieuws/het-nieuw-geregistreerd-kassasysteem-gks-20-wordt-verplicht-vanaf-1-juli-2026, published 2026-06-03] ⚠. The legal basis is royal decrees of 24 April 2024 and a ministerial decree of 29 April 2024; the tolerance period ran to 30 June 2026 [VERIFIED: https://www.geregistreerdkassasysteem.be/nl/gks-20-1, n.d., accessed 2026-11].

**Italy.**
- The Agenzia delle Entrate press release of 29 June 2019: from 1 July 2019 VAT operators with turnover above €400,000 must give the consumer, instead of the fiscal receipt, "a document with commercial value only" while "storing and transmitting the related data to the Agenzia delle Entrate" (art. 17 DL 119/2018); the obligation starts "1 July 2019 for subjects with turnover above €400 thousand and 1 January 2020 for all the others"; as an alternative to telematic cash registers the Agency's web service in the "Fatture e Corrispettivi" portal can store and send each transaction; data may be sent within 12 days of the transaction; no penalties for the first six months if data are sent within the following month (translated) [VERIFIED: https://agenziaentrate.gov.it/portale/documents/20143/1534262/057_Com.%2Bst.%2BCorrispettivi%2Btelematici%2B29.06.19/a4d1a59f-fb83-b2c6-333f-c572cbd2c774, published 2019-06-29] **(evidence age: start dates only; later decrees extended the moratorium to 1 January 2021 [WIDELY REPORTED: https://www.ecnews.it/fiscale/fisco-e-patrimonio/iva/corrispettivi-telematici-i-provvedimenti-dellagenzia-delle-entrate/, 2020])**.

**Netherlands.**
- The Belastingdienst brochure "Het kassasysteem in uw bedrijf" states that "the retention period for your administration is seven years" (translated), that detail data created digitally must also be kept digitally, and that "suppliers of cash register systems have developed a quality mark … This quality mark guarantees that transactions are properly recorded and kept" (translated); the brochure frames a keurmerk register as something you "may assume" meets the rules, not as a requirement [VERIFIED: https://download.belastingdienst.nl/belastingdienst/docs/uw_bedrijf_en_het_kassa_on2001z5fd.pdf, n.d., accessed 2026-11]. The Belastingdienst's page calls the cash register system part of the administration that "must be set up and kept in line with statutory rules" (translated) [VERIFIED: https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/bldcontentnl/themaoverstijgend/brochures_en_publicaties/uw_bedrijf_en_het_kassasysteem, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- The quality mark is run by a private foundation: "the independent and transparent quality mark for cash register and point-of-sale systems in the Netherlands" (translated), issued "exclusively for the specific brand and type of point-of-sale system that we have tested", aligned with the tax authority's guidance for reliable systems [VERIFIED: https://www.keurmerkafrekensystemen.nl/, n.d., accessed 2026-11]. [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT] No Dutch law requires a certified register or signed receipts; the obligation is evidential (complete, unaltered, retrievable records for seven years).

**For newcomers.** *Fiscalization* is a legal requirement that every sale be recorded in a way the tax authority can verify: a certified signing device (Germany), a certified black box plus signing card (Belgium), or direct transmission to the tax authority (Italy). *TSE* (technische Sicherheitseinrichtung) is Germany's certified signing module. *GKS* (geregistreerd kassasysteem) is Belgium's hospitality-only "white cash register".

**Architecture implications** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]. A POS used in Germany, Belgian hospitality or Italy cannot be a thin client of an online order service: it must produce a locally signed or transmitted record per transaction, with sequential numbering and prescribed receipt content, and keep working offline. This is why a hosted platform's POS and a country-specific POS are different products, why the ledger's merchant (NL stores) faced no fiscal mandate when it chose its POS, and why a German store opening (Group stage) is a POS decision as much as a logistics one. Store orders should be *imported* into the OMS as transactions with their fiscal identifiers, never re-created.

### 4.4 POS and payment terminals; PCI P2PE

- Adyen presents in-person payments "processed on the same platform as online payments", a Terminal API that "communicates using JSON messages, without the need for libraries" over a local network or the cloud, "fully-certified and PCI-compliant Adyen payment terminals", and shopper recognition that can "identify an online shopper when they make a purchase in one of your physical locations" for loyalty and call-centre refunds [VERIFIED: https://docs.adyen.com/point-of-sale/, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- PCI SSC publishes a list of validated P2PE solutions "assessed by a third party for compliance against corresponding PCI SSC payment security standards"; "Only the specific version (or versions) of a given Product or Solution that is identified on a List has been evaluated" [VERIFIED: https://www.pcisecuritystandards.org/assessors_and_solutions/point_to_point_encryption_solutions, n.d., accessed 2026-11]. P2PE encrypts card data at the point of interaction and keeps it encrypted until the solution provider's decryption environment; because the merchant never holds the keys, a validated solution lets the merchant treat the payment path as out of PCI DSS scope; the first version of the standard (1.1, 2011) contained over 900 requirements on the solution provider [WIDELY REPORTED: https://vikingcloud.com/blog/the-pci-point-to-point-encryption-p2pe-program and https://trustedsec.com/blog/reducing-merchant-scope-to-ease-the-compliance-burden, n.d.]. Vendor claims of "up to 90%" scope reduction are marketing [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT].
- Oracle lists "EFTLink" as the component that links Xstore to electronic funds transfer [VERIFIED: https://docs.oracle.com/en/industries/retail/index.html, n.d., accessed 2026-11].

**For newcomers.** *P2PE* (point-to-point encryption) means the card number is encrypted inside the terminal and decrypted only by the payment provider, so the merchant's POS and network never see it. *Semi-integrated* terminals receive the amount from the POS and return a result, without the POS touching card data.

**Architecture implications** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]. Use semi-integrated, P2PE-validated terminals so that the POS, the store network and the merchant's integration stay out of PCI DSS scope; keep the terminal provider's transaction reference on the store transaction for reconciliation with the PSP settlement file (RECONCILED's territory). A single provider for online and in-store (Adyen's pitch) simplifies cross-channel refunds and shopper recognition, at the cost of concentration.

## PART 5: Omnichannel fulfilment

### 5.1 The fulfilment models

| Model | Order placed | Stock used | Handed over | Vendor evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Click-and-collect / BOPIS | Online | Store stock (or warehouse stock shipped to store) | In store | Shopify: "buy online, pickup in store" |
| Ship-from-store | Online | Store stock | Carrier from the store | Zalando Connected Retail (stores "shipped orders from their stores"); UPS 2016: 27% of European retailers "use stores as fulfilment centres for online orders" |
| Return-in-store | Online | — | Return accepted at a store | Shopify POS Pro: "Returns and exchanges at any location" |
| Endless aisle | In store | Warehouse or another store's stock | Carrier to the customer | Shopify: "buy in store, ship to customer"; Savills 2018: 37% of UK stores let customers order online in store |

[VERIFIED: sections 1.6, 4.1 and 5.5] [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT] The table is the analyst's synthesis.

**For newcomers.** *Click-and-collect* (BOPIS, buy online pick up in store) is an online order collected at a store. *Ship-from-store* is an online order shipped from a store's stock. *Endless aisle* is a store sale of an item the store does not have, shipped from elsewhere. *Store stock as a location* means the store's inventory is one more place the availability calculation can promise from.

**Architecture implications** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]. Every model above requires the availability service to see store stock and the OMS to route an order line to a store (sourcing), and requires the store to have tooling to pick, pack and hand over. Click-and-collect against nightly-synced store stock is the ledger's Store Stock Mirage; the remedy is store-stock events into the availability service, with a safety buffer sized to the accuracy evidence below.

### 5.2 Store stock accuracy: what the research says

- DeHoratius and Raman: "Examining nearly 370,000 inventory records from 37 stores of one retailer, we find 65% to be inaccurate"; 26.4% of the variance in inaccuracy lies between product categories and 2.7% between stores; auditing practices reduce inaccuracy, while store complexity and distribution structure increase it [VERIFIED: https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormnsc:v:54:y:2008:i:4:p:627-641 (abstract of *Management Science* 54(4), 627–641, 2008)] **(evidence age: data from the 2000s; the mechanism, not the number, transfers)**.
- Auburn University RFID Lab, "Project Zipper" (Phase 1, 2018): across eight brands and five retailers, audits using legacy UPC data found order inaccuracies "in 69% of cases" against "less than 0.01%" with RFID; a "failure analysis" of mismatches was still underway [VERIFIED: https://www.retaildive.com/news/RFID-100-accurate-ROI-Auburn/539468, published 2018-10-12] **(evidence age)**. Trade press reporting the same programme states that RFID raises store inventory accuracy "from 63 percent to 95 percent" and reduces out-of-stocks "up to 50 percent" compared with barcodes [WIDELY REPORTED: https://www.just-style.com/news/rfid-can-help-brands-achieve-near-100-order-accuracy/ and https://www.vitag.com.au/99-9-per-cent-supply-chain-accuracy-with-rfid/, 2018–2019]; the underlying methodology for the 63% baseline was not found.
- Linnworks' marketing cites "87%" of its customers naming inventory accuracy as their "#1 value" and a customer reaching "100% inventory accuracy after migration" [VERIFIED: https://www.linnworks.com/, n.d., accessed 2026-11] (vendor claim).

**Architecture implications** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]. If roughly two-thirds of store inventory records can be wrong without RFID, store stock cannot be promised at face value. The design options are a per-store safety threshold (do not promise the last N units), a confirmation step (the store confirms the pick before the customer is told to come), cycle counting driven by the availability service's exceptions, or RFID. Which one fits depends on the stage: Growing-stage merchants use thresholds; Omnichannel-stage merchants add confirmation and counting; Group-stage fashion retailers invest in RFID.

### 5.3 "Unified commerce": vendor definitions

- Shopify: unified commerce "connects sales channels, inventory, and customer data through a single back-end platform"; omnichannel "doesn't connect back-end systems" and keeps separate databases per channel [VERIFIED: https://www.shopify.com/blog/unified-commerce, published 2026-07-02] (vendor definition; the post also cites "Shopify POS raises annual sales by 8.9% on average", a vendor claim).
- Adyen: "Point-of-sale payments are processed on the same platform as online payments" with shopper recognition across channels [VERIFIED: https://docs.adyen.com/point-of-sale/, n.d., accessed 2026-11] (vendor definition from the payments side).
- Lightspeed: "Unite all your stores and channels" on "a unified retail POS and payments platform" [VERIFIED: https://www.lightspeedhq.com/pos/retail/, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- No analyst definition could be fetched (Gartner's glossary returned 403). [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT] Analysts use the term for one order, inventory and customer record shared by every channel in real time, as opposed to *omnichannel*, which they use for a consistent customer experience achieved by integrating separate systems. Vendors use it to mean "buy our platform for everything". Chapter 17 should define it as the goal state (one record per order, stock unit and customer), say that it is achievable either with one platform or with an OMS and identity layer over several, and note which vendor is speaking.

### 5.4 Store staff tooling and customer identity across channels

- Staff tooling: Shopify POS Pro provides "Staff roles and permissions" and unlimited staff; Lightspeed lets staff transfer stock between stores and order for any location; Vendit's Premium package adds quotations, customer orders and multi-store management (translated) [VERIFIED: sections 4.1]. Zalando's Connected Retail ran on a dedicated tool for store staff that is being withdrawn in 2027 [WIDELY REPORTED: section 1.6].
- Customer identity: Shopify POS Pro offers "Rich customer profiles and customer loyalty insights" and the basic tier "simple customer profiles" [VERIFIED: https://www.shopify.com/pos/pricing, n.d., accessed 2026-11]; Adyen's shopper recognition identifies "an online shopper when they make a purchase in one of your physical locations" through the payment card, enabling "automated loyalty points" [VERIFIED: https://docs.adyen.com/point-of-sale/, n.d., accessed 2026-11]; Lightspeed says customer profiles and sales histories "follow shoppers between locations" [VERIFIED: section 4.1].

**For newcomers.** A *loyalty programme* is the mechanism that gives a store customer an identifier (card, phone number, app) so that store purchases can be attached to a customer record; without it most store transactions are anonymous.

**Architecture implications** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]. Store identity is opt-in and partial; the three practical join keys are a loyalty identifier, an e-mail address captured at the till, and a payment-card token from a shared PSP. The ledger's Familiar Stranger is the default outcome when the POS, the platform and the newsletter tool each own a customer list; chapter 27 should treat store identity as a matching problem with a declared confidence, not as a single id.

### 5.5 Adoption statistics, with caveats

- UPS "Pulse of the Omni-channel Retailer" (2016), based on logistics experts at "more than 700 retail companies in Europe": 65% of UK retailers use stores for services such as click and collect; "almost one-third (27%) of European retail companies already use stores as fulfilment centres for online orders" with "large variances" between countries; 31% planned to use stores or pick-up points within 12–24 months; 61% of UK retailers (43% European average) charged for returns of undamaged goods [VERIFIED: https://postandparcel.info/74732/news/e-commerce/uk-retailers-responding-slowly-to-needs-of-online-shoppers-says-ups, published 2016-08-08] **(evidence age: 10 years; pre-pandemic)**.
- Savills UK store survey (2018): 47% of stores offered click & collect, averaging 8% of store sales (16% in music, books and games); 37% of retailers let customers order online in store; about 1 in 10 stores participated in ship-from-store (29% in footwear); nearly one in three music/books/games store managers "did not know whether online refunds were deducted from store sales" [VERIFIED: https://www.savills.co.uk/research_articles/179587/226023-0, published 2018-01-08] **(evidence age)**.
- Zalando's Connected Retail reached "more than 3,400 active stores in nine European markets" by early 2021 and over 4,000 later that year (translated) [WIDELY REPORTED: fashionunited.de, 2021] **(evidence age)**.

**Caveats** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]. All adoption figures found are pre-2020, UK-weighted and survey-based with undisclosed sampling; no open, dated European figure for 2024–2026 was found in this session (Statista charts exist behind a paywall). Chapter 17 should use these as "order of magnitude, a decade ago" and lean on the mechanisms rather than the percentages. The Savills finding that store managers did not know how online refunds were booked is the best-sourced illustration of the organisational side of return-in-store.
