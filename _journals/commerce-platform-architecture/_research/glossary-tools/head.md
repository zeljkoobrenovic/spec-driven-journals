> **IN THIS SECTION, YOU WILL:** Look up any term the book uses, read a one- or two-sentence definition beside the terms it is confused with, and follow the link to the chapter that owns it.

> **KEY POINTS:**
>
> * **Look a term up, then read its neighbors.** Each section collects one part's terms, so the rows around a term carry the contrasts that cause real design errors: product versus variant versus stock code, on hand versus committed versus available-to-promise, authorization versus capture, order versus shipment versus invoice, system of record versus copy.
> * **One term, one meaning, one owning chapter.** Every row links to the chapter that owns or best explains the term, and the definitions follow how those chapters define them. When a chapter and this page disagree, one of them needs correcting.
> * **Rules, standards and vendor terms date quickly.** Laws, protocols, products and their status are given as of November 2026, following the sources cited in the chapters. Check the current source before a definition here goes into a contract, a vendor evaluation or a design decision.

<br>

This page gives every term the book uses one plain definition and a link to the chapter that owns it, because the same word means different things in different systems: a storefront's 'order' can differ from an invoice or a warehouse shipment, and 'in stock' may mean physical stock, sellable stock or a stale copy ([[commerce-data-and-analytics]], [[inventory-and-availability]]). Look a term up by part or in the alphabetical index and read the rows around it; slash rows define confused terms together. An opening section explains how to read the book's claims, and after the parts come a dated table of standards and regulations and a table of the fictional Kestrel & Pine names.

## How to Use This Glossary

Browse by the book's parts or jump to the **[alphabetical index](#g-index)** for a term, alias or abbreviation. The opening section explains evidence labels, take-away tiers, scale stages and failure patterns. Separate tables collect standards and regulations, plus the fictional company and character names.

The **__NROWS__ rows** give short teaching definitions. The final column links to fuller explanation, examples and sources. Slash-separated terms are defined together to show their distinction; acronyms are expanded in the row.

**Kestrel & Pine is fictional**, and its names and figures illustrate the definitions ([[meet-kestrel-and-pine]]). Legal, standards and vendor entries summarize their subject; consult the cited source for the complete requirement.
