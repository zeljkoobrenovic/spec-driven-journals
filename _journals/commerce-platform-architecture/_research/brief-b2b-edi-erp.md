# Research brief: B2B commerce, EDI and e-invoicing, and ERP integration

Prepared 7 November 2026 for chapter writers of *ORDERED: Architecting Commerce Platforms*. Feeds chapters 07 (pricing), 14 (tax, invoicing and ledger), 21 (B2B commerce), 22 (EDI and e-invoicing), 23 (ERP and back-office integration) and 36 (regulation), and Journey 3 (chapter 39). The brief is platform-neutral: vendors are named because they publish documentation, not because the book endorses them.

## How to read this brief

Every substantive claim carries one tag:

- **[VERIFIED: url, published YYYY-MM(-DD)]**: the page was fetched in this session (7 November 2026) and supports the claim. Paraphrases stay close to the source; quoted text is quoted. Undated product and documentation pages are marked "n.d., accessed 2026-11". Non-English official pages are quoted in English translation, and the translation is noted.
- **[WIDELY REPORTED: url, YYYY]**: from a reputable secondary source (trade press, vendor or advisory-firm write-up, encyclopaedia), not a primary document fetched in this session.
- **[ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]**: industry knowledge or the analyst's inference. Every statement about how a proprietary platform works internally falls here unless the vendor has documented it.

**Evidence age matters.** B2B features move fast (Shopify's B2B plan availability, commercetools' B2B objects and BigCommerce's B2B Edition APIs all changed in 2025–2026), while EDI standards move slowly (the EANCOM manual in force is the 2002 release, edition 2016, updated 2021; the cXML User's Guide linked as "current" is dated 2018). Sources older than about three years are flagged **(evidence age)**.

**⚠ Volatility flags** mark claims likely to change within 12 months: e-invoicing dates and tolerance periods, plan tiers, API versions, provider counts and prices.

**Research limitation.** Several primary sites refused automated fetches: gs1.org product pages (HTTP 403; the GS1 General Specifications PDF and EANCOM PDFs on the same domain did download), help.sap.com (JavaScript shell; SAP Commerce B2B facts come from SAP's Spartacus documentation on GitHub Pages), unece.org (403; UN/EDIFACT facts come from the GS1 EANCOM manual and an encyclopaedia page), netsuite.com and oracle.com (403; **no NetSuite primary page was verified**), oroinc.com marketing pages (403; OroCommerce facts come from its documentation site and GitHub README), economie.gouv.fr (403; French dates come from impots.gouv.fr), agenziaentrate.gov.it (404 on both English and Italian paths; Italian facts come from the European Commission's country factsheet), and eMarketer (403). Belgium's e-reporting phase (announced for 2028) and Poland's exact current threshold wording were not confirmed from a primary page. No official Walmart OTIF policy document is public; Walmart figures come from trade press (2017) and an EDI provider's guide (2024). Coupa's punchout documentation was not fetched; punchout facts rest on the cXML User's Guide and a 2026 integrator write-up for OCI.

## PART 1: B2B commerce features and how platforms implement them

### 1.1 What is different when the buyer is a company

The platforms' own documentation converges on one object model, whatever the vocabulary:

| Concept | Shopify | commercetools | SAP Commerce (Spartacus docs) | Adobe Commerce B2B | BigCommerce B2B Edition | OroCommerce |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The buying organisation | Company → CompanyLocation → CompanyContact | Business Unit (Company → Divisions, up to 5 levels) with Associates | B2BUnit tree with B2BUser | Company account, company hierarchies | Company account with users | Customer, customer group, customer user |
| Who may do what | Contact permissions (ordering only / location admin) | Associate Roles | Roles incl. approver, administrator; purchase limits | Company roles; administrators bypass rules | Roles controlling Buyer Portal access | Customer user roles |
| Customer-specific prices and assortment | Catalogs (price lists + product sets) assigned to company locations | Stores and prices per Business Unit | Cost centers, budgets | Shared catalogs with custom pricing | Price lists per company (not verified on the page fetched) | Price lists with scheduled adjustments |
| Negotiation | Draft orders for review and approval | Quote Request → Staged Quote → Quote | Quotes (not verified) | Quotes | Sales quotes | RFQ → Quote, with approval workflow |
| Spending control | — (apps) | Approval Rules → Approval Flows | Order approval, budgets, purchase limits | Purchase orders with approval rules | — | Quote approval workflows |
| Pay later | Payment terms per company location (net, fixed date, on receipt, on fulfillment) | — (payment integrations) | — | Payment on Account (offline method) | Net terms and available credit in payment settings | — |

### 1.2 Platform by platform

**Shopify.**
- B2B is native: "To use Shopify B2B, you must set up your B2B customers as companies in your Shopify admin." Merchants can run B2B and direct-to-consumer "in one store or use a separate B2B-only store", and "Shopify B2B is available on the Basic, Grow, Advanced, and Shopify Plus plans", not on Starter or Retail [VERIFIED: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/b2b, n.d., accessed 2026-11] ⚠ (B2B was a Plus-only feature when launched; the plan expansion is recent and the page does not date it).
- Object model: a Company is "Information about the business entity that makes a B2B purchase"; a CompanyLocation is "A single location or branch of the company" holding billing and shipping addresses, with catalogs, tax exemptions and payment terms assigned to it ("Catalogs can be assigned only to a company location"); a CompanyContact is "A person that acts on behalf of the company" tied to a customer record. The documented lifecycle: the merchant creates the company, contacts and locations, "can create draft orders for contacts to review and approve", the contact signs in and checks out, and apps integrate through the GraphQL Admin API; B2B orders can also be imported "from systems outside Shopify". B2B "doesn't support subscriptions, pre-orders, or try-before-you-buy" [VERIFIED: https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/build/b2b, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- Catalogs: "You can use B2B catalogs to choose which products and prices to offer your B2B customers" by including or excluding products and adjusting prices by percentage or fixed price; volume pricing gives "discounted price breaks when a customer buys a set quantity"; quantity rules limit units per order; Basic/Grow/Advanced plans allow "up to 3 active catalog assignments", Plus has no limit [VERIFIED: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/b2b/catalogs, n.d., accessed 2026-11] ⚠.
- Payment terms are typed in the Admin API (version 2026-10) as `NET` ("Payment is due a set number of days after the invoice is issued"), `FIXED` (a calendar date), `RECEIPT` (due when the invoice is received), `FULFILLMENT` (due once fulfilled) and `UNKNOWN`; a `PaymentTermsTemplate` carries `dueInDays` for net terms [VERIFIED: https://shopify.dev/docs/api/admin-graphql/latest/enums/PaymentTermsType and https://shopify.dev/docs/api/admin-graphql/latest/objects/PaymentTermsTemplate, API 2026-10, accessed 2026-11].
- Quotes: no native "request a quote" page was found in the Shopify Help Center in this session; a web search returned only third-party quote apps, most of which "convert accepted quotes into Shopify draft orders" [WIDELY REPORTED: https://apps.shopify.com/quote-me-pro-1 and similar listings, 2026]. [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT] On Shopify the draft order *is* the quote mechanism; a dedicated quote object is an app-layer concern. ⚠

**commercetools.**
- "Business Units are representations of Companies or Divisions." Units nest up to five levels (a soft limit), a top-level unit may hold up to 4,000 Divisions, Divisions can inherit Stores, Associates and Approval Rules from parents, and "The Associate Roles are used to grant Associates different permissions." A unit cannot be deleted while a Cart, Order, Quote, Shopping List or child unit references it [VERIFIED: https://docs.commercetools.com/api/projects/business-units, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- Quoting is a three-object negotiation: "With Quote Requests, Buyers can request a Quote from the Seller"; "Staged Quotes are used by a Seller to prepare Quotes for a Buyer" (with seller comment and `validTo`); "Quotes are legally binding custom offers presented to a Buyer", with update actions for renegotiation and state transitions [VERIFIED: https://docs.commercetools.com/api/projects/quote-requests, https://docs.commercetools.com/api/projects/staged-quotes, https://docs.commercetools.com/api/projects/quotes, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- Spending control: "Approval Rules provide a flexible approach to configuring and managing the approval process for Orders"; rules are tiered ("Each tier contains one or more roles that are required to approve an Order before it can proceed to the next tier"), use AND/OR approver logic, and are inherited down the unit hierarchy. "Approval Flows are automatically generated when a new Order matches any defined Approval Rules", and "a single instance of rejection will lead to the rejection of the entire Approval Flow" [VERIFIED: https://docs.commercetools.com/api/projects/approval-rules and https://docs.commercetools.com/api/projects/approval-flows, n.d., accessed 2026-11].

**SAP Commerce Cloud.** help.sap.com could not be read, so the evidence is SAP's Spartacus storefront documentation. B2B Commerce Organization "allows companies to manage purchases made through a Spartacus commerce web site" with `B2BUnit` trees, `B2BUser`, user groups, `CostCenter`, `Budget` (amount, currency, date range), "permission (also known as purchase limits)" and a `B2BApprovalProcess` per unit; introduced in Spartacus 3.0; Spartacus 4.x "is no longer maintained" and was tested only with SAP Commerce Cloud 1905–2205 [VERIFIED: https://sap.github.io/spartacus-docs/b2b-commerce-organization/, n.d., accessed 2026-11] **(evidence age: the page documents an unmaintained storefront version; the backend concepts are older still)**. Order approval: "All orders waiting for approval will have a 'pending approval' status until the manager decides", approvers see orders of their own unit or, for the main unit, of all units, and the approval dashboard "is visible only to the company administrator" [VERIFIED: https://sap.github.io/spartacus-docs/b2b-order-approval, n.d., accessed 2026-11]. [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT] The budget/cost-centre/purchase-limit model reflects SAP Commerce's procurement-style heritage; it is the richest spending-control model among the platforms surveyed and the one most often over-configured.

**Adobe Commerce B2B.** The B2B guide (last update 1 October 2026) lists Company Accounts as "the primary building block for providing support for B2B buyers", company hierarchies, Shared Catalogs "with custom pricing for different companies", Quick Orders, Purchase Orders that help companies "track and control their spending", Quotes and Requisition Lists [VERIFIED: https://experienceleague.adobe.com/en/docs/commerce-admin/b2b/guide-overview, published 2026-10-01]. When purchase orders are enabled, "all orders are automatically created as Purchase Orders (PO)"; approval rules can require a manager above an amount or an administrator above a SKU count; "Company administrators can bypass all approval rules"; offline payments (the page's example is "Payment on Account") are entered at PO creation, online payments after approval; and "If the price of an item changes after the PO is created, the original price is used" [VERIFIED: https://experienceleague.adobe.com/en/docs/commerce-admin/b2b/purchase-orders/purchase-order-flow, n.d., accessed 2026-11].

**BigCommerce B2B Edition.** Its APIs "allow you to retrieve and manage B2B resources": Company accounts with hierarchies, users with roles controlling Buyer Portal access, sales quotes taken to checkout, invoices for purchase orders with payment settings covering "payment methods, available credit, and net terms", shopping lists, sales-staff assignment and Super Admin "Masquerade". The APIs are served from a separate domain (`api-b2b.bigcommerce.com`); the V2 management API is deprecated and the B2B `authToken` was deprecated on 30 September 2025 in favour of the standard `X-Auth-Token` plus `X-Store-Hash` headers [VERIFIED: https://docs.bigcommerce.com/b2b-edition/docs/about, n.d., accessed 2026-11] ⚠. [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT] The separate API domain is the visible trace of B2B Edition having started life as a partner-built application layered on the core platform.

**Sana Commerce.** Positions itself as "The B2B e-commerce platform for manufacturers, built on your SAP or Dynamics ERP": "It works directly with your ERP, so your buyers order online with the prices you agreed and the stock you really have" [VERIFIED: https://www.sana-commerce.com/, n.d., accessed 2026-11]. [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT] This is the "ERP-as-hub" architecture productised: the web store reads prices, stock and customer terms from the ERP at request time instead of keeping its own copies (see Part 4.3).

**OroCommerce.** "a leading B2B eCommerce platform for distributors, wholesalers, and manufacturers", licensed OSL-3.0 [VERIFIED: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/oroinc/orocommerce/master/README.md, 2026]. Its back-office documentation (version 7.0) covers requests for quote submitted from the storefront and governed by RFQ workflows, quotes "from scratch or from a customer's RFQ" with three workflow variants ("simple, with approvals, and no workflow"), price lists with scheduled adjustments and multiple currencies, customer accounts, groups, users and roles, and order entry by matrix form and reorder [VERIFIED: https://doc.oroinc.com/user/back-office/sales/, version 7.0, accessed 2026-11].

### 1.3 Market figures, with caveats

- Forrester (press release 8 June 2022): "US B2B e-commerce sales reached $1.7 trillion in 2021", "estimated to exceed $2 trillion in 2023 and $3 trillion by 2027", which "will also account for 24% of total US B2B sales by 2027 — compared to 16% in 2021". Definition: "the sale of B2B products by manufacturers directly to businesses" plus wholesalers' and distributors' sales [VERIFIED: http://www.forrester.com/press-newsroom/forrester-us-b2b-e-commerce-will-reach-an-estimated-3-trillion-by-2027/, published 2022-06-08] **(evidence age: 4 years; a pre-inflation forecast)**.
- Digital Commerce 360 (7 February 2024): digital B2B sales "on ecommerce sites, password-protected sites, and apps" grew "to about $2.1 trillion in 2023", up from $1.95 trillion in 2022, against flat total US manufacturing and distribution sales of $14.87 trillion [VERIFIED: https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/article/b2b-ecommerce-market-us/, published 2024-02-07]. Note the article's own "17%" growth claim does not match its dollar figures (about 8%); treat growth rates from this source with care.
- Digital Commerce 360 (26 January 2026): "U.S. manufacturing and wholesale distribution sales reached $15.12 trillion in 2025", up 0.4%, "according to U.S. Department of Commerce data and Digital Commerce 360 analysis"; the article gives no e-commerce dollar figure [VERIFIED: https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2026/01/26/us-b2b-sales-exceed-15-trillion-2025/, published 2026-01-26].
- Secondary sources quote a 2025 US B2B e-commerce figure of about $2.9 trillion attributed to Digital Commerce 360's February 2026 forecast report; this could not be confirmed against a Digital Commerce 360 page in this session [WIDELY REPORTED: web search summary, 2026] ⚠.
- [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT] The figures are not comparable: Forrester counts manufacturer and distributor sales to businesses; Digital Commerce 360 counts sales on sites and apps and excludes EDI; the US Census "e-commerce" series for manufacturing counts EDI and is far larger. Chapter 21 should cite one definition and say which, and should not add European figures without a public source (none was found that met the brief's standard).

### 1.4 B2B2C

[ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT] No platform page fetched defines "B2B2C". In practice it names two patterns: (a) a brand sells to a retailer who sells to consumers, with the brand's platform feeding product content and sometimes stock to the retailer (the EDI and marketplace cases in Part 2 and the channels brief); (b) a platform operator lets business customers' own end-users buy under the business's terms (a dealer portal or an employee-benefits store). Pattern (a) is a data and document problem; pattern (b) is a multi-tenant account and pricing problem. Treat the term as a description of a flow, not a product category.

### 1.5 Punchout and procurement integration

- cXML, the protocol behind SAP Ariba punchout, defines "PunchOut" as "an easy-to-implement protocol for interactive sessions managed across the Internet". For procurement punchout, "procurement applications display a button instead of product or pricing details. When users click this button, their Web browsers display pages from the supplier's local website … When users are done selecting items, they click a button that returns the order information to the procurement application. The fully configured products and their prices appear within users' purchase requisitions." The guide names the messages (PunchOutSetupRequest, PunchOutOrderMessage, OrderRequest), says "Procurement applications, such as SAP Ariba Buying, SAP Ariba Buying and Invoicing, and Ariba Buyer, use cXML", and that DTDs for all versions live on cXML.org [VERIFIED: https://xml.cxml.org/current/cXMLUsersGuide.pdf, version 1.2.037, published 2018-02-09] **(evidence age: 8 years, yet still the "current" link)**.
- SAP's OCI (Open Catalog Interface) is "SAP's native standard for this type of catalog connectivity": the buyer's SAP procurement system opens the supplier catalog over HTTP (POST preferred), and at checkout the supplier posts line items to the `HOOK_URL` as `NEW_ITEM-DESCRIPTION[n]`, `NEW_ITEM-VENDORMAT[n]`, `NEW_ITEM-QUANTITY[n]`, `NEW_ITEM-PRICE[n]` and similar fields; OCI 5.0 added JSON; compared with cXML, OCI "lacks native PO and invoice automation" and usually uses a single shared login [WIDELY REPORTED: https://tradecentric.com/blog/sap-srm-oci-punchout/, 2026-05-19].
- TrueCommerce lists "punchout integrations" alongside storefronts and ordering portals as part of its B2B e-commerce offer [VERIFIED: https://www.truecommerce.com/, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT] Punchout inverts the usual flow: the *buyer's* procurement system owns the requisition and the purchase order; the supplier's web shop is only a catalogue session. The order arrives later as a cXML OrderRequest or an EDI ORDERS, so the shop must never treat a punchout cart as an order.

**For newcomers.**
- A *company account* is a buyer organisation with several people and locations, each with roles, that buys under agreed terms; it is not a consumer account with a company name.
- *Contract pricing* is a price agreed with one customer (or group), applied automatically when that customer buys; it coexists with list and tiered prices and must say which wins.
- A *quote* is a seller's offer at a stated price and validity that the buyer can accept into an order; an *approval workflow* is the buyer's own rule that an order above a limit needs a manager's consent before it becomes binding.

**Architecture implications.** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]
- The B2B objects (company, location, contact, terms, catalog) are the first place where the *customer* record stops being flat; whichever system owns them (platform or ERP) must be named, because payment terms and credit limits are finance facts that the ERP usually owns (Part 4).
- Starting: use the platform's native B2B features and draft orders; no quote object. Growing: contract price lists per customer, net terms, a single system of record for company accounts. Omnichannel: quotes and approvals as explicit objects, a B2B stock allocation (fulfillment brief), punchout only for the few buyers who demand it. Group: business units with inherited rules, per-brand price lists, and an ERP-fed credit check at checkout.
- A quote freezes a price; Adobe's "the original price is used" rule and commercetools' "legally binding" wording are the same design decision. Chapter 07 should present the quote as the one place where a price becomes a promise with a validity date.

## PART 2: EDI — documents between companies

### 2.1 Standards: UN/EDIFACT, ANSI X12, GS1 EANCOM

- UN/EDIFACT is "an international standard for electronic data interchange (EDI) developed for the United Nations", approved and published by UNECE; its syntax rules were approved as ISO 9735 in 1987; UN/CEFACT maintains it; interchanges contain messages, messages contain segments, segments contain composites and elements; directories are released twice a year (D.20B, D.22B are cited) [WIDELY REPORTED: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDIFACT, 2026]. (unece.org refused automated fetches.)
- "The GS1 EANCOM® standard is a subset of selected UN/EDIFACT messages, based on UN/EDIFACT directory D.01B, syntax version 4 which was released by UN/CEFACT in 2001. It includes only components that are mandatory and those relevant for the GS1 user community." It replaced EANCOM 1997 (based on D.96A) and "should be read in conjunction with the 'GS1 General Specifications'" [VERIFIED: https://www.gs1.org/sites/default/files/docs/eancom/s4/part1s4.pdf, "GS1 EANCOM® 2002, Edition 2016 Upd. 2021, Syntax 4"] **(evidence age: the standard is from 2002; its manual was last updated 2021)**.
- GS1's implementation survey for 2020: "A total of 43 EANCOM® messages have been reported as implemented, the most popular being ORDERS (Order), INVOIC (Invoice), DESADV (Despatch Advice), PRICAT (Price/Sales Catalogue), ORDRSP (Order Response), RECADV (Receiving Advice) and INVRPT (Inventory Report)", and "The most popular communication channel used to send the EDI messages is AS2, followed by Web Services and Value Added Networks" [VERIFIED: https://www.gs1.org/sites/gs1/files/2021-10/edi-implementationsurvey2021std.pdf, published 2021] **(evidence age: 5 years)**.
- ANSI X12: "X12 defines and maintains transaction sets that establish the data content exchanged for specific business purposes"; X12 is chartered by ANSI and works through its Accredited Standards Committee. Descriptions: 850 Purchase Order covers "the placement of purchase orders" and "should not be used to convey purchase order changes or purchase order acknowledgment information"; 855 is the seller's acknowledgment; 856 Ship Notice/Manifest is used to "list the contents of a shipment of goods as well as additional information relating to the shipment"; 810 Invoice covers "the billing for goods and services provided"; 997 Functional Acknowledgment reports "the results of the syntactical analysis" and "does not cover the semantic meaning"; 846 lets "a seller of goods and services … provide inventory information to a prospective purchaser" [VERIFIED: https://x12.org/products/transaction-sets, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- Message pairs the book should use (EDIFACT / X12): ORDERS / 850 (order), ORDRSP / 855 (order response), DESADV / 856 (despatch advice = ASN), INVOIC / 810 (invoice), INVRPT / 846 (inventory report), CONTRL / 997 (syntax acknowledgement). [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT] The pairs are functional equivalents, not field-for-field translations; a mapping between them is real work.
- Which region uses which: [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT] EDIFACT/EANCOM dominates European retail (the Dutch retailer documents below are EDIFACT-only), X12 dominates North American retail (Walmart, Target, Amazon vendor programmes); a European brand selling to a US chain needs both.

### 2.2 Transport: AS2, SFTP, VAN, Peppol

- AS2 (RFC 4130, July 2005, Proposed Standard) "describes how to exchange structured business data securely using the HTTP transfer protocol, instead of SMTP; the applicability statement for SMTP is found in RFC 3335. Structured business data may be XML; Electronic Data Interchange (EDI) in either the American National Standards Committee (ANSI) X12 format or the UN Electronic Data Interchange for Administration, Commerce, and Transport (UN/EDIFACT) format; or other structured data formats. The data is packaged using standard MIME structures. Authentication and data confidentiality are obtained by using Cryptographic Message Syntax with S/MIME security body parts. Authenticated acknowledgements make use of multipart/signed Message Disposition Notification (MDN) responses to the original HTTP message." [VERIFIED: https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc4130, published 2005-07] **(evidence age: 21 years; still the protocol in production use)**.
- A VAN is a provider-operated mailbox network; Jumbo's supplier page says the retailer "uses Descartes for EDI translation and transmission, via X400 or go-secure", that "Jumbo does not yet support the AS2 protocol", and that "Costs for setting up and maintaining the connection are borne by the supplier" [VERIFIED: https://www.jumbo.com/supplier/edi, EDI profile dated 2026-05-20 but body text referring to 2014 targets, accessed 2026-11] **(evidence age: mixed; the page's DESADV deadline is "by the end of 2014")**.
- Peppol is "an interoperability framework for exchanging standard business documents, such as eOrders and eInvoices, over an open and secure network", run by OpenPeppol AISBL (a non-profit under Belgian law, Brussels), using "the four-corner model, which enables buyers and suppliers to connect via any Peppol-accredited Service Provider"; it began in 2008 as an EU-funded pilot and OpenPeppol took over in 2012 [VERIFIED: https://peppol.org/about/, n.d., copyright 2022-2023, accessed 2026-11]. Peppol Authorities by country include BOSA (Belgium), the Netherlands Peppol Authority, KoSIT (Germany), DGFIP (France), AGID (Italy), the Polish Ministry of Economic Development, the Australian Tax Office, IMDA (Singapore) and Japan's Digital Agency; OpenPeppol "also acts as the Peppol Authority in jurisdictions where no Peppol Authority exists" [VERIFIED: https://peppol.org/about/peppol-authorities/, n.d., accessed 2026-11] ⚠.
- [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT] Four transports, one decision: AS2 and SFTP are point-to-point (each partner is a certificate and an endpoint to manage); a VAN and Peppol are networks (connect once, reach many). Peppol is the only one with a public directory (the SMP/SML lookup by participant identifier) and the only one that mandates a document standard.

### 2.3 Identifiers: GTIN, GLN, SSCC

All three are defined in the GS1 General Specifications, Release 26.0, ratified January 2026 [VERIFIED: https://ref.gs1.org/standards/genspecs/, Release 26.0, published 2026-01]:
- "The Global Trade Item Number (GTIN) is the GS1 identifier for any Made-to-Stock product or service" (the number on the barcode of a sellable unit, EAN-13 in Europe, UPC-A in North America).
- "The Global Location Number (GLN) is the GS1 identifier for a physical location that may be fixed or mobile, or for a legal or functional entity" (the address keys in EDI: a buyer's DC, a supplier's warehouse, a bill-to party).
- "The Serial Shipping Container Code (SSCC) is the GS1 identifier for an individual logistics unit"; "The SSCC is the only GS1 key that SHALL be used as the identifier of a logistic unit"; its Application Identifier is "(00)"; and "The SSCC is used with EDI communications to enable identification and traceability."
- Jumbo requires that "The GLN of the supplier in the DESADV message (NAD+SU) should be equal to the GLN where Jumbo has sent the order to" [VERIFIED: https://www.jumbo.com/supplier/edi, accessed 2026-11]; bol requires that "SSCC codes must be sent in the EDI pre-announcement without the AI" [VERIFIED: https://leveranciers.bol.com/en/need-help/delivery/sscc-edi-suppliers/, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT] GTIN identifies *what*, GLN identifies *who/where*, SSCC identifies *this pallet*. The ASN is the document that ties the three together: each SSCC lists the GTINs and quantities it contains, addressed from one GLN to another. A WMS that cannot print an SSCC per pallet and report its contents cannot produce a compliant DESADV.

### 2.4 The advance shipping notice, label compliance and chargebacks

- Jumbo's mechanised DC guidelines (version 2025-10): "Every delivery to our mechanised DC must be preceded by a DESADV message. This message must be completed at level 4, according to the GS1 Netherlands guidelines … This is only possible if all information at pallet level is available in the DESADV message"; "A delivery without an accompanying DESADV message cannot be processed and is therefore refused!"; "On the SSCC label is a GS1 barcode. Without a GS1 barcode a shipment is refused"; "The SSCC label forms the link with the DESADV despatch message" (translated from Dutch) [VERIFIED: https://www.jumbo.com/dam/leveranciers/JUMBO5735-HAND%20OUT-RICHTLIJNEN-GEMECHANISEERD-DC-NL-S2.1.doc.pdf, version 2025-10].
- Jumbo's general purchasing conditions (9 April 2026), article 5.13: "Supplier undertakes to conduct all communication concerning the flow of goods (being order, packing slip (DESADV) and invoice) via EDI. Supplier shall comply with all GS1 standards as regards the content of EDI messages … If Supplier fails to do so, Supplier forfeits € 70 (seventy euro) to Buyer per message. Buyer has the right to refuse the delivery if Supplier has not used the packing slip (DESADV) and SSCC on receipt." Article 14.4–14.5 add a contractual penalty of € 250 per order when less than 90% of the products on an order line are delivered on time, in full and as agreed, and a period-level (4-week) penalty when less than 99% is delivered correctly, computed as (99% minus the actual percentage) × net invoice value × a multiplier (translated from Dutch) [VERIFIED: https://jumbo.com/dam/service/leverancier/jumbo-algemene-voorwaarden-2026-04-09.pdf, published 2026-04-09] ⚠.

[ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT] "Mapping" is the translation between a partner's message implementation guideline and the supplier's order, shipment and invoice records; every retailer has its own guideline, so the cost of EDI is per partner, not per standard — which is the business model of the providers above.
- Why EDI persists: [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT] (1) the buyer, not the supplier, chooses the channel, and large retailers write EDI into purchasing conditions with penalties (Jumbo above); (2) the documents are stable and carry legal and logistics meaning (an ASN is a receiving plan, an INVOIC is a payment claim); (3) network effects: once a retailer's hundreds of suppliers are mapped, switching formats costs more than keeping them; (4) the e-invoicing mandates of Part 3 are the first regulation to force a different structured format on the same relationship, and Germany's rule that EDI procedures remain usable through 2027 shows the regulator accommodating EDI rather than replacing it.

**For newcomers.**
- *EDI* (electronic data interchange) is the exchange of standard business documents (order, despatch advice, invoice) between two companies' systems without e-mail, PDF or retyping.
- An *ASN* (advance shipping notice; DESADV in EDIFACT, 856 in X12) is a message sent at despatch telling the receiver what is coming, pallet by pallet, so receipt can be scanned instead of counted.
- An *SSCC* is the 18-digit GS1 serial number on a pallet or carton label that an ASN refers to, so the receiver can scan one barcode and know the pallet's contents.

**Architecture implications.** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]
- EDI is an *integration channel into the order record*, not a separate order system: an ORDERS message should create the same order object as a web order, flagged by channel, so allocation, invoicing and the ledger see one shape. The common failure is the ERP (or the EDI provider's portal) holding EDI orders while the platform holds web orders, which is the "no single order record" problem in the scenario ledger.
- The DESADV must be generated from the WMS's actual pack confirmation (which SSCC holds which GTINs), never from the order; the INVOIC must be generated from what was shipped and priced, never from the order either. Each document has a different system of truth.
- Starting: no EDI; refuse or use a provider's web portal. Growing: first retailer forces EDI; use a provider (VAN or Peppol access point) with web-EDI or a connector to the ERP. Omnichannel: integrated EDI through the provider into the order record via the iPaaS, SSCC labels from the WMS, a compliance scorecard per partner, chargeback disputes with ASN evidence. Group: an EDI/e-document gateway as a shared service with per-partner mappings under version control.

## PART 3: E-invoicing and the mandates

### 3.1 The standard stack: EN 16931, UBL, Peppol BIS Billing 3.0

- Definition: "An electronic invoice (eInvoice) is an invoice that has been issued, transmitted and received in a structured data format which allows for its automatic and electronic processing, as defined in Directive 2014/55/EU. A structured electronic invoice contains data from the supplier in a machine-readable format, that can be automatically imported into the buyer's Account Payable (AP) system without requiring manual entering." [VERIFIED: https://ec.europa.eu/digital-building-blocks/sites/display/DIGITAL/What+is+eInvoicing, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- EN 16931 is the European semantic data model of a core invoice, with syntax bindings to UBL and UN/CEFACT CII; the CEN and EC pages describing it could not be fetched in this session, so this sentence is [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT], corroborated by the German FAQ's reference to "the EN 16931 standard" and field "BT-10 (Buyer reference)" [VERIFIED: https://www.bundesfinanzministerium.de/Content/DE/FAQ/e-rechnung.html, n.d., accessed 2026-11] and by Peppol's statement below.
- Peppol BIS Billing 3.0: "This specification, is a Core Invoice Usage Specification (CIUS) of EN 16931"; seller and buyer are mandatory (`cac:AccountingSupplierParty`, `cac:AccountingCustomerParty`); "Each invoice line shall have the invoiced item VAT category code (BT-151)"; "The invoice total VAT amount (BT-110) is the sum of all VAT Category VAT amounts (BT-117)"; invoices use type code 380 and credit notes 381; examples are UBL and Appendix B covers CII [VERIFIED: https://docs.peppol.eu/poacc/billing/3.0/bis/, version 3.0.21, accessed 2026-11]. The current release is 3.0.21, "Date of publication: 2026-05-20", "Mandatory to use from: 2026-08-17"; "All validation changes apply to both UBL and CII validation rules" [VERIFIED: https://docs.peppol.eu/poacc/billing/3.0/release-notes/, published 2026-05-20] ⚠.
- [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT] Three layers to keep apart in chapter 22: the *semantic model* (EN 16931: which fields an invoice has), the *syntax* (UBL XML or CII XML), and the *network and profile* (Peppol BIS Billing 3.0 over the Peppol four-corner network). National formats are profiles of the same model (Germany's XRechnung, France's Factur-X, Italy's FatturaPA being the exception as a national XML aligned to it).

### 3.3 What an e-invoice contains, and EDI INVOIC versus a Peppol invoice

- Content, from Peppol BIS Billing 3.0 above: identified seller and buyer (with VAT identifiers), document type (380 invoice, 381 credit note), invoice lines each with a VAT category code, a VAT breakdown per category and rate whose sum equals the total VAT, and payment information; attachments may be embedded (PDF, images, CSV, spreadsheets per Belgium's FAQ). Belgium adds that VAT totals in a foreign-currency invoice must also be "stated in the national currency" [VERIFIED: https://einvoice.belgium.be/en/FAQ/specific-questions-about-e-invoicing, accessed 2026-11].
- Germany's FAQ treats EDI as a distinct procedure: an "EDI procedure that does not already meet the requirements of an e-invoice" may be used to the end of 2027, after which it must either meet EN 16931 extraction requirements or be replaced [VERIFIED: https://www.bundesfinanzministerium.de/Content/DE/FAQ/e-rechnung.html, accessed 2026-11].
- [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT] The difference in one table:

| | EDI INVOIC (EANCOM/X12 810) | Peppol / EN 16931 invoice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who defines the content | Bilateral implementation guideline per retailer, on top of the standard | The European semantic standard, with a published CIUS and validator |
| Purpose | Supply-chain settlement: match to order and receipt (the "four-way match"), with retailer-specific fields | Tax-compliant legal invoice that any receiver's AP system can read |
| Addressing | GLNs agreed per partner | Peppol participant identifier, looked up in a public directory |
| Transport | AS2, VAN, SFTP, per partner | Any accredited access point (four-corner) |
| Validation | Syntax acknowledgement (CONTRL/997) and partner-side rejection | Schematron rules at the access point; national clearance in Italy and Poland |
| Legal weight in 2026 | Accepted in Germany through 2027; in Belgium only if the structured e-invoice requirement is met | The format the mandates require |

### 3.4 For newcomers and implications

**For newcomers.**
- An *e-invoice* is an invoice issued, transmitted and received as structured data (XML) that software can process; a PDF by e-mail is not one.
- *Peppol* is the open network and document standard that Belgium mandates and the Netherlands plans to use; a business connects through an *access point* (a service provider) and reaches any other participant.
- *EN 16931* is the European standard listing what an invoice must contain; UBL is the XML syntax most e-invoices use to carry it.

**Architecture implications.** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]
- The invoice becomes a *transmitted* legal document with a delivery receipt: the system that numbers and issues invoices (usually the ERP) must also hold the Peppol transmission status, rejections and the archive. Commerce platforms do not issue tax invoices in these regimes; they produce orders and order confirmations.
- Dates are the requirement: receive in Germany since 2025 (an inbox suffices); issue in Belgium since January 2026 for any business customer there; France from September 2026 (receive) and 2027 (issue, for SMEs); the Netherlands in 2030. A European brand selling B2B in NL, BE and DE already has three different obligations.
- Clearance models (Italy's SdI, Poland's KSeF) put the state in the invoice path; the invoice is valid only once the platform accepts it, so invoicing becomes synchronous with an external system and must handle rejection as a first-class state.
- Starting: ERP or accounting package with a Peppol-capable provider. Growing: a Peppol access point connected to the ERP; invoice numbering only in the ERP. Omnichannel: an e-document gateway (EDI and Peppol) with per-country format rules and a regulatory calendar (chapter 36). Group: country entities with different clearance systems behind one invoicing service.

## PART 4: ERP and the back office

### 4.1 What an ERP does in a commerce business

- Dutch mid-market ERPs describe themselves in the same terms. Exact for trade companies: "Manage stock, orders and accounting in one system", with purchasing (reorder suggestions, supplier price lists, dropshipping, "mandatory order approval"), multi-warehouse stock with serial and batch tracking and stock reservations ("Reserve available and expected stock for customers"), quotes, sales orders, returns and invoicing, "(e-)invoicing and automatic invoice processing" included, "Seamless integration with your webshop or logistics partners" through an app store; list prices €269 to €699 per month excluding VAT (translated from Dutch) [VERIFIED: https://www.exact.com/nl/bedrijven/handel, n.d., accessed 2026-11] ⚠. AFAS: one integrated system with one database ("Data only needs to be entered once, and processes run automatically"), covering CRM, finance, HRM and payroll, projects and order management ("Sales, purchasing, stock and delivery connect directly to each other"), used by "More than 14,000 organisations" (translated from Dutch) [VERIFIED: https://www.afas.nl/software/erp, n.d., accessed 2026-11].
- Inventory valuation is an accounting function. IAS 2 measures inventories "at the lower of cost and net realisable value", allows "specific identification of cost for items of inventory that are not ordinarily interchangeable" and "the first-in, first-out or weighted average cost formula for items that are ordinarily interchangeable", and includes in cost "all costs of purchase, costs of conversion … and other costs incurred in bringing the inventories to their present location and condition" [VERIFIED: https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ias-2-inventories/, n.d., accessed 2026-11]. Business Central implements FIFO, LIFO ("Disallowed in many countries/regions, as it can be used to depress profit"), Average, Specific and Standard costing, and warns "You can't change an item's costing method if item ledger entries exist for the item" [VERIFIED: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/design-details-costing-methods, published 2026-03-17].
- [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT] The ERP's four jobs in a commerce business: the ledger (every order, payment fee, refund and VAT amount ends as a posting), purchasing (supplier orders, landed cost), inventory *valuation* (cost per unit, the balance-sheet value of stock, which is distinct from inventory *availability*), and master data (items, customers, suppliers, terms). The commerce platform knows stock *quantities*; only the ERP knows what the stock is *worth*.

### 4.2 ERP connectors as products

**Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central ↔ Shopify** (the best-documented connector found).

### 4.3 ERP-as-hub, commerce-as-hub, OMS-as-hub

- The OMS vendor OneStock argues for the OMS as hub between ERP and channels: "ERP is the master for product data (if no dedicated PIM system exists)"; "OMS is the master for enterprise inventory availability"; "ERPs update inventory in batch cycles, hourly or nightly in many implementations"; "The system was designed for process consistency, not fulfillment optimization"; "ERP isn't broken. It's just not built for everything"; "ERP is essential. It's not going anywhere, and it shouldn't." Its four reasons the ERP fails at orchestration: batch inventory updates, simple allocation rules, costly customisation for ship-from-store and marketplace fulfilment, and slow change [VERIFIED: https://www.onestock-retail.com/blog/oms-vs-erp-the-architecture-shift-reshaping-commerce/, n.d., copyright 2026, accessed 2026-11] (vendor source; favours its own category).
- Microsoft's connector documentation makes the ERP-centred case for master data without using the word hub: customer records, payment terms and credit limits are managed in Business Central and exported to Shopify, except where new buyers sign up on the storefront [VERIFIED: synchronize-customers, 2026-10-14, above].
- Sana's "built on your SAP or Dynamics ERP" is the commerce-reads-ERP variant [VERIFIED: sana-commerce.com, above].
- [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT] The three positions and when each is right:

| Hub | What it means | Fits | Breaks when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ERP as hub | Every system integrates with the ERP; the ERP routes orders to the WMS, holds stock, prices and customers | One channel, one warehouse, B2B-heavy (Sana's market), small team | Peak traffic and real-time availability (batch cycles), several channels writing to one monolith, slow change |
| Commerce platform as hub | The platform is the system of record for orders, customers and stock; the ERP is a finance subscriber | D2C-first brands on a hosted platform, one or two channels | Stores, wholesale and marketplaces each have their own order source; the platform's model cannot hold EDI or POS orders well |
| OMS as hub | An order-management layer owns the order record, allocation and availability across channels; ERP owns finance and valuation; platform owns the storefront | Omnichannel (stage 3) and group (stage 4) | Added cost and a second integration surface; wrong at stage 1–2 |
| iPaaS (no hub) | Point-to-point flows through an integration platform; no system owns the cross-channel order record | Growing stage with few flows | Thirty-four integrations and three definitions of "order" (the scenario ledger's baseline) |

The scenario's Waypoint decision ("the ERP is the system of record for finance only") is the OMS-as-hub position stated as an ownership rule.

### 4.4 Batch versus event integration with ERPs

- Evidence that ERP integration is batch by default: OneStock's "hourly or nightly" statement; Shopify's "real time or through a daily batch" for financials; Business Central's job-queue scheduling for customers, companies and orders ("You can schedule the task to be performed in an automated manner") [VERIFIED: OneStock, Shopify blog and Microsoft pages above].
- [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT] The rule the book should state: *finance can be batch, availability cannot.* Postings, payouts and invoices tolerate a daily cycle; stock and order status do not, which is why the scenario's 15-minute/hourly chain oversold at peak. Push events (order created, shipment confirmed, stock changed) from the system that owns the fact; let the ERP consume them in its own rhythm, and never let the ERP's cycle time become the storefront's availability latency.

### 4.5 Master data ownership between ERP, PIM and commerce

- Akeneo: a PIM "acts as a central source of truth for data" for product content, storing technical data ("SKU numbers", dimensions, materials), "Sales and pricing data", media ("Associate images, videos, documents with each product") and localisation ("Localize and translate product information for each market and region"); it "collects data from your ERP, MDM, PLM, and other systems" and should "integrate smoothly with existing systems such as ERP, CRM, and eCommerce platforms" [VERIFIED: https://www.akeneo.com/what-is-a-pim/, n.d., copyright 2026, accessed 2026-11] (vendor source).
- OneStock: "ERP is the master for product data (if no dedicated PIM system exists)" [VERIFIED above].
- Microsoft: name the system of record for customers; Shopify: name the system of record per field [VERIFIED above].
- [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT] The ownership table the book can use:

| Data | Usual owner | Readers | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Item identity (SKU, GTIN), cost, supplier, purchasing units | ERP | PIM, platform, WMS, EDI | The item must exist in the ERP before it can be bought or valued |
| Product content (names, descriptions, attributes, media, translations, channel variants) | PIM (or the platform at stage 1–2) | Platform, marketplaces, EDI PRICAT | The scenario's "three places" problem is the absence of this owner |
| List and contract prices | Pricing engine or ERP price lists (B2B); platform (D2C) | Platform, EDI, POS | Chapter 07's price-source decision |
| Customer accounts, payment terms, credit limits | ERP (B2B); platform (D2C) | Platform, EDI, invoicing | Business Central's advice |
| Stock quantity and availability | WMS (physical), OMS or platform (available-to-promise) | Platform, marketplaces, EDI INVRPT | Never the ERP at stage 3+ |
| Stock value | ERP | Finance | IAS 2 / costing method |
| Orders | OMS (stage 3+), platform (stage 1–2), never the EDI portal | ERP (for posting), WMS | One order record |
| Invoices, credit notes, ledger | ERP | Peppol/EDI gateway, finance | Numbering and archive live here |

**For newcomers.**
- An *ERP* (enterprise resource planning system) is the back-office system that keeps the books, buys stock from suppliers, values the stock and holds master records of items, customers and suppliers.
- An *integration hub* is the one system every other system connects through and that owns the shared records; "ERP-as-hub" and "OMS-as-hub" name which system that is.
- *Master data* is the slow-changing reference data (items, customers, prices, locations) that many systems copy; *master data ownership* says which system may create and change each record.

**Architecture implications.** [ASSUMPTION/JUDGEMENT]
- Starting: ERP or accounting package connected by a vendor connector (Business Central's preinstalled connector is the dated example); the platform owns orders and products; the ERP owns the ledger and purchasing. Growing: the ERP owns items, customers and B2B terms; the platform owns D2C customers; a connector or iPaaS carries orders in and stock out; the first ERP partner-built integration appears and becomes a dependency. Omnichannel: the ERP retreats to finance, purchasing and valuation; an OMS owns orders and availability; a PIM owns content; the ERP partner's connector is replaced by owned integration contracts. Group: a finance ERP per legal entity, shared OMS and PIM, and an e-document gateway.
- What never belongs in the ERP: storefront sessions and carts, search, promotions logic, real-time availability for checkout, marketplace listings, and customer-facing order status. What always belongs there: the posting of every order, payment, fee, refund and VAT amount, the cost of stock, and the legal invoice.
- The ERP implementation partner is an architectural dependency: the connector is usually theirs, undocumented, and priced into the ERP relationship (the scenario's "built by the ERP partner"). Treat the connector as a contract to own, with the field-level ownership table above as its specification.
