> **KEY POINTS:**
>
> * **Look a term up, then read its neighbors.** Each section collects one part's terms, so the rows around a term carry the contrasts that cause real design errors: product versus variant versus SKU, on hand versus committed versus available-to-promise, authorization versus capture, order versus shipment versus invoice, system of record versus copy.
> * **One term, one meaning, one owning chapter.** Every row links to the chapter that owns or best explains the term, and the definitions follow how those chapters define them. When a chapter and this page disagree, one of them needs correcting.
> * **Rules, standards and vendor terms date quickly.** Laws, protocols, products and their status are given as of November 2026, following the sources cited in the chapters. Check the current source before a definition here goes into a contract, a vendor evaluation or a design decision.

<br>

The words that cause the most trouble in commerce are ordinary ones. **"Order" means three different numbers** to the platform, the ERP and the warehouse report at Kestrel & Pine ([[commerce-data-and-analytics]]), and **"in stock" can mean on hand, available or "the last figure the connector received an hour ago"** ([[inventory-and-availability]]). This glossary pins each term to one definition and one owning chapter, so that the chapters, their readers and the AI tools that read them use the words the same way.

## How to Use This Glossary

The sections follow the book's structure. The first collects the **six evidence labels**, the **three take-away tiers**, the **four scale stages** and the **sixteen failure patterns** from the Preface; the next eleven follow **Parts I to XI**, from the business of commerce through catalog, order, fulfillment, channels, integration, platform and trust to the journeys, the decisions that change with scale and the public names the case studies rely on. Then come two reference tables: the **standards, protocols and regulations** with their scope (EU, a country, the US or global), and **Kestrel & Pine's fictional names**. The **alphabetical index** at the end lists every term, alias and abbreviation with a link to the section that defines it.

Each section opens with the chapters that own its terms, then a table with 391 rows in all. The definitions are short teaching descriptions for a reader new to the field, not legal text, standards or vendor documentation; the chapter in the last column gives the full treatment, the sources and the worked example. Rows that combine two or more terms with a slash define all of them, because the difference between them is the point. Acronyms are expanded in the term or the definition. Kestrel & Pine, named in some rows and in its own section, is the fictional outdoor brand this book follows ([[meet-kestrel-and-pine]]); its names and numbers are illustrations, not facts about any real company.

## <a id="g-commerce"></a>Part I — COMMERCE: What a Commerce Platform Is

The business vocabulary and the first architecture words: what an order is and who promises what, the business models and the numbers that judge them, the capability map, ownership of facts, and the kinds of platform on the market. [[journey-of-an-order]] owns the promise and the handoff; [[commerce-business-models]] the models and economics; [[commerce-capability-map]] the capability and the layer; [[boundaries-and-systems-of-record]] the system of record and the bounded context; [[commerce-platform-landscape]] the platform shapes.

| Term | Definition | Chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Promise** | A statement the merchant makes to the customer that some system will later have to make true: "in stock", "€199", "ordered before 22:00, delivered tomorrow". Promises are made early, in seconds, and kept late, over days. | [[journey-of-an-order]] |
| **Handoff** | The moment responsibility for an order passes from one system to another, for example from the platform to the warehouse. Most failures happen at handoffs. | [[journey-of-an-order]] |
| **Order** | The record of what was bought, by whom, at what price and tax, to be delivered where and how, and paid how. It is not a cart, not a shipment and not an invoice. | [[journey-of-an-order]], [[order-management]] |
| **B2C / D2C** | **B2C** (business to consumer) is selling to private consumers. **D2C** (direct to consumer) is a brand selling through channels it operates itself, its own web shop and stores, rather than through a retailer. | [[commerce-business-models]], [[meet-kestrel-and-pine]] |
| **Wholesale / B2B e-commerce** | **Wholesale** is a brand selling to retailers and distributors who resell. **B2B** (business to business) e-commerce is business buyers ordering electronically, through a web portal or by structured messages between systems. | [[commerce-business-models]], [[b2b-commerce]] |
| **B2B2C** | Business to business to consumer: a brand sells through a partner's consumer channel while keeping control of its product, content and price. | [[commerce-business-models]] |
| **Marketplace selling / marketplace operating** | Selling on someone else's marketplace as one seller among many, as against running the marketplace and taking a fee from sellers. The two have opposite architectures. | [[commerce-business-models]], [[marketplaces-and-channel-integration]] |
| **Omnichannel** | Stores and digital channels sharing stock, orders and customers, so a shopper can buy in one channel and collect, return or get service in another. Also the name of the third scale stage. | [[commerce-business-models]], [[stores-and-omnichannel]] |
| **Channel** | Any place where a customer can buy and a promise is made: a storefront, a store till, a marketplace listing, the B2B portal, an EDI link, a click-and-collect option. | [[inventory-and-availability]], [[commerce-scaling-stages]] |
| **Seller of record** | The party that legally sells to the customer: the one whose name is on the invoice, who owes the VAT and who answers for the consumer's rights. | [[commerce-business-models]], [[case-amazon]] |
| **GMV (gross merchandise value)** | The value of orders passing through a platform, including what the platform does not keep; several vendors price on it. Definitions differ on refunds and fees (Shopify's filing calls it gross merchandise volume and counts it net of refunds), so always read the source's definition. | [[commerce-business-models]], [[commerce-platform-landscape]], [[case-shopify]] |
| **AOV (average order value)** | Revenue divided by the number of orders. Kestrel & Pine's online AOV in 2026 is €92. | [[commerce-business-models]] |
| **Conversion rate / cart abandonment** | **Conversion rate** is purchases divided by visits. **Cart abandonment** is the share of carts started and not bought; extra costs at checkout are the top reason shoppers give. | [[commerce-business-models]], [[cart-and-checkout]] |
| **Gross margin** | Revenue minus the cost of the goods sold, as a share of revenue; markdowns, returns and fees eat into it. Kestrel & Pine's is 54%. | [[commerce-business-models]] |
| **Return rate** | The share of items, or of orders, that come back; always say which. Kestrel & Pine's online item return rate is 24% and its order return rate 19%. | [[commerce-business-models]], [[returns-and-exchanges]] |
| **EDI-type sales** | Orders received as structured messages between companies' systems rather than through a web page; for business customers in the EU they are the majority of electronic sales. | [[commerce-business-models]], [[edi-and-e-invoicing]] |
| **Capability** | A job the business needs done, named without saying who does it: "calculate the price a given customer pays for a given product today". | [[commerce-capability-map]] |
| **System / vendor product** | A **system** is a piece of software that does one or more capabilities; a **vendor product** is a system someone sells, usually claiming several capabilities at once. | [[commerce-capability-map]] |
| **Layer** | A group of capabilities that share a purpose and a rhythm: the ones a shopper touches, the ones that describe what is for sale, the ones that move money and orders, the ones that move goods, partners and back office, and the foundations. | [[commerce-capability-map]] |
| **Business fact** | Something the business needs to be true about the world: this variant has this GTIN, this customer pays this price, there are 14 units of this size in Duiven. | [[commerce-capability-map]], [[boundaries-and-systems-of-record]] |
| **System of record** | The one system whose value of a fact is the truth when copies disagree; every other holder keeps a copy. For master data it is often called the master. | [[boundaries-and-systems-of-record]], [[identity-and-master-data]] |
| **Copy / writer** | A **copy** is a business fact held in any system other than its system of record. A **writer** is any system, app, script, job or person that can change a fact in a given system; a fact with two writers drifts. | [[boundaries-and-systems-of-record]] |
| **Bounded context** | Eric Evans's term from *Domain-Driven Design* (2003): a boundary inside which a model, and the words in it, have one agreed meaning. "Order" and "stock" mean different things in different contexts. | [[boundaries-and-systems-of-record]] |
| **Commerce platform** | The software that shows products, holds a cart, takes an order and the payment, and hands the order to whoever fulfills it. | [[commerce-platform-landscape]] |
| **SaaS (software as a service)** | Software the vendor runs for many customers at once and rents by the month; you configure it and the vendor upgrades everyone on the same day. | [[commerce-platform-landscape]], [[build-buy-or-assemble]] |
| **Suite (enterprise suite)** | One vendor's set of commerce, order management and content modules designed to work together, usually implemented by a partner. | [[commerce-platform-landscape]], [[build-buy-or-assemble]] |
| **Open source platform** | Commerce software whose code is published under a license that lets you run and change it; you or your agency host, extend and upgrade it, and forks (such as Mage-OS from Magento) can appear. | [[commerce-platform-landscape]] |
| **Composable** | Buying commerce as separate services, from one or several vendors, and assembling them yourself rather than taking one suite; every join between them is then yours. | [[commerce-platform-landscape]], [[build-buy-or-assemble]], [[case-composable-movement]] |
| **MACH** | The acronym a vendor alliance coined in June 2020 for Microservices, API-first, Cloud-native SaaS and Headless; founders were commercetools, Contentstack, EPAM and Valtech. It names a style, not a product. | [[commerce-platform-landscape]], [[case-composable-movement]] |
| **ERP-centered commerce** | A storefront attached to the ERP that already owns stock, orders and finance, with the web shop treated as a front end. | [[commerce-platform-landscape]] |
| **Specialist layer / specialist group** | A **specialist layer** is a product for one capability (OMS, PIM, WMS, returns, search, channel, iPaaS, payments) added beside a platform. A **specialist group** is several specialist products under one owner, kept as separate brands but pre-integrated. | [[commerce-platform-landscape]] |

## <a id="g-catalog"></a>Part II — CATALOG: Products, Prices and Promises

What is for sale, at what price, with which discount, how shoppers find it and whether it is in stock. [[product-information]] owns product, variant, SKU, GTIN and PIM; [[pricing-architecture]] the price list and the contract price; [[commerce-promotions]] the promotion and stored value; [[commerce-search-and-discovery]] the search index, relevance, merchandising rule and facet; [[inventory-and-availability]] stock, available-to-promise and reservations.

| Term | Definition | Chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Product / variant** | A **product** is the sellable concept a shopper browses ("Ridgewalker shell jacket, women's"). A **variant** is one buyable combination of option values (size M, color moss) and must stay stable once it has been sold. | [[product-information]], [[journey-of-an-order]] |
| **SKU (stock-keeping unit)** | The merchant's own code for one sellable variant, the thing counted on a shelf and put in a parcel; nobody outside the company understands it, which is its strength. The book also uses SKU for the sellable variant itself. | [[product-information]], [[meet-kestrel-and-pine]] |
| **GTIN / EAN-13** | The **Global Trade Item Number** under the barcode, issued through GS1 so that every company's systems mean the same item by it; never reused for a different item. In European retail it is the 13-digit **EAN-13**. | [[product-information]], [[edi-and-e-invoicing]] |
| **Option / option value** | An **option** is a dimension of choice (size); an **option value** is one choice (M). Variants are combinations of option values. | [[product-information]] |
| **Attribute / attribute set** | An **attribute** is a typed field on a product or variant (waterproof rating in millimeters, fabric, weight). An **attribute set** is the list of fields a product of a given kind must carry. | [[product-information]] |
| **Taxonomy** | A fixed tree of categories that a channel understands; each marketplace has its own, and products must be mapped to it. | [[product-information]] |
| **Bundle (kit) / configurable product** | A **bundle** or kit is a product sold as a set of other products (a tent with its footprint). A **configurable product** is the parent that groups purchasable child variants. | [[product-information]] |
| **PIM (product information management)** | The editorial system where product data is created, enriched, translated and approved before it is published to channels; at Kestrel & Pine the system of record for product content from Q3 2027. | [[product-information]], [[commerce-platform-landscape]] |
| **Enrichment / syndication** | **Enrichment** is adding the marketing, technical and media content a channel needs beyond what the ERP holds. **Syndication** is publishing product content to each channel in its format and under its rules, with a rejection report back. | [[product-information]] |
| **Feed / structured data** | A **feed** is a file or API stream of product records in a channel's format. **Structured data** is product information embedded in the web page itself (schema.org) so that search engines and agents can read it. | [[product-information]], [[ai-and-agentic-commerce]] |
| **Price list / price dimensions** | A set of prices that applies to one combination of **market**, **customer group**, **channel** and **validity window**, with a stated **tax basis**. A price without its dimensions cannot be explained. | [[pricing-architecture]] |
| **Base price / contract price** | A **base price** is the list price for the general public. A **contract price** is agreed with one named account or group and outranks the list. | [[pricing-architecture]], [[b2b-commerce]] |
| **Tax-inclusive / tax-exclusive** | A **tax-inclusive** price is what the consumer pays, with VAT inside it (€199 includes 21% Dutch VAT); a **tax-exclusive** price has tax added later, as wholesale prices do. | [[pricing-architecture]], [[tax-invoicing-and-ledger]] |
| **Pricing engine / price history** | The **pricing engine** is the code that applies the price-selection rule to a request at a given moment. The **price history** is every price per SKU, market and channel with effective dates, needed for the 30-day prior-price rule and for audit. | [[pricing-architecture]] |
| **Channel price rule** | A derived price for a channel that cannot call the pricing engine, such as base price plus an uplift within a floor and a ceiling for a marketplace. | [[pricing-architecture]], [[marketplaces-and-channel-integration]] |
| **Coupon code / automatic discount** | A **coupon code** is a promotion the customer unlocks by typing a string; an **automatic discount** applies whenever its conditions are met, with no code. | [[commerce-promotions]] |
| **Eligibility / stacking** | **Eligibility** is a promotion's conditions: which products, customers, period, channel and minimum cart. **Stacking** is applying more than one promotion to the same cart or line, which must be decided per campaign. | [[commerce-promotions]] |
| **Discount allocation** | Spreading an order-level discount over the lines so that each line, and later each invoice line and return, carries its share. | [[commerce-promotions]] |
| **Gift card / store credit / loyalty points** | Stored value the merchant owes: a **gift card** is value the customer paid for in advance, **store credit** is value the merchant granted (often instead of a refund), **loyalty points** are value earned by buying. Each is a liability kept as a ledger. | [[commerce-promotions]], [[tax-invoicing-and-ledger]] |
| **Breakage** | The part of stored value that will never be redeemed; accounting standards require it to be estimated and recognized in proportion to redemptions, not when cards expire. | [[commerce-promotions]] |
| **Search / navigation / recommendations** | The three ways shoppers find products: typing a query, clicking categories and narrowing with filters, and accepting a suggestion such as "often bought together". | [[commerce-search-and-discovery]] |
| **Search index / indexing** | The **search index** is a separate copy of the catalog built for fast matching; it is always slightly behind the catalog. **Indexing** is the job that copies changes into it. | [[commerce-search-and-discovery]] |
| **Relevance / ranking** | **Relevance** is the search engine's ordering of results for a query; **ranking** is the same thing seen as a list. Ranking parameters shown to consumers must be explainable. | [[commerce-search-and-discovery]] |
| **Facet** | A filter with counts ("Color: moss (14)") computed from indexed attributes; it only works if attributes are complete. | [[commerce-search-and-discovery]] |
| **Merchandising rule (boost, bury, pin)** | A human override of relevance for a query or category: pin a product to the top, push one down, hide one or redirect a query. | [[commerce-search-and-discovery]] |
| **Synonyms / typo tolerance / zero-result rate** | Synonym sets and typo tolerance let different words find the same product; the **zero-result rate**, the share of searches that return nothing, is the first search metric to watch (9.4% at Kestrel & Pine in Q1 2027). | [[commerce-search-and-discovery]] |
| **Vector search / conversational search** | **Vector search** matches by meaning rather than exact words; **conversational search** lets a shopper describe a need in sentences. Both still depend on complete product data. | [[commerce-search-and-discovery]], [[ai-and-agentic-commerce]] |
| **Verified review** | A review linked to an order, with a record of how it was collected; moderation rejects on policy, never on sentiment, and incentives may not depend on the rating. | [[commerce-search-and-discovery]] |
| **On hand / committed / reserved** | **On hand** is the units physically at a location now. **Committed** (also allocated) is the part already claimed by placed orders that have not shipped. **Reserved** is a temporary claim before an order is final, such as a unit in a cart during a limited-stock sale. | [[inventory-and-availability]], [[warehouse-and-fulfillment]] |
| **Held back / safety stock / incoming** | **Held back** covers units present but deliberately not for sale, including a **safety stock** buffer against counting errors and sync lag. **Incoming** is stock on the way in from a supplier or another location. | [[inventory-and-availability]] |
| **ATP (available-to-promise)** | What a channel may still sell: on hand, minus what is committed to unshipped orders, minus what the merchant holds back, computed per channel and location. | [[inventory-and-availability]], [[commerce-capability-map]] |
| **Location / stock location** | Any place stock can be promised and shipped from: an own warehouse, a store, a 3PL, a marketplace's fulfillment center. A location outside the merchant's control is still a location to track. | [[inventory-and-availability]], [[commerce-scaling-stages]] |
| **Stock event** | A message sent the moment a stock quantity changes, saying which SKU, at which location, by how much, why and when; the alternative to periodic snapshots. | [[inventory-and-availability]], [[warehouse-and-fulfillment]] |
| **Availability service** | The component that holds the latest on hand and committed per SKU per location, applies reservations, allocations and buffers, and publishes available-to-promise per channel; at Kestrel & Pine a module of the OMS (Waypoint W1). | [[inventory-and-availability]], [[order-management]] |
| **Reserve-on-cart / reserve-on-order / allocation at fulfillment** | The three moments a unit can be claimed: when it enters the cart (with an expiry), when the order is created (the common default) or when the warehouse releases the pick (risking orders that cannot be fulfilled). | [[inventory-and-availability]] |
| **Oversell** | Selling more units than exist, usually because two channels read the same stock before either sale reduces it; the root of the Phantom Stock. | [[inventory-and-availability]], [[journey-peak-day]] |

## <a id="g-order"></a>Part III — ORDER: Cart, Checkout, Payment and the Order Record

From the draft to the legal record: the cart and its orchestration, the money and its backward flows, the order's states and routing, and tax, invoices and the books. [[cart-and-checkout]] owns cart, checkout and the idempotency key at checkout; [[commerce-payments]] the PSP, authorization and capture, SCA and refunds; [[order-management]] the OMS, state machines and routing; [[tax-invoicing-and-ledger]] tax calculation, the invoice, the credit note and order-to-ledger.

| Term | Definition | Chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cart / abandoned cart** | A **cart** is a draft of an order that the shopper can still change; it commits nobody. An **abandoned cart** is one started and not converted within some window. | [[cart-and-checkout]] |
| **Checkout / checkout orchestration** | **Checkout** is the orchestrated sequence that turns the cart into an order: in seconds the platform calls pricing, promotions, tax, availability, shipping rates and the payment provider, then creates the order. | [[cart-and-checkout]], [[journey-of-an-order]] |
| **Guest checkout** | Buying without creating an account; it raises conversion and creates duplicate customer records that matching must later link. | [[cart-and-checkout]], [[identity-and-master-data]] |
| **Express wallet** | A payment button such as Apple Pay or Google Pay that supplies address and payment in one step, so the merchant never receives the card number. | [[cart-and-checkout]] |
| **Idempotency key** | A unique token sent with a request so that a retry does nothing twice; it should be derived from the business event ("create order from cart X with payment Y", "refund return R"), not from a page load. | [[cart-and-checkout]], [[commerce-payments]] |
| **PSP (payment service provider)** | The company that takes the shopper's payment and pays it out to the merchant: one API, many methods, fraud screening, authentication, a balance, payouts and reports. | [[commerce-payments]], [[commerce-platform-landscape]] |
| **Issuer / acquirer / scheme** | The **issuer** is the shopper's bank; the **acquirer** is the licensed institution that accepts payments for the merchant (often part of the PSP); the **scheme** or network sets the rules and rails (Visa, Mastercard, iDEAL, SEPA). | [[commerce-payments]] |
| **Authorization / capture** | **Authorization** is the issuer approving and reserving the money; **capture** is the merchant taking it, often when the parcel ships. An authorization expires, so "capture on shipment" is a promise with a deadline. | [[commerce-payments]], [[journey-consumer-order]] |
| **Settlement / payout** | **Settlement** is the scheme or bank moving money to the PSP. A **payout** is the PSP paying the merchant's bank, one batch per period, net of fees, refunds and chargebacks, matched by finance. | [[commerce-payments]], [[tax-invoicing-and-ledger]] |
| **Refund** | Money returned to the shopper on the original payment method, only from what was captured; it is asynchronous and can fail later. One writer per refund and the return id as idempotency key prevent the Double Refund. | [[commerce-payments]], [[returns-and-exchanges]] |
| **Chargeback** | A dispute the cardholder raises with the issuer, which reverses the payment and charges the merchant a fee unless the merchant wins with evidence; not the merchant's decision. | [[commerce-payments]] |
| **Interchange** | The card schemes' and issuers' part of the card fee; pricing as interchange plus a margin is cheaper at volume but needs reconciliation. | [[commerce-platform-landscape]], [[commerce-payments]] |
| **iDEAL / Bancontact / purchase on account** | The dominant local methods in Kestrel & Pine's markets: **iDEAL**, a transfer from the shopper's Dutch bank account; **Bancontact**, a Belgian card scheme paid mostly by app; **purchase on account** (*Kauf auf Rechnung*), paying after delivery against an invoice, common in Germany. | [[commerce-payments]] |
| **3-D Secure / liability shift** | **3-D Secure** is the card networks' protocol for the strong customer authentication EU rules require, usually a confirmation in the banking app. When a 3-D Secure payment is disputed as fraud, liability typically shifts to the issuer; an exemption gives that up. | [[commerce-payments]], [[cart-and-checkout]] |
| **Hosted fields / hosted payment page** | The PSP draws the card-number boxes inside the merchant's page from its own domain, or redirects to its own page, so the card number never touches the merchant's code; this keeps the merchant's PCI scope small. | [[commerce-payments]], [[commerce-security]] |
| **Payment webhook** | The PSP's HTTP call to the merchant saying a payment changed state; it is a notification, not a fact, so the receiver fetches the status before acting on it. | [[commerce-payments]], [[journey-consumer-order]] |
| **OMS (order management system)** | The operational order record after checkout: every channel's orders in one state model, routing to locations, splits, holds and edits, and the service view. At Kestrel & Pine the order hub from Q4 2027 (Waypoint W2). | [[order-management]], [[commerce-platform-landscape]] |
| **Order header / order lines** | The **header** holds number, channel, customer, time, currency, market and legal seller; each **line** holds a SKU, quantity, frozen unit price, allocated discounts and tax. | [[order-management]] |
| **State machine** | A fixed set of states an object can be in and the allowed moves between them. An order needs separate machines for its lifecycle, its money, its goods (per fulfillment order) and each return, not one status string. | [[order-management]] |
| **Sourcing / routing** | **Sourcing** is choosing which location fulfills which order line; **routing** is the rule set that makes that choice. | [[order-management]], [[stores-and-omnichannel]] |
| **Split shipment** | One order shipped in more than one parcel, usually from more than one location or at more than one time; its status must not collapse into one word. | [[order-management]], [[warehouse-and-fulfillment]] |
| **Order orchestration** | The OMS's job of sequencing an order's steps across systems: driving the money, goods and return state machines from events and handing work to warehouses and stores. | [[order-management]], [[events-consistency-and-reconciliation]] |
| **Order number family** | One numbering scheme for every channel's orders, so an order has one identity in every system that copies it. | [[order-management]] |
| **VAT (value-added tax)** | Europe's consumption tax, charged at every sale and reclaimed by business buyers; each sale must carry the right country's rate. | [[tax-invoicing-and-ledger]], [[pricing-architecture]] |
| **Tax calculation / tax engine** | Working out the tax on an order from ship-to address, product tax category and customer status; estimated at checkout, final at invoicing, often by a tax engine or rate table. | [[tax-invoicing-and-ledger]] |
| **Invoice** | The legal document of a sale: required content, a number in sequence per legal entity, never edited, kept for years. The shop issues an order confirmation; the ERP issues the invoice. | [[tax-invoicing-and-ledger]], [[journey-of-an-order]] |
| **Credit note** | The invoice's mirror image: it references the invoice, reverses some or all of its lines and carries its own sequential number; a return or a correction produces one. | [[tax-invoicing-and-ledger]], [[returns-and-exchanges]] |
| **E-invoice** | An invoice issued, transmitted and received as structured data (XML) that the receiver's software can process; a PDF by e-mail is not one. | [[tax-invoicing-and-ledger]], [[edi-and-e-invoicing]] |
| **Ledger (general ledger)** | The book of accounts in which every transaction is recorded twice, as a debit and a credit, so the books always balance; the ERP posts orders, VAT, fees and refunds to it. | [[tax-invoicing-and-ledger]], [[erp-and-back-office-integration]] |
| **Reconciliation** | Regularly comparing two systems' records of the same thing and explaining every difference: orders against shipments, returns against refunds, PSP payouts against the books. | [[journey-of-an-order]], [[tax-invoicing-and-ledger]], [[events-consistency-and-reconciliation]] |

## <a id="g-fulfillment"></a>Part IV — FULFILLMENT: From Warehouse to Doorstep and Back

Where promises are kept by people, buildings and vans. [[warehouse-and-fulfillment]] owns the WMS, the fulfillment order, the pick wave and the 3PL; [[shipping-and-delivery]] the carrier integration, the delivery promise and the tracking event; [[stores-and-omnichannel]] the POS and the omnichannel flows; [[returns-and-exchanges]] the RMA, disposition and exchange; [[customer-service-and-notifications]] the transactional notification, the order status page and the service view.

| Term | Definition | Chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **WMS (warehouse management system)** | The system that runs a warehouse's physical stock and its movements: receiving, put-away, picking, packing and shipping. At Kestrel & Pine the system of record for physical stock at Duiven. | [[warehouse-and-fulfillment]] |
| **Receiving / put-away / slotting** | **Receiving** checks an inbound delivery against the purchase order and books the units in; **put-away** moves them to a storage location with its own barcode; **slotting** decides where each SKU should live. | [[warehouse-and-fulfillment]] |
| **Pick wave / batch picking** | Grouping orders into one picking run, typically by carrier cut-off, channel or zone, so one walk serves many orders. The warehouse works in waves, not order by order. | [[warehouse-and-fulfillment]] |
| **Packing / cartonization** | Choosing the box, packing and weighing; **cartonization** is the software's choice of box size from the items' dimensions. | [[warehouse-and-fulfillment]] |
| **Shipping confirmation** | The warehouse's report that a shipment left, with its tracking number; the moment on hand goes down and the order is fulfilled. | [[warehouse-and-fulfillment]] |
| **Cycle counting** | Counting a small part of the stock on a rolling schedule, instead of one annual full count, to correct the record continuously. | [[warehouse-and-fulfillment]], [[stores-and-omnichannel]] |
| **Fulfillment order** | The instruction to one location: ship these lines of this order, by this service level, with these notes. It is the contract between the OMS and the WMS; the warehouse's accept step locks the order against edits. | [[warehouse-and-fulfillment]], [[order-management]] |
| **Cut-off / capacity** | The **cut-off** is the latest moment an order can reach the warehouse and still ship today: the release of the last wave that makes the last carrier collection. **Capacity** is units or parcels per hour at the day's staffing. | [[warehouse-and-fulfillment]], [[shipping-and-delivery]] |
| **3PL (third-party logistics provider)** | A company that stores and ships goods on the merchant's behalf, from its own warehouse and usually on its own WMS. | [[warehouse-and-fulfillment]], [[inventory-and-availability]] |
| **Marketplace fulfillment program** | A marketplace's own warehouse network, stocked in advance by the seller, that picks, packs, ships and handles returns: Fulfillment by Amazon (FBA), Logistics via bol (LvB), Zalando Fulfillment Solutions (ZFS) and ZEOS. | [[warehouse-and-fulfillment]], [[marketplaces-and-channel-integration]] |
| **Carrier / carrier API** | The **carrier** moves the parcel (PostNL, bpost, DHL, DPD); the **carrier API** is its interface for creating a shipment, getting a label, closing the day and reading tracking. | [[shipping-and-delivery]] |
| **Label / manifest** | The **label** carries the carrier's barcode, the parcel's identity in the carrier's network. The **manifest** closes the day by handing the carrier the list of parcels it is collecting; a label is not a booking. | [[shipping-and-delivery]] |
| **Tracking event** | One carrier scan with a time and a place: accepted, at depot, out for delivery, delivered, attempted, exception. | [[shipping-and-delivery]], [[journey-consumer-order]] |
| **Delivery option / pickup point / locker** | What the customer chooses at checkout: home, a **pickup point** (a shop counter), a parcel **locker**, a time window or a date. | [[shipping-and-delivery]] |
| **Lead time / transit time** | Days from order to shipment (handling) and from carrier collection to delivery (transit), per lane: an origin, a destination area and a service. | [[shipping-and-delivery]] |
| **Delivery promise / EDD (estimated delivery date)** | The date or window shown before purchase, which the network then has to keep. A static promise is written once ("ordered before 22:00, delivered tomorrow"); the **EDD** is the same thing computed from cut-off, backlog and lane performance. | [[shipping-and-delivery]], [[case-amazon]] |
| **Rate shopping / multi-carrier platform** | **Rate shopping** picks the cheapest or fastest carrier service per parcel. A **multi-carrier platform** sits between merchant and carriers: one API for labels and tracking, one event vocabulary. | [[shipping-and-delivery]] |
| **POS (point of sale)** | The till software that records store sales, takes terminal payments, issues receipts and counts store stock; at Kestrel & Pine a cloud POS and the system of record for store stock. | [[stores-and-omnichannel]], [[meet-kestrel-and-pine]] |
| **Terminal provider** | The company that accepts card payments at the store counter, with its own references and payout files, separate from the online PSP. | [[stores-and-omnichannel]], [[commerce-payments]] |
| **Fiscalization** | A legal requirement that every sale be recorded in a way the tax authority can verify, in some countries with a certified device that signs each transaction (Germany's TSE). | [[stores-and-omnichannel]], [[commerce-regulation]] |
| **Click-and-collect (BOPIS)** | Order online, pick up in store (buy online, pick up in store), from store stock or from warehouse stock sent to the store; it needs store stock visible to availability and a confirmation step. | [[stores-and-omnichannel]] |
| **Ship-from-store / return-in-store / endless aisle** | **Ship-from-store** sends online orders from a store's stock; **return-in-store** accepts an online order back at the till with one refund path; **endless aisle** lets the till sell stock held elsewhere and ship it to the customer. | [[stores-and-omnichannel]] |
| **Offline operation** | The store's ability to keep selling when the connection drops; for those hours it is its own system of record, and the replayed sales must be deduplicated. | [[stores-and-omnichannel]] |
| **RFID** | Radio tags on every item with readers at the door and counter, which make store stock accurate enough to promise from; common in fashion at Group scale. | [[stores-and-omnichannel]] |
| **RMA (return merchandise authorization)** | The merchant's record that a return of these items was requested and allowed: the merchant's side of the promise "you may send this back". | [[returns-and-exchanges]] |
| **Reverse fulfillment / inspection** | **Reverse fulfillment** is the warehouse's work to process a return against its RMA; **inspection** is looking at each item and grading it. | [[returns-and-exchanges]] |
| **Disposition** | The warehouse's verdict on a returned item: back to stock, needs processing, discard or missing. It is the first moment the merchant knows what it got back, so refunds and restocking should follow it. | [[returns-and-exchanges]], [[journey-return-and-exchange]] |
| **Exchange** | A return paired with a new order for a different size, color or item, with any price difference settled as a balance; at Kestrel & Pine a held order released by the disposition. | [[returns-and-exchanges]], [[journey-return-and-exchange]] |
| **Refund on inspection** | Refunding only after the returned item has been graded, rather than on request, carrier scan or receipt; it costs the customer days and protects against empty-box and wrong-item returns. | [[returns-and-exchanges]] |
| **Reverse logistics** | The trade's name for the whole backward operation: labels, carriers, receiving, grading, resale, refurbishing and recycling. | [[returns-and-exchanges]] |
| **Returns portal** | The customer-facing page and service where a return is requested, a reason chosen, a label issued, and an exchange or refund selected. | [[returns-and-exchanges]], [[journey-return-and-exchange]] |
| **Transactional notification** | A message triggered by an event on the customer's own order (confirmed, shipped, delivered, refunded), as opposed to marketing; sent by a notification service that records what it sent. | [[customer-service-and-notifications]] |
| **Order status page** | The self-service page where the customer sees where the order is: a read model of the order record plus the tracking stream. | [[customer-service-and-notifications]] |
| **WISMO ("where is my order")** | The service contact that asks for delivery status, typically the largest category of tickets; a signal of how well status flows between systems. | [[customer-service-and-notifications]] |
| **Service view / self-service** | The **service view** shows an agent one order across systems with actions gated on its state. **Self-service** lets customers cancel, change an address or start a return within the same gates. | [[customer-service-and-notifications]] |

| **Company account** | A buying organization with several locations, users and roles (who may order, approve or see invoices), instead of one person with one login. | [[b2b-commerce]] |
| **Contract / tiered pricing** | **Contract pricing** is a price agreed with one customer or group, usually with an effective period; **tiered** (volume) pricing gives price breaks by quantity. | [[b2b-commerce]], [[pricing-architecture]] |
| **Quote** | The seller's offer at a stated price with a validity date that the buyer can accept into an order; the one place a price carries an expiry. | [[b2b-commerce]], [[pricing-architecture]] |
| **Payment terms (net 30)** | The agreed time to pay after the invoice, such as net 30 (due 30 days after the invoice); typed data, not free text, and payment becomes a receivable in the ERP rather than a PSP event. | [[b2b-commerce]], [[cart-and-checkout]] |
| **Credit limit / approval workflow** | The **credit limit** is the seller's control: the most a customer may owe. The **approval workflow** is the buyer's own control: an order above an amount needs a manager. | [[b2b-commerce]] |
| **Pre-season order / in-season reorder** | In apparel wholesale, an order placed months ahead for delivery in a window, often on better terms, followed by reorders of what sells. | [[b2b-commerce]] |

| **CONTRL / RECADV** | **CONTRL** is a syntax receipt ("your message parsed"), saying nothing about meaning; **RECADV** is the buyer's receiving advice of what it counted in. | [[journey-wholesale-order]] |
| **GLN (Global Location Number)** | The GS1 number that identifies who and where: a legal entity or a physical place such as a distribution center. | [[edi-and-e-invoicing]], [[journey-wholesale-order]] |
| **SSCC (Serial Shipping Container Code)** | The 18-digit GS1 number on a logistics unit's label that identifies this pallet or carton; the DESADV lists the contents per SSCC. | [[warehouse-and-fulfillment]], [[edi-and-e-invoicing]] |
| **VAN (value-added network) / AS2 / SFTP** | Transports for EDI: a **VAN** is a provider-run mailbox network that relays to every partner on it; **AS2** sends signed, encrypted documents over HTTP with a signed receipt; **SFTP** is secure file transfer polled on a schedule. | [[edi-and-e-invoicing]], [[journey-wholesale-order]] |
| **Compliance chargeback** | A deduction a retail buyer makes from the supplier's payment for a late, incomplete or wrongly documented delivery: a missing ASN, an unscannable label, an ASN that does not match receipt. | [[edi-and-e-invoicing]] |
| **Peppol / access point / four-corner model** | **Peppol** is an open network run by OpenPeppol in which each party connects through an accredited **access point** and reaches any participant: sender, sender's access point, receiver's access point, receiver (the **four-corner model**). | [[edi-and-e-invoicing]], [[journey-wholesale-order]] |
| **ERP (enterprise resource planning)** | The system that keeps the books: ledger, purchasing, inventory valuation, invoices and credit notes, receivables and payables, and the item master. At Kestrel & Pine it is held to finance only. | [[erp-and-back-office-integration]], [[commerce-platform-landscape]] |
| **Item master** | The ERP's record of each item for purchasing and costing: SKU, GTIN, supplier, cost, purchasing unit, weight and dimensions. | [[erp-and-back-office-integration]], [[product-information]] |
| **Inventory valuation / costing method / landed cost** | What the stock is worth: a cost per unit under a **costing method** (such as FIFO or weighted average) built from **landed cost**, the purchase price plus freight and duty. | [[erp-and-back-office-integration]] |
| **Integration hub** | The one system every other system connects through and that owns the shared records: the ERP, the commerce platform or an OMS, or no hub at all with an iPaaS. | [[erp-and-back-office-integration]] |
| **Master data ownership** | The rule that says which system may create and change each kind of slow-changing reference record (items, customers, prices, locations). | [[erp-and-back-office-integration]], [[identity-and-master-data]] |
| **ERP implementation partner** | The outside firm that configured the ERP and built its connectors; an architectural dependency most merchants do not list. | [[erp-and-back-office-integration]] |

## <a id="g-integration"></a>Part VI — INTEGRATION: Making Separate Systems Behave Like One

How separate systems move facts, survive repeats and gaps, keep their promises to each other, recognize the same customer and product, and count the same thing. [[integration-styles]] owns the styles and the inventory; [[events-consistency-and-reconciliation]] events, idempotency, the outbox, compensation and reconciliation; [[api-contracts-and-versioning]] the contract, the version and the webhook; [[identity-and-master-data]] identity, the golden record and matching; [[commerce-data-and-analytics]] the warehouse and metric definitions.

| Term | Definition | Chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **API (application programming interface)** | The contract by which one program calls another: the operations, fields and errors it accepts and returns. | [[commerce-platform-landscape]], [[api-contracts-and-versioning]] |
| **Direct API call** | The caller asks when it needs the answer and waits for it; right for decisions at checkout, but every callee's outage becomes the caller's. | [[integration-styles]] |
| **Webhook** | An HTTP request a source system sends to a URL you registered when something happens. It is a notification, not a guarantee: deliveries can be missed, duplicated or out of order, and a failing receiver can lose its subscription. | [[integration-styles]], [[api-contracts-and-versioning]] |
| **Polling / sweep** | The receiver asks on a timer what changed since its last visit; the periodic **sweep** is the safety net under webhooks. | [[integration-styles]], [[api-contracts-and-versioning]] |
| **Scheduled batch / file transfer** | Either side sends a file or batch on a clock; stale by design, and a late or partial file is a quiet failure. | [[integration-styles]] |
| **Queue / event stream** | A **queue** is a buffer in which work waits until the next system can take it; an **event stream** lets many consumers read published events at their own pace. Delivery is at least once and ordering holds only per key. | [[integration-styles]], [[peak-load-and-reliability]] |
| **CDC (change data capture)** | Reading a database's own change log with a connector to turn row changes into messages; a row change still has to be translated into a business event. | [[integration-styles]] |
| **Event notification / event-carried state transfer** | Two meanings of event-driven (Martin Fowler, 2017): the event says only that something changed and receivers call back for details, or the event carries the data and receivers keep their own copy. | [[integration-styles]] |
| **iPaaS (integration platform as a service)** | A hosted tool that runs data flows between systems: connectors, mapping, retries, errors and monitoring. A hub without owners recreates the tangle in one place. | [[integration-styles]], [[commerce-platform-landscape]] |
| **Point-to-point integration** | A direct connector between one pair of systems; their number multiplies with every system added. | [[integration-styles]] |
| **Integration inventory** | The list of every integration with its owner, style, direction, fields, frequency, system of record and pinned API version; the exit's work list and the first Waypoint document. | [[integration-styles]], [[organizing-commerce-engineering]] |
| **Integration contract** | A short written agreement for one flow: fields, direction, frequency, source of truth, and what happens on a repeat or a failure. | [[organizing-commerce-engineering]], [[integration-styles]] |
| **Event** | A message sent the moment something happens (an order placed, a price changed) so other systems can react. | [[case-zalando]], [[events-consistency-and-reconciliation]] |
| **Eventual consistency** | The promise that is possible across separate systems: copies may disagree for a while, and if updates stop they all converge on the last value. | [[events-consistency-and-reconciliation]], [[commerce-architecture-styles]], [[case-amazon]] |
| **At-least-once delivery** | A message will arrive one or more times; the receiver, not the transport, must make repeats harmless. | [[events-consistency-and-reconciliation]], [[customer-service-and-notifications]] |
| **Idempotency** | Handling the same message twice has the same effect as handling it once: a second "refund order #1072455" must not produce a second refund. | [[journey-of-an-order]], [[events-consistency-and-reconciliation]] |
| **Dead-letter queue (DLQ)** | Where a message goes after failing a set number of times, so it is neither lost nor retried forever; someone must own and empty it. | [[events-consistency-and-reconciliation]], [[integration-styles]] |
| **Outbox** | Writing the event to a table in the same database transaction as the change it describes, then publishing it from there, so "the order was saved" and "the event was sent" become one act and no event is lost. | [[events-consistency-and-reconciliation]] |
| **Saga / compensation** | A **saga** is a long-lived transaction written as a sequence of smaller ones (Garcia-Molina and Salem, 1987); when a later step fails, **compensation** reverses earlier steps by new business actions, since they cannot be rolled back. A canceled shipped order becomes a return and a refund. | [[events-consistency-and-reconciliation]], [[order-management]] |
| **Choreography / orchestration** | Two ways to run a multi-step flow: each participant reacts to events (choreography, low coupling, the flow is in nobody's code), or one orchestrator, usually the OMS, decides the next step and shows where every order is. | [[events-consistency-and-reconciliation]] |
| **Two-phase commit** | Both systems commit together or not at all; it needs both vendors' cooperation, which SaaS vendors do not give, so commerce flows use events and reconciliation instead. | [[events-consistency-and-reconciliation]] |
| **Reconciliation job** | A scheduled job that compares two systems' records by a matching key within a tolerance and sends the differences to a named owner, who repairs the source, not the copy. | [[events-consistency-and-reconciliation]], [[inventory-and-availability]] |
| **API contract** | The written description of what a system promises to accept and return: operations, fields, errors, rate limits, delivery guarantees and lifetime. A contract has shape, meaning, timing and lifetime. | [[api-contracts-and-versioning]] |
| **Versioning / API version** | Naming generations of a contract so a consumer can say which one it relies on; a vendor's **API version** is a dated edition it promises to keep working for a stated period. | [[api-contracts-and-versioning]], [[organizing-commerce-engineering]] |
| **Deprecation / sunset** | **Deprecation** is the announced, dated withdrawal of part of a contract; the **sunset** is the date it stops working. Missing one is the Deprecated Endpoint. | [[api-contracts-and-versioning]] |
| **Silent fall-forward** | A consumer on a retired or unstated API version is served a newer one without an error, so a field goes missing or changes meaning unnoticed. | [[api-contracts-and-versioning]], [[extensibility-and-app-ecosystems]] |
| **Rate limit** | The maximum number of API calls, or units of query cost, a caller may make in a period; above it the provider answers with an error. On a shared platform every app and connector spends the same budget. | [[api-contracts-and-versioning]], [[multi-tenancy-and-isolation]] |
| **Sandbox** | A copy of a service where nothing real happens, used to test a new API version or an app before production. | [[organizing-commerce-engineering]], [[api-contracts-and-versioning]] |
| **Schema registry / contract test** | A **schema registry** holds the agreed shape of each event type so producers cannot break consumers; a **contract test** checks a provider against its consumers' expectations in the delivery pipeline. | [[api-contracts-and-versioning]], [[case-zalando]] |
| **Identifier** | A value a system uses to tell one record from another: a customer id, an e-mail address, an order number, a barcode number. | [[identity-and-master-data]] |
| **Customer identity** | The set of identifiers that different systems use for the same person, plus the rules that link them; the core of the Familiar Stranger. | [[identity-and-master-data]] |
| **Master data / MDM** | **Master data** is the slowly changing reference data that transactions point to: customers, products, suppliers, locations. **MDM** (master data management) is the discipline and tooling that keeps one owned record of each. | [[identity-and-master-data]], [[erp-and-back-office-integration]] |
| **Matching / survivorship** | **Matching** decides that two records with no shared identifier refer to the same thing; **survivorship** is the rule that decides which value wins per field when they merge. | [[identity-and-master-data]] |
| **Golden record** | The one assembled record per customer or product, built by merging sources under survivorship rules. | [[identity-and-master-data]] |
| **Cross-reference / merge log** | For each master record, every other system's identifier for it and every link or merge made, kept reversibly so a wrong merge can be undone. | [[identity-and-master-data]] |
| **CDP (customer data platform)** | A product that builds a unified profile per person from every touchpoint for audiences and personalization; a consumer of the customer master, not a replacement for it. | [[identity-and-master-data]] |
| **Operational / analytical data** | **Operational data** is the live state of the business (this order's status); **analytical data** is the history laid out for questions across all records (orders by channel by week). | [[commerce-data-and-analytics]] |
| **Data warehouse** | A database built for large analytical queries, loaded with copies of every system's data and modeled into tables analysts can use; a copy, never a system of record. | [[commerce-data-and-analytics]] |
| **Metric definition / metrics layer** | A **metric definition** is the written rule for computing a business number (what is counted, which records, which date, which tax treatment). The **metrics layer** defines each metric once, as code, for every dashboard. | [[commerce-data-and-analytics]] |
| **Net sales** | Kestrel & Pine's one definition (W7): merchandise value excluding VAT, after discounts, minus returns and cancellations in the month they are refunded, excluding shipping charges and payment fees. | [[commerce-data-and-analytics]] |
| **Sell-through / weeks of cover** | **Sell-through** is units sold divided by units received for a season; **weeks of cover** is stock on hand divided by average weekly sales. Both drive buying and markdown decisions. | [[commerce-data-and-analytics]] |

## <a id="g-platform"></a>Part VII — PLATFORM: How Commerce Platforms Are Built

How the software itself is structured, shared, extended, delivered to the browser and kept up on the busiest day. [[commerce-architecture-styles]] owns the styles; [[multi-tenancy-and-isolation]] tenancy, isolation, pods and rate limits; [[extensibility-and-app-ecosystems]] apps, plugins and functions; [[storefront-architecture]] themes, headless, rendering and Core Web Vitals; [[peak-load-and-reliability]] the peak plan, backpressure, graceful degradation and the load test.

| Term | Definition | Chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Architecture style** | The shape of a system as deployed and run: how many separately running programs there are, which data each owns, and how they talk. | [[commerce-architecture-styles]] |
| **Deployment unit** | One program that is built, tested, released and run as a whole; splitting a system means creating more of them. | [[commerce-architecture-styles]] |
| **Monolith / modular monolith** | A **monolith** is one deployable application holding all the shop's logic, usually with one shared database. A **modular monolith** keeps the single deployment but divides the code into named modules with enforced boundaries. | [[commerce-architecture-styles]], [[case-shopify]] |
| **Microservices** | Many small, separately deployed services, each owned by one team, owning its own data and reached only over the network; they buy team independence at the price of operations and consistency work. | [[commerce-architecture-styles]], [[case-zalando]] |
| **Tenant / multi-tenancy** | A **tenant** is one customer of a shared platform with its own data and configuration (at a commerce platform, one shop). **Multi-tenancy** is one running system serving many tenants. | [[multi-tenancy-and-isolation]], [[case-shopify]] |
| **Isolation / silo / pool** | **Isolation** is how far one tenant's load, data and failures are kept from another's. In the **silo** model tenants get dedicated resources; in the **pool** model they share them, kept apart by a tenant identifier. | [[multi-tenancy-and-isolation]] |
| **Sharding / shard key** | Splitting one database into many slices (**shards**), each holding part of the data; the **sharding key** is the field that decides where a row lives, for example the shop. | [[multi-tenancy-and-isolation]], [[case-shopify]] |
| **Pod** | An isolated group of tenants with their own databases behind shared web servers; Shopify's 2016 design, in which a routing layer sends each request to one pod and shops can be moved between pods. | [[multi-tenancy-and-isolation]], [[case-shopify]] |
| **Noisy neighbor** | A tenant whose load harms others on shared resources; in a pool, one tenant's flash sale is every tenant's load unless the vendor meters it. | [[multi-tenancy-and-isolation]] |
| **In-process plugin (module, extension)** | Custom code that runs inside the platform's own program and can reach anything, including the database; flexible, and re-tested on every release. | [[extensibility-and-app-ecosystems]] |
| **App / app store** | An **app** is a separately hosted program that extends a platform through its APIs and webhooks within granted scopes; an **app store** is where the vendor lists, reviews and bills for third-party apps. | [[extensibility-and-app-ecosystems]] |
| **Scopes / least scope** | The permissions an app is granted (read orders, write inventory); least scope means asking only for what it needs. Apps that may write stock, prices or orders need an owner. | [[extensibility-and-app-ecosystems]], [[commerce-security]] |
| **Extension point (function, call-out)** | A place the platform lets custom logic run: a sandboxed serverless function with a fixed input and output, or a synchronous call-out to the merchant's endpoint before saving. | [[extensibility-and-app-ecosystems]] |
| **Theme / theme code** | A **theme** is the set of templates, settings and scripts the platform's own renderer turns into pages; customizing it heavily makes updates merges. | [[storefront-architecture]], [[extensibility-and-app-ecosystems]] |
| **Storefront** | The software that produces the pages shoppers see; the architectural question is where it is rendered, from which data, and who owns the machinery. | [[storefront-architecture]] |
| **Server-side / client-side rendering** | In **server-side rendering** a server builds the complete HTML page; in **client-side rendering** the browser receives JavaScript and builds the page itself from fetched data. | [[storefront-architecture]] |
| **Headless / storefront API** | **Headless** means the storefront is a separate application that talks to the commerce engine only through an API, typically a **storefront API**; checkout usually stays with the engine. | [[storefront-architecture]], [[case-composable-movement]] |
| **Edge / CDN (content delivery network)** | Servers close to shoppers that serve cached pages and images (the **CDN**) or run the merchant's front-end code (an **edge runtime**); at peak they absorb most traffic before it reaches the application. | [[storefront-architecture]], [[peak-load-and-reliability]] |
| **Core Web Vitals (LCP, INP, CLS)** | Google's three field metrics for page experience, assessed at the 75th percentile of real visits: largest contentful paint (good is 2.5 seconds or less), interaction to next paint (200 milliseconds or less) and cumulative layout shift. | [[storefront-architecture]] |
| **PWA (progressive web app)** | A website that can be installed like an app, work offline and send push notifications. | [[storefront-architecture]] |
| **Peak** | A period, planned or not, in which traffic and orders are many times their normal level: a seasonal peak, a launch or drop, a flash sale, an unplanned moment. | [[peak-load-and-reliability]] |
| **Capacity planning / load test** | **Capacity planning** decides before the day how many orders a minute each link must carry; a **load test** sends realistic artificial traffic to find where the chain breaks before customers do. | [[peak-load-and-reliability]] |
| **Backpressure** | Letting a slow downstream system slow the producers, or letting work pile up in a queue, instead of failing the customer-facing step: accept and persist the order fast, queue everything behind it. | [[peak-load-and-reliability]] |
| **Cache / waiting room** | A **cache** is a stored copy served instead of recomputing or refetching. A **waiting room** is a virtual queue in front of the storefront that admits shoppers at a set rate. | [[peak-load-and-reliability]], [[storefront-architecture]] |
| **Graceful degradation / degradation ladder** | Switching off non-essential features in a planned order so essential ones keep working; the **ladder** lists each switch with an owner and a trigger. | [[peak-load-and-reliability]] |
| **Orders per minute** | The one unit in which every link of the chain is measured and forecast on a peak day, and the health metric of a commerce system. | [[peak-load-and-reliability]], [[journey-peak-day]] |
| **Incident commander / runbook / change freeze** | The **incident commander** decides during an incident; a **runbook** is a written list of symptoms, checks and actions; a **change freeze** stops changes to apps, theme and connectors around a peak. | [[peak-load-and-reliability]] |

## <a id="g-trust"></a>Part VIII — TRUST: Security, Privacy and Regulation

What attackers try, what the law asks of customer data, and how rules become requirements. | Term | Definition | Chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Card testing** | Bots trying stolen card numbers in many small payments or card-save attempts to find which work; the merchant pays a fee for every attempt, declined or not. | [[commerce-security]] |
| **Credential stuffing / account takeover** | **Credential stuffing** is the automated injection of stolen username and password pairs; **account takeover** is any route that ends with an attacker logged in as a customer or staff member. Multi-factor authentication is the primary countermeasure. | [[commerce-security]] |
| **Web skimming (Magecart)** | Malicious script on a checkout page that copies what the shopper types into the card fields; prevented by a script inventory, integrity checks and change detection on payment pages. | [[commerce-security]] |
| **Coupon abuse / refund abuse / scalping** | Abuse that costs margin rather than data: guessing or sharing codes, refunds to another card and empty-box returns, and bots that hoard or buy out scarce stock in a launch. | [[commerce-security]] |
| **Supply-chain risk** | An app, plugin, theme or hosted script that is compromised and runs with your privileges; managed through app governance. | [[commerce-security]], [[extensibility-and-app-ecosystems]] |
| **PCI scope / SAQ** | Which systems touch card data and so fall under PCI DSS; the **SAQ** (self-assessment questionnaire) the merchant signs ranges from SAQ A for a PSP-hosted page or hosted fields to SAQ D for raw card data on its own servers. | [[commerce-security]], [[commerce-payments]] |
| **CSP / SRI** | A **Content Security Policy** is an HTTP header that tells the browser which script sources a page may load; **Subresource Integrity** is a hash on a script tag so the browser refuses a changed script. | [[commerce-security]] |
| **Bot management / scraping policy** | Controls that tell bots from shoppers per endpoint: allow product pages, challenge cart and checkout, rate-limit the storefront API. | [[commerce-security]], [[storefront-architecture]] |
| **Personal data** | Any information relating to an identified or identifiable person: a name, an e-mail, an order with an address, a cookie id tied to a browser. | [[privacy-and-customer-data]] |
| **Controller / processor** | The **controller** decides why and how personal data is used; a **processor** handles it only on the controller's instructions. Kestrel & Pine is the controller; its SaaS vendors are mostly processors. | [[privacy-and-customer-data]] |
| **DPA (data processing agreement)** | The contract the GDPR requires between a controller and each processor. | [[privacy-and-customer-data]] |
| **Lawful basis** | The legal reason a controller may use personal data at all; for commerce mostly consent, performance of a contract, a legal obligation and legitimate interest. | [[privacy-and-customer-data]] |
| **Consent / CMP / soft opt-in** | **Consent** is a recorded, freely given choice per purpose that must travel to every tool that acts on it. A **consent management platform** (CMP) is the banner and the record behind it. The **soft opt-in** lets a shop e-mail existing customers about similar products until they object. | [[privacy-and-customer-data]], [[identity-and-master-data]] |
| **DSR (data subject request)** | A person exercising a right: send me my data, correct it, delete it; it must be verified, fanned out to every system and answered within a month. | [[privacy-and-customer-data]], [[identity-and-master-data]] |
| **Retention / erase, pseudonymize, retain** | **Retention** is how long data is kept: no longer than the purpose needs and at least as long as tax law demands. Per system the action is to **erase**, **pseudonymize** (keep the record, remove the person) or **retain**. | [[privacy-and-customer-data]] |
| **Identity index / request orchestrator** | The list of which systems hold a person under which identifiers, and the small workflow that verifies a request, fans it out and records each answer. | [[privacy-and-customer-data]] |
| **Requirement card / regulatory calendar** | A rule turned into one card (scope, data requirement, system of record, channels, test, date, owner) attached to the system-of-record map, and the dated list of rules coming into force. | [[commerce-regulation]] |
