| **Trust policy** | A written, versioned rule about what is allowed on the platform, with the evidence standard for acting on it and the consequences. It exists both as text users and regulators read and as configuration the platform can evaluate. | [[stays-trust-and-safety]] |
| **Risk signal** | A fact or score about an entity that bears on a policy, such as a classifier score on a message, a photo-hash match, a login from a new device or a chargeback. Signals are evidence, not decisions. | [[stays-trust-and-safety]] |
| **Trust decision** | A recorded, versioned answer to "may this actor take this action on this object now, and if not, what happens instead?". It carries the outcome, reason codes, policy version, evidence references, the decider (rule, model or human) and an expiry; it is the unit that is audited, explained and appealed. | [[stays-trust-and-safety]] |
| **Enforcement action (stays)** | A typed, scoped and reversible restriction that a trust decision imposes, such as hiding a listing, restricting messaging, holding a payout or suspending an account. It has a scope, a start, an expiry or review date, and links to the decision and the statement of reasons. | [[stays-trust-and-safety]] |
| **Integrity operations** | The people, queues and tools that handle what automation cannot: report triage, investigations, appeals, quality assurance of decisions, safety escalations and feedback from cases into policies and models. | [[stays-trust-and-safety]] |
| **Entity graph** | A graph linking accounts, devices, payment instruments, phone numbers, addresses, photos and company registrations, used to answer "what is this connected to?". A link is evidence, not guilt, so each link type carries a confidence and a recorded reason. | [[stays-trust-and-safety]], [[travel-fraud]] |
| **Enforcement ladder: allow / allow with friction / redirect / hold / deny** | The graded set of outcomes a trust decision can return, ordered because the cost of a wrong action rises with each rung. Friction adds a check, redirect offers another product, hold pauses the next step pending review. | [[stays-trust-and-safety]] |
| **Labeled false positive** | A decision overturned on appeal. If the overturn never reaches the owner of the model that produced the decision, the model repeats the mistake, so the appeal path is architecture rather than a support process. | [[stays-trust-and-safety]] |
| **Linked-entity review** | The cross-brand rule the book recommends for a group: a suspension in one brand opens a review in the other instead of suspending automatically, and a hold applies only if the link score crosses a policy threshold. | [[stays-trust-and-safety]] |
| **Statement of reasons** | The "clear and specific" explanation a hosting provider owes an affected user under DSA Article 17 (Regulation (EU) 2022/2065, applying from 17 February 2024) for removals, demotions, payment restrictions and suspensions. If the decision record holds reason codes and policy version, the statement is a rendering of it. | [[stays-trust-and-safety]] |
| **Digital Services Act (DSA)** | EU Regulation (EU) 2022/2065, applying from 17 February 2024, setting duties for online intermediaries: notice and action, statements of reasons, internal complaints, trader traceability and transparency reporting, with more for very large platforms. | [[stays-trust-and-safety]], [[stays-regulation]] |
| **Trusted flagger** | An entity awarded that status by a national Digital Services Coordinator whose notices an online platform must process with priority under DSA Article 22. | [[stays-trust-and-safety]] |

| **Account takeover (ATO)** | Use of a genuine account by someone who is not its owner. For guests it opens stored cards, loyalty balances and personal data; for partners and hosts it opens reservations, the messaging channel and payout settings. | [[travel-fraud]] |
| **Extranet takeover** | Compromise of a partner or host account (stolen credentials or sessions) and what the attacker then does with it, typically reading upcoming reservations and messaging guests through the platform's trusted channel to collect payment. | [[travel-fraud]] |
| **Booking risk score** | A calibrated estimate of how likely a reservation is to end in a fraud loss, computed at checkout, recomputed as evidence arrives and read at each later point of no return. It is about whether the payment and account are genuine, unlike reservation screening. | [[travel-fraud]] |
| **Points of no return** | The moments in a booking after which a loss can no longer be avoided cheaply, such as virtual card activation, payout release or check-in. Fraud decisions belong there rather than only at the point of sale. | [[travel-fraud]] |
| **Risk spine / decision points** | The shared part of a fraud architecture (signal collection, entity graph, features, scoring, outcomes) and the many places that call it (login, checkout, message send, bank-detail change, payout release), each applying its own policy. | [[travel-fraud]] |
| **Step-up friction** | An extra check placed between allow and block that costs a genuine user a little and a fraudster a lot, for example an additional authentication or a payment-method check. | [[travel-fraud]] |
| **Session risk** | A score on a partner or guest session, recomputed on each meaningful action from weak signals such as a new device, an unfamiliar network or reading all upcoming reservations in minutes. | [[travel-fraud]] |
| **Third-party fraud / first-party fraud** | Third-party fraud uses someone else's identity or money (a stolen card, a taken-over account). First-party fraud is committed by the account holder, for example a guest who stays and then disputes the charge. | [[travel-fraud]] |
| **Chargeback** | A card payment reversed by the cardholder's issuer after a dispute, borne by the merchant of record. Fraud chargebacks are the main confirmed label for guest-side payment fraud and arrive weeks or months after the booking. | [[travel-fraud]] |
| **Payment request (structured)** | A platform object (amount, reason, link to the platform's own payment page) that is the only legitimate way for a property or host to ask a guest for money, so any free-text payment request is anomalous by definition. | [[travel-fraud]] |
| **Insult rate** | The share of genuine guests wrongly declined or blocked by fraud controls, estimated from appeals, retries and a holdout, and tracked beside the fraud loss rate. | [[travel-fraud]] |

| **Identity level (stays)** | The marketplace's statement of how sure it is that an identity belongs to a real, specific, reachable person, on a ladder (from IL0, a session, through IL1 reachable and IL2 paying to IL3 documented). It is not a badge or a risk score; policy requires a level only for actions that need it. | [[identity-accounts-and-access]] |
| **Partner role model** | The scopes, roles and policies, layered on delegation grants, that decide what partner staff, management companies, co-hosts and software may do on supply objects. One policy engine evaluates it, including conditions such as identity level or recent strong authentication. | [[identity-accounts-and-access]] |
| **Traveler profile** | The per-brand record of a traveler's preferences, companions, loyalty membership, payment method references and consents. It is kept separate from the identity and the evidence used to verify it. | [[identity-accounts-and-access]] |
| **Passkey** | A public-key credential under the W3C Web Authentication (WebAuthn) standard, usually synchronized across a person's devices. It is bound to the site's domain, so a lookalike phishing page cannot use it. | [[identity-accounts-and-access]] |
| **Phishing-resistant authentication** | Authentication that cannot be relayed by a fake site, such as a passkey or hardware key. NIST SP 800-63B-4 (2025) says passwords are not phishing-resistant and restricts SMS one-time codes. | [[identity-accounts-and-access]], [[travel-fraud]] |
| **Step-up authentication** | A fresh, stronger authentication required at the moment of a sensitive action, such as changing bank details, exporting data or adding an administrator, rather than only at login. | [[identity-accounts-and-access]] |
| **Know your customer (KYC) / know your business (KYB)** | KYC verifies a natural person; KYB verifies a business: that the legal entity exists, the signatory may act for it and beneficial owners are known. Onboarding owns the processes; identity treats their results as evidence. | [[identity-accounts-and-access]] |
| **Delegation grant** | A grant linking a delegate party to a target (property, listing, portfolio or legal entity) with a role, scopes and a validity period, so staff, co-hosts, PMS vendors and AI assistants act on someone else's behalf with an audit trail. | [[identity-accounts-and-access]] |
| **Scope (authorization)** | A fine-grained permission on a supply object, such as calendar write, rates write, guest contact read or payout configure. Roles bundle scopes; policies add conditions roles cannot express. | [[identity-accounts-and-access]] |

| **Verified review** | A review whose author is a party to a reservation that the reservation record shows as stayed, checked against stated eligibility rules. Claims of verification are legal statements under EU and UK consumer law. | [[reviews-and-reputation]] |
| **Double-blind review** | A two-way review in which each side's review stays hidden until both have submitted or the review window closes. It reduces retaliation and reciprocity at the cost of a publication delay. | [[reviews-and-reputation]] |
| **One-way / two-way reviews** | In one-way reviews guests review properties, the usual model for hotels on OTAs. In two-way reviews the guest reviews the listing and the host reviews the guest, the usual model on hosted-stays marketplaces. | [[reviews-and-reputation]] |
| **Quality program** | A computed credential (badge, tier) or remediation track derived from reputation and operational records, with thresholds, a period and consequences. It turns review data into visibility and money, which creates the incentive to fake it. | [[reviews-and-reputation]] |
| **Review-trading ring** | A group of accounts that exchange cheap fake stays and reviews to buy ratings, quality badges and guest reputations. Detecting it needs patterns across accounts, payments and devices, not text filters. | [[reviews-and-reputation]] |
| **Eligibility evidence** | The reservation state at invitation, arrival evidence, stay dates and eligibility policy version stored with each review, proving "verified" under the rules then in force. | [[reviews-and-reputation]] |
| **Omnibus Directive (reviews)** | Directive (EU) 2019/2161, applied from 28 May 2022, which treats information on whether and how a trader ensures reviews come from real consumers as material information and bans fake reviews. | [[reviews-and-reputation]], [[stays-regulation]] |

| **Guest guarantee** | The hosted-stays platform's promise to rebook or refund a guest if the host cancels, check-in fails or the home is materially not as described. The marketplace pays first and recovers from the host where its terms allow; on the hotel side the nearest equivalent is relocating a walked guest at the property's cost. | [[protection-and-incidents]], [[customer-service]] |
| **Damage protection** | The marketplace's promise to reimburse a host for damage a guest caused and did not pay for, within limits. It may be a contractual promise funded by the platform rather than insurance. | [[protection-and-incidents]] |
| **Liability insurance** | An insurance policy that pays third parties, such as an injured guest or a neighbor, when the host is legally liable. Claims are often handled by a claims administrator. | [[protection-and-incidents]] |
| **Security deposit / post-stay charge** | Money taken from the guest before or during a stay and released afterward, as a card pre-authorization, a collected payment or a charge the guest agreed to at checkout. | [[protection-and-incidents]], [[stay-payments]] |
| **Recovery / subrogation** | Recovery is collecting a loss from the party who caused it, usually the guest. Subrogation is an insurer's right, after paying, to recover in the insured's place. | [[protection-and-incidents]] |
| **Reservation screening** | A risk decision made before a proposed reservation is confirmed, about physical and behavioral risk such as parties, damage and unsafe use. It is distinct from the booking risk score, which concerns payment and account fraud. | [[protection-and-incidents]] |
| **Incident workflow** | The intake, triage, response and closure of reported or detected events during a stay (party, injury, intrusion, lockout) as records linked to the reservation, deduplicated by listing and time window. | [[protection-and-incidents]] |
| **Holdout** | A small share of moderate-risk bookings given a lighter action so that a screening or fraud model's misses can be measured, since blocked bookings produce no label. | [[protection-and-incidents]] |
| **Active-stay roster** | The list of who is in a destination zone now and who arrives in the next 72 hours, used for outreach during a declared event. Hotels know who is in-house; hosted-stays marketplaces often do not. | [[protection-and-incidents]] |
| **Bordereau** | The periodic report of claims and premiums sent to an insurance partner under the program contract. | [[protection-and-incidents]] |
| **Reserve (claims)** | An estimate of what open claims will cost, booked so finance sees the protection liability before claims are paid. | [[protection-and-incidents]] |

| **Short-term rental (STR) registration** | The bundle of duties around a unit's registration number: collecting, displaying, checking and reporting it. A registration number is a unique identifier issued by the competent authority for a unit in that Member State. | [[stays-regulation]] |
| **EU Short-Term Rental Regulation** | Regulation (EU) 2024/1028, applying from 20 May 2026. It harmonizes registration data and platform duties (number before offering, display, random checks, removal orders, monthly activity data) but does not decide whether short-term renting is allowed. | [[stays-regulation]] |
| **Unit (EU STR)** | In the EU regulation, furnished accommodation rented short term. It excludes hotels and similar accommodation (NACE group 55.1), hostels and campsites, so most homes are in scope and most hotels are not. | [[stays-regulation]] |
| **Single digital entry point (SDEP)** | The national interface through which a Member State's authorities receive platform reports and orders and publish registration lists under Regulation (EU) 2024/1028. | [[stays-regulation]] |
| **Market policy registry** | The versioned, effective-dated store of jurisdiction rules (registration areas, number formats, night caps, reporting duties) that other systems evaluate. A rule records its legal source, scope, parameters and owner. | [[stays-regulation]] |
| **Compliance by design (stays)** | Enforcing rules at a few fixed points (publish, book, scheduled sweep) with one evidence trail, so decisions carry rule version and reason code. In the DSA it is also the duty in Article 31 for marketplaces. | [[stays-regulation]] |
| **Obligation register** | The legal team's register of obligations, each with its source, effective period, authority, scope, implementing controls, control owners and the evidence that proves they ran. | [[stays-regulation]] |
| **Jurisdiction resolver / eligibility evaluator** | The resolver maps a unit's location to the country, region, municipality and registration areas that apply. The evaluator answers whether a unit may be offered, booked or kept live under current rules and returns a reason code. | [[stays-regulation]] |
| **Rule primitive** | One of about ten reusable rule types (registration required, number format, validating register, night cap, minimum stay, zone prohibition, license expiry and others) parameterized per jurisdiction, so adding a city means adding data. | [[stays-regulation]] |
| **Night cap / primary residence rule** | A limit on nights per year a unit may be rented, often applying to a host's primary residence, for example Paris reducing its cap from 120 to 90 nights from 1 January 2025. | [[stays-regulation]] |
| **Dry run (policy)** | A preview that lists the units a rule change would affect before it goes live, so a regulatory lead can approve a change by its impact rather than by a code diff. | [[stays-regulation]] |
| **Regulator gateway** | The integration with authorities, run as a product with service levels: it sends reports, order confirmations and statements of reasons and takes in orders, list updates and validity lookups. | [[stays-regulation]] |
| **Platform-to-Business (P2B) Regulation** | Regulation (EU) 2019/1150, applying from 12 July 2020, protecting business users such as properties and hosts. Terms must state the main ranking parameters and the effect of any payment on ranking (Article 5), give notice of terms changes, and give a statement of reasons for restricting or terminating a partner. | [[stays-regulation]], [[stays-ranking]] |
| **Digital Markets Act (DMA)** | Regulation (EU) 2022/1925, applying only to designated gatekeepers. Article 5(3) bans parity clauses; Booking.com was designated a gatekeeper on 13 May 2024. | [[stays-regulation]] |
| **Trader traceability (DSA Art. 30)** | The duty for marketplaces where consumers contract with traders to collect and verify trader identity data. The Omnibus Directive adds a displayed trader or non-trader status. | [[stays-regulation]] |
| **Very large online platform (VLOP)** | A DSA designation for platforms above 45 million average monthly EU recipients, with extra duties. | [[stays-regulation]] |
