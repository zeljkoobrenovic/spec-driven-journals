| **Quote** | A priced offer with an identity, an itemized breakdown, the versions of the data it was computed from and a validity window. Checkout starts from a quote ID, not from a price passed by the client. | [[checkout-orchestration]] |
| **Re-validation (price check)** | Re-computes the selected quote from the system of record and returns one of three designed outcomes: unchanged within tolerance, changed (shown with the old price and a reason) or unavailable. | [[checkout-orchestration]] |
| **Inventory hold** | A short reservation of the unit or room type for the minutes the guest spends paying. It is an efficiency device; the guarantee is the commit against the allocation ledger. | [[checkout-orchestration]] |
| **Checkout attempt** | One guest's intention to buy one quote, recorded by the server when the guest enters checkout. One attempt may produce at most one reservation. | [[checkout-orchestration]] |
| **Booking saga** | The sequence of local steps that turns a checkout attempt into a confirmed reservation, each step having either a compensation that undoes it or a guarantee of retry until it succeeds. The term comes from a 1987 paper on long-lived transactions. | [[checkout-orchestration]] |
| **Pivot (saga)** | The saga's point of no return. Steps after it must be retryable and idempotent, because the saga can no longer be compensated as a whole. | [[checkout-orchestration]] |
| **Idempotency key (stays)** | An identifier every system on the booking path uses to recognize a repeated request and return the original result instead of repeating the effect. In stays it must survive several hops, including legacy XML, where it may be a reservation ID plus a message version. | [[checkout-orchestration]] |
| **Instant book** | Booking mode in which the reservation is confirmed when the guest completes payment, against rules set in advance (minimum stay, lead time, guest requirements). Hotel bookings work this way; on hosted-stays marketplaces it is a host choice. | [[checkout-orchestration]] |
| **Request to book** | Booking mode that inserts a host or property decision between payment and confirmation, typically within 24 hours. The hold, the payment authorization and the quote need validity windows that match that window. | [[checkout-orchestration]] |
| **Terms snapshot** | The cancellation policy, payment schedule, house rules, price breakdown and amount payable at the property, frozen by version at checkout and attached to the reservation. Later disputes are judged against it. | [[checkout-orchestration]] |
| **Unknown outcome** | The result of a remote call that timed out: the caller does not know whether it succeeded. It needs its own state and a polling or reconciliation path, not a blind retry. | [[checkout-orchestration]] |

| **Reservation** | The marketplace's record of an agreement that a guest will stay at a property or home on specific nights, under specific terms, for a specific price. One reservation may hold several rooms. | [[reservation-lifecycle]] |
| **Reservation state machine** | The model of a reservation's commercial lifecycle: pending, confirmed, declined or expired, canceled, no-show, stayed, completed. Modification is a new version, not a state; delivery, stay detail and money run as separate machines. | [[reservation-lifecycle]] |
| **Reservation version** | One immutable state of the agreement's terms. A modification creates a new version while the reservation keeps its identity. | [[reservation-lifecycle]] |
| **Booking snapshot** | The copy of commercial terms and key facts taken at confirmation and at each new version: room type or listing, occupancy, rate plan, policies, itemized price and content version. It records what the guest agreed to, independent of later catalog changes. | [[reservation-lifecycle]] |
| **Reservation delivery** | Making a reservation version known to every party that must act on it (the property's PMS, a channel manager, a host, a co-host) and recording proof that each one has it. Tracked per recipient and version. | [[reservation-lifecycle]] |
| **Acknowledgement (sent / received / accepted)** | A recipient's statement that it has a version, recorded at three levels. Only acceptance by the system the front desk uses, or confirmation by a person at the property, closes delivery. | [[reservation-lifecycle]] |
| **Confirmation numbers** | The identifiers each system gives the reservation: the marketplace's reservation ID, the channel manager's ID and the PMS confirmation number. The guest quotes one; the front desk searches by another. | [[reservation-lifecycle]] |
| **No-show** | A guest who neither arrives nor cancels, usually reported by the supplier. It changes commission, fees and payouts, and the book's model lets it be reversed to stayed within a window. | [[reservation-lifecycle]] |
| **Stayed / completion** | Stayed means the reservation's nights were used or are treated as used under marketplace rules. Completion is the later point when nothing more can change the reservation's money or reputation outcome. | [[reservation-lifecycle]] |
| **Reservation exception** | A reservation that needs a human decision before a deadline: undelivered, rejected by the PMS, overbooked, card declined, no-show to report or change requested. | [[reservation-lifecycle]] |
| **Property management system (PMS)** | The hotel's system of record for reservations, rooms and front-desk operations. A reservation is not delivered until the PMS has accepted it. | [[reservation-lifecycle]] |
| **Event sourcing** | Rebuilding an aggregate's state from its event log instead of storing current state. The book recommends an append-only version table plus an outbox instead, as it gives the audit trail with simpler support queries. | [[reservation-lifecycle]] |

| **Cancellation policy** | The rule that says what a guest gets back, and what the supplier keeps, if the guest cancels at a given time. Expressed as ordered time windows before arrival, each with a typed penalty, and evaluated in the property's local time. | [[changes-and-cancellations]] |
| **Policy snapshot** | The cancellation policy frozen onto the reservation at booking, with its version. Later edits to the rate plan or listing do not change it. | [[changes-and-cancellations]] |
| **Cancellation policy engine** | The single service that evaluates a policy snapshot, an action, an actor, a time and any overrides into an itemized decision. | [[changes-and-cancellations]] |
| **Non-refundable rate** | A rate that returns nothing on cancellation, usually cheaper. EU law gives no cooling-off right for accommodation, so cancellation terms are a commercial attribute of the offer. | [[changes-and-cancellations]] |
| **Free cancellation** | A policy window before arrival in which a guest cancels without penalty. On hosted-stays marketplaces it comes from a small set of platform-defined tiers, not per-property rules. | [[changes-and-cancellations]] |
| **Guest-facing / supplier-facing policy** | The policy the marketplace sold to the guest and the one the supplier granted the marketplace. Usually identical; sometimes deliberately not, in which case the marketplace underwrites the difference. | [[changes-and-cancellations]] |
| **Modification** | A change to a confirmed reservation that keeps its identity: dates, guests, room type, rate plan, guest name or special requests. It creates a new reservation version. | [[changes-and-cancellations]] |
| **Self-service change** | A guest- or supplier-initiated change completed without an agent, from quote to confirmation. It is complete only when the guest knows the cost, the outcome and what was sent to the property. | [[changes-and-cancellations]] |
| **Extenuating circumstances** | Events outside the guest's and supplier's control that justify overriding a booking's policy. The book models them as declared disruption events. | [[changes-and-cancellations]] |
| **Declared disruption event** | A record with an owner, class, places, stay window, booking cutoff, party filter, policy override, funding and expiry, created when something happens and applied by machines. | [[changes-and-cancellations]] |
| **Disruption registry** | The shared store of declared disruption events that the cancellation engine, support, notifications and payments all consult, so the same event gets one answer everywhere. | [[changes-and-cancellations]] |
| **Booking cutoff** | The time of a declared disruption event after which new bookings are excluded, because a guest booking after the strike is announced accepted the risk. | [[changes-and-cancellations]] |
| **Party filter** | The rule on a declared event that limits it to affected guests, for example by origin market or itinerary for transport events. | [[changes-and-cancellations]] |
| **Merchant model / agency model** | Under the merchant model the marketplace collects the guest's payment and can refund it itself; under the agency model the property collects, so the marketplace can only waive its own share and ask the property to waive the rest. | [[changes-and-cancellations]], [[merchant-and-agency-models]] |
| **Host or property cancellation** | A cancellation initiated by the supplier, including an overbooked hotel walking a guest. | [[changes-and-cancellations]] |

| **Trip** | The guest-facing view of one or more reservations for one journey, from confirmation to the end of the review window. It owns no booking, payment or supply fact. | [[trip-and-messaging]] |
| **Trip timeline** | The ordered list of past and upcoming events in a trip, each with who acts, by when and what the guest should do. It is a read model built from events owned by other services. | [[trip-and-messaging]] |
| **Read model** | A view built from events owned by other services and used to answer queries. In the trip, a fact belongs to the service that owns it and the read model only explains it. | [[trip-and-messaging]], [[event-driven-stays]] |
| **Trip status explanation** | The one or two sentences at the top of the trip that say where the guest stands, derived from facts with a source and a time. | [[trip-and-messaging]] |
| **Arrival package** | Everything a guest needs to get from the street to the bed: address, check-in window, how to get in, parking, Wi-Fi, house rules, emergency contact. For a home part of it is secret and per reservation. | [[trip-and-messaging]] |
| **Access credential** | A code or digital key valid for one reservation's window, generated by a lock platform, a PMS or the marketplace's own lock integration. Its lifecycle follows reservation events. | [[trip-and-messaging]] |
| **Guest-host messaging** | A governed three-party channel: a conversation between guest and property or host about an inquiry or reservation, in which the platform can annotate, redact and intervene. | [[trip-and-messaging]] |
| **Structured request** | A message with a type and fields rather than free text, such as arrival time, early check-in, date change or cancel for less. It can be routed, answered with one tap and turned into a reservation change. | [[trip-and-messaging]] |
| **Notification routing** | The decision whether, when, through which channels and in which language to send a notification, and what to do when a channel stays silent. | [[trip-and-messaging]] |
| **Notification class** | The category of a notification (arrival-critical, disruption, transactional, conversation, account security, engagement, marketing) that sets priority, channels, mutability and sending lane. | [[trip-and-messaging]] |
| **Disintermediation** | Guest and host completing the transaction off the platform, which removes the marketplace's fee and protections. Contact-sharing rules before booking exist mainly to prevent it. | [[trip-and-messaging]] |
| **Sender Policy Framework (SPF) / DomainKeys Identified Mail (DKIM)** | E-mail authentication standards that mailbox providers require from bulk senders. Separate sending domains for transactional and marketing mail protect arrival-critical delivery. | [[trip-and-messaging]] |

| **Contact** | One inbound or outbound interaction in one channel: a call, a chat session, an e-mail or a message to the AI assistant. It is the unit vendors bill and staffing models count. | [[customer-service]] |
| **Contact driver (contact reason)** | The underlying reason for a contact, from a controlled taxonomy such as "cancel a refundable booking" or "property says no reservation". It routes to self-service, AI or human skills and links support to product defects. | [[customer-service]] |
| **Case** | The record of one problem, binding parties, reservations, contact driver, deadlines, actions and outcome. It may span many contacts and channels, and is the unit a marketplace should manage and measure. | [[customer-service]] |
| **Arrival clock** | The deadline set by the stay itself: time to check-in, time to check-out or a cancellation deadline. It decides urgency more than any service level does. | [[customer-service]] |
| **Service level (SLA)** | A target such as "80% of calls answered in 60 seconds" or "first reply to e-mail within 24 hours". It measures the queue, not the outcome. | [[customer-service]] |
| **Recovery decision** | A recorded remedy for a case (refund, rebooking, relocation, credit, goodwill payment, fee waiver) with the cause code, entitlement, funder and liable party. It turns support outcomes into money facts that finance and partner terms can use. | [[customer-service]] |
| **Entitlement** | What a guest, partner or host is owed under the applicable policy and rule version. Entitlements are versioned rules, not agent scripts. | [[customer-service]] |
| **Funder / liable party** | The funder pays a remedy now; the liable party ultimately bears the cost and the recovery path says how it is recovered, for example a line on a partner statement. | [[customer-service]] |
| **Relocation (walked guest)** | Moving a guest to another property when theirs cannot honor the booking. It is modeled as a linked reservation with a cause code and a liable party, not a state of the original. | [[customer-service]], [[reservation-lifecycle]] |
| **Guest guarantee** | A marketplace-funded promise to rebook or compensate a guest whose host cancels or whose check-in fails. The marketplace pays first and recovers from the host where its terms allow, for example through a cancellation fee. | [[customer-service]] |
| **Deflection / containment** | The share of contacts that self-service or an AI agent ends without a human. Easy to inflate; it says nothing about whether the problem was solved. | [[customer-service]] |
| **Resolution** | The case's problem is solved and stays solved, measured by no reopening or repeat contact within a window. | [[customer-service]] |
