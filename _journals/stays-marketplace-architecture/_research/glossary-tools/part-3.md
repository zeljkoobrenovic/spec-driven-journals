| **Availability, rates and inventory (ARI)** | The data a supplier sends a marketplace about what can be sold: how many units of a room type are free on each night, at what prices, under which restrictions. In this book most of what looks like ARI data is really ARI rules. On hosted-stays marketplaces the equivalent is one calendar per unit with a nightly price and day rules. | [[rates-and-availability-model]] |
| **Inventory / availability** | Inventory is the number of sellable units of a room type on a night. Availability is what a guest can actually book for given dates, occupancy and conditions after restrictions are applied. | [[rates-and-availability-model]] |
| **Rate plan / product** | A rate plan is a named commercial offer: a pricing logic plus conditions such as cancellation, meal plan and payment terms. A product is a room type combined with a rate plan, the thing ARI prices and restricts. Every rate plan of a room type sells from the same inventory pool. | [[rates-and-availability-model]] |
| **Pricing type** | The rule for what one stored price means: standard (price at maximum occupancy), derived occupancy (base plus offsets), occupancy-based (one price per guest count) or length-of-stay. It decides how many price cells a product needs. | [[rates-and-availability-model]] |
| **Length-of-stay (LOS) pricing** | A price stored per arrival date and stay length, so the price of a stay is no longer the sum of its nightly prices. It multiplies the key space of a cache and forces tiering. | [[rates-and-availability-model]] |
| **Derived rate** | A rate plan whose price, and often its restrictions and open or closed status, follows a parent plan through a rule such as a percentage offset. The order of operations (discount, supplement, rounding) changes the final price and must be defined once. | [[rates-and-availability-model]] |
| **Best available rate (BAR)** | The base public rate sold to unqualified guests, which a hotel revenue management system sets and from which most other rates are fixed offsets. Some systems price segments and channels independently instead, so the marketplace's model must hold both styles. | [[rates-and-availability-model]], [[dynamic-pricing]] |
| **Restriction** | A rule that removes availability for some stays without changing inventory, for example minimum or maximum stay, closed to arrival (CTA), closed to departure (CTD), stop-sell or advance purchase. Restrictions have stay-through and on-arrival variants, so they must be evaluated per stay, not per night. | [[rates-and-availability-model]] |
| **Minimum stay (MinLOS / MaxLOS)** | The fewest or most nights a stay may have. A stay-through rule applies to every night the stay includes; an on-arrival rule applies only to stays arriving that day. | [[rates-and-availability-model]] |
| **Gap night** | A short gap, often one or two nights, between two bookings that a minimum-stay rule can leave unsellable. Host pricing tools discount such gaps. Hosts also use restrictions as operational controls (for example a cleaner's schedule), while hotels use them as revenue controls. | [[rates-and-availability-model]] |
| **Inclusion flag / fee basis** | Metadata stored with each amount saying whether it includes VAT, city tax or mandatory fees, and how each fee is charged (per stay, per night, per person per night or percentage). Without them each surface guesses and prices drift. | [[rates-and-availability-model]] |
| **Stay evaluator** | The single versioned component that turns stored facts (inventory, prices, restrictions), rules, fees and policies into an answer for one stay: bookable or not, and at what price. | [[rates-and-availability-model]] |

| **Availability engine** | The read model that answers "what can I book, and for how much" at search scale, from precomputed offers with an explicit staleness budget, falling back to on-demand computation. On hosted-stays marketplaces the hard part shifts from combinatorics to confidence that a free night is really free. | [[availability-engine]] |
| **Availability query / offer** | A query names candidates, a stay and a party, plus audience and display currency. The answer is an offer: a priced, conditioned promise to sell a specific product for a specific stay to a specific party, valid for a limited time. | [[availability-engine]] |
| **Offer computation / offer kernel** | Offer computation turns ARI into offers: per-night eligibility, stay-level restrictions, occupancy fit, discounts, fees, taxes and currency. The offer kernel is its one versioned implementation, shared by the grid builder, the on-demand path and the price check. | [[availability-engine]] |
| **Card price** | The price a property shows on a search result card, usually the cheapest or best qualifying room-rate for the stay. | [[availability-engine]] |
| **Price accuracy** | The probability that a price shown at an earlier step (search card, property page, checkout, statement) equals the price charged at the final step for the same stay, party and conditions, measured on a sample. Metasearch engines grade partners on it. | [[availability-engine]] |
| **Cache staleness** | How far a served value is from the system of record. Age-based staleness is time since computation; divergence-based staleness is the probability the value differs from what the source would answer now. Age is an engineering metric and divergence is a business metric. | [[availability-engine]] |
| **Staleness budget** | A written limit, per tier (search card, property page, metasearch feed), on how stale a served offer may be and what the price check does when the limit is breached. | [[availability-engine]] |
| **Price check** | A re-computation of the selected offer from the system of record, with the offer kernel, when the guest moves to pay. It is the point where an offer becomes binding. | [[availability-engine]] |
| **Cell (precomputed grid)** | The unit an availability grid stores and the key it is stored under, for example property, arrival date, stay length and party. Each key dimension multiplies the grid; dimensions left out must be computed at read time. | [[availability-engine]] |
| **Occupancy-based pricing (OBP)** | A pricing type with a separate price for each number of guests. | [[rates-and-availability-model]], [[availability-engine]] |
| **Drip pricing** | Adding unavoidable charges later in the purchase process than the first price shown. EU, UK and US rules treat it as misleading or unfair, which makes total price a design requirement. | [[availability-engine]], [[rates-and-availability-model]] |

| **Physical, sellable and available inventory** | Physical inventory is the units that exist on a night. Sellable inventory is the count a supplier offers a channel after closures, and can exceed physical inventory when a hotel overbooks on purpose. Availability is what a specific search can book after restrictions. | [[property-and-listing-model]], [[inventory-integrity]] |
| **Allotment** | A block of rooms a hotel commits to one channel for a period, with unsold rooms returned on a release date before arrival. It is a scheduled obligation, not just a number, and is rare outside wholesale, tour operating and groups. | [[inventory-integrity]] |
| **Free sale** | A channel may sell up to the shared count without asking the hotel before each booking, each booking decrementing the count. Some wholesale contracts use the term differently, so the contract governs. | [[inventory-integrity]] |
| **Release date** | The date before arrival on which unsold allotment rooms return to the hotel. A release that fires late or in the wrong time zone causes oversells. | [[inventory-integrity]] |
| **Inventory hold** | A temporary claim on inventory while a guest completes checkout, released automatically after a time limit unless committed. It is an efficiency device, not the guarantee against selling twice. | [[inventory-integrity]] |
| **Overbooking / oversell / double booking** | Overbooking is deliberate selling beyond physical inventory to offset no-shows, decided by the hotel's revenue management. Oversell is accidental, caused by a wrong copy of the count. A double booking is an oversell of a one-unit listing. Overbooking has an owner and a reason; the other two are defects. | [[inventory-integrity]] |
| **Walk / relocation** | A walk is when the property turns away a guest with a confirmed reservation and arranges a room elsewhere. A relocation is when the marketplace moves the guest because the original cannot honor the reservation. A home has no corridor of identical rooms to walk a guest into. | [[inventory-integrity]] |
| **Auto-replenishment** | A marketplace default that puts a canceled room back on sale, which can resell a room the hotel already resold or closed. | [[inventory-integrity]] |
| **Unit cardinality** | The number of identical units behind a listing or room type. One-unit listings commit against a uniqueness constraint; multi-unit room types commit by conditional decrement in an allocation ledger. | [[inventory-integrity]] |
| **Acknowledgement watermark** | A control that computes the sellable limit as the supplier's last claim minus the marketplace's own reservations the supplier had not yet acknowledged when it made that claim. | [[inventory-integrity]] |
| **Negative-headroom signal** | An oversell detector: a supplier claim of fewer rooms than the marketplace's own committed but unacknowledged reservations for that night. | [[inventory-integrity]] |
| **Fencing token** | An increasing number checked by the storage layer so that a client whose lock lease expired cannot write after another client took the lock. Locks reduce contention but do not make a commit correct. | [[inventory-integrity]] |

| **Revenue management (RM) / revenue management system (RMS)** | Selling fixed, perishable capacity to the right customer at the right price and time, using price and availability controls driven by a demand forecast. An RMS is the software that forecasts demand and recommends prices and controls. | [[dynamic-pricing]] |
| **Occupancy / ADR / RevPAR / RevPAN** | Occupancy is the share of available nights sold. Average daily rate (ADR) is room revenue per room-night sold. Revenue per available room (RevPAR) is room revenue per room-night available. Revenue per available night (RevPAN) is the same for one home and is the right objective for host pricing. | [[dynamic-pricing]] |
| **Booking curve / pickup / lead time** | The booking curve is cumulative bookings for a stay date by days before arrival; pickup is bookings added in a period; lead time is days between booking and arrival. | [[dynamic-pricing]] |
| **Unconstrained demand** | The demand that would have arrived if capacity and price had not limited it. Forecasts try to estimate it because sold-out nights hide it. | [[dynamic-pricing]] |
| **Last room value / displacement** | Last room value is the minimum price worth accepting for the last rooms. Displacement is the revenue lost by accepting a booking that blocks a better one, such as a cheap one-night stay splitting a week. | [[dynamic-pricing]] |
| **Comp set (competitive set)** | The competing properties or listings a price is judged against. Hotels pick four to eight by hand; for homes it is computed from location, capacity, quality and type. It feeds recommendations, anomaly checks and partner insight. | [[dynamic-pricing]] |
| **Rate recommendation / smart pricing** | A rate recommendation (price tip) is an advisory price the host or partner decides on, so it is a read. Smart pricing is marketplace-computed nightly prices written into a host's calendar with consent, so it is a write. | [[dynamic-pricing]] |
| **Holdout** | A standing random group of listings priced by the previous model version so the current model is always compared with something. | [[dynamic-pricing]] |

| **Promotion / discount** | A promotion is a time- or condition-bound reduction of a published price, created by a party that agrees to fund it. It has eligibility conditions, a discount, a combination class and a funder. | [[promotions-and-loyalty]] |
| **Funder (partner-, marketplace- and co-funded)** | The party that bears a discount. Partner-funded reduces what the property or host receives; marketplace-funded leaves the partner's amount unchanged; co-funded splits the cost by formula. Every discount line carries its funder to the ledger. | [[promotions-and-loyalty]] |
| **Combination class** | The attribute of a promotion that says what it may stack with. Discounts do not commute once caps and fixed amounts are involved, so composition order is a contract. | [[promotions-and-loyalty]] |
| **Closed user group (CUG) / conditional rate** | A CUG is an audience that must identify itself to see a price, such as signed-in members, app users or a corporate account. A CUG rate is a conditional rate: the same room and night has a different price depending on who asks. | [[promotions-and-loyalty]] |
| **Loyalty program / loyalty currency** | A loyalty program recognizes repeat guests with tiers, benefits or a currency. A loyalty currency is a liability in a ledger: a promise of future value that accrues, reverses on cancellation, expires and is redeemed. | [[promotions-and-loyalty]] |
| **Partner program** | A commercial arrangement in which a property or host gives something (higher commission, discounts, quality commitments) for something (a badge, a ranking boost, marketing spend). Regulators have begun to examine such programs. | [[promotions-and-loyalty]], [[stays-ranking]] |
| **Rate parity (wide / narrow)** | A contractual commitment to offer a platform rates and conditions at least as good as elsewhere. Wide parity covers all other channels including other OTAs; narrow parity covers only the property's own direct channels. In the EU the Digital Markets Act and the 2024 CJEU ruling in C-264/23 have ended it for most platforms. | [[promotions-and-loyalty]] |
| **Redemption / incrementality** | A redemption is a booking that used a discount. Incrementality is the share of redemptions that would not have happened without it. | [[promotions-and-loyalty]] |
