> **IN THIS SECTION, YOU WILL:** Meet Corvane Group, the fictional travel group this book follows: its four businesses, 2026 volumes, 2027 systems, people and starting problems, the One Stay program approved to solve them, the group platforms the chapters introduce, and the worked examples whose numbers every chapter shares.

> **WHY THE BUSINESS CARES:** Corvane earns €1.692 billion a year from two different ways of selling a night's stay, plus software and payments around them. Its owners want the merchant share, the hosted-stays business and the margin to grow at once, and the group pays for most of its capabilities twice.

> **WHY ARCHITECTS SHOULD CARE:** Corvane's difficulty is not size; it is that the two marketplaces hold the truth about availability, money and trust in different places. Three clouds, two monoliths, two identity systems and two trust stacks must converge without forcing a hotel and a home into one supply model, and that is the Chief Architect's problem in miniature.

> **KEY POINTS:**
>
> * **Corvane is a fictional composite, not a disguised company.** It borrows patterns common in European travel groups so the book can show real architectural tensions with consistent numbers. Roomstead is not a disguised Booking.com or Expedia, and Nookaway is not a disguised Airbnb or Vrbo; every name, figure, system and event on this page is invented.
> * **One group, two marketplace types.** Roomstead copies the truth about rooms and rates from 640,000 properties' systems and earns mostly agency commission; Nookaway holds the truth about 1.15 million homes itself, holds the guest's money until after check-in and earns split fees. The same words, "available" and "booked", mean different machinery in each.
> * **One Stay shares contracts and platforms, not supply engines.** The €145 million program (2027–2030) builds one identity, ledger, trust, messaging, support, tax and data layer, and one offer and reservation contract across both brands, while Roomstead keeps its room-type inventory and Nookaway its one-unit calendar; Keystone's reservation module is to be retired by the end of 2029.

> **[FAILURE PATTERNS THIS SECTION ADDRESSES](where-stay-platforms-go-wrong.html):**
>
> * **The Hotel-Shaped Home** — in early 2027 a Roomstead team proposed loading Nookaway's listings into Tally as one-unit room types, and the group rejected it before One Stay was approved.
> * **The Double-Booked Night** — 47% of Nookaway's multichannel listings sync by iCal only, and the Seville double booking is what that looks like for one family.
> * **Phantom Availability** — Roomstead's metasearch price accuracy was 91% in 2026 against a 97% target, mostly because its price cache was stale.

<br>
This book tests its argument against **one fictional travel group, detailed enough to be realistic and consistent enough to be followed across fifty-one chapters.** A toy booking site, one hotel table and a search box, is too simple to show why the hard decisions are hard; a real company's internals cannot be verified and its choices do not transfer. This page is the group's reference. The failure patterns of [[where-stay-platforms-go-wrong]] appear here as Corvane's 2027 problems, and every chapter from Part I on opens with a scene whose people, systems and numbers come from this page.

**Corvane Group is fictional.** It is a composite of *patterns* common in the travel industry: an agency-model hotel site that later added merchant payments, a hosted-stays marketplace bought rather than built, a channel manager acquired from the supply side, a payments institution created to hold guests' money, and private-equity owners who want growth and efficiency together. It is **not a portrait of any real company**. **Roomstead and Nookaway are not disguised versions of Booking.com, Expedia, Agoda, Airbnb, Vrbo or anyone else**: they carry none of those companies' documented systems, program names, fee levels or history, and their fees were set so that they match no real platform. Real companies appear in this book under their own names, with dated sources, and never as Corvane.

## Two Marketplaces, Four Businesses

The two marketplaces differ in who supplies the stay, where the truth about availability lives and how money moves. [[two-stay-marketplaces]] owns the definitions; this book calls Roomstead an **accommodation marketplace** (OTA-style, after the online travel agency) and Nookaway a **hosted-stays marketplace**.

| Brand | What it is | Business model | 2026 revenue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Roomstead** (founded 2001, Dublin) | Accommodation marketplace: hotels, B&Bs, hostels and guesthouses, plus apartments and holiday homes mostly from professional managers | **Agency** for **70%** of room nights (guest pays at the property; Roomstead invoices commission); **merchant** for **30%** through "Pay with Roomstead" (Corvane Payments charges the guest and pays the property, mostly by virtual card), target 40% by end 2028 | **€1.200B**: agency commission €860M, merchant margin €270M, partner programs and advertising €70M |
| **Nookaway** (founded 2012, Lisbon; acquired 2021) | Hosted-stays marketplace: whole homes, apartments and private rooms run by individual hosts and professional managers | Facilitated payments: the guest pays Nookaway through Corvane Payments at booking or with Pay Later; the host is paid **48 hours after scheduled check-in**. Split fee: guest service fee **12%** of the subtotal (nights plus cleaning) and host fee **4%**; PMS-connected professional managers may choose a **host-only fee of 17%** | **€430M** |
| **Corvane Connect** (ChannelPier, acquired 2024; rebranded 2025; Utrecht) | Channel manager and connectivity SaaS for **14,500** hotels and **8,200** vacation-rental managers with **96,000** units; distributes to **60+ channels**, including Roomstead's and Nookaway's competitors | SaaS subscriptions | **€62M** |
| **Corvane Payments** (founded 2023) | Corvane Payments B.V., an EMI licensed by De Nederlandsche Bank in 2024, passported in the European Economic Area (EEA); UK through a partner | Processes all Nookaway payments and Roomstead merchant bookings; issues virtual cards to hotels through a partner bank; holds guest funds until payout; margin reported in the brands' revenue | (internal) |

The three revenue lines add up to €1.692 billion: Roomstead about 71%, Nookaway 25% and Corvane Connect 4%.

Four features of the portfolio recur through the book.

- **Roomstead is an agency business learning to be a merchant.** In Pay with Roomstead, Corvane Payments acts as the property's **limited payment collection agent**: the property remains the supplier of the stay and issues the guest's VAT invoice, and Corvane Payments is merchant of record for every collected booking in the group ([[merchant-and-agency-models]]).
- **Nookaway holds the money and the trust.** It holds the guest's payment until after check-in, makes about **6.3 million** payouts a year in 19 currencies, and runs **Nookaway Cover**: host damage protection up to **€500,000**, liability insurance up to **€1 million** through an insurance partner, and a guest guarantee to rebook or refund if check-in fails or the home is materially not as described ([[protection-and-incidents]]).
- **Corvane Connect must stay neutral.** It sends its hotels' rates to Roomstead's competitors, so since **May 2027** a neutrality rule makes it an ordinary partner of Switchyard, Roomstead's connectivity gateway, with no private endpoints and no Roomstead access to its data on other channels ([[connectivity-and-channel-managers]]).
- **Realized take rates differ from the headline.** Roomstead takes about **13%** of stayed booking value. Nookaway's split is 16% on paper but about **14.4%** realized, after long-stay reductions, fee credits, refunded fees and guest-guarantee costs ([[stays-marketplace-economics]]).

Each brand also has named products the chapters use. Roomstead has **Roomstead Circle** (three tiers; Circle rates funded by partners, Circle credit funded by Roomstead), **Front Row** (paid visibility at +2, +4 or +6 commission points), **Rate Insights** (rate recommendations in the extranet, launched 2025) and **Perks** (its coupon and campaign service). Nookaway has **Smart Rate** (automatic pricing, rule-based from 2022 and ML-based from 2025), **Pay Later** (launched October 2026) and **Hearth**, a quarterly host credential. All are fictional; none is a renamed real program.

| Metric | Roomstead | Nookaway |
| --- | --- | --- |

| Volume | **109 million** room nights booked, **34%** canceled, **72 million** stayed; about **45 million** reservations made and **30 million** stayed, 2.4 room nights each | **21 million** nights stayed; about **6.9 million** reservations made and **6.0 million** stayed, 3.5 nights each; **13%** of reservations canceled |
| Price | average daily rate (ADR) **€128**; stayed booking value about **€9.2 billion** | average **€142** a night including cleaning; booking value about **€2.98 billion** |
| Supply integration | **82%** of hotel room nights arrive through **140 certified connectivity partners**; 18% are managed in the **Partner Hub** extranet; Corvane Connect serves 21% of connected Roomstead hotels | **61%** of listings are also sold elsewhere: 47% of those sync by iCal only, 53% through PMS or channel-manager APIs; iCal imports polled every **3 hours** by default |
| Traffic | about **650 million** ARI (availability, rates and inventory) messages a day, peak **38,000/s**; about **1,400 shopping requests** per gross reservation, each making about ten price-cache reads: **21,000** reads/s on average, **64,000/s** at the 2026 peak | about **420** search requests per gross reservation, 85% from Nookaway's own web and apps |
| Booking behavior | median booking window **18 days**; metasearch price accuracy **91%** (target 97%) | median booking window **41 days**; instant book on **68%** of listings, the rest request-to-book with a 24-hour response window and **79%** host acceptance |

Two cautions. The 1,400 counts **shopping requests**; the per-second figures are price-cache reads, about ten per request ([[availability-engine]]). And ratios carry more architecture than totals: Roomstead earns about €40 per stayed reservation after paying about €13 to acquire it, largely through metasearch, so a stale price costs it money every hour; Nookaway earns about €72 and barely uses metasearch ([[stays-marketplace-economics]]).

Identity, experimentation (Trialroom and Flipside), warehouses (Snowflake and BigQuery), event buses and customer-service tools are duplicated in the same way. Three facts in this landscape shape most of the book.

- **Keystone still owns Roomstead's commitments.** Reservations, partner contracts, commission invoicing, reviews, the extranet's web module and the checkout module that creates the reservation live in one Java application on Oracle, with a manually failed-over standby last rehearsed in 2025 ([[global-scale-and-reliability]]). Every One Stay contract that touches a Roomstead reservation goes through Keystone until 2029.
- **Two inventory engines encode two truths.** Tally holds about **5.8 million** active products (room type × rate plan); Almanac about **840 million** listing-nights. Roomstead answers shopping from a precomputed cache and pays in staleness; Nookaway computes at query time and pays in latency ([[rates-and-availability-model]], [[availability-engine]]).
- **Three clouds, by acquisition.** A group platform must work across AWS, Google Cloud (Nookaway, one region) and Azure (Corvane Connect), or be reached through a bridge.

## The Problems Corvane Starts 2027 With

When Mariana arrives, both marketplaces work and the group is profitable, but its duplication grows faster than the business. Six problems define her starting position, each an instance of a pattern from [[where-stay-platforms-go-wrong]].

1. **Two of almost everything.** Two identity systems, trust stacks, search engines, experimentation platforms, warehouses and customer-service tools, on three clouds. Posed in [[stays-capability-map]] and [[stays-bounded-contexts]]; worked in [[identity-accounts-and-access]], [[stays-trust-and-safety]], [[customer-service]] and [[marketplace-intelligence]].
2. **Roomstead's prices drift from reality.** Metasearch price accuracy is 91% against 97%, and Pricegrid staleness is the main cause. *(Phantom Availability, Price Drift.)* Worked in [[availability-engine]] and [[property-page-and-room-selection]].
3. **Nookaway sells the same night twice.** About 3,100 calendar-conflict host cancellations in 2026, 78% of them on iCal-only multichannel listings. *(The Double-Booked Night.)* Worked in [[calendar-sync]] and [[inventory-integrity]].
4. **Regulation arrives city by city.** The EU Short-Term Rental Regulation has applied since 20 May 2026, Spanish registration rules have already forced listing removals, and the VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) package makes platforms the deemed VAT supplier of most short-term rentals from dates between 1 July 2028 and 1 January 2030, at each member state's choice. *(City-by-City Firefighting, The Tax Patchwork.)* Worked in [[stays-regulation]] and [[taxes-on-stays]].
5. **Merchant growth on agency rails.** Roomstead's move toward 40% merchant bookings runs through Keystone's agency-era reservation model. *(Ledgerless Money.)* Worked in [[merchant-and-agency-models]], [[payouts-and-settlement]] and [[refunds-and-reconciliation]].
6. **Keystone owns the commitments.** The Java and Oracle monolith still owns Roomstead's reservations, partner contracts and commission invoicing. Worked in [[reservation-lifecycle]], [[event-driven-stays]] and [[modernizing-a-live-marketplace]].

None of these is a technology problem alone. The price drift exists because fees, taxes and currency are composed in several places; the double bookings because a home sold on two platforms has no single calendar anyone controls; the merchant problem because a reservation record designed when Roomstead never touched the money now has to say who holds it.

## The One Stay Program

In **April 2027** Corvane's board approved **One Stay**, on a proposal from Tobias Lindqvist and Mariana Costa: **€145 million** over three years (2027–2030), with a target to retire Keystone's reservation module by **the end of 2029**. Its scope has three tiers.

- **Shared:** Corvane ID (one identity across brands), payments and ledger (already shared, to be extended), one trust platform, one messaging and notifications platform, one data platform and metric layer, one customer-service platform and one tax engine.
- **Shared contracts, separate engines:** a common **offer** and **reservation** contract across both marketplaces. Roomstead keeps Tally and Pricegrid for room-type inventory; Nookaway keeps Almanac for one-unit calendars. The argument is made in [[converging-two-marketplaces]].
- **Not shared, for now:** supply models, connectivity (Switchyard and Nookaway's partner API) and ranking models.

The principle behind the tiers came out of a rejected proposal. In early 2027 a Roomstead supply team proposed loading Nookaway's listings into Tally as one-unit room types and retiring Almanac. It was rejected because Roomstead's 435,000 homes are mostly professional supply that behaves like hotels, while Nookaway's are mostly individual hosts whose calendar is the product ([[two-stay-marketplaces]]). **Share what the guest and the money see; keep apart what the supply sees.**

![A three-tier stack: Roomstead and Nookaway on top, one shared contracts band in the middle, and below it separate Tally and Almanac engines beside one block of shared group platforms.](assets/images/meet-corvane/00-one-stay-in-one-picture.jpeg)
**Figure 3:** *One Stay in one picture: the brands keep their products and their supply engines, both speak one offer, reservation and place contract, and the platforms beneath the contracts are built once for the group.*

Contract and boundary changes go to the **architecture forum**, chaired by Mariana weekly since February 2027; funding, disputes and accepted risks go to the monthly **group architecture board**, chaired by Tobias ([[organizing-stays-engineering]]). A **contracts stewardship group** in the Group Chief Architect's office owns the offer, reservation and payment-arrangement schemas, but no engine. The capability map behind the board proposal (v1, presented to the forum on 23 March 2027) counted **48** level-2 capabilities: **16** shared engines, **6** shared contracts and **26** brand-specific ([[stays-capability-map]]); the December 2027 annual review adopted v2 with **49**: 23, 6 and 20 ([[converging-two-marketplaces]]). The context map of **5 May 2027** drew **23** bounded contexts: 12 shared, 4 shared contracts and 7 per marketplace ([[stays-bounded-contexts]]). The largest budget lines are contracts, Stayline and Keystone retirement (**€34 million**) and the data, ML and experimentation platforms (**€29 million**).

Most decisions created the named platforms of the next section. Five others recur: the **checkout attempt contract** of February 2027, one server-minted idempotency key at every hop ([[checkout-orchestration]]); the **price-composition and display policy** decision of March 2027, after which both brands show the all-in stay total from September ([[property-page-and-room-selection]]); the June 2027 **search decision**, one query contract over separate indexes and rankers ([[stays-search-architecture]]); **Ranking Policy v1**, live in EEA markets on 12 October 2027 ([[stays-ranking]]); and the **Keystone Exit Plan** of 20 December 2027, which moves Roomstead's future reservations to Stayline in market cohorts during 2029 ([[modernizing-a-live-marketplace]]).

## The Recurring Worked Examples

Eleven stories recur through the book. Their facts never change, so a reader can follow one booking or one incident across many chapters.

**The €12 rate at Hotel Miradouro.** On Tuesday **9 March 2027**, between 07:14 and 07:52 WET, Duarte Silva's bulk rate update for **14–16 May 2027** passed through Corvane Connect, where a decimal-mapping error in a newly mapped rate plan sent **€12.00** instead of €120.00 for "Standard Double – Flexible" to Roomstead and two other channels. **41** Roomstead reservations (**63** room nights) were made in **38** minutes, because the anomaly check compared each rate only with the same plan's previous value, and the plan was new. Roomstead honored all 41; Corvane Connect compensated the hotel 63 × €108 = **€6,804**. Worked in [[connectivity-and-channel-managers]]; also in [[rates-and-availability-model]], [[availability-engine]], [[stays-bounded-contexts]] and [[event-driven-stays]].

**The Seville double booking.** On **2 February 2027** at 10:12 CET the Bauer family from Munich booked Carmen Ruiz's apartment on Nookaway for **20–25 April 2027**, Feria week: 5 × €260 + €60 = **€1,360** before guest fees. At 10:40 a booking for 21–24 April arrived on the competing platform, which had last imported Nookaway's iCal export at 10:00; Nookaway's own imports ran at 09:00 and 12:00. Carmen canceled the Nookaway booking at 13:05. The guest guarantee rehoused the Bauers for **€1,780**, Nookaway funded the **€420** difference, and Carmen paid a **€130** host cancellation fee from her next payout. Worked in [[calendar-sync]] and [[inventory-integrity]]; the money side is in [[payouts-and-settlement]] and [[refunds-and-reconciliation]].

**King's Day overbooking.** For **27 April 2027**, Hotel Prinsenkade's channel manager queued updates for **47 minutes** during an outage on 26 April, and the hotel ended **9 rooms** oversold across channels. Roomstead customer service relocated **4** walked Roomstead guests to Haarlem and Amstelveen for **€2,150** and recovered **€1,600** from the hotel under partner terms that cap the hotel's liability at €400 per relocated reservation. Worked in [[inventory-integrity]] and [[customer-service]]; also in [[connectivity-and-channel-managers]], [[changes-and-cancellations]] and [[payouts-and-settlement]].

**The Spanish registration purge.** Between **February and April 2026**, under the Spanish national registry then in force and regional registration rules, Nookaway removed **6,900** Spanish listings without a valid registration number; **2,900** future reservations with check-in within 30 days were honored and **8,300** later ones canceled with full refunds. On 21 May 2026 Spain's Supreme Court annulled the national registry; regional numbers still apply. The removal is fictional; the annulment is real and sourced in [[stays-regulation]], which works the example; also in [[partner-onboarding]] and [[stays-capability-map]].

**The Dubrovnik damage claim.** On **14 August 2027** a party took place at Ivan Horvat's villa despite reservation screening. Ivan claimed **€3,800**; after evidence review Nookaway Cover paid **€3,450** and recovered **€900** from the guest. Worked in [[protection-and-incidents]]; also in [[stay-payments]], [[refunds-and-reconciliation]] and [[reservation-lifecycle]].

**The extranet phishing wave.** In **November 2027** attackers phished staff credentials at **230** Roomstead partner properties and used in-platform messaging to send fake "payment verification" messages to **4,100** guests. **310** paid the fraudsters; Roomstead reimbursed them **€212,000** (about €684 each). Worked in [[travel-fraud]]; also in [[stays-trust-and-safety]], [[identity-accounts-and-access]], [[trip-and-messaging]], [[customer-service]] and [[protection-and-incidents]].

**The 212 duplicate bookings.** On **14 January 2027** a payment-service-provider latency spike made Roomstead's mobile app retry checkout without reusing its idempotency key; **212** duplicate reservations were created, **198** of them with duplicate charges. Worked in [[checkout-orchestration]]; also in [[inventory-integrity]], [[payouts-and-settlement]] and [[global-scale-and-reliability]].

**The ATC strike weekend.** On **5–7 June 2027** an air-traffic-control strike over France disrupted travel across Europe. About **17,000** Roomstead and **2,600** Nookaway reservations were affected, and Roomstead received **6,200** cancellation requests in 48 hours. Worked in [[changes-and-cancellations]], which builds Clause from it; also in [[dynamic-pricing]], [[trip-and-messaging]], [[refunds-and-reconciliation]], [[global-scale-and-reliability]] and [[stays-experimentation]].

**The €1.8 million reconciliation gap.** In **Q1 2027** Corvane Payments found **€1.8 million** unreconciled between virtual-card issuing statements and Roomstead merchant reservations, mostly cards charged by hotels for reservations later canceled or modified. Worked in [[refunds-and-reconciliation]]; also in [[merchant-and-agency-models]], [[stay-payments]], [[payouts-and-settlement]] and [[marketplace-intelligence]].

**The Novak family's Lisbon trip.** On **11 February 2027** the Novaks searched for Lisbon, 2–9 August, two adults and two children, and abandoned the search. After a price alert, on **14 February** they booked a Nookaway apartment in Alfama: 7 × **€145** = €1,015 plus **€70** cleaning, a guest service fee of **€130.20** and Lisbon tourist tax of **€56** (€4 per guest per night for the two adults; Lisbon exempts guests under 13), **€1,271.20** in total, **25%** above what the nightly price suggested. The journey runs through [[personalization-and-recommendations]], [[stays-search-architecture]], [[destinations-and-maps]], [[property-page-and-room-selection]], [[checkout-orchestration]], [[taxes-on-stays]] and [[trip-and-messaging]].

**Black Friday 2027.** On **26 November 2027** Roomstead's availability lookups peaked at **96,000** price-cache reads a second, 1.5 times the 2026 peak; Pricegrid's hit rate fell from 94% to 71% and Roomstead ran a planned degradation mode for 40 minutes. Worked in [[global-scale-and-reliability]]; also in [[stays-search-architecture]], [[promotions-and-loyalty]], [[event-driven-stays]] and [[stays-experimentation]].

The **Novaks' trip is the guest-side spine**: one booking crosses search, maps, alerts, price display, checkout, tax, messaging and payout. The **€12 rate is the supply-side spine**: one decimal error crosses connectivity, inventory, pricing, delivery and settlement. Between them they show each marketplace failing in its own way: the hotel's truth copied in wrong, the guest's price composed in too many places.

Smaller stories belong to the chapters that introduce them: the **Spain and Portugal metasearch downgrade** of May 2027 ([[availability-engine]]), the **Semana Santa declines** of Pay Later charges ([[stay-payments]]), the **review-trading ring** ([[reviews-and-reputation]]), **Turia Homes** on both brands ([[stays-trust-and-safety]]), **Hotel Miradouro's missing reservations** in July 2027 ([[reservation-lifecycle]]), the **check-in instructions that never arrived** in August 2027 ([[trip-and-messaging]]), **Smart Rate on the strike weekend** ([[dynamic-pricing]]), the **Autumn Cities coupon** ([[promotions-and-loyalty]]), the **two definitions of "booking"** ([[marketplace-intelligence]]), the **free-cancellation badge** experiments ([[stays-experimentation]]) and Nookaway's stretched **"Lisbon"** ([[destinations-and-maps]]).
