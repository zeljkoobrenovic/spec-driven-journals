Check the current source before a definition here goes into a contract, a partner agreement, a policy or a design decision.

> **[FAILURE PATTERNS THIS SECTION ADDRESSES](where-stay-platforms-go-wrong.html):**
>
> * **The Hotel-Shaped Home** — the same failure at the level of words: when "unit", "listing", "rate plan" and "availability" mean one thing on the hotel side and another on the homes side, one model gets forced onto both; naming the difference is the first defense.
> * **Price Drift** — when "price", "total", "fee" and "tax" are composed in different places, the numbers disagree because the words did first; one definition of the offer and the total display is where the repair starts.

[[introduction]] explains the labels; [[where-stay-platforms-go-wrong]] tells each failure pattern in full, with its warning signs, mechanism, cost, the marketplace type it hits hardest and the chapters that address it. The last column below names the guide and the chapter that does most of the repair work.

| --- | --- | --- |
| **Documented fact** | A company's public statement, a standard, a regulation, a date or a figure from a filing, linked to its source with the year of publication. Company engineering evidence older than about three years may describe systems that have since been replaced. | [[introduction]] |
| **Industry practice** | What accommodation marketplaces commonly do, phrased as "commonly" or "a typical pattern", without invented percentages. | [[introduction]] |
| **Reference-architecture inference** | A plausible design derived from engineering principles. When it concerns a real company it is labeled "INFERENCE, NOT DOCUMENTED" and is never a description of that company's systems. | [[introduction]] |
| **Recommendation (evidence label)** | The author's architectural judgement, given with its reasoning and the conditions under which it does not hold. | [[introduction]] |
| **Illustration** | Anything about Corvane, the fictional travel group the book follows. Its numbers are invented for teaching and agree with one shared scenario ledger. | [[meet-corvane]] |
| **Failure pattern** | A named, recurring way stay platforms go wrong, with warning signs that can be checked in a design review and a note on which marketplace type it hits hardest. The book uses sixteen. | [[where-stay-platforms-go-wrong]] |
| **Phantom Availability** | Search shows rooms and prices that are gone or different at checkout, so "sold out" and "price changed" become steps in the funnel. Hits OTAs hardest, because they copy counted inventory and derived prices at extreme look-to-book ratios. | [[where-stay-platforms-go-wrong]], [[availability-engine]] |
| **The Double-Booked Night** | One home or room is sold twice because calendars on different channels or services disagree for minutes or hours. Hits hosted stays hardest, where one unit has no spare room to absorb the error. | [[where-stay-platforms-go-wrong]], [[calendar-sync]] |
| **Rate Plan Sprawl** | Properties carry dozens of rate plans and derived rates nobody can explain, and every new product adds another combination to cache, map and test. | [[where-stay-platforms-go-wrong]], [[rates-and-availability-model]] |
| **Price Drift** | The price in search, on the property page, at checkout and on the card statement are four different numbers, because fees, taxes and currency are added in different places. | [[where-stay-platforms-go-wrong]], [[property-page-and-room-selection]] |
| **The Same Hotel Twice** | One property appears as several products from different suppliers or channels, with mismatched rooms, content and prices. | [[where-stay-platforms-go-wrong]], [[content-and-media]] |
| **Connectivity Roulette** | Partner integrations run without contracts, certification, ordering guarantees or health monitoring, so every rate update is a surprise. | [[where-stay-platforms-go-wrong]], [[connectivity-and-channel-managers]] |
| **Lost at the Front Desk** | Reservations are sent to the property or host without acknowledgement or reconciliation, and guests arrive to "we have no booking for you". | [[where-stay-platforms-go-wrong]], [[reservation-lifecycle]] |
| **The Hotel-Shaped Home** | One supply model is forced onto both marketplaces: homes squeezed into room types and rate plans, or hotels flattened into one-unit calendars. | [[where-stay-platforms-go-wrong]], [[property-and-listing-model]] |
| **Pay-to-Rank Creep** | Commission levels, programs and boosts accumulate in ranking until guests stop trusting the order and regulators ask for an explanation. | [[where-stay-platforms-go-wrong]], [[stays-ranking]] |
| **Policy Spaghetti** | Cancellation, refund and change rules are encoded separately in checkout, support tools, payments and partner systems, and give different answers. | [[where-stay-platforms-go-wrong]], [[changes-and-cancellations]] |
| **Ledgerless Money** | Money moves between guests, the marketplace, properties, hosts and tax authorities without one ledger that can say who is owed what. | [[where-stay-platforms-go-wrong]], [[payouts-and-settlement]] |
| **The Tax Patchwork** | VAT, tourist taxes and platform reporting are added market by market as special cases, and nobody can say what was collected for whom. | [[where-stay-platforms-go-wrong]], [[taxes-on-stays]] |
| **Trust After the Incident** | Verification, screening and protection arrive as reactions to incidents and headlines, each a separate tool with its own data. | [[where-stay-platforms-go-wrong]], [[stays-trust-and-safety]] |
| **City-by-City Firefighting** | Each new short-term-rental rule is hand-coded as an emergency project, so compliance is always late and never reusable. | [[where-stay-platforms-go-wrong]], [[stays-regulation]] |
| **Built for Sunny Days** | The platform handles normal booking traffic but not mass cancellations, rebookings, peaks or supplier outages, which arrive all at once. | [[where-stay-platforms-go-wrong]], [[global-scale-and-reliability]] |
| **The Unwatched Model** | A pricing, ranking, screening or fraud model makes consequential decisions without evaluation, monitoring or an owner. | [[where-stay-platforms-go-wrong]], [[stays-ml-platform]] |

## <a id="g-marketplace"></a>Part I — Marketplace

The business vocabulary: which kind of accommodation marketplace this is, how it makes money and how it is decomposed. [[two-stay-marketplaces]] owns the marketplace types, intermediaries and money roles; [[stays-marketplace-economics]] owns take rate, look-to-book and liquidity; [[stays-capability-map]] and [[stays-bounded-contexts]] own the two decompositions and the offer and reservation as aggregates.

| Term | Definition | Chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Accommodation marketplace (OTA-style marketplace)** | A platform where guests search, compare and book stays from many independently operated, mostly professional properties, and the reservation is passed to the property to fulfil. Supply is described as property, room type and rate plan, and the property's own systems usually assert availability and price, which the marketplace copies in. | [[two-stay-marketplaces]] |
| **Online travel agency (OTA)** | The industry's historical name for the accommodation-marketplace type. It says "agency" even when the OTA acts as merchant of record, which is why the book names the money roles separately. | [[two-stay-marketplaces]] |
| **Hosted-stays marketplace** | A platform where individuals and professional managers offer homes, apartments and private rooms, usually one unit with one calendar per listing. The platform holds the calendar, collects the guest's payment, and runs the verification, reviews and protection that make a stay with a stranger acceptable. | [[two-stay-marketplaces]] |
| **Vacation-rental marketplace** | The older, narrower relative of hosted stays: a platform for whole holiday homes, historically run like classifieds where the owner paid to advertise and the guest paid the owner directly. Today it mostly names a supply segment (whole homes, weekly stays, changeover days, damage deposits) rather than a distinct business model. | [[two-stay-marketplaces]] |
| **Metasearch** | A price-comparison engine that queries OTAs, hotel sites and other sellers for the same stay, shows prices side by side and sends the guest elsewhere to book. It holds the guest's attention but not the booking, and earns mainly per click or referral. | [[two-stay-marketplaces]] |
| **Bedbank (wholesaler)** | A business-to-business intermediary that contracts hotel rates, usually net rates the reseller marks up, and resells them through APIs to travel agents, tour operators, OTAs and other distributors. It never meets the guest. | [[two-stay-marketplaces]] |
| **Direct booking** | A guest booking with the property or host itself, without a marketplace: the hotel's own site and booking engine, call center or front desk, or a host's own site. It is the marketplace's competitor and, for many hotels, its benchmark for what commission is worth. | [[two-stay-marketplaces]] |
| **Global distribution system (GDS)** | The network that connects travel agents to airlines, hotels and car rental companies (for example Sabre, Amadeus, Travelport). A GDS holds no hotel inventory of its own; it routes requests to the hotel's reservation system. | [[two-stay-marketplaces]] |
| **Professional versus individual supply** | The axis the book prefers to building type: who operates the supply and with what systems. Professional operators (chains, independents, property managers) connect by API like hotels; individual hosts type calendars in by hand or sync by iCal, and need platform-supplied trust and protection. | [[two-stay-marketplaces]] |
| **Room night** | One room or home occupied for one night, the unit in which accommodation volume is counted. A reservation of two rooms for three nights is six room nights; hosted-stays marketplaces usually count nights per listing, since one listing is one unit. | [[stays-marketplace-economics]] |
| **Take rate** | The marketplace's revenue from stays divided by the gross booking value of those stays over the same period. The book requires stating whether advertising is in the numerator, whether the denominator is gross or stayed, and that revenue is usually recognized at check-in, not at booking. | [[stays-marketplace-economics]] |
| **Gross booking value (GBV)** | The total value of bookings made, including the partner's or host's share. It can be measured gross (at booking) or stayed (after cancellations), and the definition must say whether taxes, cleaning fees and guest service fees are included. | [[stays-marketplace-economics]] |
| **Look-to-book ratio** | The number of shopping requests a marketplace serves per reservation made. Units must be stated: what counts as a request (a page versus each property lookup), whether the denominator is gross or stayed reservations, and which channel. It sizes the read path; metasearch and affiliate traffic sit an order of magnitude above a marketplace's own apps. | [[stays-marketplace-economics]] |
| **Booking window (lead time)** | The time between making a reservation and the first night of the stay, reported as a distribution (median and 90th percentile) rather than an average. It sets the availability horizon, cache refresh priorities, how long money is held, experiment length and data retention. | [[stays-marketplace-economics]] |
| **Local liquidity** | The likelihood that a guest searching a given cell finds and books a suitable stay, and that supply offered in that cell is booked. A cell is destination, dates, party, price band and lead time, so the same city can be liquid for hotels and thin for homes, or the reverse, depending on the party. | [[stays-marketplace-economics]] |
| **Cross-side network effects** | The effect where more guests make a marketplace more attractive to supply and more supply attracts guests. In stays they leak because hotels and many hosts sell on every channel and guests compare through metasearch. | [[stays-marketplace-economics]] |
| **Multi-homing** | Selling or searching on several platforms at once. Professional supply multi-homes through a channel manager by design; hosts multi-home less well, often by iCal alone, which brings double-booking risk. | [[stays-marketplace-economics]] |
| **Bookable nights / availability coverage** | Bookable nights are nights that are open, priced and not blocked by restrictions within the horizon. Availability coverage is bookable nights as a share of the nights a property or listing could sell. | [[stays-marketplace-economics]] |
| **Host acceptance rate** | The share of booking requests that hosts accept on request-to-book listings, where the host has a set time to respond. Each declined request is a search session that ended without a booking. | [[stays-marketplace-economics]] |
| **Contribution per stayed reservation** | Revenue per stayed reservation minus marketing and search costs. The book uses it, not revenue, to judge whether the cost of search and distribution is affordable. | [[stays-marketplace-economics]] |
| **Business capability** | Something the business is able to do, named without saying how, where or by whom (for example room and rate selection). It is not a system, a team or a product. | [[stays-capability-map]] |
| **Capability map** | The structured set of an organization's capabilities, usually in two or three levels. It is useful when stable against org changes, free of implementation names and comparable across business units. | [[stays-capability-map]] |
| **Reference model** | A generic capability map for a type of business, written so a real organization can compare itself against it. It is a starting hypothesis, not a description of anyone. | [[stays-capability-map]] |
| **Overlap matrix** | A table that lists two capability maps side by side and classifies each pair as the same capability, a partial overlap, or one that only one side has. Matching must be by meaning, not by name. | [[stays-capability-map]] |
| **Shared versus specific capability** | A shared capability is implemented once by the group and used by both marketplaces; a specific capability is implemented separately by each, because only one needs it or their needs differ too much. The book's rule scores differentiation, divergence and duplication per capability. | [[stays-capability-map]] |
| **Shared contract** | A third grade between shared and specific: both marketplaces agree on the meaning and shape of a thing (an offer, a reservation, a cancellation outcome) but keep separate engines that produce it. | [[stays-capability-map]] |
| **Shared engine** | One implementation used by both marketplaces, typically with per-brand configuration, chosen for capabilities that mean the same for hotels and homes, such as identity, tax calculation and notification delivery. | [[stays-capability-map]] |
| **Brand-specific capability** | A capability each marketplace keeps for itself, because its meaning differs between hotels and homes (for example counted inventory versus a unit calendar) or it is core to one brand. | [[stays-capability-map]] |
| **Core / supporting / generic (subdomain grades)** | Three grades of strategic value: core is why guests or suppliers choose you, supporting is necessary and specific but not a reason to choose you, generic is needed by everyone and can be bought. Scored per brand. | [[stays-capability-map]], [[stays-bounded-contexts]] |
| **Domain-driven design (DDD) domain / subdomain** | The domain is the area of business the software serves; a subdomain is a part of it with its own concerns, such as inventory, payments or trust. Subdomains are classed as core, supporting or generic. | [[stays-bounded-contexts]] |
| **Bounded context** | The boundary inside which one model and its vocabulary apply consistently, with explicit relationships to other contexts. In the book's rule, one team owns one or more contexts, each context has exactly one owning team, and a context contains one or more services. | [[stays-bounded-contexts]] |
| **Ubiquitous language** | The vocabulary used consistently inside one context, in conversation, documents and code. It is ubiquitous within the context, not across the company, so "night" or "booking" may legitimately differ between contexts. | [[stays-bounded-contexts]] |
| **Context map** | A diagram of the contexts and the relationships between them: who is upstream, who adapts to whom, and where translation happens. | [[stays-bounded-contexts]] |
| **Aggregate** | A cluster of domain objects treated as a single unit and the boundary of a transaction: invariants inside one aggregate hold immediately, and invariants across aggregates hold eventually through events and compensation. | [[stays-bounded-contexts]] |
| **Domain event** | A record that something meaningful happened in a context, such as a reservation being confirmed, published so other contexts can react. | [[stays-bounded-contexts]] |
| **Offer** | A priced, conditioned promise to sell a specific stay to a specific party, valid for a limited time: what is sold, price lines, conditions, payment arrangement, and provenance and validity. It is computed, immutable and short-lived; a changed input yields a new offer. | [[stays-bounded-contexts]] |
| **Reservation aggregate** | The agreement that results from accepting an offer: identity, versions, frozen terms per version, state, parties and unit assignment. It excludes inventory decrement, payment capture, payout and commission invoice, which belong to other contexts and react to reservation events. | [[stays-bounded-contexts]] |
| **Saga across aggregates** | A booking made as a sequence of steps across several aggregates (claim inventory, authorize payment, confirm with supplier or host, confirm reservation), with a compensating action for each step that fails. | [[stays-bounded-contexts]] |
| **Anti-corruption layer (ACL)** | A translator that keeps an upstream model from leaking into the downstream one. Examples are the connectivity gateway in front of channel managers, the iCal import (which enters as a claim, not a reservation), and a translator in front of a monolith. | [[stays-bounded-contexts]] |
| **Open host service / published language** | An open host service is one well-defined protocol offered by an upstream context to many consumers. A published language is a documented shared language for exchange, such as OpenTravel messages or the group's offer and reservation contracts. | [[stays-bounded-contexts]] |
| **Customer-supplier / conformist** | Two upstream-downstream relationships. In customer-supplier the upstream team plans for the downstream's needs; in conformist the downstream adopts the upstream model as it is. | [[stays-bounded-contexts]] |
| **Shared kernel** | A small part of the model co-owned by two teams, legitimate only if small, stable and co-owned by teams that talk weekly (identifier formats, money and date-range types). A shared supply model or a shared reservation table is the case to avoid. | [[stays-bounded-contexts]] |

## <a id="g-supply"></a>Part II — Supply

Properties, homes and the partners behind them. [[property-and-listing-model]] owns property, room type, unit and listing; [[partner-onboarding]] owns activation and verification; [[content-and-media]] owns mapping and content quality; [[connectivity-and-channel-managers]] and [[calendar-sync]] own the two integration models; [[extranets-and-host-tools]] owns the partner and host workspace.

| Term | Definition | Chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Property** | A place operated as one accommodation business at one address or site: a hotel, hostel, aparthotel, building of managed apartments or single house. It has an operator, a location, a jurisdiction and property-wide policies. | [[property-and-listing-model]] |
| **Room type** | A category of units that a property treats as interchangeable for sale, with a unit count, a description and an occupancy. A room type promises a kind of room; the specific room is often assigned at check-in. | [[property-and-listing-model]] |
| **Unit** | The smallest space sold to one party for exclusive use on a night: a hotel room, an apartment, a whole house, a private room in a home or a dormitory bed. It has its own occupancy limit and, where regulation requires one, its own registration number. | [[property-and-listing-model]] |
| **Listing (stays)** | A published offer of one unit, or a small pool of identical units, on one hosted-stays marketplace, with host-authored content, one calendar, one price set and one cancellation policy. The accommodation marketplace has no listing; it sells room types. | [[property-and-listing-model]] |
| **Multi-unit listing** | A listing that publishes a small pool of identical units with one calendar counting free units per night. It is a room type in all but name and still has to record which unit each guest received. | [[property-and-listing-model]] |
| **Physical layer / commercial layer** | The physical layer describes places that exist whether or not anyone sells them (property, unit, spaces, beds); the commercial layer describes how a marketplace offers them (room types, rate plans, listings). Most modeling errors put an attribute on the wrong layer. | [[property-and-listing-model]] |
| **Physical inventory / sellable inventory** | Physical inventory is a fact about the building (84 rooms). Sellable inventory is a commercial decision per room type and night, which may be lower (rooms held out) or higher (planned overbooking); storing both in one field corrupts both. | [[property-and-listing-model]] |
| **Pool versus unit-level selling** | A room type sells a pool counted as a number per night; a listing sells one specific unit with a free-or-taken flag. The difference decides how inventory is counted, how a guest is made whole and what can be reported to a regulator. | [[property-and-listing-model]] |
| **Partner / host** | The partner is the party behind a property on an accommodation marketplace; the host is the party behind a listing on a hosted-stays marketplace. Onboarding models the party, the supply unit, the authority to sell and the payout account as four separate records. | [[partner-onboarding]] |
| **Activation** | The decision, made by evaluating readiness checks, that a property or listing is bookable. It has a state and can go backwards, for example when a registration number lapses or a content change fails a check. | [[partner-onboarding]] |
| **Readiness check** | A named, machine-evaluable condition that must hold for a supply unit to be bookable, such as a signed contract or a valid registration number. Each check has an owner, a severity (blocking or advisory), a scope and a version. | [[partner-onboarding]] |
| **Know your business (KYB) for partners** | Verification of the legal party behind supply: the entity exists and is active in a trade register, the signatory is authorized, and beneficial owners are identified and screened. It is the core check on the accommodation side and for professional managers on both. | [[partner-onboarding]] |
| **Know your customer (KYC)** | Identity verification of a natural person: document, likeness, address and sanctions screening. On hosted-stays marketplaces it applies to nearly every host. | [[partner-onboarding]] |
| **Right to sell** | The authority to let a unit for short stays here and now, separate from identity. For hotels it is mostly the hotel license; for homes it is increasingly a registration number issued by a public authority. | [[partner-onboarding]] |
| **Registration number (short-term rental)** | A public-authority identifier for a rental unit. The EU Short-Term Rental Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2024/1028, applying from 20 May 2026) requires platforms to obtain it before a listing is offered, display it and randomly check it. | [[partner-onboarding]] |
| **Trader declaration** | A recorded statement of whether a host acts in a professional capacity. It drives the trader-traceability duties of Article 30 of the Digital Services Act (Regulation (EU) 2022/2065, applying from 17 February 2024) and consumer-law labeling. | [[partner-onboarding]] |
| **Anti-Money-Laundering Regulation (AMLR)** | Regulation (EU) 2024/1624, which moves customer due diligence into directly applicable EU law and applies from 10 July 2027. Platforms that pay hosts themselves carry these duties directly. | [[partner-onboarding]] |
| **Time to first booking** | Elapsed time from sign-up to the first confirmed reservation. It splits into time to bookable (platform-controlled) and time from bookable to first booking (content, price, ranking). | [[partner-onboarding]] |
| **Content mapping** | The process of deciding whether a property or room arriving from one source is the same real-world property or room the marketplace already knows. It is the stays version of entity resolution and mainly an accommodation-marketplace problem. | [[content-and-media]] |
| **Content score** | A measured, explainable rating of how complete, current and consistent a property's or listing's content is. Each component is measurable, visible to the partner or host, and tied to outcomes such as conversion or complaints. | [[content-and-media]] |
| **Media pipeline (stays)** | The system that ingests, checks, classifies, transforms, moderates and serves photos and other media. In stays it must attach photos to rooms, detect re-used photos and compare photos with structured content. | [[content-and-media]] |
| **Provenance / precedence** | Provenance records which source wrote a field value, when and through which channel. Precedence defines which sources may overwrite which fields, for example a PMS owning room amenities and a bedbank only filling empty fields. | [[content-and-media]] |
| **Verification state** | Whether a content value is an unchecked claim, has been reviewed by a person or model, or is backed by evidence such as a photo or registry entry. Hosted-stays accessibility features are an example. | [[content-and-media]] |
| **Property Catalog File / Property Content File** | A distribution-side pair of files from a content provider: the catalog lists properties and is used for mapping supplier IDs, while the content file carries property, room and rate content. | [[content-and-media]] |
| **Perceptual hash** | A fingerprint of an image that survives resizing and recompression, used to find photos re-used across listings and to support mapping of wholesale records. | [[content-and-media]] |
| **Descriptive content standards (OTA RMA codes)** | OpenTravel messages and code lists such as room amenity (RMA) codes carry property and room content. In practice they supply names, not interoperability, so the marketplace keeps its own content model and translates at the edge. | [[content-and-media]] |
| **OpenTravel / OTA XML** | The XML message set from the OpenTravel Alliance that most hotel connectivity is built on. Compliance means using its element names, not interoperability without mapping, because channels pin different versions and add extensions. The acronym OTA here is distinct from online travel agency. | [[connectivity-and-channel-managers]] |
| **HTNG** | Hospitality Technology Next Generation, a hotel-technology standards body founded in 2002 that builds most distribution specifications on OpenTravel messages. | [[connectivity-and-channel-managers]] |
| **ARI message** | A single update to availability, rates or inventory for one property, sent from a supply system to a channel. It addresses products by code, covers a date range and sets values such as rooms to sell, price per occupancy or a restriction. | [[connectivity-and-channel-managers]] |
| **Channel manager** | Software that keeps one property's availability, rates and inventory consistent across many sales channels and brings their reservations back to the PMS. Its core asset is the mapping table from the hotel's codes to each channel's identifiers. | [[connectivity-and-channel-managers]] |
| **Property management system (PMS)** | The hotel's operational record: physical rooms, in-house guests, reservations, folios and housekeeping. For an independent hotel it is usually the system of record for inventory; here it is an integration counterparty. | [[connectivity-and-channel-managers]] |
| **Central reservation system (CRS)** | A chain-level system holding one image of inventory and rates across properties and distributing to websites, call centers, GDSs and OTAs. | [[connectivity-and-channel-managers]] |
| **Connectivity partner** | A third party certified to write availability, rates and content to a marketplace on a property's behalf, or to receive its reservations: a channel manager, a PMS with a direct connection or a CRS. The property authorizes it; the marketplace certifies it. | [[connectivity-and-channel-managers]] |
| **Full sync / delta update** | A full sync re-sends a property's whole availability and rates horizon, repairing drift but rewriting every downstream value. A delta carries only changes. | [[connectivity-and-channel-managers]] |
| **Mapping table** | A channel manager's table from a hotel's own room and rate codes to each channel's identifiers. Because the mapping lives at the partner, a mapping error passes the channel's schema validation. | [[connectivity-and-channel-managers]] |
| **Semantic guard** | A validation rule that checks an incoming value against the property's own state, such as its sibling rates, to catch errors that schemas and fixed bounds miss. | [[connectivity-and-channel-managers]] |
| **Partner registry / partner health score** | The registry records which partner may act for which property and function and which certifications it holds. The health score measures partner quality, for example by how often its digests drift from the marketplace's stored values. | [[connectivity-and-channel-managers]] |
| **Certification (connectivity)** | Testing a connectivity partner against a channel's profile (code lists, pricing models, limits, guard rails) before it goes live, often with tiers and ongoing monitoring afterwards. | [[connectivity-and-channel-managers]] |
| **Digest comparison** | A drift-detection method in which the gateway publishes a hash per property, product and month of values it holds, and the partner resends only the months that do not match. | [[connectivity-and-channel-managers]] |
| **Multichannel host** | A host who sells the same home on more than one platform. The key question is where the system of record for that home's availability lives: one marketplace, an iCal mesh or a hub such as a vacation-rental PMS. | [[calendar-sync]] |
| **iCal sync** | Keeping a home's availability aligned across platforms by exporting and polling iCalendar feeds. It is eventually consistent by design: the standard has no polling interval, push or acknowledgement, and carries no price or guest data. | [[calendar-sync]] |
| **iCalendar (RFC 5545)** | The IETF calendar data format (September 2009) behind hosted-stays feeds: a file of VEVENT components with exclusive all-day end dates and a persistent UID per event. | [[calendar-sync]] |
| **Import / export feed** | Export is a marketplace serving its own calendar as an iCal feed for others to poll; import is polling a feed URL the host pasted from another platform. The marketplace controls the contents of the export and the schedule of the import, not the reverse. | [[calendar-sync]] |
| **Conflict window** | The time from a booking confirmed on platform A until platform B can no longer sell the booked nights: export lag on A plus wait for B's next successful poll plus B's processing time. | [[calendar-sync]] |
| **System of record (calendar)** | The one system authoritative for a home's availability. In an iCal mesh there is none: each platform is authoritative only for its own bookings. | [[calendar-sync]] |
| **Capability URL** | A long unguessable URL whose possession is the access right. An iCal export URL is one: anyone holding it can see when the home is empty, so exports carry no personal data. | [[calendar-sync]] |
| **Extranet** | The partner-facing application in which a property manages its presence on an accommodation marketplace: content, policies, rates and availability, reservations, messages, reviews, invoices and performance. The hosted-stays equivalent is the host app. | [[extranets-and-host-tools]] |
| **Host app** | The supply-side surface of a hosted-stays marketplace, built for one person running a few homes from a phone. | [[extranets-and-host-tools]] |
| **Partner workspace** | The book's umbrella term for everything a supplier uses: extranet or host app, mobile app, supply-side APIs and notifications. It is a product backed by several systems. | [[extranets-and-host-tools]] |
| **Co-host** | A person or business that operates a hosted-stays listing on behalf of its owner, with rights the owner grants. The accommodation-marketplace counterpart is partner staff with extranet roles. | [[extranets-and-host-tools]] |
| **Delegation (as a relationship)** | A grant modeled as party, scope, role, granted-by and validity rather than a user flag. Money rights (payouts, tax data) stay separate from operating rights. | [[extranets-and-host-tools]] |
| **Turnover task** | A unit of on-property work derived from reservations (cleaning, linen, inspection, key or code handover) in the window between one check-out and the next check-in. It has its own state and must react to reservation changes. | [[extranets-and-host-tools]] |
| **Partner analytics** | Reporting a marketplace gives suppliers about their own visibility, conversion, demand, prices, quality and programs, usually benchmarked against comparable properties or listings. | [[extranets-and-host-tools]] |
| **Restrictive override** | A marketplace-only change to rates or availability that can close or reduce but never open or raise, with an expiry, so it cannot contradict the system of record. | [[extranets-and-host-tools]] |

## <a id="g-inventory"></a>Part III — Inventory

Availability, rates and price. [[rates-and-availability-model]] owns the ARI vocabulary; [[availability-engine]] owns offer computation, caches and price accuracy; [[inventory-integrity]] owns how a night is held, committed and, when it fails, oversold; [[dynamic-pricing]] owns revenue management and host pricing; [[promotions-and-loyalty]] owns discounts, audiences and parity.

| Term | Definition | Chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Availability, rates and inventory (ARI)** | The data a supplier sends a marketplace about what can be sold: how many units of a room type are free on each night, at what prices, under which restrictions. In this book most of what looks like ARI data is really ARI rules. On hosted-stays marketplaces the equivalent is one calendar per unit with a nightly price and day rules. | [[rates-and-availability-model]] |
| **Inventory / availability** | Inventory is the number of sellable units of a room type on a night. Availability is what a guest can actually book for given dates, occupancy and conditions after restrictions are applied. | [[rates-and-availability-model]] |
| **Rate plan / product** | A rate plan is a named commercial offer: a pricing logic plus conditions such as cancellation, meal plan and payment terms. A product is a room type combined with a rate plan, the thing ARI prices and restricts. Every rate plan of a room type sells from the same inventory pool. | [[rates-and-availability-model]] |
| **Pricing type** | The rule for what one stored price means: standard (price at maximum occupancy), derived occupancy (base plus offsets), occupancy-based (one price per guest count) or length-of-stay. It decides how many price cells a product needs. | [[rates-and-availability-model]] |
| **Length-of-stay (LOS) pricing** | A price stored per arrival date and stay length, so the price of a stay is no longer the sum of its nightly prices. It multiplies the key space of a cache and forces tiering. | [[rates-and-availability-model]] |
| **Derived rate** | A rate plan whose price, and often its restrictions and open or closed status, follows a parent plan through a rule such as a percentage offset. The order of operations (discount, supplement, rounding) changes the final price and must be defined once. | [[rates-and-availability-model]] |
| **Best available rate (BAR)** | The base public rate sold to unqualified guests, which a hotel revenue management system sets and from which most other rates are fixed offsets. Some systems price segments and channels independently instead, so the marketplace's model must hold both styles. | [[rates-and-availability-model]], [[dynamic-pricing]] |
| **Restriction** | A rule that removes availability for some stays without changing inventory, for example minimum or maximum stay, closed to arrival (CTA), closed to departure (CTD), stop-sell or advance purchase. Restrictions have stay-through and on-arrival variants, so they must be evaluated per stay, not per night. | [[rates-and-availability-model]] |
| **Minimum stay (MinLOS / MaxLOS)** | The fewest or most nights a stay may have. A stay-through rule applies to every night the stay includes; an on-arrival rule applies only to stays arriving that day. | [[rates-and-availability-model]] |
| **Gap night** | A short gap, often one or two nights, between two bookings that a minimum-stay rule can leave unsellable. Host pricing tools discount such gaps. Hosts also use restrictions as operational controls (for example a cleaner's schedule), while hotels use them as revenue controls. | [[rates-and-availability-model]] |
| **Inclusion flag / fee basis** | Metadata stored with each amount saying whether it includes VAT, city tax or mandatory fees, and how each fee is charged (per stay, per night, per person per night or percentage). Without them each surface guesses and prices drift. | [[rates-and-availability-model]] |
| **Stay evaluator** | The single versioned component that turns stored facts (inventory, prices, restrictions), rules, fees and policies into an answer for one stay: bookable or not, and at what price. | [[rates-and-availability-model]] |
| **Availability engine** | The read model that answers "what can I book, and for how much" at search scale, from precomputed offers with an explicit staleness budget, falling back to on-demand computation. On hosted-stays marketplaces the hard part shifts from combinatorics to confidence that a free night is really free. | [[availability-engine]] |
| **Availability query / offer** | A query names candidates, a stay and a party, plus audience and display currency. The answer is an offer: a priced, conditioned promise to sell a specific product for a specific stay to a specific party, valid for a limited time. | [[availability-engine]] |
| **Offer computation / offer kernel** | Offer computation turns ARI into offers: per-night eligibility, stay-level restrictions, occupancy fit, discounts, fees, taxes and currency. The offer kernel is its one versioned implementation, shared by the grid builder, the on-demand path and the price check. | [[availability-engine]] |
| **Card price** | The price a property shows on a search result card, usually the cheapest or best qualifying room-rate for the stay. | [[availability-engine]] |
| **Price accuracy** | The probability that a price shown at an earlier step (search card, property page, checkout, statement) equals the price charged at the final step for the same stay, party and conditions, measured on a sample. Metasearch engines grade partners on it. | [[availability-engine]] |
| **Cache staleness** | How far a served value is from the system of record. Age-based staleness is time since computation; divergence-based staleness is the probability the value differs from what the source would answer now. Age is an engineering metric and divergence is a business metric. | [[availability-engine]] |
| **Staleness budget** | A written limit, per tier (search card, property page, metasearch feed), on how stale a served offer may be and what the price check does when the limit is breached. | [[availability-engine]] |
| **Price check** | A re-computation of the selected offer from the system of record, with the offer kernel, when the guest moves to pay. It is the point where an offer becomes binding. | [[availability-engine]] |
| **Cell (precomputed grid)** | The unit an availability grid stores and the key it is stored under, for example property, arrival date, stay length and party. Each key dimension multiplies the grid; dimensions left out must be computed at read time. | [[availability-engine]] |
| **Occupancy-based pricing (OBP)** | A pricing type with a separate price for each number of guests. | [[rates-and-availability-model]], [[availability-engine]] |
| **Physical, sellable and available inventory** | Physical inventory is the units that exist on a night. Sellable inventory is the count a supplier offers a channel after closures, and can exceed physical inventory when a hotel overbooks on purpose. Availability is what a specific search can book after restrictions. | [[property-and-listing-model]], [[inventory-integrity]] |
| **Allotment** | A block of rooms a hotel commits to one channel for a period, with unsold rooms returned on a release date before arrival. It is a scheduled obligation, not just a number, and is rare outside wholesale, tour operating and groups. | [[inventory-integrity]] |
| **Free sale** | A channel may sell up to the shared count without asking the hotel before each booking, each booking decrementing the count. Some wholesale contracts use the term differently, so the contract governs. | [[inventory-integrity]] |
| **Release date** | The date before arrival on which unsold allotment rooms return to the hotel. A release that fires late or in the wrong time zone causes oversells. | [[inventory-integrity]] |
| **Inventory hold** | A temporary claim on inventory while a guest completes checkout, released automatically after a time limit unless committed. It is an efficiency device, not the guarantee against selling twice. | [[inventory-integrity]] |
| **Overbooking / oversell / double booking** | Overbooking is deliberate selling beyond physical inventory to offset no-shows, decided by the hotel's revenue management. Oversell is accidental, caused by a wrong copy of the count. A double booking is an oversell of a one-unit listing. Overbooking has an owner and a reason; the other two are defects. | [[inventory-integrity]] |
| **Walk / relocation** | A walk is when the property turns away a guest with a confirmed reservation and arranges a room elsewhere. A relocation is when the marketplace moves the guest because the original cannot honor the reservation. A home has no corridor of identical rooms to walk a guest into. | [[inventory-integrity]] |
| **Auto-replenishment** | A marketplace default that puts a canceled room back on sale, which can resell a room the hotel already resold or closed. | [[inventory-integrity]] |
| **Unit cardinality** | The number of identical units behind a listing or room type. One-unit listings commit against a uniqueness constraint; multi-unit room types commit by conditional decrement in an allocation ledger. | [[inventory-integrity]] |
| **Acknowledgement watermark** | A control that computes the sellable limit as the supplier's last claim minus the marketplace's own reservations the supplier had not yet acknowledged when it made that claim. | [[inventory-integrity]] |
| **Negative-headroom signal** | An oversell detector: a supplier claim of fewer rooms than the marketplace's own committed but unacknowledged reservations for that night. | [[inventory-integrity]] |
| **Fencing token** | An increasing number checked by the storage layer so that a client whose lock lease expired cannot write after another client took the lock. Locks reduce contention but do not make a commit correct. | [[inventory-integrity]] |
| **Revenue management (RM) / revenue management system (RMS)** | Selling fixed, perishable capacity to the right customer at the right price and time, using price and availability controls driven by a demand forecast. An RMS is the software that forecasts demand and recommends prices and controls. | [[dynamic-pricing]] |
| **Occupancy / ADR / RevPAR / RevPAN** | Occupancy is the share of available nights sold. Average daily rate (ADR) is room revenue per room-night sold. Revenue per available room (RevPAR) is room revenue per room-night available. Revenue per available night (RevPAN) is the same for one home and is the right objective for host pricing. | [[dynamic-pricing]] |
| **Booking curve / pickup / lead time** | The booking curve is cumulative bookings for a stay date by days before arrival; pickup is bookings added in a period; lead time is days between booking and arrival. | [[dynamic-pricing]] |
| **Unconstrained demand** | The demand that would have arrived if capacity and price had not limited it. Forecasts try to estimate it because sold-out nights hide it. | [[dynamic-pricing]] |
| **Last room value / displacement** | Last room value is the minimum price worth accepting for the last rooms. Displacement is the revenue lost by accepting a booking that blocks a better one, such as a cheap one-night stay splitting a week. | [[dynamic-pricing]] |
| **Comp set (competitive set)** | The competing properties or listings a price is judged against. Hotels pick four to eight by hand; for homes it is computed from location, capacity, quality and type. It feeds recommendations, anomaly checks and partner insight. | [[dynamic-pricing]] |
| **Rate recommendation / smart pricing** | A rate recommendation (price tip) is an advisory price the host or partner decides on, so it is a read. Smart pricing is marketplace-computed nightly prices written into a host's calendar with consent, so it is a write. | [[dynamic-pricing]] |
| **Holdout** | A standing random group of listings priced by the previous model version so the current model is always compared with something. | [[dynamic-pricing]] |
| **Promotion / discount** | A promotion is a time- or condition-bound reduction of a published price, created by a party that agrees to fund it. It has eligibility conditions, a discount, a combination class and a funder. | [[promotions-and-loyalty]] |
| **Funder (partner-, marketplace- and co-funded)** | The party that bears a discount. Partner-funded reduces what the property or host receives; marketplace-funded leaves the partner's amount unchanged; co-funded splits the cost by formula. Every discount line carries its funder to the ledger. | [[promotions-and-loyalty]] |
| **Combination class** | The attribute of a promotion that says what it may stack with. Discounts do not commute once caps and fixed amounts are involved, so composition order is a contract. | [[promotions-and-loyalty]] |
| **Closed user group (CUG) / conditional rate** | A CUG is an audience that must identify itself to see a price, such as signed-in members, app users or a corporate account. A CUG rate is a conditional rate: the same room and night has a different price depending on who asks. | [[promotions-and-loyalty]] |
| **Loyalty program / loyalty currency** | A loyalty program recognizes repeat guests with tiers, benefits or a currency. A loyalty currency is a liability in a ledger: a promise of future value that accrues, reverses on cancellation, expires and is redeemed. | [[promotions-and-loyalty]] |
| **Partner program** | A commercial arrangement in which a property or host gives something (higher commission, discounts, quality commitments) for something (a badge, a ranking boost, marketing spend). Regulators have begun to examine such programs. | [[promotions-and-loyalty]], [[stays-ranking]] |
| **Rate parity (wide / narrow)** | A contractual commitment to offer a platform rates and conditions at least as good as elsewhere. Wide parity covers all other channels including other OTAs; narrow parity covers only the property's own direct channels. In the EU the Digital Markets Act and the 2024 CJEU ruling in C-264/23 have ended it for most platforms. | [[promotions-and-loyalty]] |
| **Redemption / incrementality** | A redemption is a booking that used a discount. Incrementality is the share of redemptions that would not have happened without it. | [[promotions-and-loyalty]] |

## <a id="g-discovery"></a>Part IV — Discovery

From intent to a bookable stay. [[stays-search-architecture]] owns availability-aware search; [[destinations-and-maps]] owns places and geography; [[stays-ranking]] owns ranking and its transparency; [[personalization-and-recommendations]] owns intent, wishlists and alerts; [[property-page-and-room-selection]] owns the total price shown and the choice of room and rate.

| Term | Definition | Chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Stays search pipeline** | The sequence of stages that turns a stay query into a rendered results page: destination resolution, retrieval, exact availability and offer selection, pricing, ranking and presentation. Availability enters twice, as a coarse projection inside retrieval and as an exact check on the candidates that will be shown. | [[stays-search-architecture]] |
| **Availability-aware search** | Search whose result set contains only stays bookable for the query's dates and party, with filters, facet counts and sorts computed over that set. The weaker alternative retrieves by content and checks availability afterward, which leaves gaps, wrong facet counts and unsortable prices. | [[stays-search-architecture]] |
| **Flexible search** | A query where dates or destination are a range rather than a value, such as "a weekend in June" or "cabins anywhere in Portugal". It turns one availability question into dozens, so it needs a plan for which dates to price. | [[stays-search-architecture]] |
| **Stay query** | The structured request every stays search answers: destination, dates, party, filters and sort. The dates and party are part of the key of every computed result. | [[stays-search-architecture]] |
| **Stay price / price hint** | The stay price is the total the guest will pay for the exact stay: nightly rates, discounts, mandatory fees and displayed taxes, in the display currency. A price hint is anything less exact, such as a "from" or nightly price, usable for coarse filtering but never to be shown as the stay price. | [[stays-search-architecture]] |
| **Availability projector** | The component that reads every rate and availability change and writes to the search index only when the coarse open/closed summary changes. Most messages change a rate, not whether a night is open, so it cuts index load sharply. | [[stays-search-architecture]] |
| **Allocation problem (party)** | The multi-room search task of finding combinations of room types whose occupancies cover the party, each with an open rate plan for the whole stay, and pricing each combination. On hosted-stays marketplaces it reduces to a capacity filter on one unit (sleeps, bedrooms, beds). | [[stays-search-architecture]] |
| **Classification pipeline** | A write-time process, often machine learning followed by human curation, that tags listings with categories or themes so that a flexible-destination search becomes an ordinary search on a tag. | [[stays-search-architecture]] |
| **Degradation order** | The planned sequence of steps search takes under load instead of timing out: shrink the exact stage, show labeled "from" prices lower on the page, disable flexible dates and price sort, then serve cached results for common queries. | [[stays-search-architecture]] |
| **Federated query** | A cross-brand search in which the API sends the same stay query to both marketplaces' engines and shows the second brand's results in a separate, labeled section rather than merging them into one ranked list, because the rankers' scores are not calibrated against each other. | [[stays-search-architecture]] |
| **Place / destination** | A place is a named geographic entity with a stable identifier, geometry, multilingual names and relationships to other places. A destination is a place that guests search for and the marketplace sells; all destinations are places, but not all places are destinations. | [[destinations-and-maps]] |
| **Destination graph** | The versioned dataset of places, their geometries, names and relationships, read by search, landing pages, pricing, tax and regulation. Edges include contains, part of, served by, alias of and supersedes. | [[destinations-and-maps]] |
| **Place resolution** | Turning a guest's input (a typed string, an autocomplete pick or a deep link) into a place identifier, resolving ambiguity on the way. | [[destinations-and-maps]] |
| **Search area** | The geographic region the marketplace actually searches for a resolved place. It often differs from the place's official boundary, for example by adding resort or airport areas or leaving out low-demand districts. | [[destinations-and-maps]] |
| **Viewport search** | A query whose geographic constraint is the rectangle of map the guest is looking at, with the guest's dates, party size and filters attached. Every pin carries a price that depends on those dates. | [[destinations-and-maps]] |
| **S2 cells / H3 cells** | Hierarchical grids for spatial indexing. S2 cells nest exactly, so the book recommends them for retrieval terms and search areas; H3 hexagons smooth well, so the book recommends them for analytics, features and clusters. | [[destinations-and-maps]] |
| **Geocoding / reverse geocoding** | Geocoding converts an address to coordinates; reverse geocoding converts coordinates to an address or place. For hosted stays, city, region and country should be derived from the geocoded point and the destination graph, never typed by the host. | [[destinations-and-maps]] |
| **Approximate location** | The location shown to guests before booking, made of a public point derived from the exact point plus a display radius. Pins, clusters, distance labels and the property-page circle are all computed from it. Hotels publish their address; homes usually do not. | [[destinations-and-maps]] |
| **Public point** | The coordinate derived from a home's exact point and used for everything visible before booking. The search index for hosted stays holds only the public point. | [[destinations-and-maps]] |
| **Ranking** | In EU consumer law (Omnibus Directive (EU) 2019/2161), the relative prominence given to products as presented, organized or communicated by the trader, by any technological means. Badges, carousels, map pins and landing-page pins count, not only the sort order. | [[stays-ranking]] |
| **Learning to rank (stays)** | Training a model to order priced offers by the estimated probability of an honored stay, with logged features and outcomes. The book recommends a gradient-boosted ranker with a LambdaMART-style objective and a multi-task target. | [[stays-ranking]] |
| **Net booking** | A booking minus its cancellation, used as a ranking label. It rewards bookings that survive, but the label arrives weeks or months later. | [[stays-ranking]] |
| **Relevance model** | The model that estimates, for one search and one candidate offer, how likely the guest is to complete a stay with it. It is kept apart from commercial rules. | [[stays-ranking]] |
| **Ranking policy** | The explicit, versioned rules applied to the relevance model's scores: commercial adjustments, reliability and trust demotions, diversity and exploration. Each rule carries reason codes. | [[stays-ranking]] |
| **Program-driven visibility** | Exposure a partner gets by joining a program, usually in exchange for a higher commission or a funded discount. Its effect on position must be bounded and disclosed. | [[stays-ranking]] |
| **Ranking transparency** | The ability to describe the main ranking parameters, their relative importance and the effect of payment, both in general and for one position. The EU P2B Regulation (2019/1150, applying from 12 July 2020), the Omnibus Directive and the Digital Services Act require it. | [[stays-ranking]] |
| **Position bias** | The tendency of higher positions to get more clicks regardless of quality, so engagement data is a biased label. It hurts new supply most, which has no engagement and so ranks low. | [[stays-ranking]] |
| **Exploration slots / per-manager cap** | Exploration slots are positions reserved for new candidates chosen by a content-only score. A per-manager cap limits how many listings from one professional manager appear in the top positions, which addresses the diversity problem of single-unit inventory. | [[stays-ranking]] |
| **Reliability demotion** | A ranking policy term that lowers a property's score after host-side or property-side failures, such as a walked guest. In the book's example it is a multiplier applied for a fixed period. | [[stays-ranking]] |
| **Trip intent** | The marketplace's current estimate of a trip a guest is planning: where, when, who, what kind of stay and roughly what budget. It is inferred from searches, filters, views and saves, kept as a first-class record, and ends at booking or expiry. | [[personalization-and-recommendations]] |
| **Wishlist** | A guest-owned, named list of specific properties or homes, optionally bound to dates and a party and shareable with co-travelers. It is a commitment the marketplace re-evaluates against live supply, not a bookmark. | [[personalization-and-recommendations]] |
| **Price alert (watch)** | A standing request to notify the guest when the bookable price for a defined stay changes in a way they care about. In practice a watch has a target, a condition, a frequency cap and an expiry. | [[personalization-and-recommendations]] |
| **Listing embedding / guest embedding** | A listing embedding is a learned vector that places substitutable properties or homes close together, learned from session behavior (in the style of word2vec) and from content. A guest embedding does the same for a guest's history. | [[personalization-and-recommendations]] |
| **Continuous cold start** | The problem, named by Booking.com researchers in 2015, that frequent visitors look new because they book rarely, their interests shift and they switch personas, so history is thin or misleading. | [[personalization-and-recommendations]] |
| **Recommendation surface** | Any place where the marketplace proposes stays or destinations without a complete query, each with its own context and failure cost. Under the Digital Services Act, recommender systems must explain their main parameters. | [[personalization-and-recommendations]] |
| **Profiling (GDPR)** | Automated evaluation of personal aspects such as preferences (GDPR Art. 4(4)). Guests may object to profiling for direct marketing (Art. 21), so the book keeps a non-profiling path for every recommender. | [[personalization-and-recommendations]] |
| **Proximity features** | Features computed per coarse location bucket (about 1,000 users, derived from IP address and consent-aware) instead of per user, so cold-start traffic can be personalized without identity. | [[personalization-and-recommendations]] |
| **Property detail page / listing page** | The page for one property (OTA) or home (hosted stays), usually opened from search with dates and party already set. | [[property-page-and-room-selection]] |
| **Property detail assembly** | How the property page is built from many sources, such as content, offers, reviews and trust evidence, by fragments owned by different teams around one quote. | [[property-page-and-room-selection]] |
| **Quote / price breakdown** | A quote is a priced offer with an identity: quote ID, itemized price breakdown, the inputs it was computed from, and a validity window. It makes "the price you saw" a fact that later steps can check. | [[property-page-and-room-selection]] |
| **Total price display** | Composing and showing the price so that the total, including all mandatory charges, holds from search to checkout, with the headline price equal or close to the total. | [[property-page-and-room-selection]] |
| **Headline price** | The most prominent price number on a surface. Regulation in several markets requires it to equal, or be close to, the total price. | [[property-page-and-room-selection]] |
| **Drip pricing** | Showing a lower price first and adding unavoidable charges later in the purchase flow. The European Commission's 2021 UCPD guidance treats it as unfair; the UK bans it under the DMCC Act 2024 (consumer provisions from April 2025); the US FTC fees rule took effect 12 May 2025. | [[property-page-and-room-selection]] |
| **Display policy** | A versioned, data-driven component that takes a quote and a context (market, surface, device, guest state) and decides which lines enter the headline, per-stay or per-night framing, wording, rounding and what to show when the quote is not exact. | [[property-page-and-room-selection]] |
| **Payable at property** | A price line the guest pays at the property, such as city tax or a resort fee, rather than at booking. Display policy in the EU and UK still includes it in the headline total. | [[property-page-and-room-selection]] |
| **Rate comparison** | How room and rate offers are enumerated, normalized into comparable fields (cancellation, payment timing, meal plan, occupancy), pruned and grouped so the rooms table helps the guest decide. | [[property-page-and-room-selection]] |
| **Roomrate** | Booking.com's connectivity term for the sellable product: a unique combination of room type, rate plan and conditions. | [[property-page-and-room-selection]] |
| **Dominated offer** | An offer for which another offer for the same room has equal or better conditions on every normalized field and a lower or equal total. Pruning removes it from the rooms table. | [[property-page-and-room-selection]] |
| **Reference price ("was" price)** | A strikethrough comparison price, which is a claim that needs evidence. The book computes it from the platform's own sales history and stores the evidence. The Omnibus Directive's 30-day prior-price rule may not apply to accommodation. | [[property-page-and-room-selection]] |
| **Trader status** | Whether a host or partner is a professional trader or a private peer. It determines which consumer rights apply and is required in the EU for professional hosts. | [[property-page-and-room-selection]] |

## <a id="g-booking"></a>Part V — Booking

Making and keeping the reservation. [[checkout-orchestration]] owns the booking saga and the two booking modes; [[reservation-lifecycle]] owns reservation states and delivery; [[changes-and-cancellations]] owns cancellation policy and disruption; [[trip-and-messaging]] owns the trip and the conversation; [[customer-service]] owns cases and recovery.

| Term | Definition | Chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Quote** | A priced offer with an identity, an itemized breakdown, the versions of the data it was computed from and a validity window. Checkout starts from a quote ID, not from a price passed by the client. | [[checkout-orchestration]] |
| **Checkout attempt** | One guest's intention to buy one quote, recorded by the server when the guest enters checkout. One attempt may produce at most one reservation. | [[checkout-orchestration]] |
| **Booking saga** | The sequence of local steps that turns a checkout attempt into a confirmed reservation, each step having either a compensation that undoes it or a guarantee of retry until it succeeds. The term comes from a 1987 paper on long-lived transactions. | [[checkout-orchestration]] |
| **Pivot (saga)** | The saga's point of no return. Steps after it must be retryable and idempotent, because the saga can no longer be compensated as a whole. | [[checkout-orchestration]] |
| **Idempotency key (stays)** | An identifier every system on the booking path uses to recognize a repeated request and return the original result instead of repeating the effect. In stays it must survive several hops, including legacy XML, where it may be a reservation ID plus a message version. | [[checkout-orchestration]] |
| **Instant book** | Booking mode in which the reservation is confirmed when the guest completes payment, against rules set in advance (minimum stay, lead time, guest requirements). Hotel bookings work this way; on hosted-stays marketplaces it is a host choice. | [[checkout-orchestration]] |
| **Request to book** | Booking mode that inserts a host or property decision between payment and confirmation, typically within 24 hours. The hold, the payment authorization and the quote need validity windows that match that window. | [[checkout-orchestration]] |
| **Terms snapshot** | The cancellation policy, payment schedule, house rules, price breakdown and amount payable at the property, frozen by version at checkout and attached to the reservation. Later disputes are judged against it. | [[checkout-orchestration]] |
| **Unknown outcome** | The result of a remote call that timed out: the caller does not know whether it succeeded. It needs its own state and a polling or reconciliation path, not a blind retry. | [[checkout-orchestration]] |
| **Reservation** | The marketplace's record of an agreement that a guest will stay at a property or home on specific nights, under specific terms, for a specific price. One reservation may hold several rooms. | [[reservation-lifecycle]] |
| **Reservation state machine** | The model of a reservation's commercial lifecycle: pending, confirmed, declined or expired, canceled, no-show, stayed, completed. Modification is a new version, not a state; delivery, stay detail and money run as separate machines. | [[reservation-lifecycle]] |
| **Reservation version** | One immutable state of the agreement's terms. A modification creates a new version while the reservation keeps its identity. | [[reservation-lifecycle]] |
| **Booking snapshot** | The copy of commercial terms and key facts taken at confirmation and at each new version: room type or listing, occupancy, rate plan, policies, itemized price and content version. It records what the guest agreed to, independent of later catalog changes. | [[reservation-lifecycle]] |
| **Reservation delivery** | Making a reservation version known to every party that must act on it (the property's PMS, a channel manager, a host, a co-host) and recording proof that each one has it. Tracked per recipient and version. | [[reservation-lifecycle]] |
| **Acknowledgement (sent / received / accepted)** | A recipient's statement that it has a version, recorded at three levels. Only acceptance by the system the front desk uses, or confirmation by a person at the property, closes delivery. | [[reservation-lifecycle]] |
| **Confirmation numbers** | The identifiers each system gives the reservation: the marketplace's reservation ID, the channel manager's ID and the PMS confirmation number. The guest quotes one; the front desk searches by another. | [[reservation-lifecycle]] |
| **No-show** | A guest who neither arrives nor cancels, usually reported by the supplier. It changes commission, fees and payouts, and the book's model lets it be reversed to stayed within a window. | [[reservation-lifecycle]] |
| **Stayed / completion** | Stayed means the reservation's nights were used or are treated as used under marketplace rules. Completion is the later point when nothing more can change the reservation's money or reputation outcome. | [[reservation-lifecycle]] |
| **Reservation exception** | A reservation that needs a human decision before a deadline: undelivered, rejected by the PMS, overbooked, card declined, no-show to report or change requested. | [[reservation-lifecycle]] |
| **Event sourcing** | Rebuilding an aggregate's state from its event log instead of storing current state. The book recommends an append-only version table plus an outbox instead, as it gives the audit trail with simpler support queries. | [[reservation-lifecycle]] |
| **Cancellation policy** | The rule that says what a guest gets back, and what the supplier keeps, if the guest cancels at a given time. Expressed as ordered time windows before arrival, each with a typed penalty, and evaluated in the property's local time. | [[changes-and-cancellations]] |
| **Policy snapshot** | The cancellation policy frozen onto the reservation at booking, with its version. Later edits to the rate plan or listing do not change it. | [[changes-and-cancellations]] |
| **Cancellation policy engine** | The single service that evaluates a policy snapshot, an action, an actor, a time and any overrides into an itemized decision. | [[changes-and-cancellations]] |
| **Non-refundable rate** | A rate that returns nothing on cancellation, usually cheaper. EU law gives no cooling-off right for accommodation, so cancellation terms are a commercial attribute of the offer. | [[changes-and-cancellations]] |
| **Free cancellation** | A policy window before arrival in which a guest cancels without penalty. On hosted-stays marketplaces it comes from a small set of platform-defined tiers, not per-property rules. | [[changes-and-cancellations]] |
| **Guest-facing / supplier-facing policy** | The policy the marketplace sold to the guest and the one the supplier granted the marketplace. Usually identical; sometimes deliberately not, in which case the marketplace underwrites the difference. | [[changes-and-cancellations]] |
| **Modification** | A change to a confirmed reservation that keeps its identity: dates, guests, room type, rate plan, guest name or special requests. It creates a new reservation version. | [[changes-and-cancellations]] |
| **Self-service change** | A guest- or supplier-initiated change completed without an agent, from quote to confirmation. It is complete only when the guest knows the cost, the outcome and what was sent to the property. | [[changes-and-cancellations]] |
| **Extenuating circumstances** | Events outside the guest's and supplier's control that justify overriding a booking's policy. The book models them as declared disruption events. | [[changes-and-cancellations]] |
| **Declared disruption event** | A record with an owner, class, places, stay window, booking cutoff, party filter, policy override, funding and expiry, created when something happens and applied by machines. | [[changes-and-cancellations]] |
| **Disruption registry** | The shared store of declared disruption events that the cancellation engine, support, notifications and payments all consult, so the same event gets one answer everywhere. | [[changes-and-cancellations]] |
| **Booking cutoff** | The time of a declared disruption event after which new bookings are excluded, because a guest booking after the strike is announced accepted the risk. | [[changes-and-cancellations]] |
| **Party filter** | The rule on a declared event that limits it to affected guests, for example by origin market or itinerary for transport events. | [[changes-and-cancellations]] |
| **Host or property cancellation** | A cancellation initiated by the supplier, including an overbooked hotel walking a guest. | [[changes-and-cancellations]] |
| **Trip** | The guest-facing view of one or more reservations for one journey, from confirmation to the end of the review window. It owns no booking, payment or supply fact. | [[trip-and-messaging]] |
| **Trip timeline** | The ordered list of past and upcoming events in a trip, each with who acts, by when and what the guest should do. It is a read model built from events owned by other services. | [[trip-and-messaging]] |
| **Read model** | A view built from events owned by other services and used to answer queries. In the trip, a fact belongs to the service that owns it and the read model only explains it. | [[trip-and-messaging]], [[event-driven-stays]] |
| **Trip status explanation** | The one or two sentences at the top of the trip that say where the guest stands, derived from facts with a source and a time. | [[trip-and-messaging]] |
| **Arrival package** | Everything a guest needs to get from the street to the bed: address, check-in window, how to get in, parking, Wi-Fi, house rules, emergency contact. For a home part of it is secret and per reservation. | [[trip-and-messaging]] |
| **Access credential** | A code or digital key valid for one reservation's window, generated by a lock platform, a PMS or the marketplace's own lock integration. Its lifecycle follows reservation events. | [[trip-and-messaging]] |
| **Guest-host messaging** | A governed three-party channel: a conversation between guest and property or host about an inquiry or reservation, in which the platform can annotate, redact and intervene. | [[trip-and-messaging]] |
| **Structured request** | A message with a type and fields rather than free text, such as arrival time, early check-in, date change or cancel for less. It can be routed, answered with one tap and turned into a reservation change. | [[trip-and-messaging]] |
| **Notification routing** | The decision whether, when, through which channels and in which language to send a notification, and what to do when a channel stays silent. | [[trip-and-messaging]] |
| **Notification class** | The category of a notification (arrival-critical, disruption, transactional, conversation, account security, engagement, marketing) that sets priority, channels, mutability and sending lane. | [[trip-and-messaging]] |
| **Disintermediation** | Guest and host completing the transaction off the platform, which removes the marketplace's fee and protections. Contact-sharing rules before booking exist mainly to prevent it. | [[trip-and-messaging]] |
| **Contact** | One inbound or outbound interaction in one channel: a call, a chat session, an e-mail or a message to the AI assistant. It is the unit vendors bill and staffing models count. | [[customer-service]] |
| **Contact driver (contact reason)** | The underlying reason for a contact, from a controlled taxonomy such as "cancel a refundable booking" or "property says no reservation". It routes to self-service, AI or human skills and links support to product defects. | [[customer-service]] |
| **Case** | The record of one problem, binding parties, reservations, contact driver, deadlines, actions and outcome. It may span many contacts and channels, and is the unit a marketplace should manage and measure. | [[customer-service]] |
| **Arrival clock** | The deadline set by the stay itself: time to check-in, time to check-out or a cancellation deadline. It decides urgency more than any service level does. | [[customer-service]] |
| **Service level (SLA)** | A target such as "80% of calls answered in 60 seconds" or "first reply to e-mail within 24 hours". It measures the queue, not the outcome. | [[customer-service]] |
| **Recovery decision** | A recorded remedy for a case (refund, rebooking, relocation, credit, goodwill payment, fee waiver) with the cause code, entitlement, funder and liable party. It turns support outcomes into money facts that finance and partner terms can use. | [[customer-service]] |
| **Entitlement** | What a guest, partner or host is owed under the applicable policy and rule version. Entitlements are versioned rules, not agent scripts. | [[customer-service]] |
| **Funder / liable party** | The funder pays a remedy now; the liable party ultimately bears the cost and the recovery path says how it is recovered, for example a line on a partner statement. | [[customer-service]] |
| **Deflection / containment** | The share of contacts that self-service or an AI agent ends without a human. Easy to inflate; it says nothing about whether the problem was solved. | [[customer-service]] |
| **Resolution** | The case's problem is solved and stays solved, measured by no reopening or repeat contact within a window. | [[customer-service]] |

## <a id="g-money"></a>Part VI — Money

Who pays whom, when and how much. [[merchant-and-agency-models]] owns the money roles; [[stay-payments]] owns charging the guest; [[payouts-and-settlement]] owns paying partners and hosts; [[taxes-on-stays]] owns VAT, tourist taxes and platform reporting; [[refunds-and-reconciliation]] owns refunds and proving that the money agrees.

| Term | Definition | Chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Merchant model / agency model** | In the agency model the property charges the guest, usually at the stay, and the marketplace invoices a commission afterward. In the merchant model the marketplace (or its payment affiliate) charges the guest, holds the money and pays the supplier the agreed net amount later. | [[merchant-and-agency-models]] |
| **Facilitated payments** | The hosted-stays form of collection: the platform's payment entity collects the guest's payment as the host's limited payment collection agent, holds it until a trigger (usually check-in), deducts fees and pays out the rest. Economically close to the merchant model; legally framed as collecting on behalf of the host. | [[merchant-and-agency-models]] |
| **Limited payment collection agent** | The legal role in which a hosted-stays platform's payment entity accepts the guest's funds on the host's behalf. Once the guest pays, the guest's debt to the host is extinguished and the platform owes the host, so the platform's ledger must say so. | [[merchant-and-agency-models]] |
| **Merchant of record (MoR)** | The party the guest's payment is made to, who appears on the card statement and carries the card-scheme obligations for that payment: refunds, chargebacks and strong customer authentication. It answers "who did the guest pay?" and is a separate question from who supplies the stay. | [[merchant-and-agency-models]], [[stay-payments]] |
| **Supplier of the stay** | The party that sells the accommodation itself, for VAT and revenue purposes. A marketplace can be merchant of record for the payment while the hotel or host remains the supplier, or it can resell the stay in its own name. | [[merchant-and-agency-models]], [[taxes-on-stays]] |
| **Payment-model normalization** | Resolving, in one place, the payment arrangement of each booking (collector, collection schedule, supplier settlement method, commission direction, liability matrix) at quote time, freezing it on the reservation and versioning it on change, so no other system re-derives it. | [[merchant-and-agency-models]] |
| **Payment arrangement record** | The per-offer, per-reservation-version record that makes the liability matrix concrete: model, collector, guarantee, supplier settlement, commission direction and who carries chargeback, fraud and currency risk. Checkout, ledger, issuing, invoicing and payouts all read it. | [[merchant-and-agency-models]] |
| **Liability matrix** | A table that lists, for agency, merchant and facilitated models, who holds the money, who bears chargebacks, fraud and currency risk, who pays the supplier and who needs a license. Filled in before design work on any new payment option. | [[merchant-and-agency-models]] |
| **Safeguarding** | The duty of a licensed payment or e-money institution to keep client money separate and protected, for example in bankruptcy-remote accounts. For architects it is a ledger requirement: the platform must show at any moment which funds belong to customers. | [[merchant-and-agency-models]], [[refunds-and-reconciliation]] |
| **Electronic money institution (EMI)** | A licensed institution that may issue electronic money and provide payment services, with safeguarding duties. A marketplace holds one, or uses a licensed partner, to collect and hold guest funds under the merchant or facilitated model. | [[merchant-and-agency-models]] |
| **Payment Services Directive 2 (PSD2) commercial-agent exclusion** | Directive (EU) 2015/2366 excludes payments through a commercial agent acting for only the payer or only the payee. Collecting platforms rely on it by appointing themselves the host's agent, but regulators decide case by case, so large collectors hold licenses anyway. | [[merchant-and-agency-models]] |
| **Principal / agent (accounting)** | Under IFRS 15 and ASC 606, an agent presents only its fee or margin as revenue and a principal that controls the service presents the full price. A merchant-model marketplace usually stays an agent, so the model changes the balance sheet, not the revenue line. | [[merchant-and-agency-models]] |
| **Payment timing** | When the guest's money moves relative to booking and stay: pay now, pay at property, pay later online, pay in parts or third-party installments. It is a property of the offer, fixed on the reservation, and is a separate axis from the payment model. | [[stay-payments]] |
| **Deferred charge** | Any charge made after checkout without the guest present, under an agreement accepted at checkout: pay-later collections, balances, no-show and late-cancellation fees and post-stay damage charges. It can fail with nobody to fix it, so it needs retries and a deadline. | [[stay-payments]] |
| **Payment plan** | A versioned list of money obligations per booking (charges, holds, virtual cards, refunds), each with type, amount, currency, due date or trigger, instrument and state. Checkout creates it, modifications version it and one orchestration layer executes it. | [[stay-payments]] |
| **Virtual card (VCC)** | A single-use card number the marketplace issues per reservation, loads with the amount owed to the property and sends with the reservation; the hotel charges it at its own terminal. Parameters are activation, amount and expiry. | [[stay-payments]], [[payouts-and-settlement]] |
| **Payment orchestration (stays)** | The layer that turns a booking's payment obligations into payment service provider calls, in the right order, at the right time and idempotently, and reports the outcome. In stays the hard part is the right time. | [[stay-payments]] |
| **Customer-initiated transaction (CIT) / merchant-initiated transaction (MIT)** | A CIT involves the cardholder at the time. A MIT is started later by the merchant under a prior agreement and is generally outside SCA if the card was authenticated when saved. Later charges quote the network transaction identifier from the first CIT. | [[stay-payments]] |
| **Strong Customer Authentication (SCA)** | The PSD2 requirement (Directive (EU) 2015/2366, Art. 97) to authenticate with two of knowledge, possession and inherence, dynamically linked to amount and payee. Online card payments usually meet it with 3-D Secure. Most exemptions fit stays poorly. | [[stay-payments]] |
| **3-D Secure (3DS)** | The card issuer's authentication challenge inside the checkout flow, the usual way to apply SCA to online card payments. | [[stay-payments]] |
| **Network transaction identifier / scheme transaction reference** | A scheme-level reference returned by the first authenticated payment that proves the credential was set up for later charges. Each merchant-initiated charge quotes it; a checkout that fails to obtain it pushes later charges back into SCA. | [[stay-payments]] |
| **Payment service provider (PSP) / acquirer** | A PSP processes payments for the merchant through one API; an acquirer is the bank or processor that settles card transactions to the merchant. Orchestration may route across several of each. | [[stay-payments]] |
| **Presentment currency / payout currency** | The presentment currency is what the guest sees and pays; the payout currency is what the supplier receives. A third, listing or rate currency, is the one the host or hotel priced in. Each conversion has a rate, a time and an owner. | [[stay-payments]] |
| **Chargeback** | A reversal of a card payment initiated by the cardholder's issuer through the card network after a dispute. The merchant of record bears it. For future stays the dispute window can start on the stay date, so it can arrive after the host is paid. | [[stay-payments]], [[refunds-and-reconciliation]] |
| **Payable / receivable** | A payable is an amount the marketplace owes a partner. A receivable is an amount a partner owes the marketplace: agency commission, cancellation fees, recoveries. The payable belongs to the booking, not to the payout. | [[payouts-and-settlement]] |
| **Settlement** | The process of determining, netting and discharging payables and receivables per partner, for a period or per booking. | [[payouts-and-settlement]] |
| **Settlement ledger** | The double-entry record of every payable and receivable per partner, at line level, with the event that caused each. It is a sub-ledger feeding the general ledger in summary and decides who is owed what. | [[payouts-and-settlement]] |
| **Payout** | Any transfer that discharges what the marketplace owes the supply side (hotels call it remittance). A payout settles many payables minus receivables at one moment; "paid" is a ledger state, not a booking property. | [[payouts-and-settlement]] |
| **Commission invoice** | A legal tax document issued to an agency partner for commission earned on stays in a period, with VAT treatment and a due date. It creates a receivable. | [[payouts-and-settlement]] |
| **Partner statement** | A periodic, line-level account of everything that changed a partner's balance (bookings, fees, commission, adjustments, payouts, card loads, invoices, payments received), usable for reconciling against the partner's own books. | [[payouts-and-settlement]] |
| **Balance states (pending, available, held, paid)** | Stages of a payable: pending (not yet releasable), available (releasable, unpaid), held (stopped by a risk, compliance or dispute decision) and paid. A release rule moves a payable from pending to available, usually tied to check-in or check-out. | [[payouts-and-settlement]] |
| **Debit balance** | A negative partner balance: receivables exceed payables, for example after a cancellation or claim following payout. It is recovered by deduction from future payables or collected. | [[payouts-and-settlement]] |
| **Netting** | Paying the difference between payables and receivables instead of paying the payables in full and invoicing the receivables separately. | [[payouts-and-settlement]] |
| **Beneficiary** | Anyone the marketplace pays, which may differ from the account managing the listing, as with co-hosts, property managers and split payouts. | [[payouts-and-settlement]] |
| **Payout rail** | The mechanism that moves payout money: bank credit transfer (SEPA, domestic or wire), card push, wallet, PSP balance or virtual card. | [[payouts-and-settlement]] |
| **Verification of the payee (VoP)** | A name check of the payee against the receiving account, which the EU Instant Payments Regulation requires payment service providers to offer for euro transfers. Used as a defense against changed bank details. | [[payouts-and-settlement]] |
| **Value-added tax (VAT)** | The EU consumption tax charged on the stay and, separately, on the marketplace's commission or service fee. Who must charge VAT on the stay depends on who supplies it: the property or host today, and under ViDA the platform as deemed supplier when the host does not charge VAT. | [[taxes-on-stays]] |
| **Tourist tax** | A levy set by municipalities, regions or states, usually owed by the guest and collected by the property or host. It comes as a fixed amount per person per night, a percentage of price, tiered by classification or per room. Platforms display it or collect and remit under agreement or law. | [[taxes-on-stays]] |
| **Deemed supplier** | Under ViDA (Council Directive (EU) 2025/516, Art. 28a of the VAT Directive), a platform facilitating short-term rentals of up to 30 nights is treated as supplying the stay itself unless the host gives a VAT number and declares they will charge VAT. Applies from 1 July 2028 to 1 January 2030, by member state. | [[taxes-on-stays]] |
| **VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA)** | The EU reform package whose Directive (EU) 2025/516 adds the deemed-supplier rule for short-term accommodation platforms. Member states may apply it from 1 July 2028 and must by 1 January 2030. | [[taxes-on-stays]] |
| **Tax engine** | A bounded context beside pricing that decides tax per booking. It calculates at quote time from the seller's tax profile, the jurisdictions and effective-dated rules, freezes the tax determination at booking and feeds the ledger and reporting. | [[taxes-on-stays]] |
| **Tax determination** | The frozen record on a reservation of the tax inputs, rule versions, outputs and engine version, plus the liability decision of who collects. Later rate changes apply as deltas, never by silent recalculation. | [[taxes-on-stays]] |
| **Taxpayer / collector / remitter / reporter** | Four separate tax roles: who owes the tax, who takes it from the guest, who pays it to the authority and who reports to it. A hotel on an agency booking plays all four; on hosted stays they are often split between host and platform. | [[taxes-on-stays]] |
| **Place of supply (accommodation)** | The country where a service is taxed for VAT. In the EU, accommodation is taxed where the property is. The platform's own fee is taxed where a business customer is established, or where the property is for a consumer. | [[taxes-on-stays]] |
| **Tour operators' margin scheme (TOMS)** | The EU special VAT scheme for travel agents (Art. 306 of the VAT Directive) that deal with customers in their own name. A merchant-model OTA that resells rooms in its own name may owe VAT on its margin under it. | [[taxes-on-stays]], [[merchant-and-agency-models]] |
| **DAC7** | Council Directive (EU) 2021/514, requiring digital platforms to report sellers' rental activity, including immovable property, from 1 January 2023, with reports due by 31 January of the following year. Agency bookings count as consideration. | [[taxes-on-stays]] |
| **Reportable seller** | Under DAC7, an active seller not excluded, who is resident in a member state or rented out property located in one. A host living outside the EU with an apartment in Seville is reportable. | [[taxes-on-stays]] |
| **Tax identification number (TIN)** | The seller's tax identifier and its issuing state, collected for DAC7 reporting together with name, address, VAT number where available and date of birth. | [[taxes-on-stays]] |
| **Property listing (DAC7)** | Under DAC7, all property units at the same street address, owned by the same owner and offered on a platform by the same seller. It is the unit for which the platform reports address, consideration and rentals per quarter. | [[taxes-on-stays]] |
| **One-Stop Shop (OSS)** | An EU scheme, in place since 2021, that lets a business declare cross-border business-to-consumer services in one return instead of registering in every member state. | [[taxes-on-stays]] |
| **Income-tax withholding (platform)** | A duty, as in Italy's 2017 short-term-rental law, for a platform that processes payments to withhold and remit host income tax. It makes the seller's tax classification an input to payouts. | [[taxes-on-stays]] |
| **Refund funding** | The decision about whose money pays for a refund: amounts not yet paid out, a partner already paid, the platform or a third party such as an insurer. A refund is two events: money back to the guest, and its funding. | [[refunds-and-reconciliation]] |
| **Recovery** | An amount the marketplace claims back from a property or host after paying them, such as a cancellation fee or a refund the partner is liable for. In the settlement ledger it is a partner receivable. | [[refunds-and-reconciliation]], [[payouts-and-settlement]] |
| **Representment** | The merchant's contesting of a chargeback with evidence such as the checkout policy, guest-host messages, check-in confirmation and the authentication result. | [[refunds-and-reconciliation]] |
| **Foreign-exchange (FX) result** | The gain or loss when an amount recorded in one currency is settled in another at a different rate, arising on payouts, card settlements, refunds and chargebacks, often months after booking. | [[refunds-and-reconciliation]] |
| **Reconciliation** | A control that compares two or more independent records of the same money, matches them line by line and turns each difference into an exception with an owner. It proves completeness, accuracy and existence. | [[refunds-and-reconciliation]] |
| **Three-way reconciliation (stays)** | Matching three kinds of record: the business record (reservation, payment arrangement version, payment plan: what should have happened), the ledger (what was recorded) and external evidence from PSPs, banks, issuers, rails and partners (what actually moved). Differs from the usual platform-processor-bank sense. | [[refunds-and-reconciliation]] |
| **Exception (reconciliation break)** | A difference found by the matcher, held in a queue with an owning team, a service level and an age. An exception without an owner is a design defect. | [[refunds-and-reconciliation]] |
| **Finance adjustment** | A ledger entry posted outside an automated business flow to correct an error, recognize a loss, apply a goodwill decision or settle an exception. It has a type, reason, evidence, maker and checker. | [[refunds-and-reconciliation]] |
| **Ledger adjustment rate** | Adjustments per thousand reservations, by type and originating system. The book's best single indicator of where automated money flows are wrong. | [[refunds-and-reconciliation]] |
| **Suspense account** | A holding account for money that has arrived or left but cannot yet be attributed, such as an unmatched bank credit. A queue, not a place to keep money. | [[refunds-and-reconciliation]] |
| **Unclaimed funds** | Money owed to someone who cannot be paid, such as a returned payout, an uncashed refund or a virtual card the hotel never charged. It remains a liability to its owner, not income. | [[refunds-and-reconciliation]] |

## <a id="g-trust"></a>Part VII — Trust

Making strangers safe to stay with. [[stays-trust-and-safety]] owns trust decisions and enforcement; [[travel-fraud]] owns fraud and account takeover; [[identity-accounts-and-access]] owns identity levels and partner roles; [[reviews-and-reputation]] owns reviews and quality programs; [[protection-and-incidents]] owns screening, protection and incidents; [[stays-regulation]] owns short-term-rental and platform regulation.

| Term | Definition | Chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Trust policy** | A written, versioned rule about what is allowed on the platform, with the evidence standard for acting on it and the consequences. It exists both as text users and regulators read and as configuration the platform can evaluate. | [[stays-trust-and-safety]] |
| **Risk signal** | A fact or score about an entity that bears on a policy, such as a classifier score on a message, a photo-hash match, a login from a new device or a chargeback. Signals are evidence, not decisions. | [[stays-trust-and-safety]] |
| **Trust decision** | A recorded, versioned answer to "may this actor take this action on this object now, and if not, what happens instead?". It carries the outcome, reason codes, policy version, evidence references, the decider (rule, model or human) and an expiry; it is the unit that is audited, explained and appealed. | [[stays-trust-and-safety]] |
| **Enforcement action (stays)** | A typed, scoped and reversible restriction that a trust decision imposes, such as hiding a listing, restricting messaging, holding a payout or suspending an account. It has a scope, a start, an expiry or review date, and links to the decision and the statement of reasons. | [[stays-trust-and-safety]] |
| **Integrity operations** | The people, queues and tools that handle what automation cannot: report triage, investigations, appeals, quality assurance of decisions, safety escalations and feedback from cases into policies and models. | [[stays-trust-and-safety]] |
| **Entity graph** | A graph linking accounts, devices, payment instruments, phone numbers, addresses, photos and company registrations, used to answer "what is this connected to?". A link is evidence, not guilt, so each link type carries a confidence and a recorded reason. | [[stays-trust-and-safety]], [[travel-fraud]] |
| **Enforcement ladder: allow / allow with friction / redirect / hold / deny** | The graded set of outcomes a trust decision can return, ordered because the cost of a wrong action rises with each rung. Friction adds a check, redirect offers another product, hold pauses the next step pending review. | [[stays-trust-and-safety]] |
| **Labeled false positive** | A decision overturned on appeal. If the overturn never reaches the owner of the model that produced the decision, the model repeats the mistake, so the appeal path is architecture rather than a support process. | [[stays-trust-and-safety]] |
| **Linked-entity review** | The cross-brand rule the book recommends for a group: a suspension in one brand opens a review in the other instead of suspending automatically, and a hold applies only if the link score crosses a policy threshold. | [[stays-trust-and-safety]] |
| **Statement of reasons** | The "clear and specific" explanation a hosting provider owes an affected user under DSA Article 17 (Regulation (EU) 2022/2065, applying from 17 February 2024) for removals, demotions, payment restrictions and suspensions. If the decision record holds reason codes and policy version, the statement is a rendering of it. | [[stays-trust-and-safety]] |
| **Digital Services Act (DSA)** | EU Regulation (EU) 2022/2065, applying from 17 February 2024, setting duties for online intermediaries: notice and action, statements of reasons, internal complaints, trader traceability and transparency reporting, with more for very large platforms. | [[stays-trust-and-safety]], [[stays-regulation]] |
| **Trusted flagger** | An entity awarded that status by a national Digital Services Coordinator whose notices an online platform must process with priority under DSA Article 22. | [[stays-trust-and-safety]] |
| **Account takeover (ATO)** | Use of a genuine account by someone who is not its owner. For guests it opens stored cards, loyalty balances and personal data; for partners and hosts it opens reservations, the messaging channel and payout settings. | [[travel-fraud]] |
| **Extranet takeover** | Compromise of a partner or host account (stolen credentials or sessions) and what the attacker then does with it, typically reading upcoming reservations and messaging guests through the platform's trusted channel to collect payment. | [[travel-fraud]] |
| **Booking risk score** | A calibrated estimate of how likely a reservation is to end in a fraud loss, computed at checkout, recomputed as evidence arrives and read at each later point of no return. It is about whether the payment and account are genuine, unlike reservation screening. | [[travel-fraud]] |
| **Points of no return** | The moments in a booking after which a loss can no longer be avoided cheaply, such as virtual card activation, payout release or check-in. Fraud decisions belong there rather than only at the point of sale. | [[travel-fraud]] |
| **Risk spine / decision points** | The shared part of a fraud architecture (signal collection, entity graph, features, scoring, outcomes) and the many places that call it (login, checkout, message send, bank-detail change, payout release), each applying its own policy. | [[travel-fraud]] |
| **Step-up friction** | An extra check placed between allow and block that costs a genuine user a little and a fraudster a lot, for example an additional authentication or a payment-method check. | [[travel-fraud]] |
| **Session risk** | A score on a partner or guest session, recomputed on each meaningful action from weak signals such as a new device, an unfamiliar network or reading all upcoming reservations in minutes. | [[travel-fraud]] |
| **Third-party fraud / first-party fraud** | Third-party fraud uses someone else's identity or money (a stolen card, a taken-over account). First-party fraud is committed by the account holder, for example a guest who stays and then disputes the charge. | [[travel-fraud]] |
| **Payment request (structured)** | A platform object (amount, reason, link to the platform's own payment page) that is the only legitimate way for a property or host to ask a guest for money, so any free-text payment request is anomalous by definition. | [[travel-fraud]] |
| **Insult rate** | The share of genuine guests wrongly declined or blocked by fraud controls, estimated from appeals, retries and a holdout, and tracked beside the fraud loss rate. | [[travel-fraud]] |
| **Identity level (stays)** | The marketplace's statement of how sure it is that an identity belongs to a real, specific, reachable person, on a ladder (from IL0, a session, through IL1 reachable and IL2 paying to IL3 documented). It is not a badge or a risk score; policy requires a level only for actions that need it. | [[identity-accounts-and-access]] |
| **Partner role model** | The scopes, roles and policies, layered on delegation grants, that decide what partner staff, management companies, co-hosts and software may do on supply objects. One policy engine evaluates it, including conditions such as identity level or recent strong authentication. | [[identity-accounts-and-access]] |
| **Traveler profile** | The per-brand record of a traveler's preferences, companions, loyalty membership, payment method references and consents. It is kept separate from the identity and the evidence used to verify it. | [[identity-accounts-and-access]] |
| **Passkey** | A public-key credential under the W3C Web Authentication (WebAuthn) standard, usually synchronized across a person's devices. It is bound to the site's domain, so a lookalike phishing page cannot use it. | [[identity-accounts-and-access]] |
| **Phishing-resistant authentication** | Authentication that cannot be relayed by a fake site, such as a passkey or hardware key. NIST SP 800-63B-4 (2025) says passwords are not phishing-resistant and restricts SMS one-time codes. | [[identity-accounts-and-access]], [[travel-fraud]] |
| **Step-up authentication** | A fresh, stronger authentication required at the moment of a sensitive action, such as changing bank details, exporting data or adding an administrator, rather than only at login. | [[identity-accounts-and-access]] |
| **Delegation grant** | A grant linking a delegate party to a target (property, listing, portfolio or legal entity) with a role, scopes and a validity period, so staff, co-hosts, PMS vendors and AI assistants act on someone else's behalf with an audit trail. | [[identity-accounts-and-access]] |
| **Scope (authorization)** | A fine-grained permission on a supply object, such as calendar write, rates write, guest contact read or payout configure. Roles bundle scopes; policies add conditions roles cannot express. | [[identity-accounts-and-access]] |
| **Verified review** | A review whose author is a party to a reservation that the reservation record shows as stayed, checked against stated eligibility rules. Claims of verification are legal statements under EU and UK consumer law. | [[reviews-and-reputation]] |
| **Double-blind review** | A two-way review in which each side's review stays hidden until both have submitted or the review window closes. It reduces retaliation and reciprocity at the cost of a publication delay. | [[reviews-and-reputation]] |
| **One-way / two-way reviews** | In one-way reviews guests review properties, the usual model for hotels on OTAs. In two-way reviews the guest reviews the listing and the host reviews the guest, the usual model on hosted-stays marketplaces. | [[reviews-and-reputation]] |
| **Quality program** | A computed credential (badge, tier) or remediation track derived from reputation and operational records, with thresholds, a period and consequences. It turns review data into visibility and money, which creates the incentive to fake it. | [[reviews-and-reputation]] |
| **Review-trading ring** | A group of accounts that exchange cheap fake stays and reviews to buy ratings, quality badges and guest reputations. Detecting it needs patterns across accounts, payments and devices, not text filters. | [[reviews-and-reputation]] |
| **Eligibility evidence** | The reservation state at invitation, arrival evidence, stay dates and eligibility policy version stored with each review, proving "verified" under the rules then in force. | [[reviews-and-reputation]] |
| **Omnibus Directive (reviews)** | Directive (EU) 2019/2161, applied from 28 May 2022, which treats information on whether and how a trader ensures reviews come from real consumers as material information and bans fake reviews. | [[reviews-and-reputation]], [[stays-regulation]] |
| **Guest guarantee** | The hosted-stays platform's promise to rebook or refund a guest if the host cancels, check-in fails or the home is materially not as described. The marketplace pays first and recovers from the host where its terms allow; on the hotel side the nearest equivalent is relocating a walked guest at the property's cost. | [[protection-and-incidents]], [[customer-service]] |
| **Damage protection** | The marketplace's promise to reimburse a host for damage a guest caused and did not pay for, within limits. It may be a contractual promise funded by the platform rather than insurance. | [[protection-and-incidents]] |
| **Liability insurance** | An insurance policy that pays third parties, such as an injured guest or a neighbor, when the host is legally liable. Claims are often handled by a claims administrator. | [[protection-and-incidents]] |
| **Security deposit / post-stay charge** | Money taken from the guest before or during a stay and released afterward, as a card pre-authorization, a collected payment or a charge the guest agreed to at checkout. | [[protection-and-incidents]], [[stay-payments]] |
| **Recovery / subrogation** | Recovery is collecting a loss from the party who caused it, usually the guest. Subrogation is an insurer's right, after paying, to recover in the insured's place. | [[protection-and-incidents]] |
| **Reservation screening** | A risk decision made before a proposed reservation is confirmed, about physical and behavioral risk such as parties, damage and unsafe use. It is distinct from the booking risk score, which concerns payment and account fraud. | [[protection-and-incidents]] |
| **Incident workflow** | The intake, triage, response and closure of reported or detected events during a stay (party, injury, intrusion, lockout) as records linked to the reservation, deduplicated by listing and time window. | [[protection-and-incidents]] |
| **Active-stay roster** | The list of who is in a destination zone now and who arrives in the next 72 hours, used for outreach during a declared event. Hotels know who is in-house; hosted-stays marketplaces often do not. | [[protection-and-incidents]] |
| **Bordereau** | The periodic report of claims and premiums sent to an insurance partner under the program contract. | [[protection-and-incidents]] |
| **Reserve (claims)** | An estimate of what open claims will cost, booked so finance sees the protection liability before claims are paid. | [[protection-and-incidents]] |
| **Short-term rental (STR) registration** | The bundle of duties around a unit's registration number: collecting, displaying, checking and reporting it. A registration number is a unique identifier issued by the competent authority for a unit in that Member State. | [[stays-regulation]] |
| **EU Short-Term Rental Regulation** | Regulation (EU) 2024/1028, applying from 20 May 2026. It harmonizes registration data and platform duties (number before offering, display, random checks, removal orders, monthly activity data) but does not decide whether short-term renting is allowed. | [[stays-regulation]] |
| **Unit (EU STR)** | In the EU regulation, furnished accommodation rented short term. It excludes hotels and similar accommodation (NACE group 55.1), hostels and campsites, so most homes are in scope and most hotels are not. | [[stays-regulation]] |
| **Single digital entry point (SDEP)** | The national interface through which a Member State's authorities receive platform reports and orders and publish registration lists under Regulation (EU) 2024/1028. | [[stays-regulation]] |
| **Market policy registry** | The versioned, effective-dated store of jurisdiction rules (registration areas, number formats, night caps, reporting duties) that other systems evaluate. A rule records its legal source, scope, parameters and owner. | [[stays-regulation]] |
| **Compliance by design (stays)** | Enforcing rules at a few fixed points (publish, book, scheduled sweep) with one evidence trail, so decisions carry rule version and reason code. In the DSA it is also the duty in Article 31 for marketplaces. | [[stays-regulation]] |
| **Obligation register** | The legal team's register of obligations, each with its source, effective period, authority, scope, implementing controls, control owners and the evidence that proves they ran. | [[stays-regulation]] |
| **Jurisdiction resolver / eligibility evaluator** | The resolver maps a unit's location to the country, region, municipality and registration areas that apply. The evaluator answers whether a unit may be offered, booked or kept live under current rules and returns a reason code. | [[stays-regulation]] |
| **Rule primitive** | One of about ten reusable rule types (registration required, number format, validating register, night cap, minimum stay, zone prohibition, license expiry and others) parameterized per jurisdiction, so adding a city means adding data. | [[stays-regulation]] |
| **Night cap / primary residence rule** | A limit on nights per year a unit may be rented, often applying to a host's primary residence, for example Paris reducing its cap from 120 to 90 nights from 1 January 2025. | [[stays-regulation]] |
| **Dry run (policy)** | A preview that lists the units a rule change would affect before it goes live, so a regulatory lead can approve a change by its impact rather than by a code diff. | [[stays-regulation]] |
| **Regulator gateway** | The integration with authorities, run as a product with service levels: it sends reports, order confirmations and statements of reasons and takes in orders, list updates and validity lookups. | [[stays-regulation]] |
| **Platform-to-Business (P2B) Regulation** | Regulation (EU) 2019/1150, applying from 12 July 2020, protecting business users such as properties and hosts. Terms must state the main ranking parameters and the effect of any payment on ranking (Article 5), give notice of terms changes, and give a statement of reasons for restricting or terminating a partner. | [[stays-regulation]], [[stays-ranking]] |
| **Digital Markets Act (DMA)** | Regulation (EU) 2022/1925, applying only to designated gatekeepers. Article 5(3) bans parity clauses; Booking.com was designated a gatekeeper on 13 May 2024. | [[stays-regulation]] |
| **Trader traceability (DSA Art. 30)** | The duty for marketplaces where consumers contract with traders to collect and verify trader identity data. The Omnibus Directive adds a displayed trader or non-trader status. | [[stays-regulation]] |
| **Very large online platform (VLOP)** | A DSA designation for platforms above 45 million average monthly EU recipients, with extra duties. | [[stays-regulation]] |

## <a id="g-platform"></a>Part VIII — Platform

The distributed system underneath. [[event-driven-stays]] owns reservation events and ARI streams; [[global-scale-and-reliability]] owns journey objectives, degradation and cost; [[marketplace-intelligence]] owns governed metrics and partner insight; [[stays-experimentation]] owns delayed outcomes and interference; [[stays-ml-platform]] owns the ML platform and model governance; [[demand-channels]] owns metasearch and partner distribution.

| Term | Definition | Chapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Reservation event** | The published record that one reservation reached a new version, carrying that version's full contract: stay, unit or room, party, price lines, policy, payment arrangement and status. Losing one is an incident, not noise. | [[event-driven-stays]] |
| **Availability, rates and inventory (ARI) stream** | The ordered sequence of applied changes to availability, rate and inventory facts, keyed by property or listing, from which every price and availability read model is derived. Very high volume; a lost update is usually superseded by the next. On hosted-stays marketplaces the equivalent is the stream of calendar claims for one unit. | [[event-driven-stays]] |
| **Outbox (stays)** | A table in the producer's own database into which a service writes each outgoing event in the same transaction as the state change; a relay then publishes committed rows. Distinct from a connectivity gateway's delivery outbox, which holds each reservation version until the property acknowledges it. | [[event-driven-stays]] |
| **Event notification / event-carried state transfer** | Two patterns Fowler separates: a notification says only that something changed and the receiver calls back for details; event-carried state transfer includes the data so receivers keep their own copy. Thin notifications cause the read-back race. | [[event-driven-stays]] |
| **Change data capture (CDC)** | Reading a database's transaction log and turning committed row changes into a stream. Used for legacy systems that cannot write an outbox, with a translator that maps raw table changes to domain events so consumers never learn the legacy schema. | [[event-driven-stays]] |
| **Idempotent consumer** | A consumer that records the event IDs and highest reservation version it applied, in the same transaction as its own effect, so duplicates and late older versions change nothing. A gap in versions is held and alarmed, not guessed through. | [[event-driven-stays]] |
| **Schema compatibility rule** | A rule, enforced by a schema registry when a new event schema is published, that governs which changes a producer may make without breaking registered consumers; backward compatibility lets consumers on the new schema read data written with the old. | [[event-driven-stays]] |
| **Supersession** | Dropping an ARI event because a later event for the same product and night has already been applied. Allowed for price read models; never allowed across different rate plans, so a closure is not lost. | [[event-driven-stays]] |
| **Bootstrap events** | Contract events generated from a consistent database snapshot, marked with the snapshot's log position, to backfill a new consumer with history that the broker no longer holds. | [[event-driven-stays]] |
| **Shadow run** | Running a new consumer or ledger on real events in parallel with the existing system and comparing results nightly, without letting it act, to find disagreements before cutover. | [[event-driven-stays]] |
| **Completeness (event stream)** | The share of producer versions that each registered consumer has applied within its freshness objective; the direct measure of "nothing was lost". | [[event-driven-stays]] |
| **Journey SLO (stays)** | A service-level objective for one of the four guest journeys (search, book, pay, deliver), counting an outcome as good only if it was fast, definite and correct. Service SLOs remain as diagnostics; the journey SLO is the one the business signs. | [[global-scale-and-reliability]] |
| **Service-level objective (SLO) / error budget** | A target level of reliability for a service; the error budget is 100% minus the SLO, the amount of failure a team may spend on releases before it must stop. | [[global-scale-and-reliability]] |
| **Four journeys: search, book, pay, deliver** | The book's split of a stay platform's promise. Deliver is the one most platforms forget: the reservation must reach the property's system or the host, and other channels' availability must close. | [[global-scale-and-reliability]] |
| **Degradation mode** | A named, pre-agreed state in which the platform deliberately does less so the most important journeys keep their objectives under load or partial failure, with a trigger, ordered steps, guest-visible labels, an owner and an exit condition. | [[global-scale-and-reliability]] |
| **Disruption mode** | A flagged state that changes business rules, not capacity, after an event such as a flight strike or wildfire: price decreases freeze, extenuating-circumstance cancellation rules apply and service routes affected trips first. | [[global-scale-and-reliability]] |
| **Cost per booking** | Run cost for a period (cloud, data centers, licenses, CDN, maps, third-party APIs) divided by reservations made and also by reservations stayed, split by journey. A canceled reservation still paid for its search. | [[global-scale-and-reliability]] |
| **Unit economics (FinOps)** | Linking what an organization spends on technology to the value that spending creates, for example cost per transaction. | [[global-scale-and-reliability]] |
| **Traffic classes (edge)** | Categories into which every request is classified at the edge (guests, metasearch, affiliates, bots) so each gets its own budget and shedding order. Every rejection should say when to retry. | [[global-scale-and-reliability]] |
| **Load shedding** | Deliberately rejecting lower-priority requests at the edge, by traffic class, so higher-priority journeys keep working; a backstop inside services is by latency. | [[global-scale-and-reliability]] |
| **Retry budget** | A contractual limit on how much a client may retry after a rejection. Without a Retry-After header, a client may retry at once and triple its request rate. | [[global-scale-and-reliability]] |
| **Single-home writes with failover** | Each property, listing or reservation has one home region where writes apply; a standby elsewhere holds a replica and takes over after a declared failover. The default for inventory and reservations. | [[global-scale-and-reliability]] |
| **Recovery time objective (RTO) / recovery point objective (RPO)** | The longest acceptable time to restore service after a failure, and the longest acceptable window of lost writes. | [[global-scale-and-reliability]] |
| **Warm standby** | A second-region copy of a system that is kept running and replicated, but not serving writes, ready to be promoted after a declared failover. | [[global-scale-and-reliability]] |
| **Marketplace intelligence** | The capability that collects, models and governs a marketplace's data and turns it into decisions: reporting, experiment verdicts, model labels, partner and host analytics, and regulatory data. | [[marketplace-intelligence]] |
| **Governed metric** | A business measure whose definition is written once, as code, with an owner, a grain, a time basis, inclusions and exclusions, a restatement policy and a tier saying who may change and consume it. | [[marketplace-intelligence]] |
| **Metric layer (semantic layer)** | The component that holds governed metric definitions and serves them to every consumer: dashboards, notebooks, experimentation, feature pipelines and APIs. It is credible only if reconciled to the ledger. | [[marketplace-intelligence]] |
| **Data product (stays)** | A dataset or stream a named team publishes for others, with a schema contract, a freshness and quality objective, documentation, access rules and a support channel. A consumer must be able to use it without talking to the producer. | [[marketplace-intelligence]] |
| **Partner insight** | Analytics a marketplace gives its supply side (hotels, property managers, hosts) about their own performance, their market and the effect of their choices, computed from the data the marketplace uses to rank, price and pay them. | [[marketplace-intelligence]] |
| **Honored stay** | A stayed reservation without a property or host cancellation, relocation or material "not as described" case. The basis for supply-reliability and ranking labels. | [[marketplace-intelligence]], [[stays-experimentation]] |
| **Reservations created / net / cancellation-adjusted / stayed** | The booking metric family: created counts every confirmed reservation by booking date; net subtracts cancellations by event date; cancellation-adjusted keeps a cohort's survivors at a measurement point; stayed counts completed stays by check-out. The basis is part of the name. | [[marketplace-intelligence]] |
| **Behavioral event / business event** | A behavioral event records what a person did in a client and is collected under consent; a business event records a change of state in a system of record, and losing one is an incident. | [[marketplace-intelligence]] |
| **Event taxonomy (tracking plan)** | The controlled, versioned list of behavioral event names, properties and meanings. | [[marketplace-intelligence]] |
| **Metric tiers (1 to 3)** | Tier 1 metrics leave the company (board, partner invoices, regulators) and change only with the finance owner's approval; tier 2 drive internal decisions; tier 3 is exploration and barred from external surfaces. | [[marketplace-intelligence]] |
| **Customer data platform (CDP)** | A system that unifies a guest's profiles and events under consent rules and builds audiences for marketing and personalization. | [[marketplace-intelligence]] |
| **Rule-based / modeled attribution and incrementality** | Rule-based attribution (last click, position-based) runs daily channel operations; modeled attribution plans budgets; incrementality from holdouts and experiments is the truth the other two are calibrated against. | [[marketplace-intelligence]], [[stays-experimentation]] |
| **Online controlled experiment (A/B test)** | An experiment that randomly assigns units to variants and compares outcomes, so the difference is causal. | [[stays-experimentation]] |
| **Randomization unit** | The entity assigned to a variant: a visitor, account, listing, cluster of competing listings, market, or market in a time slot. The choice decides what the test can measure without bias. | [[stays-experimentation]] |
| **Trigger and counterfactual exposure** | A unit is triggered when the variants actually differ for it; control logs a counterfactual exposure recording that the treatment would have shown, so analysis can drop untouched visitors. | [[stays-experimentation]] |
| **Overall evaluation criterion (OEC)** | The single metric, or small set, on which the ship decision is made; every other metric is a guardrail or a diagnostic. In stays, stayed reservations for funnel changes and honored stays for ranking and supply changes. | [[stays-experimentation]] |
| **Guardrail metric** | A metric that must not degrade beyond a set threshold for a change to ship. | [[stays-experimentation]] |
| **Delayed-outcome metric** | A metric whose value for a unit is final only long after exposure, such as a stayed reservation, honored stay, refund or host revenue per available night. It needs an outcome, a maturation rule and a maturity share. | [[stays-experimentation]] |
| **Maturity share** | The fraction of an experiment's units whose delayed outcomes are final at readout time; a delayed-outcome result without it cannot be interpreted. | [[stays-experimentation]] |
| **Surrogate metric** | An early-observable quantity that predicts the delayed outcome, such as booked reservations weighted by predicted probability of staying. Valid only if checked against matured outcomes of past experiments, kept in a surrogate ledger. | [[stays-experimentation]] |
| **Marketplace interference (stays)** | One unit's treatment changes another unit's outcome, breaking the stable unit treatment value assumption (SUTVA). Channels: shared inventory, supply response and market feedback. It adds bias, usually overstating a launch's effect. | [[stays-experimentation]] |
| **Global treatment effect** | The difference between a world where everyone gets the treatment and one where no one does; the number a launch decision needs. | [[stays-experimentation]] |
| **Switchback / cluster randomization** | A switchback alternates treatment within a unit (such as a market) over time periods; cluster randomization assigns whole groups of competing listings or markets. Both cost power but reduce interference bias. | [[stays-experimentation]] |
| **Randomized encouragement** | A design for features that partners or guests must choose to adopt: randomize the offer of the feature, then estimate the effect of the rollout and of adoption. | [[stays-experimentation]] |
| **Sample-ratio mismatch (SRM)** | A split between arms that differs from the planned one (for example 50.6% in a 50/50 test), showing that assignment or logging is broken. The readout is blocked until the check passes. | [[stays-experimentation]] |
| **CUPED** | Controlled experiment using pre-experiment data: a variance-reduction method that adjusts each unit's outcome by its pre-period behavior. It helps less in stays, where most guests have no recent history. | [[stays-experimentation]] |
| **Meta-experiment** | An experiment that randomizes over experiment designs, used to measure how much a design's estimate is biased by interference. | [[stays-experimentation]] |
| **ML platform (stays)** | The shared infrastructure and governance record to build, run and stop models that make decisions about stays, guests, partners and hosts: features, training and evaluation, serving, monitoring, registry and lineage, and governance. | [[stays-ml-platform]] |
| **Feature / feature platform** | A feature is a named, typed value about an entity as of a point in time. A feature platform holds feature definitions and compiles each into offline training data and an online store computation; broader than a feature store, which is only the storage. | [[stays-ml-platform]] |
| **Point-in-time correctness** | A training example for a decision at time t contains only feature values knowable at t. Stays violates it easily because reservations are modified, canceled and re-priced after the fact. | [[stays-ml-platform]] |
| **Training-serving skew** | Any difference between the feature values a model saw in training and those it receives in production. | [[stays-ml-platform]] |
| **Model governance (stays)** | The owner, approved uses, consequence tier, evaluation and approval record, and regulatory classification kept for every model, scaled to the consequence of a wrong decision rather than model complexity. | [[stays-ml-platform]] |
| **Consequence tier** | Four-level classification of a model by its highest-tier consumer: 0 internal analytics, 1 operational, 2 third-party facing, 3 decisions about a person. A new consumer can raise the tier without any code change. | [[stays-ml-platform]] |
| **Model registry / approved uses** | The record of every model version with its data, features, code, consumers, owner and limits. Approved uses list the decisions a model may feed; a new consumer must request one. | [[stays-ml-platform]] |
| **Online store / offline log** | The online store is a low-latency key-value store read at inference time; the offline log records served feature vectors by request ID as ground truth for the next training set. | [[stays-ml-platform]] |
| **Refit / respecify / widen** | Choices at each retrain: refit keeps the model structure and uses newer data; respecify changes the structure; widen extends uncertainty intervals when the world is shocked. | [[stays-ml-platform]] |
| **Demand channel** | A route by which a guest's intent reaches the marketplace, defined by who controls the surface the guest sees and how the marketplace pays: owned, search engines, metasearch or partner distribution. | [[demand-channels]] |
| **Metasearch integration** | The interfaces through which a marketplace supplies a comparison engine with its properties, prices for specific stays and landing pages, and measures what the clicks became. The engine writes the rules, so keep them in one service. | [[demand-channels]] |
| **Hotel list feed / point-of-sale file** | The hotel list feed is the XML file of a partner's properties that the engine matches to its own map listings; the point-of-sale file defines landing URL templates per market, language, currency and device. | [[demand-channels]] |
| **Price delivery modes** | The ways a metasearch engine gets prices: ARI push, pull, changed pricing, and live pricing queries answered within a deadline. Choice depends on supply type. | [[demand-channels]] |
| **Cost per click (CPC) / cost per acquisition (CPA)** | CPC pays per visit sent; CPA pays per booking or stay. Metasearch is paid per click or by commission per booking, depending on engine and program. | [[demand-channels]] |
| **B2B distribution API** | An interface that lets another business sell the marketplace's supply to its own customers: search, price, book and manage stays under the reseller's brand or inside its tools. | [[demand-channels]] |
| **Link affiliate / white label / API reseller** | Forms of partner distribution: an affiliate sends guests to the marketplace with a tracking ID; a white label is the marketplace's experience in the partner's brand; an API reseller renders the experience itself from API data. | [[demand-channels]] |
| **Landing assembly** | Composing an entry page (destination, metasearch landing, campaign) from a stay query, search pipeline results, editorial content and merchandising slots, rendered so crawlers, humans and accuracy checkers see the same prices. | [[demand-channels]] |
| **Merchandising slots** | Defined places on a landing or results page where promoted or editorial offers can be placed under rules. | [[demand-channels]] |

## <a id="g-leadership"></a>Part IX — Leadership

The Chief Architect's agenda. | Term | Definition | Chapter |

| **Convergence decision** | The group-level decision, per capability and per supply segment, of what is built once, what is built behind one contract and what stays with each brand. It is recorded with an owner, a reason and a review date. | [[converging-two-marketplaces]] |
| **Shared core** | The shared engines and shared contracts that every brand in a group must use. A brand may not build its own copy of them. | [[converging-two-marketplaces]] |

| **Cross-type supply / cross-type routing** | Supply of one marketplace type sold by a brand of the other type, such as boutique hotels on a homes marketplace. The routing rule is that the supply segment chooses the engine, while the brand chooses storefront, commercial terms and policies. | [[converging-two-marketplaces]] |
| **Storefront / supply engine** | The storefront is the brand-facing surface (apps, web, search, ranking, page templates) that presents offers to guests. The supply engine is the catalog, inventory and connectivity that produce offers for one supply segment. | [[converging-two-marketplaces]] |
| **Cross-listing** | A storefront showing offers produced by another brand's supply engine, with the supplier's consent, usually in a labeled section of results. | [[converging-two-marketplaces]] |
| **Stays team topology** | The assignment of journeys, engines and contracts to teams across two marketplaces, using stream-aligned, platform, complicated-subsystem and enabling teams, with a stated interaction mode and end date for each important pair. It separates journey ownership from engine ownership. | [[organizing-stays-engineering]] |
| **Architecture governance (stays)** | The rules for who may change what, after consulting whom, and where the decision is recorded. For two marketplaces it sorts decisions into three classes by blast radius: team, contract and group. | [[organizing-stays-engineering]] |
| **Blast radius (of a decision)** | How many teams, brands and money flows a decision binds. It determines which decision class applies and who decides. | [[organizing-stays-engineering]] |
| **Architecture decision record (ADR)** | A short document recording context, decision, options considered, consequences and the advice received. It is kept with the code or in a shared decision log. | [[organizing-stays-engineering]] |
| **Advice process** | Andrew Harmel-Law's model in which anyone may make an architectural decision, provided they first consult the people affected and those with relevant expertise. Advice must be sought and recorded but need not be followed. | [[organizing-stays-engineering]] |
| **Stream-aligned / platform / enabling team** | Team Topologies types: a stream-aligned team owns a flow of work end to end, a platform team provides an internal product that speeds up other teams, and an enabling team helps others overcome obstacles and is meant to be temporary. | [[organizing-stays-engineering]] |
| **Cognitive load (team)** | The amount of domain and technology a team can understand and own. Team boundaries exist to keep it bounded. | [[organizing-stays-engineering]] |
| **Ownership catalog** | A register of owners for journeys, engines and contracts, used to route alerts and questions, including partner-caused incidents, to the right team. | [[organizing-stays-engineering]] |
| **Modular monolith** | One deployable service divided into domain components whose boundaries are enforced by architecture tests in the build, not by documents. | [[organizing-stays-engineering]] |
| **Strangler fig** | Martin Fowler's pattern of replacing a legacy system gradually by moving behavior into new code behind an interception point. For stays platforms it must also move reservations already made for future dates, not only new requests. | [[modernizing-a-live-marketplace]] |
| **Seam** | A point where behavior can be redirected without changing the code on either side, such as a contract, an event stream or an API facade. | [[modernizing-a-live-marketplace]] |
| **System of record** | The one system whose write decides the truth of an entity. During a migration each reservation has exactly one at any moment. | [[modernizing-a-live-marketplace]] |
| **In-flight reservation migration** | Moving a confirmed reservation whose stay has not finished to a new system of record, keeping its identifiers, version history, delivery state, payment arrangement and policy snapshots intact. | [[modernizing-a-live-marketplace]] |
| **Open book** | The set of confirmed reservations with arrival dates still ahead, which a migration must account for. | [[modernizing-a-live-marketplace]] |
| **Parallel run (stays)** | Old and new systems process the same live input and their outputs are compared. In a shadow run only the old result is used; in a dual run a share of traffic uses the new result. | [[modernizing-a-live-marketplace]] |
| **Drain** | Sending new bookings to the new system while old reservations finish their lifecycle in the old one. It is slow where booking windows are long. | [[modernizing-a-live-marketplace]] |
| **Reverse sync** | After cutover, the new system writes its changes back into the old one so remaining readers keep working and rollback stays possible. | [[modernizing-a-live-marketplace]] |
| **Point of no return** | The dated decision after which rollback is no longer supported, usually because reverse sync is switched off. | [[modernizing-a-live-marketplace]] |
| **Post-stay tail** | The period after checkout during which a reservation still needs its system of record: no-show reporting, commission invoicing, refunds after complaints, chargebacks and tax records. | [[modernizing-a-live-marketplace]] |
| **Cutover unit** | The slice of the platform that moves at once. For reservation state it is the property or listing, so that one property's reservations stay in one engine. | [[modernizing-a-live-marketplace]] |
| **Agentic booking** | A reservation created, changed or canceled by software acting for a traveler, where the software rather than the traveler made at least one of the choices (place, property, rate, dates). | [[ai-in-travel-stays]] |
| **AI governance (stays)** | The register, tool contracts, side-effect classes, booking authority and evaluation rules that control which AI uses may read or change stays data, and who owns them. | [[ai-in-travel-stays]] |
| **AI use-case register** | A register of every AI use, built or bought, with owner, consequence class, approved tools and evaluation plan. Tool registries and gateways read it at runtime. | [[ai-in-travel-stays]] |
| **Model Context Protocol (MCP)** | An open standard, published by Anthropic in November 2024, for connecting AI applications to data and tools. A marketplace can expose its domain operations to external agents as an MCP server. | [[ai-in-travel-stays]] |
| **Agent gateway / tool registry** | The gateway identifies the agent platform and the traveler behind each call, verifies booking authority, enforces quotas and records the actor chain. The registry lists the operations an AI may call, with schema, side-effect class, scopes and owner. | [[ai-in-travel-stays]] |
| **Side-effect class** | The category of a tool by what it can change: read, draft, reversible write, money or contract, and decision about a person. The class, not the model, decides the controls. | [[ai-in-travel-stays]] |
| **Human final click (handoff) / mandate** | The two booking authorities for agents. In handoff the traveler confirms on a marketplace-hosted step. In a mandate the traveler signs constraints in advance (place, dates, maximum total, refundability) and the gateway verifies each booking against them. | [[ai-in-travel-stays]] |
| **Actor chain** | The record on a reservation of who acted: traveler, agent platform, agent session and authority ID. | [[ai-in-travel-stays]] |
| **EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689)** | EU regulation on AI systems. Article 50, applicable from 2 August 2026 (as of 2026), requires systems that interact directly with people to tell them they are dealing with an AI system unless that is obvious. | [[ai-in-travel-stays]] |
| **Residuality theory** | Barry O'Reilly's approach that treats a business system as unpredictable and designs it by stressing it with environmental changes rather than forecasting them. | [[evolving-the-platform]] |
| **Stressor / attractor / residue** | A stressor is any change in the environment, likely or not. The attractor is the state the business falls into under it. The residue is what remains of the architecture, and the structural change, usually a separation, that lets the next version survive. | [[evolving-the-platform]] |
| **Naive architecture / residual architecture** | The naive architecture satisfies today's requirements and assumes today's world stays put. The residual architecture is the design after many stressors have each left their residue. | [[evolving-the-platform]] |
| **Stressor analysis (stays)** | Testing a stays platform against a list of environmental changes, tracing each through the guest's promise and recording the attractor, the residue and its status: integrated, candidate, gap or survived by composition. | [[evolving-the-platform]] |
| **Combined stressor** | Several stressors hitting one guest at once. If existing residues handle it with a coordinating case and no new structure, the decomposition is sound. | [[evolving-the-platform]] |
| **Fitness function (stays)** | An outcome measure of one part of the guest's promise, taken the same way across engines and years, with an owner, a threshold, a cadence and a decision that a breach triggers. It protects the decade, where a journey SLO protects the month. | [[evolving-the-platform]] |
| **Optionality** | Paying now to keep a hard-to-reverse decision open, justified where the stressor list holds a plausible reason to reverse it. | [[evolving-the-platform]] |
| **Guaranteed allotment / on-request contract** | Two agency hotel contract types. Under guaranteed allotment the OTA holds a room counter and confirms the guest at once, then notifies the hotel. Under on-request the hotel's answer decides confirmation. | [[case-agoda-trip-com]] |
