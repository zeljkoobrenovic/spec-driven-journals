# Spec: Part IX — LEADERSHIP: The Chief Architect's Agenda

## Intent

Orient the reader to Part IX: the Chief Architect's decisions that follow the design: sourcing, portfolio integration, organization, modernization, AI and decade-long evolution. Frame them through the One Dossier program (€38 million, 2027–2029) and state the part's argument: a small set of keys, records and contracts must stay shared, owned and measurable while products, suppliers, people and owners change. Introduce each chapter by the decision it records and the Axleby case that drives it.

Productscape bricks: the union of the part's chapters (see PLAN for [[build-buy-or-partner]], [[one-platform-from-many-products]], [[organizing-engineering]], [[modernizing-the-platform]], [[ai-in-dealer-workflows]] and [[evolving-the-vehicle-platform]]).

## Audience

Chief architects, CTOs, engineering directors and owners' operating partners of vehicle data and dealer software groups.

## Success criteria

- [ ] Opens with IN THIS PART, WHY THE BUSINESS CARES, WHY ARCHITECTS SHOULD CARE, KEY POINTS (exactly three) and a FAILURE PATTERNS THIS PART ADDRESSES callout using exact pattern names linked to where-vehicle-platforms-go-wrong.html.
- [ ] States the argument (own and share a small kernel, give each part an owner, steer by retirements and fitness functions) and ties it to One Dossier.
- [ ] One mermaid diagram with a Figure caption.
- [ ] A "Chapters in this part" list with one paragraph per chapter in config order, each linking by `[[permalink]]` and saying what it decides and which case drives it.
- [ ] Axleby facts match the ledger and the chapters (€38 million, March 2027, €7.8 million build data, €6.9 million flat fee, 58 records and 19 invoices, 38 teams and 438 components with 57 unowned, Connect XML sunset 31 December 2029, 16 stressors, nine residues, twelve fitness functions).
- [ ] Ends with a hand-off to [[build-buy-or-partner]]; 500–800 words of body.

## Non-goals

Teaching sourcing method, integration sequencing, team design, strangler patterns, agent permissions or residuality (the chapters own them); external factual claims.
