> **IN THIS SECTION, YOU WILL:** Find the template that fits the design or decision in front of you, copy it, see which fields differ between the data half and the workflow half or between markets, and see it filled in once for Axleby, with the mistakes that most often make it useless and the chapter that explains every field.

> **KEY POINTS:**
>
> * **Every template names an owner, a version and the identifier other records cite.** An Axleby Vehicle Key (AVK), a `fact_version`, a Charter decision id that is also Meterline's `licence_ref`, a valuation id, a register transaction id: that chain lets a support agent, a data client or an auditor walk from an invoice line or a complaint back to the fact, rule and version that produced it.
> * **Keep the common core and the market adapter in separate fields.** A register transaction card is Dutch until a German or British one is written; a channel adapter contract is one portal's; a license card is one supplier's. Fields that read the same everywhere belong to the shared platform. A field copied from one market or channel into another without checking is how a single-market assumption gets into shared code.
> * **Start with the record the next incident will ask for.** Most fields here came from an incident: the withdrawal lane from the Zombie Golf, the Ambiguous state from the Month-End Register Outage, `corrects_record_id` from the Odometer Correction, read-back of owner and queue from the Lost Leads of Velto, the client grant from the License Breach, outcome rules from the No-Hit Credit. Fill the fields that change a decision, write "unknown" rather than guess, and add a field when a post-incident review asks a question no field answers.

> **[FAILURE PATTERNS THIS SECTION ADDRESSES](where-vehicle-platforms-go-wrong.html):**
>
> * **Feed Roulette** — the source onboarding checklist, channel adapter contract and DMS adapter card give every supplier, portal and DMS a written contract, a status signal and a monitor before it goes live.
> * **Provenance Amnesia** — the provenance envelope card and the API contract card make source, observation date, license and confidence part of every fact and every answer.
> * **License Blindness** — the license and purpose card and the entitlement matrix put what a source allows and what a client bought in machine-checked records, not in contracts nobody reads.

<br>
These are **working templates for a group that sells vehicle data and dealer software**: the records that Parts I to IX ask a Chief Architect to keep, from the capability map of [[vehicle-capability-map]] to the decision record of [[organizing-engineering]], each tested against Axleby, the fictional vehicle data and dealer software group this book follows ([[meet-axleby]]). Each is the smallest form I have found that makes a design or decision explicit, assigns an owner, and leaves evidence someone else can check. **Adapt the wording; keep the fields that carry identity, provenance, ownership and money.**

Four conventions run through all sixteen.

- **They are maintained data, not documents.** License rules live in Charter, the license and purpose register; routing rules in Baseline, the tenant configuration service; API contracts in Folio, the contract registry; decision records in one log. A template copied into a slide deck stops being true within a quarter.
- **They reference each other by identifier.** An API contract card cites license classes and SKUs; a decision record cites the capability rows and entities whose owner it changes.
- **They say which half and which market.** The data half sells facts under license; the workflow half runs dealers' work inside tenants. Where a field differs between them, or between the Netherlands and Belgium, Germany, the United Kingdom and the United States, the template says so.
- **Each Axleby example agrees with the chapter that teaches it.** All Axleby names, figures and dates are fictional; the linked chapter works the full case.

## <a id="tool-1"></a>Tool 1: Capability Map

**Purpose.** A capability map describes what the group must be able to do along the chain Identify → Check → Value → Stock → Advertise → Sell → Register → Trade → Insight ([[vehicle-data-ecosystem]]), independent of systems, teams or acquired brands. It **classifies each capability twice**: by investment class (how much to invest and whether to build) and by scope (where it lives). Use it to prepare a sourcing or sharing decision (Tools 15 and 16), a program budget or an acquisition case. Review it yearly and after every acquisition, incident review or market entry.

**The template.** One row per level-2 capability: drawn where one owner can be accountable and sharing or sourcing is a single decision.

| Field | What to record | Guidance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Domain and capability** | Level-1 domain; a business ability as a noun phrase | Never a system ("Specbook"), a team or a brand |
| **Systems** | Every implementation today | Two or three implementations is the duplication signal |
| **Class** | D differentiating, P parity, C commodity | D means a customer would leave if it were merely as good as a competitor's; require a named comparison |
| **Condition** | Green, Amber, Red, Grey (missing) | Funding follows condition as much as class |
| **Scope** | Shared, data half, workflow half | Shared only if both halves need **the same answer about the same car or customer** |
| **Market flag** | Market-specific or not | A market difference is a rule set or adapter, not a separate capability |
| **Shared promise** | For shared rows: latency, freshness, consistency, availability per consumer | Incompatible consumers get two serving paths over one model |
| **Owner** | One accountable person | Consumers are not owners |

**Axleby example** (capability map v1, March 2027; the full map is in [[vehicle-capability-map]]):

| Field | A3 Specification and equipment |
| --- | --- |
| Systems | Specbook with licensed build data; Lotwise's dealer-selected edition as a second, unmanaged answer |
| Class, condition, scope | **P** parity, **Amber**, **Shared**, market-specific |
| Evidence | 28.5 million build-data fetches in 2026, about €13.1 million; 9% of sampled Lotwise adverts carried an edition different from the resolved one |
| Owner | Priya Raman (catalog, precedence rules, purchase policy) |

v1's totals: **39 capabilities; 13 differentiating, 21 parity, 5 commodity; 18 shared, 6 data half, 14 workflow half**.

**Common mistakes.**

- The org chart in disguise. "Lotwise features" is not a capability; stock, dossier, adverts and sales administration are.
- Everything differentiating. If more than about a third of rows are D, ask which ones a customer would leave over.

## <a id="tool-2"></a>Tool 2: Entity–Owner Table

**Purpose.** The entity–owner table names, for each principal entity, its identifier, the one context whose committed state is the answer, the copies others may hold and who moves it through its lifecycle. It turns "which system is right?" into a lookup. Use it when two systems disagree, a new product or acquisition needs vehicle data, or a field is written in two places. Review it with every decision record that changes an owner.

**The template.**

| Field | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| **Entity** | A business object with identity and a lifecycle |
| **Identifier** | Opaque, never reused, minted by the owner; plates, stock and invoice numbers are labels |
| **Source of truth** | The one context allowed to change it; its **system of record** (the store) may differ during a migration |
| **Allowed copies** | By kind: **reference** (the key), **decision snapshot** (id and version used), **observation** (another party's claim with source), **projection** (rebuildable read model with measured lag) |
| **Lifecycle** | States and the context that moves it through them; events published on the backbone ([[events-webhooks-and-sync]]) |
| **Team and degraded rule** | The owning team; what consumers do when the owner is unavailable |

**Axleby example** (version 1, approved by the architecture board on 3 June 2027; excerpt of 29 entities):

| Entity | Identifier | Source of truth | Allowed copies |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vehicle | AVK (26 characters, never reused); VIN as evidence | Vehicle identity (Anchor) | References everywhere; merges publish `vehicle.merged` |
| Register record | The register's key | The register; inside the platform, register access (Turnstile) | Mirror projection; theft and awaiting-inspection status asked live, never mirrored |
| Valuation | Valuation id | Vantage ledger | Decision snapshots in dossier, deal, trade listing |
| Stock item and dossier | Stock item id; one open per tenant and AVK per stay | Stock and dossier; system of record still Lotwise Classic MySQL in 2027 | Projections (stock lists, websites); references in leads, deals, listings |
| Channel listing | (stock item, channel) | Syndication for desired state; the portal for actual state | Observations of portal state |
| Prospect | Per dealer legal entity | Leads (Switchboard) | None across legal entities or tenants |
| Register transaction | Transaction id; idempotency key from the dealer's click | Axleby Trade ledger | Dossier register track; proof by reference |
| Usage event | Client request reference + SKU + billing account | Meterline | Usage views as projections; corrections by reversal |

**Contested rows are written down with reasons.** Example: a signed contract, deposit or hold makes a stock item **Reserved**, which withdraws its adverts in the same transaction; it becomes **Sold** when the deal's conditions are met and the invoice is issued (or at delivery, where the dealer configures it), as [[deals-and-sales-documents]] defines.

**Common mistakes.**

- "The DMS is the system of record." The DMS owns the accounting stock unit; the dossier owns the stock item; Hitch's cross-reference links them ([[dms-integration]]).
- A copy that fits no kind. Lotwise Classic's register cache, median age 11 days, let Autobedrijf Brandsma agree a €9,800 trade-in the register then refused.

## <a id="tool-3"></a>Tool 3: Provenance Envelope Card

**Purpose.** The provenance envelope answers five questions about one fact: who said it, about which car, when it was true, when we learned it, and what we may do with it. The card defines the envelope for a fact type and the behavior each confidence tier allows. Use it when a new fact type is stored, a new source is onboarded (Tool 4), or a client asks what you told them on a past date.

**The template.** Envelope v1 fields from [[provenance-and-data-quality]].

| Field | What to record | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `fact_id`, `fact_version` | Stable id; version per correction | Downstream records cite the version |
| `subject` | AVK plus the source's own key | Re-attach facts when identity is corrected |
| `attribute`, `value`, `value_as_delivered` | Normalized and original | Keep the original for disputes |
| `source`, `source_class` | Official / commercial / first-party / derived | Drives wording and survivorship |
| `source_record_ref`, `batch_ref` | Supplier record id; landing batch and hash | Batch-level fields stored once, by reference |
| `observed_at` | Valid time | When it was true of the car |
| `recorded_at`, `superseded_at` | Transaction time | Append-only; a correction closes the interval |
| `licence_ref` | Charter license id and display class | What products may show, cache, resell |
| `confidence` | Confirmed / Corroborated / Asserted / Suspect | Tiers, not probabilities |
| `derivation` | Rule or model version and input fact versions | Lineage for judgements and valuations |
| `status` | Active / superseded / retracted / disputed | Lifecycle |
| **Freshness class** | Immutable, registration state, decision-critical, accumulating, market, derived | Sets the SLO and the degraded answer |

Products obey the tier: **Confirmed** and **Corroborated** facts may be stated as fact with their sources; **Asserted** facts are shown attributed ("reported by a workshop") and make a valuation provisional; **Suspect** facts are never stated as fact.

**Axleby example** (one odometer reading after the Odometer Correction):

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Subject | AVK plus the UK plate on the reading date |
| Value | 66,337 km; redelivered as `41220` with unit `mi`, version 2 (version 1 carried unit `km`, the error) |
| Source | Kilometra, commercial; license `KM-2024-03`, consumer display allowed |
| Observed / recorded | 2027-01-18 workshop visit / 2027-05-24, when the corrected batch loaded |
| Confidence | Asserted |

**Common mistakes.**

- A separate audit log. The envelope is written in the same transaction as the value, or it drifts.
- Overwriting in place. The May 2027 loader upserted corrections over the originals, which made "what did you tell us on 17 May?" a six-day reconstruction.

Learn more: [[provenance-and-data-quality]]

## <a id="tool-4"></a>Tool 4: Source Onboarding Checklist

**Purpose.** A source goes live only when four owners have signed: the license steward for what may be done with the data, procurement and finance for its cost, the integration owner for delivery and correction terms, and the data and valuation leader for quality and precedence. The checklist is the gate. Use it for every register channel, supplier file, portal data agreement, connected-car route and DMS-sourced feed, and again at every contract renewal.

**The template.**

*License (license steward, [[data-licensing-and-governance]])*
- [ ] License class assigned in Charter; every rule linked to its contract clause
- [ ] Permitted purposes and display audiences; onward transfer; attribution duty
- [ ] Raw retention, cache retention and the `derivatives` rule (free, aggregate-only, prohibited)
- [ ] Personal data: controller, lawful basis, DPIA reference ([[privacy-and-retention]])

*Cost ([[scale-and-cost]])*
- [ ] Price per request, per no-hit and per redelivery; retention that decides repurchases; rate limits

*Delivery contract (integration owner)*
- [ ] Schema with meanings, units and code lists, not just columns
- [ ] Stable `source_record_id`; `corrects_record_id` for corrections
- [ ] Delivery window, manifest with counts and coverage-through
- [ ] Correction notice period and remedies

*Pipeline ([[ingestion-and-entity-resolution]])*
- [ ] Landing with manifest and content hash; replay possible
- [ ] Thresholds for the four validation layers: delivery, schema, semantics, distribution per segment
- [ ] Quarantine owner and alert; release by replay, never by bypass

*Quality and product*
- [ ] Ground truth and overlap sample named; default confidence tier
- [ ] Survivorship precedence and the recompute impact estimate
- [ ] Freshness class and the product wording when it is late
- [ ] Monthly scorecard measures agreed with the supplier

**Axleby example** (Kilometra workshop readings, contract v2, August 2027):

| Item | Entry |
| --- | --- |
| License | `KM-2024-03`, consumer display allowed; signed by Femke de Boer's team |
| Delivery | Nightly file, about 410,000 readings (about 34,000 UK imports, about 93% with a valid VIN), due 02:30 CET; push feed quoted at €95,000 a year and declined |
| Contract clauses added | Unit declared per workshop chain, `workshop_chain_id`, stable `source_record_id`, `corrects_record_id`, coverage-through in the manifest, correction notice within 1 business day |
| Distribution checks | **Per-workshop-chain check** (the fix): share of a chain's readings below the vehicle's previous reading above 0.5% *(assumption)* quarantines that chain's segment and releases the rest. The first post-incident gate, a median reading per age band and origin (0.85–1.15 of trailing 30 days), is **insufficient**: replayed in September 2027 it would not have fired on the May 2027 batch, because one chain's 3,050-record export with 94% failing barely moved the medians |
| Precedence | Survivorship v3: a commercial reading becomes current only if consistent with prior official readings |
| Scorecard, Q3 2027 | 91 of 92 batches on time; 96.8% agreement with MOT readings within 2% on 4,100 pairs |

**Common mistakes.**

- A contract that defines columns but not meanings, corrections and provenance. That is an invoice, not a contract.
- License and cost reviewed after integration. The 30-day build-data retention clause made 41% of fetches repurchases.

Learn more: [[ingestion-and-entity-resolution]], [[provenance-and-data-quality]]

## <a id="tool-5"></a>Tool 5: Valuation Model Card

**Purpose.** A valuation model is a product release. The model card states what a release values, on what evidence, how well it did in each segment, which segments it refuses, and what switches it off. Dealers spend money on the engine's word; the card is how the engine keeps that trust. Use it for every release, every challenger, and every complaint that a segment is mispriced.

**The template.**

| Field | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| **Release and owner** | Model and adjustment-table versions; approver |
| **Value types and markets** | Retail, trade-in, trade, private, future; basis (VAT scheme, registration tax included or not) |
| **Training** | Window, recency weighting, sources and their licenses' `derivatives` rules |
| **Inputs and flags** | Fact types and confidence; flags that widen the range (`thin_sample`, `stale_market`, `spec_uncertain`) |
| **Applicability contract** | Segments valued; segments refused, and what is returned instead |
| **Back-test per segment** | Median absolute percentage error, share within ±5% and ±10%, **signed bias**, 80% range coverage; against transaction and against asking prices separately; rolling origin |
| **Drift monitors** | Leading indicators per segment and the thresholds for `watch` and `restricted` |
| **Release gates** | Challenger beats champion on bias and coverage in every segment with enough data; shadow period; staged release; rollback |
| **Known weaknesses** | Segments with thin data or lagging ground truth |

**Axleby example** (Vantage's recency-weighted release after the EV Overvaluation, 2027):

| Field | Entry |
| --- | --- |
| What changed | Twelve months of equally weighted prices replaced by recency weighting plus daily per-segment corrections from asking-price movement |
| Segment key | Fuel type, age band, price band, body type, market, written to the ledger at issue |
| Thresholds | `watch`: a leading indicator out of band for two weeks, or early-outcome bias above 3%; `restricted`: bias above 5% or three indicators out of band, which switches off suggested maximum purchase prices (MPP) and automated offers |
| Replay of the 2026 BEV window | `watch` in week 3, `restricted` in week 5 at +5.8%; replayed bias +2.4% by week 7; estimated overpayment on the 610 trade-ins about €0.3 million instead of €1.1 million |
| Known weakness | About 7% of BEV valuations in the window used an edition with unknown battery size; now flagged `spec_uncertain` with a wider range |
| Release gates | Two-week shadow period as unbilled ledger entries with purpose `shadow`; staged by market and segment |

**Common mistakes.**

- Global error only. In 2026 global MdAPE moved from 6.1% to 6.6% under an 8% alert while BEVs ran +9.5% for 14 weeks.
- No refusals. An applicability contract that says "no valuation" is cheaper than a confident wrong number.

Learn more: [[valuation-engine]]

## <a id="tool-6"></a>Tool 6: Channel Adapter Contract

**Purpose.** Each portal or ad channel is a small integration product depending on a contract that changes on someone else's calendar. The channel adapter contract, or channel profile, records everything the adapter knows about one channel. A profile that cannot be filled in is a channel the platform should not sell as supported. Use it when a channel is added, its changelog announces a change, or reconciliation finds drift.

**The template.**

| Field | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| **Channel kind** | Portal write API, file feed, ad catalog, or portal as syndicator |
| **Reached via** | Other channels this one exports to, so the dealer makes one decision per destination |
| **Operations** | Create, full update, PATCH allow-list, delete, lifecycle states |
| **Identity and create idempotency** | Platform external id on the listing; native creation key, or look up by external id before creating again |
| **Withdrawal saga** | Steps, deadline, how absence is confirmed |
| **Error classes** | Each response code mapped to transient, throttled, permanent for this content, or locked |
| **Rate budget** | Token bucket, ramp-up, reserved share for withdrawals |
| **Observers** | Push (callbacks, streams) and pull (list reads); evidence level: confirmed, asynchronous, or file without status |
| **Field ownership** | Per field: platform wins, portal wins, or flag |
| **Mandatory fields per market** | With the date each became mandatory |
| **Mapping tables** | Equipment, make and model mapping versions, keyed by identifiers, never display names |
| **Owner and probes** | Named engineer; weekly changelog review into dated tickets; hourly synthetic car on a test rooftop |

**Axleby example** (AutoScout24 profile as Relay 2 holds it; channel facts from AutoScout24's public documentation, accessed October 2026):

| Field | Entry |
| --- | --- |
| Reached via | `mobile_de` and `AutoTrack` channel flags export the listing; never also publish to mobile.de directly for the same rooftop without the dealer choosing it |
| Create idempotency | POST documented as not idempotent; a 500 on fetch does not mean absence; look up by external id before creating |
| Update | Full desired state by default; PATCH silently drops fields such as `financingOffer`, so it is excluded from the allow-list |
| Throttling | 429 slows the whole channel's bucket; batch jobs ramp up over 5–10 minutes |
| Market rules | From 2026-06-01, Belgian used-car listings require `belgianCarpassMileageUrl` (HTTP 400 otherwise); validated before publish |
| Mapping | `natCode` since 2024-06-13; localized reference names changed 2026-10-19, so mappings key by id |
| Observer | Listing reads with `include=overwriteProtection`; locked listings marked *Locked*, not overwritten |

**Common mistakes.**

- Mapping errors classified as transient. The Zombie Golf's updates threw on an unmapped equipment code and requeued at the head, blocking its deletes for 4 days.
- Trusting push alone. Of Axleby's 61 channels, 19 confirm state, 25 accept and process silently, 17 are file feeds; every push needs a pull behind it.

Learn more: [[listing-syndication]]; for the receiving side of the same problem, [[connectivity-and-channel-managers]]

## <a id="tool-7"></a>Tool 7: Lead Routing Card

**Purpose.** A lead routing card is a rooftop's or group's rule set, written down with its targets, fallback and proof of delivery. Rules live as versioned, effective-dated configuration in Baseline; the card is how a group administrator and the leads team agree what the rules should do before anyone saves them. Use it at onboarding, at every reorganization, and when a rooftop opens, closes or changes CRM.

**The template.**

| Field | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| **Scope and version** | Tenant, rooftops, rule-set version and effective date |
| **Delivery mode** | Inbox, external CRM, or both (and which leads go where) |
| **Evaluation order** | 1 lead type, 2 vehicle to rooftop, 3 group policy, 4 calendar, 5 assignment, 6 terminal fallback |
| **Targets** | Each queue, team or CRM queue, validated against the tenant directory and integration registry |
| **Closure handling** | Successor rooftop on `rooftop.closed`; alert to the group administrator |
| **Delivered means** | Read-back of the CRM record's owner and queue; inbox copy until confirmed |
| **After hours** | Contact center, auto-acknowledgement, callback slot |
| **Consent scopes** | Answer the enquiry; marketing by channel; text version |
| **Simulation** | Replay of the last 30 days: leads per target, targets with leads and no active users |
| **Monitors** | Unacknowledged leads per rooftop; portal counts reconciled daily |

**Axleby example** (Velto Mobility Group after the Lost Leads of Velto, autumn 2027):

| Field | Entry |
| --- | --- |
| Scope | 46 rooftops in 9 regional hubs; 7 legal entities, so 7 prospect boundaries |
| Delivery mode | External CRM: each hub's BDC queue in DriveDesk CRM, delivered through Hitch with owner-and-queue read-back; Lotwise inbox as fallback and audit view |
| Group policy | Hub rooftops' leads to the hub BDC queue first; after 19:00 to the group contact center |
| Closure handling | The three closed rooftops route to their named successors; a target with leads and no active users blocks activation |
| Simulation output | For example, "Hub Noord-Brabant BDC would receive 2,310 leads; 0 leads to rooftops in Closing" |
| Monitor | Alert when a rooftop's leads stay unacknowledged for 4 business hours while leads still arrive |

**Common mistakes.**

- HTTP success counted as delivery. DriveDesk's inactive queue returned success and discarded 1,460 leads for 9 days.
- No terminal fallback. A rule set without a watched fallback must not validate.

Learn more: [[leads-and-crm]], [[journey-lead-to-dms]]

## <a id="tool-8"></a>Tool 8: Register Transaction Card

**Purpose.** A register transaction changes the official record of a vehicle and produces proof. It is synchronous, all-or-nothing and state-guarded: a blind retry turns a success into an apparent failure. The card specifies one transaction type in one market. Use it when a type or market is added, or the register changes its channel or rules.

**The template.**

| Field | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| **Legal act, market, register** | The act in the local term with an English gloss; channel (direct, provider, partner) |
| **Authority chain** | Legal entity, recognition, credential and expiry, provider route, user, customer mandate; stored as it was at submission |
| **Prerequisites** | Each check, its source, and whether it can run from a mirror or needs a live call |
| **Idempotency** | Key from the dealer's click to the fee line; business guard on open transactions per vehicle and type |
| **Ordering** | Lane key (AVK); fairness across legal entities; pacing set by channel health |
| **Timeout and Ambiguous** | Timeout; probe that asks the register what it did; outcomes matches, does not match, inconclusive |
| **Proof** | What is stored verbatim; documents produced; retention |
| **Fees** | Register fee (pass-through) and own fee; metered on confirmation only |
| **Degraded mode** | Honest queue; what dealer and buyer are told |
| **Owner** | Product owner and the on-call team |

**Axleby example** (Dutch registration in the buyer's name, *tenaamstellen*, after the June 2027 redesign; Autobedrijf Brandsma's Corolla):

| Field | Entry |
| --- | --- |
| Authority | Brandsma's recognitions; certificate expiring in 11 months; designated register agent; buyer's DigiD authorization (*machtiging*) signed in the showroom |
| Prerequisites | Validated at signing, not on delivery day; awaiting-inspection status asked live |
| Ordering | One lane per AVK; the Polo trade-in's stock-in with release (*vrijwaren*) ran in its own lane on delivery day and confirmed in seconds |
| Timeout | 90 seconds (was 30); first submission timed out, probe showed the change committed two minutes earlier: Confirmed by probe |
| Fees | €6.50 plus the register's fee; one Meterline usage event on confirmation |
| Dealer sees | "Confirmed (verified with the register)" |
| Evidence it works | Quarter-end 30 June 2027: 41 ambiguous outcomes, all resolved by probe, no double submissions |

**Common mistakes.**

- No Ambiguous state. On 31 March 2027, 214 acts were submitted twice and recorded as "rejected" for cars already in the buyer's name.
- Fees on submission. A double submission then becomes a double charge.

## <a id="tool-12"></a>Tool 12: License and Purpose Card

**Purpose.** Two linked records decide what may leave the platform. The **source license rule** says what Axleby may do with a source; the **client grant** says what one client may do with what it receives. Charter checks both on every request and writes a decision id that Meterline carries as `licence_ref`. Use the card when a source agreement or client contract is signed, renewed or changed, and when a misuse monitor fires.

**The template.**

*Source license rule*

| Field | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Source, license class, clause refs | One of: open, register-restricted, supplier-retainable, supplier-per-request, display-limited, tenant-owned, derived |

| Permissions | Purposes × audiences, and products allowed to serve them |
| Prohibitions | Onward transfer, bulk export |

| Field | What to record |
| --- | --- |

| Retention and onward transfer | `retain_until` returned in every response |
| Sub-clients | Each end customer of a software vendor, named in calls |
| Lifecycle | Proposed, probation (90 days, volume ceiling), active, under review, suspended, purge pending, closed |

**Axleby example** (the damage-data partner's rule as revised in April 2027, and the Lintvelt grant it would have refused):

## <a id="tool-13"></a>Tool 13: API Contract Card

**Purpose.** A data API sells category blocks: identity, registration, specification, history, valuation, market, tax, battery health. Each block is sold, licensed, sourced and failed independently. The API contract card defines one block as Folio, the contract registry, holds it. Use it when a block or field is added, an outcome rule or code list changes, or a version is deprecated.

**The template.**

| Field | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| **Block and owning context** | One owner, which decides outcomes; the composer only assembles |
| **Schema** | JSON Schema reference; code lists documented as open |
| **Sources and license classes** | Which purposes Charter must find; what is `withheld` and why |
| **Outcome rules** | `hit`, `partial`, `no_hit`, `failed`: the business definition of each, and its SKU and price rule |
| **Freshness class** | `current`, `stale`, `unavailable` thresholds; whether a copy may ever serve it |
| **Error semantics** | Block-level failures stay HTTP 200 with a labeled block; 5xx only when the service cannot answer |
| **Version status** | Beta (90 days' notice), stable (12 months' notice to remove), dated version supported 24 months after its successor |
| **Behavioral change record** | Changes to outcome rules, code lists and computation bases, shipped only in the April and October releases |
| **Service level** | Availability of well-formed answers; per-block `current` rate |

**Axleby example** (history block, JSON API v2, October 2027):

| Field | Entry |
| --- | --- |
| Owner | Tracewell, whose assembler decides hit, partial, no hit or failed |
| Sources | Register and MOT data (official), Kilometra (commercial), damage-data partner (display-limited) |
| Outcome rules | `partial` when one source times out, with coverage per section; `no_hit` free under no-hit-no-fee; `failed` free; re-opening the same report for the same vehicle within 30 days free; a refresh billed |
| Freshness | Report states coverage-through per source; a stale block is still a `hit` |
| Withheld | Damage section listed under `withheld` when the purpose does not allow it, so "not returned" never reads as "nothing found" |
| Example client | Fleetmoor's return lookup on a 2022 Kia e-Niro: history rated as a business history report (€4.95 list), `partial` because the damage source timed out, with credit on request; the four-block lookup €5.22 at list |

**Common mistakes.**

- One outcome rule per stack. The No-Hit Credit (€186,000 to 41 clients) came from two definitions of a hit.
- Client-specific schemas. Of Connect XML's 57 response profiles, v2 replaces subsets with block selection and license hiding with Charter's filter; renamings are not reproduced.

## <a id="tool-14"></a>Tool 14: Journey SLO Card

**Purpose.** A journey SLO sets a service-level objective on what a dealer or client does, not on a service. Each attempt is **good**, **degraded** (a labeled partial or stale answer, or an act honestly queued) or **bad**. The objective covers good plus degraded, and a second cap stops "always degraded" from passing as healthy. Upstream failures count. Use the card when objectives are set, a synchronous dependency is added, or a review finds every service green while dealers failed.

**The template.**

| Field | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| **Journey** | Where it starts and ends, in dealer or client terms |
| **Good / degraded / bad** | Exact conditions, including "never a silent loss" |
| **Objective and window** | Good-or-degraded target; cap on degraded; rolling window |
| **Owner** | One named leader |
| **Dependency budget** | Share reserved for own code; share per synchronous dependency × share of attempts touching it |
| **Dependencies** | Tier 1 (blocks a legal or commercial act), 2 (degrades a decision), 3 (delays a background flow) |
| **Degraded modes** | Labeled copy, partial answer, honest queue, local system of record, or fail fast with a reason |
| **Never degrades** | The checks that must stay live |
| **Attribution** | Every bad outcome tagged own code, named dependency or client input |
| **Error-budget policy and game days** | What stops when the budget burns; last exercise |

**Axleby example** (appraisal in Lotwise, May 2027; targets are assumptions from [[reliability-and-dependencies]]):

| Field | Entry |
| --- | --- |
| Objective | 99.5% good or degraded per month *(assumption)*; budget 0.5%: 0.2% own code, 0.3% dependencies |
| Proposed change | Blocking live register status call at appraisal open |
| Budget test | Register call 0.40% + Connect XML 0.30% + build data 0.03% + Vertex AI 0.10% = **0.83%** against a 0.3% line *(illustrative failure rates)* |
| Decision | Call made non-blocking ("status unchecked" counts as degraded); Appraise moved off Connect XML; build-data failure returns catalog-level specification |
| Never degrades | Purchase confirmation: live theft and transfer status, in its own journey whose objective accepts the register's availability |
| Sibling objectives | Withdrawals: 99% within 10 minutes; price updates: 95% within 15 minutes; lead delivery to a watched place within 60 seconds *(assumption)* |

**Common mistakes.**

- Excluding upstream failures. That hides **Borrowed Uptime** exactly where it lives.
- Latency and errors only. The 214 double submissions and 1,460 discarded leads were fast, successful requests.

Learn more: [[reliability-and-dependencies]]

## <a id="tool-15"></a>Tool 15: Build, Buy or Partner Decision Card

**Purpose.** A sourcing decision chooses one of five options for one capability: **build**, **adopt** (run open source in-house), **buy** (vendor software or SaaS), **license** (data under use limits) or **partner** (another business acts for a shared customer). The card applies four gates in order, records the data-rights test, and names what the group keeps in-house so that leaving stays possible. Use it for every Tool 1 row whose sourcing is open, at every renewal, and when a vendor changes terms.

**The template.**

| Field | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| **Capability and requirement** | Tool 1 row; the requirement the source must meet, with numbers |
| **Gate 1: supply** | Can anyone else do it, and can anyone but us? |
| **Gate 2: data rights** | The nine questions: store, scope, derive, redistribute, train, billing basis, evidence, exit, personal data. **An unanswered question is a "no"** |
| **Gate 3: judgement or liability** | Will a customer hold us responsible for the answer? If yes, logic, evidence and record stay in-house |
| **Gate 4: cost curve** | Unit cost at today's and forecast volume; crossover; team capacity *(assumptions recorded)* |
| **Options compared** | Including "do nothing", with cost, rights, risk and verdict |
| **Decision and key assumption** | One option; the assumption under which it holds |
| **Owned to keep exit open** | Adapter boundary, canonical model, ground truth, ledger |
| **Review trigger and owner** | The event that reopens it; recorded as a Tool 16 record |

**Axleby example** (as-built build data, in the sourcing register approved on 21 October 2027; [[build-buy-or-partner]] works the full comparison):

| Field | Entry |
| --- | --- |
| Requirement | As-built data where the candidates' value spread matters: the purchase gate's appraisal threshold of €750 |
| Gate 1 | Only manufacturers know what a car received at the factory: license the as-built layer; build the catalog and crosswalk |
| Gate 2 | 30-day retention caused 41% repurchases in 2026; renegotiated in July 2027 to life-of-vehicle retention within Axleby products at about €0.58 a first fetch |
| Gate 4 | Flat-fee offer of €6.9 million a year against about €6.4 million per request at an assumed 11 million fetches in 2028 |
| Options | Per request with life retention (**keep**, with a yearly price ceiling); flat fee (decline); second provider for catalog-only makes (pilot two makes); own catalog without as-built (reject: Spec Guesswork) |
| Result so far | From €13.1 million on 28.5 million fetches (2026) to a run rate of about €7.8 million on 13.5 million (Q4 2027) |
| Owned for exit | Resolved views with license refs; the canonical catalog |
| Review trigger | Renewal, or fetch volume below 9 million |

**Common mistakes.**

- Price before rights. The retention clause saved more than any price negotiation.
- Buying the judgement with the product. Buy damage detection; own the confirmed-findings dataset that measures any provider ([[condition-and-damage]]).

## <a id="tool-16"></a>Tool 16: Architecture Decision Record

**Purpose.** An architecture decision record (ADR) captures one decision, the options rejected and its consequences in about a page. A vehicle platform adds the **halves and markets** affected, the **entities whose owner changes**, the **contracts touched** and the **advice received**. Under Axleby's advice process, anyone may decide after seeking advice; a team record stays in the team's repository, and a decision that crosses a team boundary or a shared standard (vehicle key, tenant model, event catalog, field ownership, public contracts) becomes a forum record before the first production change. A few vetoes are written down: Identity Resolution on any plate or VIN used as a key, the Group Chief Architect on a tenant-specific code path, Billing on a billable event outside Meterline, governance on a data call without a license check ([[organizing-engineering]]).

**The template.** Builds on Michael Nygard's 2011 record ([Nygard, 2011](https://www.cognitect.com/blog/2011/11/15/documenting-architecture-decisions)) and Andrew Harmel-Law's advice process ([Harmel-Law, 2021](https://martinfowler.com/articles/scaling-architecture-conversationally.html)).

```markdown
# DR-YYYY-NNN — <title that states the decision, not the topic>

Status: Proposed | Accepted <date> | Superseded by DR-... | Rejected
Proposer: <name, team>     Decided by: <team after advice | architecture forum>
Vetoes consulted: <identity | tenant code path | billing | license check | none>
Halves and brands: <data half | workflow half; Axleby Data, Lotwise, Showroomly, Axleby Trade, Odolo>
Markets: <NL | BE | DE | UK | US; what is a market adapter>
Capability rows: <Tool 1 rows; grade before -> after>
Entities and owners: <Tool 2 rows whose owner or copies change>
Contracts touched: <API blocks | events | channel or DMS adapters | license rules>

## Context        The incident or finding; facts with dates and numbers.
## Drivers        What a good answer must achieve; the journey SLO it protects.
## Options        At least two real options plus "do nothing".
                  | Option | For | Against | Cost | Reversibility |
## Decision       "We will ...", with scope, owner and dates.
## Consequences   Better / worse / new work, carried by whom.
## Data and law   Licenses, personal data, register terms, Data Act.
## Advice received  Who advised; followed or not, and why.
## Confirmation   How conformance is checked (reconcilers, replays, SLOs).
## Review         Date, or the trigger that reopens it.
```

**Axleby example.** *One Dossier event backbone.* Design record approved on 14 December 2027 ([[events-webhooks-and-sync]]).

| Section | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Context | *Stock item sold* reached 8 consumers by 6 mechanisms; in October 2027, 83 of 152,400 sold transitions never reached Relay; 19 API clients received the Odometer Correction's wrong mileage in 2,310 lookups and learned of it by email, if at all |
| Options | Dual write (status quo); outbox with a polling relay; table-level change data capture as the event stream; event sourcing; **outbox with log-tailing relay, entity-keyed topics and reconcilers** |
| Decision | Catalog with one owner per event type; CloudEvents 1.0 with `tenantid`, `rooftopid`, `avk`, `entityversion`; outbox and Debezium relay, Lotwise Classic first; no events from Connect XML; JSON API v2 webhooks on Standard Webhooks with a 30-day cursor feed |
| Consequences | Q1 2028 Lotwise outbox; Q2 Switchboard, pricing and valuation ledger on topics; Q3 webhooks for dealer groups, replacing Velto's 30-minute diff script; Q4 `vehicle.fact.corrected` for Rijnstaal and Fleetmoor |
| Data and law | Lead, deal and fact-correction events thin; public gateway filters by tenant, entitlement and Charter license |
| Advice received | Marta Nowak (platform) and Hendrik Maes (partner integrations), who traced the audit |
| Confirmation | Sold-but-live job becomes a check, not the delivery path; reconcilers per flow |

**Common mistakes.**

- One option. Include "do nothing" and the cheap answer, so the record shows why they lost.
- No halves-and-markets line. A decision that names one brand is implemented in the other by assumption.

## Using the Toolkit Over Time

The templates stay true only when they are revisited, and three habits make that happen.

- **Review on a calendar.** Capability map and sourcing cards yearly and after every acquisition; source contracts and channel profiles at each renewal and each portal changelog entry; model cards with every release; journey SLO cards before month-end and quarter-end peaks; register transaction cards whenever a register changes its channel or rules; decision records on their review dates.
- **Let incidents add fields.** Beyond those named in the key points, the per-chain check came from the Odometer Correction and tenant id in every cache key from the Cross-Tenant Lead. When a post-incident review finds a gap, the fix usually includes a field in one of these templates.
- **Keep the set small.** Every field should change a decision or preserve evidence. When a field is filled by habit and read by nobody, remove it; when a review keeps asking a question no field answers, add it.

[[evolving-the-vehicle-platform]] explains how twelve fitness functions, among them identity merge precision, correction propagation time, publish and withdrawal lag, lead delivery, segment valuation bias and cost per lookup, keep these records honest over years; [[glossary]] defines the terms the templates use, and [[bibliography]] lists the sources behind them.
