# Axleby Group — Canonical Scenario Ledger

Axleby Group is **fictional**. Every figure, system choice, name and event below is invented for teaching. Chapters must agree with this ledger. If a chapter needs a new fact, add it here (append under "Additions by chapter") rather than inventing a conflicting one. Never present Axleby as a real company, give it real brand or executive names, or imply that any real company runs this landscape.

Axleby is a composite: it borrows *patterns* that are common in the industry, not any one company's history. In particular, Axleby is **not** a disguised VWE, Autotelex, Cox Automotive, CDK, Auto Trader, DAT or Carfax: it is pan-European (four markets), was assembled by acquisitions, and none of its product names, prices, numbers or incidents are any real company's. Do not give Axleby real product names (MijnVWE, Occasion Manager, Finnik, vAuto, Fortellis, Interdata, Marktinzicht, Co-Driver …). Real **suppliers and standards** may appear where this ledger names them (AWS, Google Cloud, Snowflake, Keycloak, OpenFGA, Datadog, SEPA, ISO 3779, ADF/XML, CloudEvents). Real **registers** (RDW, DIV, KBA, DVLA, DVSA) appear as the public bodies they are; Axleby's dealings with them are fictional, and no incident below is attributed to a real register — chapters say "the Dutch register's transaction channel" or "the register" when describing an Axleby incident, never "RDW was down". Data suppliers in incidents are fictional (Kilometra, DriveDesk).

## The group

- **Axleby Group B.V.** — a privately held vehicle-data and dealer-software group headquartered in **Utrecht, the Netherlands**. Majority owned since **2022** by **Harrowgate Capital** (a fictional private-equity fund); founders and management hold the rest.
- Engineering hubs: **Utrecht** (Axleby Data, Axleby Trade, group platform), **Ghent** (Lotwise), **Munich** (Wertlauf, valuation and market data), **Leeds** (Showroomly), **Kraków** (shared platform and SRE, opened 2024).
- **2026 figures (fiscal year = calendar year):** revenue **€214 million**; adjusted EBITDA about **€61 million**; about **1,150 employees**, of whom about **420** work in product and technology (about **300 engineers**).

## The 2027 situation — problems Axleby starts with

1. **Five products, three logins, two vehicle keys.** Lotwise, Showroomly, Axleby Data portal, Axleby Trade and Odolo each have their own customer accounts; Lotwise Classic keys vehicles by its own stock id plus plate, Axleby Data by plate (Connect XML) and AVK (Anchor).
2. **The vehicle dossier is split.** History reports, valuations and register transactions are ordered from different screens and stored in different systems; dealers re-type plates.
3. **Syndication drift.** Channel status is not reconciled; sold cars linger on portals (see the Zombie Golf).
4. **Two API stacks.** Connect XML (38% of calls, 210 XML-only clients) and JSON API v1, with different metering rules (see the No-Hit Credit).
5. **Licensing is tribal knowledge** until Charter (see the License Breach).
6. **Valuation drift** after the used-EV price fall (see the EV Overvaluation).

## The One Dossier program

Approved in **March 2027** by the board on a proposal from **Ruben Dekker** (CTO) and **Ines Marchetti** (Group Chief Architect); three-year horizon (**2027–2029**); budget **€38 million**.

- **Year 1 (2027–28): one dossier, one account.** Axleby ID and Gatekeeper; Anchor as the only vehicle key across products (AVK); the Lotwise Dossier shows history, valuation, condition and register transactions in one place; **Meterline** usage metering for every billable event; Charter in the request path of every data API.
- **Year 2–3 (2028–29): event-driven and market-driven.** One Dossier event backbone on MSK with an outbox in Lotwise Classic; Relay rebuilt as desired-state sync with reconciliation; JSON API v2 with webhooks; automated pricing signals; digital returns for lessors; Showroomly moved onto shared identity, leads and syndication.
- **Retirements:** Connect XML sunset announced for **31 December 2029**; Lotwise Classic reduced to a dossier-UI shell by end of 2029 (strangler).
- **Capability map v1** (March 2027, in `vehicle-capability-map`) and **v2** (December 2027, in `one-platform-from-many-products`).

## Recurring worked examples (amounts and dates are canonical)

1. **The Cloned Plate** (2026): two Audi A4s carried the same Dutch plate; Tracewell merged damage events of the clone into the genuine car's history; a consumer complaint through Odolo exposed it. Fix: VIN wins over plate in Anchor; plate assignments are time-bounded edges; reports show the VIN. (Owner: `vehicle-identity`.)
2. **The Zombie Golf** (**February 2027**): Velto sold a 2021 Volkswagen Golf at its Breda rooftop; the sold status from DriveDesk failed to sync; Relay's delete operations were queued behind failed updates; the car stayed live on **4** portals for **4 days**, producing **37** leads and **9** booked test drives. (Owner: `listing-syndication`; journey: `journey-inventory-to-portal`.)
3. **The Month-End Register Outage** (**31 March 2027**, last working day of the quarter): the Dutch register's transaction channel was unavailable to Axleby for **5 hours 40 minutes**; **3,100** transactions queued; the retry queue preserved order, but **214** were submitted twice after ambiguous timeouts and needed correction. (Owner: `registration-and-trade-services`; journey: `journey-sale-to-registration`; reliability: `reliability-and-dependencies`.)
4. **The Odometer Correction** (**May 2027**): a Kilometra batch recorded miles as kilometres for **2,870** UK-imported vehicles; corrected after **11 days**; by then **6,140** valuations, **412** history reports (**96** to consumers) and **1,180** live adverts carried wrong mileage. (Owner: `journey-data-correction`; provenance: `provenance-and-data-quality`.)
5. **The Cross-Tenant Lead** (**June 2027**): a cache key without tenant id in Showroomly's lead-inbox BFF showed **112** leads of one dealer to users of **3** other dealers for **47 minutes**; reported to the UK ICO as a personal-data breach. (Owner: `tenancy-and-dealer-groups`.)
6. **The Lost Leads of Velto** (**September 2027**): after Velto reorganized **46** rooftops into **9** regional hubs, routing rules sent three closed rooftops' leads to an inactive DriveDesk CRM queue that returned success but discarded them; **1,460** leads went unworked for **9 days**. (Owner: `journey-lead-to-dms`; leads: `leads-and-crm`.)
7. **The DriveDesk Outage** (**November 2027**): DriveDesk was hit by ransomware and offline for **12 days**; **1,900** Lotwise rooftops lost DMS sync; Lotwise served as the fallback for stock, adverts and sales documents. Industry context: the June 2024 CDK Global attack (documented, cite). (Owner: `dms-integration`; reliability: `reliability-and-dependencies`.)
8. **The EV Overvaluation** (**2026**): after used battery-electric prices fell, Vantage overvalued used BEVs by **9.5%** on average for **14 weeks** before drift monitoring caught it; Fleetmoor and two dealer groups complained. (Owner: `valuation-engine`.)
9. **The No-Hit Credit** (**2026**): the no-hit-no-fee rule was implemented differently in Connect XML and JSON API v1; Axleby credited **€186,000** to **41** API clients. (Owner: `billing-and-metering`.)
10. **The License Breach** (**January 2027**): a small lead-generation API client resold history-report data to a consumer website against its license; detected through a customer complaint, not monitoring; led to Charter. (Owner: `data-licensing-and-governance`.)
11. **The Plate Enumeration Attack** (**August 2027**): a scraper used **3,800** rotating IPs and leaked trial API keys to enumerate Odolo's free check and the JSON API, about **9 million** requests in 6 days. (Owner: `vehicle-platform-security`.)
