This journal is **my platform operating model, written down as records** — sixteen of them, one per source chapter checklist, covering both how internal platforms are built and run and the strategy that decides whether and why they should exist. If you build, fund, or depend on a platform in my organization, you should not have to guess what I think a good one looks like.

**What changes**

* **The charter is explicit**: this is an operating model, not a book report. Every record is written in first person, states what I commit to and what I hold platform work to, and names the conditions under which I would revise it.
* **Two books, two sections, one journal.** Camille Fournier and Ian Nowland's *Platform Engineering* grounds the ten engineering records — the four pillars, the team, the product mindset, operations, delivery, rearchitecting, stakeholders, success. Gregor Hohpe's *Platform Strategy* grounds the six strategy records — what platforms really are, and how to design, organize for, implement, and grow them. The sections answer different questions about the same thing — *how* versus *whether and why* — and the seams between them are cross-linked record to record.
* **The map is complete.** The introduction lists every record by section with links, so the whole model is navigable from one page.
* **Each record ships its working checklist.** The article carries the principle and the rationale; the Checklist tab carries the runnable self-assessment distilled from the source — the part you run against a real platform.
* **There is a reading path for each audience**: platform leads start with [[four-pillars]] and their rung of the lifecycle; application-team leads read what they are entitled to expect; my peers can read the highlight blockquotes across both sections and have the whole model in fifteen minutes.

**What it costs**

* Written commitments are inspectable: platform teams know what I will hold them to, and everyone else knows what they may expect — including the right to hear "no, that does not belong in the platform."
* The journal is a living document that must be maintained. Records start as `draft` — adopted from sources I trust ahead of being fully worn in — and move to `accepted` only as practice earns it, each on its own declared revisiting conditions.

**What we are not doing**

* Not summarizing either book — each record carries its own adapted material; this post is only the map, and both books remain worth reading in full.
* Not publishing a platform inventory or a statement about any specific internal platform — the records are the model, not the catalog.
* Not covering the sibling journals' ground: engineering management, people-management tools, and the executive operating model live in their own journals.

*The Article tab carries the full map and reading guide; every record's Checklist tab carries the part you run.*
