![Comic panel: Kai stands knee-deep in a swamp of tangled cables and duplicated tool crates while Vera watches from dry ground.](assets/images/what-is-platform-engineering/comic-01-the-swamp.jpeg)
**Panel 1:** *The hook: the over-general swamp — duplicated tools, per-application glue, systems too expensive to upgrade or leave. Nobody chose it.*

![Comic panel: three teams each hold a different tool while a tangle of pipes grows behind them; Vera comments.](assets/images/what-is-platform-engineering/comic-02-one-sensible-choice.jpeg)
**Panel 2:** *The problem: the swamp accumulates one sensible local choice at a time — until an ever-growing share of engineering time goes to glue and upgrades nobody owns.*

![Comic panel: Kai behind a ticket counter with a huge stack of tickets hands a custom scroll to one of many queued developers.](assets/images/what-is-platform-engineering/comic-03-the-ticket-bureau.jpeg)
**Panel 3:** *Wrong way one: the ticket-driven service bureau — bespoke work per request, headcount scaling with demand, no self-service product.*

![Comic panel: developers nod at a megaphone proclaiming a standard, then walk a hidden path around the gate; Vera watches.](assets/images/what-is-platform-engineering/comic-04-the-mandate.jpeg)
**Panel 4:** *Wrong way two: adoption by decree — the standard is announced, teams comply on paper, and the real work routes around it.*

![Comic panel: Kai presents a tidy self-service counter with a few labeled capability boxes and a Not Supported sign; Vera approves.](assets/images/what-is-platform-engineering/comic-05-an-internal-product.jpeg)
**Panel 5:** *The principle: the platform is an internal product — curated, self-service, clear boundaries — and saying what it does not support is the defining act.*

![Comic panel: Kai lowers a smooth deck over churning machinery while developers build calmly on top.](assets/images/what-is-platform-engineering/comic-06-hide-not-relocate.jpeg)
**Panel 6:** *How it plays out: abstractions hide complexity instead of relocating it — which is what makes 'you build it, you run it' a fair ask.*

![Comic panel: Kai swaps an engine beneath the deck with a hoist while app houses above stay lit and undisturbed.](assets/images/what-is-platform-engineering/comic-07-migrate-once.jpeg)
**Panel 7:** *The economics: migrations done once, centrally, with tooling and dependency metadata — instead of dozens of teams rediscovering the same move.*

![Comic panel: Kai lifts a huge block of developer teams with a lever labeled Platform while Vera shows a rising adoption chart.](assets/images/what-is-platform-engineering/comic-08-the-leverage-test.jpeg)
**Panel 8:** *The closer: the one test — a small platform team makes a much larger organization measurably more productive, and adoption grows because teams choose it.*
