![Comic panel: an engineer unrolls a features-only roadmap while an executive lifts a rug hiding lumps of other work.](assets/images/planning-and-delivery/comic-01-the-feature-only-plan.jpeg)
**Panel 1:** *The hook: a plan that only lists features is a plan to disappoint — the other work exists whether the plan admits it or not.*

![Comic panel: toll collectors labeled KTLO, mandates, and improvements take slices of a capacity pie, leaving a sliver for features.](assets/images/planning-and-delivery/comic-02-reality-collects-its-tax.jpeg)
**Panel 2:** *The problem: KTLO, mandates, and system improvements are real all along — a feature-only budget just makes them invisible, unfunded, and late.*

![Comic panel: engineers row a boat labeled year two through fog with a blank flag and oars pointing different ways.](assets/images/planning-and-delivery/comic-03-never-quite-about-anything.jpeg)
**Panel 3:** *The wrong way: the multi-year project that was never quite about anything — no written problem, no definition of done, just drift.*

![Comic panel: Vera holds a one-page proposal with problem, options, and done sections while two people sign it.](assets/images/planning-and-delivery/comic-04-proposal-before-project.jpeg)
**Panel 4:** *The principle, part one: no long-running project starts without a written proposal — problem first, options with trade-offs, a measurable 'done', and buy-in before the first commit.*

![Comic panel: four labeled pipes fill one capacity tank while an engineer holds a valve with a gauge marked forty percent.](assets/images/planning-and-delivery/comic-05-four-categories-one-budget.jpeg)
**Panel 5:** *The principle, part two: the roadmap is built bottom-up from all four work categories — with KTLO measured from history and capped near 40%.*

![Comic panel: a bridge built span by span with customers already walking on finished spans as an engineer places the next one.](assets/images/planning-and-delivery/comic-06-milestones-reality-votes.jpeg)
**Panel 6:** *How it plays out: value-bearing milestones — monthly in year one — give reality a regular vote, so scope creep and dead ends surface early.*

![Comic panel: Kai helps a team carry moving boxes through a launch ribbon at the midpoint of a road while Vera points toward an adoption signpost.](assets/images/planning-and-delivery/comic-07-launch-is-the-middle.jpeg)
**Panel 7:** *How it plays out: launch is a milestone, not the goal — migration and adoption are first-class, staffed project work.*

![Comic panel: an engineer hands over a short wins-and-challenges note while a messy ticket chart sits in a wastebasket.](assets/images/planning-and-delivery/comic-08-wins-and-challenges.jpeg)
**Panel 8:** *The cost worth paying: narration is part of delivery — biweekly wins and challenges, situation–action–result, written for the people who read them.*

![Comic panel close-up: Vera shows a notebook sketch of a balanced capacity pie while Kai holds the signed proposal.](assets/images/planning-and-delivery/comic-09-forecast-not-a-wish.jpeg)
**Panel 9:** *The closer: honest capacity accounting turns the plan from a wish into a forecast — and honest narration proves what it produced.*
