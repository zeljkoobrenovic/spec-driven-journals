![Comic panel: an engineer unrolls a very long cloud invoice with a huge circled total while an executive asks who saw it coming.](assets/images/cost-performance-scalability/comic-01-the-month-end-surprise.jpeg)
**Panel 1:** *The hook: the month-end surprise — every cost spike discovered on the invoice, weeks after it started.*

![Comic panel: a single giant invoice on a finance desk while unaware developer teams work in the background without any price tags.](assets/images/cost-performance-scalability/comic-02-the-invisible-bill.jpeg)
**Panel 2:** *The problem: one org-level invoice and no attribution — cost stays finance's problem, and nobody who could fix it ever sees it.*

![Comic panel: an engineer with giant scissors beside a tiny cut-down server with a discount tag while a latency gauge points into the red.](assets/images/cost-performance-scalability/comic-03-broken-at-a-discount.jpeg)
**Panel 3:** *The wrong way: the lowest bid — a cheaper platform that misses its latency target has not been optimized, it has been broken at a discount.*

![Comic panel: an executive at a whiteboard showing a balance scale weighing an SLO shield against a coin stack.](assets/images/cost-performance-scalability/comic-04-slos-before-savings.jpeg)
**Panel 4:** *The principle: SLOs before savings — the question is never 'what is cheapest' but 'what is the cheapest way to meet the SLO the business needs.'*

![Comic panel: a giant invoice splitting into many small labeled price tags landing on different team desks.](assets/images/cost-performance-scalability/comic-05-every-price-tag-an-owner.jpeg)
**Panel 5:** *How it plays out: labels and showback turn one invoice into hundreds of small, ownable numbers — and the team that sees its own number is the team that fixes it.*

![Comic panel: an engineer walking a circular track with stations for baseline, rightsize, scale, and measure.](assets/images/cost-performance-scalability/comic-06-the-standing-loop.jpeg)
**Panel 6:** *The loop: baseline, rightsize, scale, measure — then run it again.*

![Comic panel: a load-testing machine fires traffic at duplicating server pods while an SLO gauge stays green.](assets/images/cost-performance-scalability/comic-07-autoscaling-earns-trust.jpeg)
**Panel 7:** *How it plays out: autoscaling earns trust by being load-tested against the SLO — the difference between having autoscaling and hoping for it.*

![Comic panel: an engineer holds a cost-drive trophy while a hedge of money-shaped leaves regrows wildly behind him.](assets/images/cost-performance-scalability/comic-08-the-savings-project.jpeg)
**Panel 8:** *The cost: the savings project with an end date — the waste grows back, because the forces that created it were never put under a standing loop.*

![Comic panel: a developer views their workload's cost dashboard with an alert bell while an unopened invoice envelope waits in the corner.](assets/images/cost-performance-scalability/comic-09-before-the-invoice-does.jpeg)
**Panel 9:** *The closer: the test that never stops running — can any engineer see what their workload costs today, and does a bad cost decision get caught before month-end?*
