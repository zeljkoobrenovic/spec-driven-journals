![Comic panel: Kai shows off a poster claiming developers love the platform while Vera asks whether that is measured or assumed.](assets/images/devex-evaluation/comic-01-measured-or-assumed.jpeg)
**Panel 1:** *The hook: every platform team believes developers love their platform — the question is whether that claim is measured or assumed.*

![Comic panel: a platform engineer demos a flawless pipeline while workaround sticky notes and spare keyboards hide under the desk, and Vera tells the audience their hands know the workarounds.](assets/images/devex-evaluation/comic-02-the-demo-day-platform.jpeg)
**Panel 2:** *The problem: the demo-day platform — the journey works flawlessly when the platform team drives, because their hands skip the friction.*

![Comic panel: Kai holds a fully ticked feature checklist while a developer is stranded between gapped stepping stones holding a ticket.](assets/images/devex-evaluation/comic-03-the-capability-inventory-v2.jpeg)
**Panel 3:** *The wrong way: the capability inventory — every feature can be present while the seams between them still cost three tickets and a YAML archaeology session.*

![Comic panel: Vera shows a demo application traveling the full road from code change to production while Kai records a baseline on a clipboard.](assets/images/devex-evaluation/comic-04-the-honest-instrument.jpeg)
**Panel 4:** *The principle: deploy a realistic demo application through the entire journey — and record the baseline: friction, DORA metrics, efficiency, satisfaction, impact.*

![Comic panel: a developer feeds two cards labeled name and Git repo into a machine that outputs a complete monitored HTTPS application.](assets/images/devex-evaluation/comic-05-minimal-inputs.jpeg)
**Panel 5:** *How it plays out: an application name and a Git repository in — a running, scaled, monitored, HTTPS-served application out. Every ticket in between is a named defect.*

![Comic panel: a car with safety gear left in the trunk versus a car with everything installed, as Vera points at the second.](assets/images/devex-evaluation/comic-06-the-optional-seatbelt.jpeg)
**Panel 6:** *The defaults decide: probes, limits, scanning, TLS, observability — if they are opt-in, the median application runs fragile, dark, and exposed.*

![Comic panel: Kai triggers a failure and compares a cryptic error screen with a plain-language error panel that has a rollback button.](assets/images/devex-evaluation/comic-07-break-it-on-purpose.jpeg)
**Panel 7:** *Break it on purpose: errors must read as human guidance at the point of failure — and rollback must be discoverable, tested, and ticket-free.*

![Comic panel: a new developer works alone behind glass while Kai sweats and resists helping and Vera writes notes, saying no hints, just write down the friction.](assets/images/devex-evaluation/comic-08-fresh-eyes-v2.jpeg)
**Panel 8:** *The final examiner: a developer who has never seen the platform, observed without handholding — everything else is the platform team grading its own homework.*

![Comic panel: Vera and Kai at a circular deploy-measure-improve-repeat diagram with friction bars shrinking each cycle.](assets/images/devex-evaluation/comic-09-the-loop-reopens.jpeg)
**Panel 9:** *The closer: one evaluation is an audit; the repeating loop — improve, re-measure, compare, attack the highest friction — is the operating practice.*
