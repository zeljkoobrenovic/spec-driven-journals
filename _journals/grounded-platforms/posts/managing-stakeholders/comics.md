![Comic panel: Kai presents a platform diagram while Vera points at distant executives holding a money bag and an off switch.](assets/images/managing-stakeholders/comic-01-they-decide-your-fate.jpeg)
**Panel 1:** *The hook: a platform does not set its own budget, grant its own mandate, or vote on its own survival — stakeholders do.*

![Comic panel: Kai awkwardly introduces himself to an angry executive in an escalation meeting while Vera watches from the doorway.](assets/images/managing-stakeholders/comic-02-escalation-day-introduction.jpeg)
**Panel 2:** *The problem: the trust you need in the storm can only be built in the calm — the escalation meeting is too late for introductions.*

![Comic panel: Kai stamps yes on a conveyor of requests while a huge pile of accepted tickets teeters behind him.](assets/images/managing-stakeholders/comic-03-the-soft-yes.jpeg)
**Panel 3:** *The wrong way: the soft yes — accept everything to avoid conflict, then fail to deliver the pile and convert comfort into distrust.*

![Comic panel: Vera places stakeholder magnets on a power–interest grid while Kai allocates time tokens to the top quadrant.](assets/images/managing-stakeholders/comic-04-the-power-interest-grid.jpeg)
**Panel 4:** *The principle: map stakeholders by power and interest — manage closely, keep satisfied, keep informed, monitor — and spend scarce relationship time accordingly.*

![Comic panel: Vera offers a small outcome-risk-next-step card while Kai unrolls a huge architecture blueprint.](assets/images/managing-stakeholders/comic-05-the-right-altitude.jpeg)
**Panel 5:** *How it plays out: decide the takeaway first — outcomes, risks, next steps — with detail on request; engineering detail never fixes a trust problem.*

![Comic panel: Kai catches a floating verbal promise in a notebook and pins it to a board of tracked commitments.](assets/images/managing-stakeholders/comic-06-no-promise-in-the-air.jpeg)
**Panel 6:** *How it plays out: capture every agreement in writing, review what is outstanding, and close every loop — no promise lives only in the air.*

![Comic panel: Kai offers a stakeholder a not-yet card and an alternative card, with a crossed-out fortress and vending machine behind.](assets/images/managing-stakeholders/comic-07-a-structured-no.jpeg)
**Panel 7:** *The trade-off: not every request gets a yes — but a no with reasons in the stakeholder's terms, plus an alternative, is a disagreement about priorities, not a rejection of the person.*

![Comic panel: Vera lowers Kai's toy sword and examines a duct-taped shadow platform with a magnifying glass showing an unmet-need tag.](assets/images/managing-stakeholders/comic-08-shadow-platforms-are-feedback.jpeg)
**Panel 8:** *Also this: a shadow platform is a signal — unmet urgency, novel demand, or broken collaboration — and the right response is diagnosis, not suppression.*

![Comic panel: Kai holds a protect-reduce-stop list in a calm office while a storm cloud looms outside the window.](assets/images/managing-stakeholders/comic-09-ready-before-the-storm.jpeg)
**Panel 9:** *The closer: arrive at budget season with your own cut proposal — what to protect, reduce, or stop — decided in the calm, before the storm asks.*
