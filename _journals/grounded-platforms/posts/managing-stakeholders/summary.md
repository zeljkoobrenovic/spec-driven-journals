A platform's stakeholders decide its budget, its mandate, and its survival in a downturn — so I treat stakeholder management as **first-order platform leadership work**, not politics and not something to start when the escalation arrives. The whole record rests on one asymmetry: relationships, commitments, and business framing are built in the calm so they hold in the storm.

**What changes**

* Every platform leader in my organization **maps stakeholders by power and interest** before spending relationship time: manage closely, keep satisfied, keep informed, monitor — and redraws the map as the organization changes, because relationship time allocated by last year's map is misallocated.
* Communication moves to **business altitude**: takeaway decided first, outcomes, risks, and next steps leading, detail on request. 1:1 cadence scales with the quadrant — roughly monthly for manage-closely, quarterly for the rest — and communication turns **up** during rough patches, until they have genuinely passed.
* **Every commitment gets captured**: private notes, short written follow-ups that invite correction, regular review of what is outstanding, and a closed loop when work completes, slips, or changes. No promise lives only in the air.
* Requests get structure instead of reflexes: understand the underlying business need first, then a **yes with explicit compromises** (smaller first version, limited scope, negotiated timing) or a **no that preserves the relationship** — no distinguished from not yet, the constraint explained in the stakeholder's terms, alternatives offered, escalation when I lack the authority to hold the line.
* Shadow platforms are **diagnosed as signals** of unmet need rather than fought as insubordination, and budget season arrives prepared: work grouped into team-sized chunks, tied to protected business outcomes, with my own cut proposal ready before executives make one for me.

**What it costs**

* Deliberate relationship work in calm periods, exactly when it feels least urgent — the grid exists because that time is scarce and must be spent unevenly.
* Structured nos and honest not-yets cost short-term comfort; the soft yes is cheaper today and converts into long-term distrust.
* Budget-defense preparation that may never be used — knowing what I would protect, reduce, or stop before anyone asks.

**What we are not doing**

* Not the product discipline — discovering what customers need and shaping the roadmap is [[platform-as-a-product]]; this record covers the relationships and negotiations around that roadmap.
* Not planning mechanics — those live in [[planning-and-delivery]]; this record governs how plans are communicated, defended, and renegotiated with the people who fund them.
* Not a communication style guide — the postures and disciplines are fixed here; templates and update formats remain team choices.
