# Spec: Managing Platform Stakeholders

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State how I manage stakeholders as a platform leader: map them by power and
interest before spending relationship time, build trust before I need it,
communicate at the altitude of business impact, capture every commitment,
and answer requests with a structured yes-with-compromises or a
no-that-preserves-the-relationship. The post turns the "Managing
Stakeholders" chapter of Fournier & Nowland's *Platform Engineering* into an
operating principle: a platform's stakeholders decide its budget, its
mandate, and its survival in a downturn, so stakeholder management is
platform-leadership work of the first order — not politics, and not
something to start when the escalation arrives. The load-bearing idea:
relationships, commitments, and business framing are built in the calm so
they hold in the storm.

## Audience

Platform leaders in my organization (engineering and product) who own
stakeholder relationships; their managers, who I expect to hold them to this
model; peer executives who are themselves stakeholders of our platforms and
should know what treatment to expect. First-person declarative.

## Success criteria

- [x] **Principle is quotable** — highlight states that stakeholders decide
      the platform's fate, that I map them by power and interest, invest in
      relationships before crises, communicate in business terms, track
      every commitment, and say yes-with-compromises or a
      relationship-preserving no.
- [x] **The mapping discipline survives** — power–interest grid with its
      four postures (manage closely, keep satisfied, keep informed,
      monitor), plus revisiting the map as the organization changes.
- [x] **The communication discipline survives** — decide the takeaway
      first, lead with outcomes and risks, right level of detail, business
      impact made visible, 1:1 cadence proportional to the quadrant,
      more communication during rough patches.
- [x] **The commitment discipline survives** — private notes, written
      follow-ups, reviewing outstanding commitments, closing the loop, no
      uncaptured promises.
- [x] **The yes/no discipline survives** — understand the underlying
      business need, smaller first versions, explicit scope, no versus not
      yet, alternatives offered, escalation when authority is lacking.
- [x] **Shadow platforms and budget pressure survive** — shadow platforms
      read as signals and managed constructively; budget defense prepared in
      advance with team-sized chunks and my own cut proposal.
- [x] **Credit is explicit** — References name Fournier & Nowland's
      *Platform Engineering* and the stakeholder-management chapter.
