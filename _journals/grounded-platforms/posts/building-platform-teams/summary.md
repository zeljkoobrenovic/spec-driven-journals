A great platform team is a deliberately mixed team: people who can write substantial production code alongside people with broad systems and operational expertise, with both kinds of work treated as equally valuable. I staff explicitly against the two cultural failure modes that kill platform teams — the "too much systems" culture that piles up automation and manual process where engineering could remove the problem, and the "too much development" culture that chases new architecture while production burns.

**What changes**

* **Composition is a decision, not an accident.** Every platform team mixes software and systems expertise by design, reliability expertise is added where reliability is a real organizational need, and deep specialists stay few, stay close to the systems that need them, and contribute beyond their specialty.
* **Both failure modes get named tests.** "Too much systems": does the team build abstractions that permanently reduce operational complexity, or glue backed by rules — and does it improve the platform or blame the user when something breaks? "Too much development": maintenance counts as valuable engineering, technical debt is not "someone else's code", and every engineer understands their systems in production.
* **Interviews mirror the actual job**: platform design rather than only application design, an inverted design interview on a real system the candidate built, coding that opens up testing and operational constraints, behavioral evidence from incidents and ambiguity — and customer empathy assessed explicitly, because internal users are customers of the platform.
* **Careers reward what the platform needs.** Ladders stay shared where practical, and promotion evidence counts adopted tools, support quality, incident leadership, and reduced operational burden — not lines of code or launch counts.
* **Leadership carries the balance**: managers who have operated business-critical systems and defend a deliberate delivery pace; platform-aware PMs added at scale; TPMs and supporting roles only when genuinely required; one shared roadmap across development and operational work.

**What it costs**

* Deliberate composition is slower and harder than hiring whoever applies — and a bespoke, calibrated interview loop is real ongoing investment.
* Rewarding operational and support work equally means some traditional prestige signals (launches, new architecture) carry less promotion weight.
* Holding roles to a demonstrated-need bar means living with gaps — no speculative specialists, PMs, or TPMs before the need is shown.

**What we are not doing**

* Not deciding when a platform team is justified or what it does first — that is [[getting-started]].
* Not the operational discipline itself ([[operating-platforms]]) or the full product operating mode ([[platform-as-a-product]]) — this record staffs the team so it can carry both.
* Not a compensation or leveling policy — ladders and pay bands stay with the wider organization; this record constrains only how platform work is evaluated within them.
