A platform grows **adoption**, not merely functionality — and an internal platform nobody is forced to use lives or dies on voluntary adoption, so growth is a product discipline, not a feature race. Growth runs along three balanced dimensions — reach, breadth, and depth — through a product lifecycle of Explore, Expand, and Extract, and it is largely decided in the new user's first hour.

**What changes**

* Adoption becomes the growth metric. Features are what we spend; adoption is what we earn — a roadmap celebrating shipped capabilities while usage stays flat is a platform in denial.
* Reach (who adopts), breadth (how much of the problem space is covered), and depth (how complete and polished the key capabilities are) are balanced deliberately. Building the perfect platform for one customer before broader adoption is the named failure mode.
* Each lifecycle phase gets its own test: Explore validates that users *value* the platform, not merely that the technology works; Expand removes adoption obstacles through self-service and documentation; Extract improves the economics at scale. Phase transitions trigger an explicit check on whether team, skills, and leadership still fit.
* The on-ramp is engineered as a first-class feature: minimize the initial cliff, build on familiar concepts, default the complicated parameters, give useful feedback on failure — and avoid the hockey-stick experience where simple tasks are easy but the first advanced need takes a week and an expert.
* Every request is triaged on business impact × roadmap fit: prioritize, investigate, backlog, or decline — with the principles behind a decline explained, because an unexplained "no" spends trust. The roadmap is published and credible.
* We scale **down** as deliberately as up: vertical tiers and horizontal slices on shared assets give smaller customers an appropriately sized way in without forking the platform.

**What it costs**

* Saying no to real customers, repeatedly and in writing — cohesion is bought with declined requests.
* Studying non-users, not just the biased sample of existing users, before calling the roadmap demand-driven.
* Measuring time-to-first-useful-result and treating regressions as bugs — on-ramp work that never shows up in a feature list.

**What we are not doing**

* Not the product operating mode itself — that is [[platform-as-a-product]]; this record is the growth discipline inside it.
* Not the self-service machinery — the on-ramp this record demands is built in [[implementing-platforms]]; what success ultimately looks like lives in [[platform-success]].
* Not a mandate policy — the record's whole premise is voluntary adoption; forcing adoption by decree is out of scope.
