## 1. Legal responsibilities

- [ ] Know your legal responsibilities as a manager in the jurisdiction(s) in which you operate

## 2. You and individual reports

- [ ] Hold regular 1:1s on a weekly or biweekly cadence
- [ ] Rarely reschedule them
- [ ] Build agendas that include contributions from both sides
- [ ] Take notes during the 1:1
- [ ] Capture agreed-upon actions
- [ ] Set quarterly goals and track them together
- [ ] Hold bidirectional feedback sessions every three to six months
- [ ] Hold more formal performance conversations every 3, 6, or 12 months (shorten the cycle the faster your company is changing)
- [ ] Make sure each performance conversation covers the results the person achieved (or didn't)
- [ ] Make sure each performance conversation also covers *how* the person did or did not achieve those results

## 3. You and your teams

- [ ] Hold weekly or biweekly team meetings
- [ ] Show up on time
- [ ] Avoid rescheduling team meetings
- [ ] Confirm the team benefits from the time together — aligning on plans, making decisions, or workshopping challenges to help one another get the work done

## 4. You and your division

- [ ] Maintain a job description for each role you hire for
- [ ] Maintain an interview rubric for each role you hire for
- [ ] Maintain job ladders and levels
- [ ] Communicate ladders and levels clearly to employees
- [ ] Maintain a compensation philosophy
- [ ] Maintain a clear compensation framework that employees understand

## 5. You

- [ ] Understand your own job description
- [ ] Understand the function of your own team
- [ ] Have quarterly goals agreed with your own manager
- [ ] Hold 1:1s with your own manager on a regular cadence
- [ ] Can access regular context from your manager or organizational leaders on overall company priorities and goals
