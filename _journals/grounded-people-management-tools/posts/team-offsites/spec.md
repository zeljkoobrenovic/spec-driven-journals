# Spec: Team Offsites

> Working doc for the post in this folder. Lives next to `index.md`, versioned
> in git, surfaced as a "View spec" link in the rendered post. The spec drives
> the post; the post is the artifact.

## Intent

State how I run a team offsite as a high-cost meeting that has to earn its
cost, not as a reward or a change of scenery. The post turns the workbook's
"Planning and Running Your Offsite" tool into an operating principle: a
five-stage planning timeline running backward from the offsite date (1+
month, 1 month, 1 week, 1 day, day-of, after), a fixed five-part day
structure (welcome, check-in, icebreaker, sessions, check-out), a named DRI
per agenda section, pre-work and pre-reads distributed in advance, and a
closing discipline — feedback form plus tracked action items — that turns
the offsite into decisions rather than a nice day out.

## Audience

Managers and leads in my organization planning or running a team or
leadership offsite (so they have a timeline and a template, not a blank
page); their offsite DRIs (so they know what owning a session means); peer
executives comparing operating models for how offsites should work.
First-person declarative.

## Success criteria

- [ ] **Principle is quotable** — highlight states that an offsite is a
      high-cost meeting: it gets goals, a DRI per section, pre-work, and
      tracked action items, or it does not happen.
- [ ] **The timeline survives** — all five checklist stages (1+ month, 1
      month, 1 week, 1 day, day-of, after) with their concrete items
      preserved in the Checklist tab.
- [ ] **The DRI mechanic is explicit** — every agenda section has a named
      facilitator assigned at the 1-month mark, not improvised on the day.
- [ ] **The five-part day structure survives** — welcome, check-in,
      icebreaker, sessions, check-out, in that order.
- [ ] **The welcome-email template survives** — purpose line plus logistics
      block, reproduced in the Checklist tab.
- [ ] **The worked agenda example survives** — session, time, length, and
      facilitator columns intact as a table.
- [ ] **Credit is explicit** — References name Claire Hughes Johnson's
      *Scaling People* companion workbooks; no named external contributor
      is credited by the workbook for this specific tool.

## Non-goals

- Not [[team-charter]] — that record covers the team's durable written
  identity; this one covers a single planned event on the calendar.
- Not [[writing-okrs]] — objectives get *finalized* in the worked agenda's
  Session 4, but this record does not define how OKRs are written.
- Not a general meeting-hygiene record — [[meetings]] (engineering journal)
  covers day-to-day meeting discipline; this one is specific to the
  higher-cost, off-calendar offsite format.
- No claim that an offsite substitutes for the weekly operating cadence —
  it is a periodic, high-cost complement to it, not a replacement.

## Decision log

- **2026-07-28** — This record is grounded in Claire Hughes Johnson,
  *Scaling People: Tactics for Management and Company Building* (Stripe
  Press, 2023), the companion workbooks' "Planning and Running Your
  Offsite" tool (pp. 93–95): the planning checklist, the day-of-offsite
  templates, and the worked agenda example. Unlike several adjacent tools in
  this journal (OKRs, QBRs, interview questions), the workbook does not
  credit a named external contributor for this tool, so references cite
  Johnson and the workbooks directly.
- **2026-07-28** — Framed the record around cost: an offsite is expensive in
  people-time and calendar disruption, so the operating principle is that
  every offsite pays for itself through a named goal, a DRI per session,
  distributed pre-work, and tracked follow-through — the checklist is what
  makes a "great offsite" reproducible rather than a matter of luck or a
  strong facilitator.
