# Spec: The Interview Loop

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State how I design interview loops: the whole loop — competency → stage →
question → follow-ups → rubric — is designed before the first interview,
and the workbook's recruiter-role example (a level-2+ recruiter) is the
pattern I copy for every role. The post carries the operating principle;
the Checklist tab is the "design your loop" working tool: the reusable
pattern, rubric-quality tests, and the worked recruiter loop condensed as
reference tables — seven named competencies mapped to a 30-minute recruiter
screen, a 45-minute team screen run in tandem with a written project
(two samples, 1–3 scoring on structure/grammar/tone, pass 7–9, second
reviewer 5–6, fail 3–4), and four 45-minute onsites including a
hiring-manager intake roleplay, every question with Situation/Action/Result
follow-ups and a written rubric.

## Audience

Hiring managers and interviewers in my organization (so they know a loop is
designed, not improvised, and what a complete stage definition contains);
recruiters partnering with me (so they see the pattern their own loops are
built on); peer executives comparing hiring operating models. First-person
declarative.

## Success criteria

- [x] **Principle is quotable** — highlight states that the loop is designed
      before the first interview and that competency → stage → question →
      follow-ups → rubric is the fixed chain.
- [x] **The worked example survives condensed** — seven competencies, the
      stage map, per-stage timings (30-minute screen, 45-minute team screen
      and onsites), and the intake-roleplay three-part structure appear as
      reference tables, not a 23-page transcription.
- [x] **The numbers survive** — written-project scoring 1–3 per area across
      three areas, pass 7–9, second reviewer 5–6, fail 3–4; 2–3 days to
      submit; 35–45 minutes of questions plus 5–10 for candidate questions;
      rubric anchors like 4–6 unique searches and ~7 hires per quarter.
- [x] **Rubrics are the load-bearing idea** — every question ships with a
      Poor/Good/Strong (or Poor/Weak/Good/Strong) rubric written before any
      candidate answers, and the checklist includes rubric-quality tests.
- [x] **The checklist is a design tool** — it walks the reader through
      designing a loop for their own role, with the recruiter loop as
      reference, and is runnable without the article.
- [x] **Credit is explicit** — How to Read This and References name Claire
      Hughes Johnson's *Scaling People* companion workbooks as the source of
      the framework and the worked example.

## Non-goals

- Not [[interview-question-bank]] — that record is the reusable question
  library; this one is the loop architecture the questions slot into.
- Not [[hiring-written-exercises]] — that record covers written exercises in
  depth; this one only places the written project inside the loop and keeps
  its scoring bands.
- Not [[candidate-review]] — that record covers the debrief and
  decision-making after the loop runs; this one ends when the interviews do.
- Not a sourcing or pipeline record — diversity sourcing appears here only
  as an interview question with a rubric, not as a sourcing strategy.
