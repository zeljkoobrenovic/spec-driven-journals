# Spec: Career Conversations

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State why I run a one-off, 60-minute career conversation with every report —
30–45 minutes for someone fresh out of school — and why it is separate from
the weekly 1:1 and the performance review. The post turns the *Scaling
People* workbook's Career Conversations tool into an operating principle: a
pre-conversation that announces the plan up front and sends a goals reminder
exactly one day before (early enough to orient, late enough that no one
over-prepares), a "walk me through your history" conversation structure that
moves from childhood and school through the first post-college choice,
favorite and least-favorite jobs, and a five-years-out projection framed as
type of work rather than title, and a wrap-up that turns the conversation
into two or three jointly written development goals I reference later. The
load-bearing idea: I cannot develop someone whose motivations I know only
from their last performance cycle — one deliberate hour on their whole arc
pays back for years.

## Audience

Managers in my organization (so they know this conversation is a standard
tool, not an occasional nicety); the people who will have this conversation
with me or their own manager (so they know what to expect and why no prep is
required); peer executives comparing operating models. First-person
declarative.

## Success criteria

- [x] **Principle is quotable** — highlight states that I cannot develop
      someone whose motivations I know only from their last performance
      cycle, and that one deliberate hour on their whole arc pays back for
      years.
- [x] **The mechanics survive** — 60 minutes (30–45 for someone fresh out of
      school), announce the plan up front in a team meeting or 1:1, send the
      goals reminder exactly one day before (not earlier), no prep required,
      résumé optional.
- [x] **The conversation structure survives** — "walk me through your
      history": where they grew up (without prying), school and why, the
      first post-college choice and why, favorite and least favorite jobs
      and how each role was chosen, then the five-years-out projection by
      type of work (management, research, execution, leadership,
      communication, analytics, problem-solving) rather than by title.
- [x] **The "why" discipline is explicit** — ask why a lot, take notes,
      surface patterns together rather than diagnose alone.
- [x] **The wrap-up survives** — recap, share notes, jointly write two or
      three development goals, and reference the conversation later when the
      person faces a real choice about projects or delegation.
- [x] **Credit is explicit** — References name Claire Hughes Johnson's
      *Scaling People* and its companion workbooks.

## Non-goals

- Not [[performance-reviews]] — that record covers the recurring written
  evaluation of current performance; this one is a one-off conversation
  about a person's whole history and future direction, run once, not on a
  review cadence.
- Not the weekly 1:1 — this conversation feeds context into every future
  1:1, but it is a single dedicated session, not a recurring meeting format.
- Not [[coaching]] (product journal) — that record covers the ongoing
  assessment-plan-1:1 loop; this one is the single conversation that gives
  the coach the motivational context to make that loop personal.
- No claim that the five-year projection is a commitment — the workbook is
  explicit that no one is held to a five-year plan; it is a reference point,
  not a contract.
