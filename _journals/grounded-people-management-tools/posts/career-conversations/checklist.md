*The working tool behind this record. The Article tab carries the rationale and anti-patterns.*

## 1. Setup

- [ ] Schedule a one-off 1:1 with each report: 60 minutes (30–45 minutes for someone fresh out of school)
- [ ] Announce the plan before the invite lands — at a team meeting, or by email / in a 1:1 for a single new person
- [ ] Frame it as a get-to-know-you conversation — say plainly that these are among your favorite conversations: their past career moves and a start on future development goals
- [ ] State explicitly: no preparation required beyond being ready to talk through their history; a résumé is optional

## 2. Reminder — exactly one day before

- [ ] Send the goals reminder the day before the meeting, not earlier (earlier invites over-preparation)
- [ ] Restate the goals: I mainly want to get to know you; we'll focus on the choices you made and what motivated them
- [ ] Note that toward the end we'll project out five years — not a title, but what the general work and day-to-day might look like
- [ ] Say plainly that no one is held to a five-year plan; it becomes a reference point for later choices

## 3. The conversation — "walk me through your history"

Follow the person's thread rather than a script. Actively listen, take notes for future reference, and ask "why" a lot. The aim: what motivates them, why they made their choices, what they think of those choices now, what they haven't explored or want to return to.

- [ ] Early background — keep to what the person volunteers; if reluctant, narrow to plain facts: location, schooling, early interests
- [ ] School — what interested them, why that school, why that course of study
- [ ] Right after school — what they did and why; where they thought they'd end up, and whether they did (early motivations tend to persist — linger here)
- [ ] Favorite and least favorite roles — how each role was actually chosen; what they learned about work they're good at and enjoy versus work they find hard
- [ ] Work up to the present, dig into telling details, and surface patterns together
- [ ] Five years out — type of work, not title: management, research, execution, leadership, communication, analytics, problem-solving, consulting; identify what exposure they still need

## 4. Wrap-up

- [ ] Recap what you heard and share your notes with the person
- [ ] Jointly write down 2–3 development goals to track together
- [ ] File the notes where you will actually re-read them

## 5. Afterward

- [ ] Reference the conversation in later 1:1s when the person faces choices — which project to take, what to delegate
- [ ] Weigh those choices against the five-year direction, not only this quarter's needs
- [ ] Feed the development goals into the coaching plan and the next performance conversation
- [ ] On a manager change, hand the notes and goals over as part of the transition
