*The working tool behind this record. The Article tab carries the rationale and anti-patterns.*

## 1. Before the loop

- [ ] Confirm every interviewer is drawing from this shared bank, not improvising their own questions
- [ ] Confirm the loop mixes all three question types: situational, behavioral, and strict competency
- [ ] Assign the decision-making rubric question to at least one interviewer, typically for a leadership-focused slot
- [ ] Remind interviewers that each question's "seeking" note is the scoring key, not disclosed to the candidate

## 2. Working with others

**Q: Tell me about a time you worked on something that you knew you wouldn't enjoy but which was necessary for the success of your team or company.**
- [ ] Follow-up: How did you end up working on it? What did you accomplish? What would you do differently if you had another chance?
- [ ] Seeking: An eager attitude and an ability to get up to speed and execute regardless of feelings toward the work. Bonus if they actively volunteered to take it on.

**Q: Tell me about the lowest-morale team you've been part of.**
- [ ] Seeking: Did they bring levity to the group? Did they try to fix the situation? Did they run away?

**Q: When have you felt most connected to your coworkers? Tell me about a time when that connection was tested and how you adapted.**
- [ ] Seeking: Good humor and a positive, productive attitude while in the trenches with others. Support and collaboration in difficult situations. Valuing results-oriented team achievement and learning from coworkers.

## 3. Getting work done

**Q: What project are you most proud of? What was your contribution?**
- [ ] Seeking: Do they seek out challenging work? Do they value navigating an unfamiliar experience? Did this project have a strong impact?

**Q: Tell me about a project where you've had to pull out all the stops to get it done.**
- [ ] Seeking: Can they discuss the specifics of the obstacles they had to overcome and how they influenced others to do excellent work?

**Q: Tell me about a project you've led that failed. What role did you play in the project's failure? What did you learn? What would you do differently if given the chance to do it over?**
- [ ] Seeking: Accountability, a growth mindset, an ability to self-reflect and be self-critical, resilience, learning from mistakes, and not blaming others or circumstances.

## 4. Personal motivation

**Q: Work can be challenging, and it's not the only thing on your mind. How do you approach balancing competing demands for your attention?**
- [ ] Seeking: Resilience. Can they do what's necessary to maintain focus? Do they have self-awareness about how to maintain focus (e.g., exercise, therapy, a flexible work schedule)? How do they seek meaning from work? When necessary, do they work with people to plan for coverage if things are getting really tough?
- [ ] Bonus: Do they help those around them do the same? Do they derive purpose from how their work helps others?
- [ ] Avoid / red flag: Reliance primarily on colleagues to carry them through tough situations. A lack of coping mechanisms or self-awareness — e.g., "Nothing ever bothers me."

**Q: What's the best example of something in your current job that you don't like?**
- [ ] Follow-up: How did you fix it, or have you already tried to fix it?
- [ ] Seeking: Are they focused primarily on fixing things instead of complaining about them? (Fixers vs. complainers.)
- [ ] Tip: Let them explain the example, then assess its magnitude and whether it's meaningful and substantive.

**Q: What are you really excited about?**
- [ ] Seeking: Are they passionate about anything? Are they energetic and optimistic? Would they "set the room on fire" (in a good way)?

## 5. Leadership

**Q: Tell me about the best leader you've worked for.**
- [ ] Seeking: Were they selfless, results-oriented, kind? Did they give indications of effectiveness or deliver feedback? What kind of feedback did they give?
- [ ] Follow-up: What made them a great leader?
- [ ] Tip: Dig into the *why* behind the answer — it's not important who the person specifically is, as long as the candidate can speak cogently about why this leader is the best they've worked with.
- [ ] Follow-up: What are that leader's weaknesses?
- [ ] Tip: This is important — it reveals what the candidate values in a leader (e.g., extrinsic impact vs. creating a safe environment).

**Q: Tell me about a time when you personally worked super hard to get something important done quickly.**
- [ ] Follow-up (engineering-specific): Tell me about the worst incident you handled and how you approached it.
- [ ] Seeking: Are they willing to pull out all the stops and burn the midnight oil when needed? Are they willing to still "hold a pager" and do the work? Are they calm under pressure? Can they act as a prescriptive leader when needed?

**Q: Who was the best hire you ever made?**
- [ ] Tip: This should be a specific individual — dig into the role and the hiring process.
- [ ] Follow-up: Why exactly? What made them good for the role? What were their weaknesses? How did you convince yourself they would be a strong performer given those qualities? How did they work out in the role? Why? What was different from what you expected?
- [ ] Seeking: Is the person comfortable attracting and deciding on talent? Are they effective at hiring and building teams? Are they able to develop a good hiring process and learn from mistakes?

## 6. Decision-making (leadership deep dive, scored)

**Q: Describe a time when you had to make a difficult decision regarding your team's priorities or strategy.**

- [ ] **Situation** — What was it? What were the options, and how did you weigh them? How did you consult with others on your decision? How did you get data or other perspectives on your decision? What company values did you consider in your decision-making?
- [ ] **Action** — How did you communicate your decision? How did you respond to skepticism or questions? How did you defend your decision?
- [ ] **Result** — What was the end result of the decision you made? What data would you have wanted to help with your decision-making?

**Scoring rubric:**

| Dimension | Poor | Good | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Data | Makes uninformed or rash decisions lacking substantial support. | Takes a considered approach but could have leveraged more robust data sources. | Well-rounded, informed approach; uses examples of how they anchored the decision with data. |
| Consultation | Meets opposing opinions with obstinacy or arrogance; decides unilaterally with little to no consultation. | Accepts others' opinions but dismisses them when explaining how the ultimate decision was reached; only consults a select group with existing relationships. | Demonstrates ownership when faced with skepticism, doubt, or questions; open to adjusting; consults a wide range of people with varying opinions, including those most affected. |
| Values alignment | Cannot identify any company values considered, or dismisses values as non-factors. | Expresses admiration for company values but cannot articulate how they were specifically considered. | Clearly outlines how the decision-making aligns with company values and priorities, not only personal priorities. |
| Reflection | Cannot give a clear explanation of the final decision; leaves the situation unclear; cannot identify missing data. | Gives a concise explanation of the decision reached; can identify gaps in the data but not how to close them. | Reflects thoughtfully on the outcome, naming benefits and drawbacks for all stakeholders; identifies what data was missing, how to get it, and how it would change future decisions. |

## 7. After the loop

- [ ] Write feedback against each question's stated "seeking," not against a general impression
- [ ] For the decision-making question, record the Poor/Good/Strong dimension scores explicitly in the debrief
- [ ] Flag red-flag answers ("nothing ever bothers me," complaining without a fix attempt) explicitly for the debrief discussion
- [ ] Hand scored feedback into the candidate review process
