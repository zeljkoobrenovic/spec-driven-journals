## 1. Understand your values (adapted from an exercise by Stan Slap)

- [ ] Imagine yourself at 80 years old, reflecting on your career
- [ ] Ask: what in my work life gave me a sense of fulfillment and meaning?
- [ ] Write down the 10 core values represented in that imagined, fulfilled life
- [ ] Narrow the list to 5 values
- [ ] Narrow the list to 3 values
- [ ] If working with a group, discuss your 3 values and why you chose them
- [ ] For each of the 3 values, write the activities that give you a sense you're living it
- [ ] For each value, reflect on the positive outcomes it produces
- [ ] For each value, reflect on the trade-offs it costs — not just the upside
- [ ] Redo the exercise when a role or life change would shift the answer at 80

- [ ] Prefer a precise value name over a vague one — check it against a one-line definition

### Running this as a team

- [ ] Have each person complete their own worksheet and narrow to their top 3
- [ ] Share the 3 values and stories in small groups
- [ ] Have individuals share their stories with the larger group
- [ ] Look for the values the team shares in common
- [ ] Home in on 3 shared values as a team
- [ ] Consider whether those 3 shared values can become the team's mission

## 5. Consider your work style preferences

- [ ] Place yourself on the grid below, thinking about your actual behavior, not your ideal self
- [ ] Axis 1 — introvert (left) to extrovert (right)
- [ ] Axis 2 — people-oriented (bottom) to task-oriented (top)
- [ ] Identify your quadrant: **Analyzer** (introvert, task-oriented), **Director** (extrovert, task-oriented), **Collaborator** (introvert, people-oriented), or **Promoter** (extrovert, people-oriented)
- [ ] Place yourself once for your "at-home" preferences
- [ ] Place yourself again for your "at-work" preferences
- [ ] Compare the two placements — note any gap between them
- [ ] Treat a gap as a signal of where the role is costing you energy, not an error to correct

## 6. Identify your strengths

- [ ] List your strengths — the things you're best at (example: *Communication*)
- [ ] For each strength, break down the underlying skills and capabilities into innate vs. acquired

- [ ] Repeat the skills/capabilities breakdown for each strength you listed
- [ ] Note which acquired skills you could teach or coach — proof they can be built, not just born
