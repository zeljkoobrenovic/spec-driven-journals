# Spec: Self-Awareness

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State the first tool in my people-management toolkit: I do the self-awareness
work on myself, in writing, before I ask anyone to accept my management. The
post turns the Chapter 1 self-awareness exercises from the *Scaling People*
companion workbooks into an operating principle: the six-step values exercise
adapted from Stan Slap (80-year-old reflection, 10 values narrowed to 5, then
3, activities per value, positive outcomes *and* trade-offs), the values
worksheet that can also surface a team's shared values, the articulate-your-
values template (value / why / example story), the work-style 2×2
(introvert–extrovert × task-oriented–people-oriented: Analyzer, Director,
Collaborator, Promoter — placed twice, at home and at work), and the
strengths breakdown into innate versus acquired skills and capabilities. The
load-bearing idea: management quality is downstream of self-awareness, and
the trade-offs column is the part most managers skip.

## Audience

Managers in my organization (so they know this written self-work is the
entry ticket to managing people here, starting with me); people who report
to a manager here (so they know what their manager should be able to
articulate about themselves); peer executives comparing operating models.
First-person declarative.

## Success criteria

- [x] **Principle is quotable** — highlight states that management quality
      is downstream of self-awareness and that I do this work on myself
      first, in writing, trade-offs included.
- [x] **The mechanics survive** — the six steps, the 80-year-old framing,
      the 10 → 5 → 3 narrowing, the four worksheet columns, the
      value/why/story template, the four 2×2 quadrants with both axes, and
      the innate-versus-acquired split all appear with their real numbers
      and labels.
- [x] **Trade-offs are load-bearing** — the record argues explicitly that a
      values list without a trade-offs column is self-marketing, not
      self-awareness.
- [x] **The team extension is present** — the worksheet run as a group to
      find three shared values, which can become the team's mission.
- [x] **The runnable tool ships** — `checklist.md` operationalizes every
      exercise with its tables and step order intact.
- [x] **Credit is explicit** — Claire Hughes Johnson and the *Scaling
      People* workbooks are credited, and Stan Slap is credited for the
      values exercise and the example-values list from *Bury My Heart at
      Conference Room B*.

  colleagues; this one covers the private self-work the guide is built from.
- Not [[operating-principles]] — that record covers the management
  principles I commit to; this one supplies the self-knowledge those
  principles must be honest about.
- Not [[organizational-values]] — the engineering-executive record covers
  values for an organization; this one covers my personal values and their
  trade-offs.
- Not a performance or development review of anyone else — the exercises
  here are pointed at me before they are pointed at anyone.
