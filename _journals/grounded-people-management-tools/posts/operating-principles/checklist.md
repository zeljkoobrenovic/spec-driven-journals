*The working tool behind this record: a write-your-own kit for a set of operating principles. The Article tab carries the rationale and anti-patterns.*

## 1. Before you write

- [ ] Confirm this is worth writing down — you have real, recurring tensions people currently resolve by guessing
- [ ] Gather 5–10 recent decisions or disagreements that would have gone differently with a written principle
- [ ] Read a strong published example (e.g. Stripe's operating principles) for its **form**, not its words: section count, principles per section, sentence length, whether leaders get a separate tier
- [ ] Rule out copy-paste: if you cannot rewrite every line from scratch in your own words, you are not ready to publish
- [ ] Decide who drafts (usually the senior leadership team) and who reviews before it ships

## 2. Section 1 — How we work

*Method: the behaviors that get things done under pressure.*

- [ ] Draft 4–6 principles covering how your organization actually operates, e.g.:
  - [ ] Whose needs come first when priorities conflict
  - [ ] Speed and bias for action vs. deliberation
  - [ ] Craft and quality standards
  - [ ] How feedback and challenge are invited
  - [ ] What "outstanding results" and accountability mean here
- [ ] For each: write a short bold name (2–3 words) plus a 2–3 sentence body
- [ ] Test each principle: does it take a side on a real tradeoff, or would every competitor happily sign it too?

## 3. Section 2 — Who we are

*Character: the disposition expected regardless of role.*

- [ ] Draft 4–6 principles covering the traits you want in every hire, e.g.:
  - [ ] Curiosity vs. certainty
  - [ ] Resilience and how the organization treats failure
  - [ ] Humility vs. defensiveness about current practice
  - [ ] Optimism vs. cynicism about hard problems
  - [ ] Energy and warmth the organization wants to be known for
- [ ] For each: name plus 2–3 sentence body, same discipline as Section 1
- [ ] Check these read as character, not method — if a "who we are" line is really about process, move it to Section 1

## 4. Section 3 — And leaders

*A separate, harder tier: demands, not aspirations.*

- [ ] Draft 5–7 principles that apply only to people who lead others, e.g.:
  - [ ] Talent bar — obligation to recruit and hold a high bar, and to act on mishires
  - [ ] Ambition horizon — next year, several years out, a decade out
  - [ ] Pace and energy leaders are expected to set for their teams
  - [ ] Decision-making and accountability under murky ownership
  - [ ] Translating context into a clear, referenced plan
  - [ ] Persistence on hard problems, across team boundaries
- [ ] Verify this section reads sharper and less negotiable than Sections 1–2 — if it reads softer, rewrite it
- [ ] Confirm every current leader, including yourself, could be evaluated against each line

## 5. Optional layer — Classic slogans

- [ ] Collect 5–10 short, memorable one-liners that carry cultural memory (may predate the current formal set)
- [ ] Keep each to a single sentence — no bodies, no explanation
- [ ] Label this layer explicitly as secondary/lighter-weight, not equivalent to the named principles
- [ ] Drop any slogan that has quietly become false

## 6. Quality tests (run against every principle)

| Test | Pass condition |
| --- | --- |
| Takes a side | Names which side of a real tradeoff wins, not both |
| Not generic | Could not be lifted verbatim into a competitor's document without editing |
| Tested against a decision | You can name a real past decision this principle would have changed |
| Right length | One short bold name + 2–3 sentences, not a paragraph or a single word |
| Leaders held tighter | The leaders' section reads as more demanding than the general sections, never softer |
| Memorable | Someone could invoke the name in a meeting six months from now and be understood |

## 7. Publish and date it

- [ ] Add a visible "as of [month, year]" date stamp at the top of the document
- [ ] Confirm the three section headers are present: how we work / who we are / and leaders
- [ ] Circulate to leadership for a final read against Section 6's tests before wide distribution
- [ ] Store the document somewhere every current and future employee can find it
- [ ] Set a recurring review trigger (see Section 8) rather than treating publication as final

## 8. Revisit triggers

- [ ] A principle has been quoted zero times in six months — cut it or rewrite it
- [ ] A leader is visibly not held to the leaders' section — fix the enforcement gap before rewriting the words
- [ ] The organization's stage, size, or market has shifted enough that a past tradeoff no longer applies
- [ ] A new leadership team member can't explain what a principle means in practice — the wording has failed
- [ ] It has been more than 12–18 months since the date stamp was last updated
