*The working tool behind this record. The Article tab carries the rationale and anti-patterns.*

## 1. What must be true after the QBR meeting

- [ ] The QBR provided clear charter (long-term) and operating (short-term) metrics, with quarterly targets through the end of the year
- [ ] I can answer: where do we want to be in two years, and are we on track?
- [ ] The QBR explained which goals we executed, which we did not, why, and how we'll close any gaps (or why the plan is changing)
- [ ] The QBR explained how much we're investing relative to what the area is producing
- [ ] Participants left with clear mutual expectations for the coming period
- [ ] We know what potential obstacles may come up through the end of the year
- [ ] Leadership understands what it needs to unblock to help us accelerate and produce outstanding results

## 2. Delivering a stellar QBR

- [ ] **Focus on results** — show, with metrics, how execution is progressing, rather than holding a winding discussion of potential plans
- [ ] **Be candid** — write a candid assessment of what the team accomplished, what it needs to do better at, and what it plans to do; this is not a summary of successes
- [ ] **Be concise** — six-page document maximum (the narrative section itself at most two pages), plus an appendix with tables and/or charts
- [ ] **Prepare in advance** — share the memo at least 24 hours before the meeting; reserve reading time inside the meeting itself
- [ ] **Be present** — attendees minimize work distractions during the discussion (snooze notifications, close other windows); consume materials digitally without linking out to supplementary materials

## 3. Before the QBR meeting

- [ ] Link the QBR document in the calendar invite and leadership guidebook at least 24 hours in advance
- [ ] If there are confidential topics, put them in a separate, access-restricted document and link it from the guidebook
- [ ] Email the discussion materials directly to attendees
- [ ] Prepare an agenda of the key topics to cover with leadership

## 4. During the QBR meeting

- [ ] Allow dedicated time for pre-reads and announce when reading time has ended
- [ ] Gather top-of-mind questions and topics from attendees and add them to the prepared agenda
- [ ] Prioritize topics so the most important ones are covered first within the allotted time
- [ ] Include metrics as a standard discussion topic
- [ ] The QBR leader drives the discussion based on the agenda's key topics
- [ ] Take comprehensive notes, or assign someone in the meeting to take them — capture action items and owners

## 5. After the QBR meeting

- [ ] Share the notes with all QBR participants
- [ ] Add action items to the QBR action-item tracker
- [ ] Reflect on the quality of the QBR, including the quality of the metrics
- [ ] Gather feedback from partners and leadership if it wasn't offered readily
- [ ] Name what to work on and what to change for next time

## 6. Document outline and limits

Directional word/page limits keep the document within six pages total. If content exceeds the limit, the QBR leader decides what to shorten.

| Section | Limit | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Executive summary | ≤ 250 words | Candid assessment of performance and strategic direction — not a summary of successes |
| Narrative | ≤ 2 pages | Grounded in charter and operating metrics; discusses performance against goals |
| Metrics performance | ≤ 2 pages | Additional metrics beyond the narrative go in the appendix |
| Progress on goals | ≤ 1 page | Top 5–10 goals only; additional goals go in the appendix |
| Product P&L | — | Product teams work with finance to include this as a table |
| Cross-functional focus areas | — | Scores plus a comment on what's being done to improve each score |
| Appendix A | — | Required tables (see section 10) |
| Appendix B | — | Optional supporting materials, at the leader's discretion |

- [ ] Executive summary written to 250 words or fewer
- [ ] Narrative written to 2 pages or fewer
- [ ] Metrics performance section written to 2 pages or fewer, overflow metrics moved to the appendix
- [ ] Progress-on-goals table limited to the top 5–10 goals, overflow goals moved to the appendix
- [ ] Relevant metric visualizations embedded directly in the narrative where they help the reader

## 7. Narrative content rules

- [ ] Narrative speaks for itself — any live "voiceover" only focuses the discussion, it doesn't repeat the memo
- [ ] Discussion of results is candid and appropriately balanced against relative performance
- [ ] If goals were missed, the discussion skews toward lowlights — why didn't we deliver?
- [ ] If goals were met, still discuss any areas that did not go well
- [ ] Narrative covers quarterly results, outlook for the remainder of the year, and longer-term strategic considerations
- [ ] At least 50 percent of the discussion is spent on strategy and outlook, not backward-looking results

## 8. Example narrative outline

Use at the leader's discretion — structure the narrative to best describe your team's performance and trajectory.

1. **Current quarter reflection** — explain performance against goals
   - [ ] Support performance against goals with operating and charter metrics
   - [ ] Explain how metrics were impacted and how they changed over time
   - [ ] Explain what the team did (or did not do) to influence them
   - [ ] Discuss key ships, and if key ships were missed, explain why
   - [ ] Discuss user feedback: top user asks and how well they're being addressed, plus user compliments in areas performing well
   - [ ] Discuss overall velocity
2. **Outlook** — look forward to the next quarter and the next year
   - [ ] State the top three goals in order of priority
   - [ ] Identify which metrics those goals will impact
   - [ ] Explain how user stories and pain points will be impacted
   - [ ] State whether the team is on track to meet these goals, and if not, why
   - [ ] Answer: what keeps you up at night? (Read: what should leadership be worried about?)
3. **Other sections** — use at your discretion, based on relevance
   - [ ] Cross-functional asks
   - [ ] Hiring progress
   - [ ] Team morale

## 9. Metrics performance

- [ ] Metrics cover a roughly two-year time horizon where available, not just the current period
- [ ] Charts briefly describe changes to the metrics used (since the last QBR, or anticipated changes)
- [ ] If metrics were revised during planning, charts and/or commentary show both the original and the revised target
- [ ] Metrics not already embedded in the narrative are included here
