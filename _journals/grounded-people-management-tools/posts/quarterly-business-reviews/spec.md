# Spec: Quarterly Business Reviews

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State how I run Quarterly Business Reviews: a QBR is a written, candid,
six-page accountability ritual — not a showcase. The post turns the *Scaling
People* QBR system into an operating principle: the six statements that must
be true after the meeting (charter vs. operating metrics with targets through
year end, goals executed or not and why, investment vs. production, mutual
expectations, known obstacles, what leadership can unblock), the five
delivery rules (results, candor, concision, 24-hour pre-share with in-meeting
reading time, presence), the before/during/after meeting mechanics, the
six-page document outline with its hard limits (250-word executive summary,
two-page narrative skewed to lowlights when goals were missed, at least half
the discussion on strategy and outlook, two-year metric horizons with
original and revised targets, cross-functional focus-area scoring, top 5–10
goals on one page), and the required appendix tables.

## Audience

Leaders in my organization who own a QBR (so they know exactly what the
document and the meeting must deliver); the leadership team that reads them
(so they know what to demand); peer executives comparing review rituals.
First-person declarative.

## Success criteria

- [x] **Principle is quotable** — highlight states that the QBR answers
      "where do we want to be in two years, and are we on track?" and that
      the executive summary is "a candid assessment, not a summary of your
      successes."
- [x] **The six post-meeting truths survive** — charter vs. operating
      metrics with quarterly targets through year end, goals and gaps,
      investment vs. production, mutual expectations, known obstacles,
      what leadership can unblock.
- [x] **The numbers survive** — six pages plus appendix, ≤250-word
      executive summary, ≤2-page narrative, ≥50% of discussion on strategy
      and outlook, ~two-year metric horizons, top 5–10 goals on one page,
      materials shared ≥24 hours ahead, reading time in the meeting.
- [x] **The document outline and tables survive** — narrative, metrics
      performance, product P&L, cross-functional focus areas (Reliability,
      Security, Efficiency, Operations, Product quality), goal scoring, and
      the Appendix A required tables reproduced in the Checklist tab.
- [x] **Credit is explicit** — How to Read This and References credit
      Claire Hughes Johnson's workbooks and Kailey Stockenbojer of the
      Stripe finance team as the author of the QBR documents.

## Non-goals

- Not [[writing-okrs]] — that record covers setting goals; this one covers
  reviewing performance against them each quarter.
- Not [[leadership-team-updates]] — that record covers the lightweight
  recurring update cadence; the QBR is the deep quarterly review.
- Not [[team-charter]] — the charter and its long-term metrics are inputs
  the QBR reports against, not something this record defines.
- Not a metrics-design guide — what to measure is
  [[measuring-engineering-organizations]]; this record covers how the
  measurements get reviewed.
