# Spec: Writing OKRs

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State my operating model for writing, scrutinizing, publishing, and scoring
OKRs. The post turns three adjacent Chapter 2 workbook tools — the
Objectives and Metrics Checklist, the Writing Good OKRs guide, and the
Iterating and Scoring guidance — into one record: the fixed grammar of 3–5
objectives with 1–5 key results each, a scrutiny checklist that catches
unmeasurable or gameable metrics before publication, calibrated ambition
(70–80 percent typical, must-hit goals at 95 percent or better), the
write–collaborate–publish sequence, and disciplined mid-quarter scoring and
change handling.

## Audience

Managers and team leads in my organization writing quarterly OKRs (so the
grammar, scrutiny checklist, and ambition bands are the standard, not
optional); leaders reviewing OKRs across teams (so they can hold drafts to
the same bar); peer executives comparing operating models. First-person
declarative.

## Success criteria

- [x] **Principle is quotable** — highlight states the fixed grammar (3–5
      objectives, 1–5 key results), the scrutiny checklist, and the
      calibrated-ambition bands in one paragraph.
- [x] **The metrics table survives** — objective/metric/baseline/target
      columns and the full scrutiny checklist (inspirational, actionable,
      tied to mission and priorities, SMART, leading indicator, counterweight,
      evaluation framework, owner, relative not absolute, accounts for
      existing growth) reproduced in the Checklist tab.
- [x] **Ambition calibration is explicit** — 70–80 percent typical range;
      20–30 percent of goals as must-hit at 95 percent or better attainment,
      sacrificing other goals if needed.
- [x] **Objectives vs. key results best practices both land** — outcomes
      describing the state of the world, user-problem focus, nesting,
      aspirational vs. committed for objectives; measurable and binary,
      causal theory, compounding for key results.
- [x] **Common pitfalls are named** — activity-as-outcome, unmeasurable key
      results, roadmap-as-OKRs, too many objectives or key results.
- [x] **Iteration discipline survives** — mid-quarter green/yellow/red with
      on-track/off-track/at-risk, documented changes with impacted-team
      notification, and the rule that a dropped objective is rare and stays
      in for scoring.
- [x] **Credit is explicit** — References name Claire Hughes Johnson's
      *Scaling People* companion workbooks and credit Stripe's finance and
      tech-enablement teams with CTO David Singleton for the Writing Good
      OKRs guide.

## Non-goals

- Not [[team-objectives]] — that product-journal record covers how leaders
  assign problems and teams propose OKRs; this one covers the writing,
  scrutiny, and scoring grammar those OKRs are held to.
- Not [[quarterly-business-reviews]] — that record covers how scored OKRs
  meet the business narrative each quarter; this one produces the inputs.
- Not [[team-charter]] — that record covers mission and plan; this one covers
  how objectives trace to it, not how the charter itself is written.
- No performance-management claim — OKR attainment can inform
  [[performance-reviews]], but this record does not define that process.
