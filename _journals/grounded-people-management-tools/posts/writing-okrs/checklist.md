## 2. Scrutinize the objectives

- [ ] Inspirational?
- [ ] Actionable by your team?
- [ ] Related to your team's mission and vision?
- [ ] Related to the company's priorities and goals?

## 3. Check the objectives are SMART

- [ ] Specific
- [ ] Measurable
- [ ] Achievable
- [ ] Results-oriented
- [ ] Targeted

## 4. Scrutinize each metric

- [ ] Is the metric a leading indicator?
- [ ] Does it have a counterweight metric? (e.g., a user-growth metric needs a
      counterweight revenue metric — you could always grow users by giving
      the product away for free)
- [ ] Is there a framework to evaluate the metric — a calculation or a query?
- [ ] Does the metric have a named owner?
- [ ] Is the target relative, not absolute? (e.g., not "increase users by
      1,000" but "increase users by 20 percent, from 5,000 to 6,000")
- [ ] Does the target account for existing user growth? (hitting a revenue
      target purely from existing-user growth earns nothing new)

## 5. Write, collaborate, publish

- [ ] **Write**: note the top-level 3–5 objectives, with 1–5 key results per
      objective, that the team is committed to accomplishing this quarter
- [ ] Identify which objectives are must-hit
- [ ] **Collaborate**: for every team you partner with closely, get feedback
      and confirm agreement on the OKRs set
- [ ] **Publish**: when done, change "Draft" to "On track" in the document
      title
- [ ] Publish OKRs where the company can find them (intranet or your
      document-sharing method), in progress

## 6. Calibrate ambition

- [ ] Most goals are ambitious yet attainable — expect to score 70–80 percent
      over the quarter across the majority of goals
- [ ] Mark 20–30 percent of a team's goals as **must-hit** hard commitments
- [ ] Expect must-hit goals to reach 95 percent attainment or better
- [ ] If a must-hit goal is trending behind, sacrifice other (non-must-hit)
      goals to protect it

## 7. Best practices for good objectives

- [ ] Objective answers "Where do I want to go?"
- [ ] Objective is an outcome — the state of the world at the end of the
      quarter — not an activity
- [ ] Objective focuses on the user problem being solved
- [ ] No more than 3–5 objectives per team
- [ ] Objective charts intent and direction, not a task list
- [ ] Objective can be long-term, spanning several quarters or years
- [ ] Objective nests up and down the organization to some degree
- [ ] Objective is ambitious and open to some interpretation and debate
- [ ] Objective matters — the best objectives are inspirational
- [ ] Labeled aspirational (70 percent) or committed (100 percent) explicitly
- [ ] Shared across teams where relevant

## 8. Best practices for good key results

- [ ] Key result is a concrete definition of success
- [ ] Key result answers "How will I pace myself to see if I'm on track?"
- [ ] 1–5 key results per objective
- [ ] Key result lists an outcome, not an activity
- [ ] Key result is measurable and binary — it either happened or it did not,
      not open to interpretation
- [ ] Key result flows from a plausible causal theory connecting it to the
      objective and to your own team's work
- [ ] Key result helps build a shared model of reality across teams
- [ ] Key result should compound over time where possible

## 9. Common pitfalls — check none apply

- [ ] An activity or action is being used as an outcome
- [ ] A key result can't be measured in any way
- [ ] The roadmap has been turned directly into objectives and key results
- [ ] More than 3–5 objectives, or more than 1–5 key results per objective

## 10. Mid-quarter scoring

- [ ] Get the team together mid-quarter to discuss progress to date
- [ ] Give each objective/key result an approximate score using green,
      yellow, red (or a percentage-based score if that fits the team better)
- [ ] Pair the color score with an assessment: on track, off track, or at
      risk
- [ ] Discuss every item scored off track or at risk
- [ ] Find ways to redirect energy and build in time to recover

## 11. Handling changes in information and priorities

- [ ] Agree on a common practice for tracking and handling changes before
      you need one
- [ ] Communicate every change to any impacted team
- [ ] Clearly document what changed and why
- [ ] If adding or dropping a key result: document the decision and notify
      impacted teams
- [ ] If adding an objective (e.g., an urgent shift in the world): document
      whether it deprioritized existing work, and if so, what was deprioritized
- [ ] Treat dropping an objective as rare — only for a real change in
      constraints or because it truly isn't providing value to users
- [ ] When an objective is dropped, stop working on it but keep it in the
      OKRs for scoring and discussion — it does not disappear from the sheet
