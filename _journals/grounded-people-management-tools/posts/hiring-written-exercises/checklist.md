## 1. Design the exercise

- [ ] Confirm the role is writing-heavy enough to justify a written exercise
- [ ] Write clear, unambiguous instructions
- [ ] Cap effort at about three hours
- [ ] Cap output at no more than a few pages
- [ ] Frame the deliverable explicitly: treat it as a pre-read you'd send before a meeting
- [ ] Provide the full dataset (or link to it) up front
- [ ] Provide all data definitions so the exercise tests thinking, not data archaeology
- [ ] Tell candidates reasonable assumptions are expected and welcome
- [ ] State plainly what the exercise will be used to assess

## 2. Product manager written exercise (reference prompt)

- [ ] Set the scenario: users love the product, but it churns; the org takes churn seriously
- [ ] Ask the candidate to analyze the example churn data
- [ ] Ask: what is leading to churn?
- [ ] Ask: what are the most important takeaways from the data?
- [ ] Ask the candidate to imagine building a retention roadmap
- [ ] Ask: what projects and product changes would you prioritize to reduce churn?
- [ ] Require the response to be specific enough to display product sense and technical thinking

## 3. What the exercise assesses

- [ ] Written communication
- [ ] Data analysis and the ability to translate data into actions
- [ ] Understanding the customer (customer sense)
- [ ] Product sense

## 4. Score the rubric — 12 points total

| Points (out of 12) | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| 11–12 | Strong yes |
| 8–10 | Yes |
| 7 | Judgment call |
| 6 or below | No |

- [ ] Write down the rubric anchors before reading any submission
- [ ] Score each of the four skills independently, 1–3 points each
- [ ] Sum to a total out of 12
- [ ] Do not let a strong score on one skill silently offset a weak score on another
- [ ] Treat a score of 7 as a real decision, not a rounding rule
- [ ] Record the score and rationale alongside the candidate's file

## 5. Rubric — Written communication (3 pts)

| Poor (1 pt) | Good (2 pts) | Excellent (3 pts) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hard to follow, long-winded, or sloppy. | Deliverable is easy to follow and conveys the main points. Structured, crisp, and clear. | You would feel comfortable sending this as a pre-read. Deliverable is coherently organized and well-articulated. |

## 6. Rubric — Data analysis, ability to translate data into actions (3 pts)

| Poor (1 pt) | Good (2 pts) | Excellent (3 pts) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Only provides basic summary or is unable to pick a small number of cuts (e.g., presents a pivot table of every view of the data without a clear approach for which slices are most impactful). Data barely informs the recommended set of actions, or actions are contradictory to the data analysis. Obvious errors in the data. | Conclusions drawn from the data are strong and accurate. Clarifies which data is most important and why it's worth actioning; parses the insights into "so-whats." | Picked a small number of important metrics to guide next steps and explained their impact on the business. Identifies why next steps clearly fix problems highlighted by the data. |

- [ ] Check for a clear approach to which data cuts matter, not an exhaustive dump
- [ ] Check that recommended actions are actually supported by the data
- [ ] Check for obvious data errors
- [ ] Check whether insights are parsed into explicit "so-whats"

## 7. Rubric — Customer sense (3 pts)

| Poor (1 pt) | Good (2 pts) | Excellent (3 pts) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Doesn't identify customer segments and their needs; fails to differentiate recommendations by customer segment. Dismisses particular customer segments as non-addressable (e.g., suggests that companies being acquired should not be considered). | Understands customer needs and provides concrete suggestions that vary by customer segment. | Carries customer insights and needs throughout many sections of the exercise, not just one. |

- [ ] Check whether segments are named and differentiated
- [ ] Check whether segment thinking is carried through the whole response, not mentioned once
- [ ] Watch for segments dismissed as non-addressable without justification

## 8. Rubric — Product sense (3 pts)

| Poor (1 pt) | Good (2 pts) | Excellent (3 pts) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unclear product ideas or inability to tie the product to user or business impact (e.g., recommends numerous "investigate why" steps but fails to articulate any product solutions). | Explains product gaps that need to be addressed. Makes a logical, balanced argument about why the company should consider these investments, but may lack a clear prioritization. | Provides creative, specific product ideas, highlighting users or strategy impact; clear prioritization of what is most important. Addresses something unique about their ideas (e.g., their go-to-market strategy would not just generically apply to any segment; calls out technical complexity or other risks associated with pursuing it, etc.). Recommendations are put in the context of the overall industry; discusses the implications for long-term business success. |

- [ ] Check for concrete, prioritized ideas — not just diagnosis
- [ ] Check for named risks (technical complexity, go-to-market, etc.)
- [ ] Check for industry context and long-term business implications
- [ ] Check that ideas are specific to this segment/product, not generic
