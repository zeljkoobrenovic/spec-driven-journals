# Spec: Managing Underperformance

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State how I handle underperformance as one continuous arc, not a single
event: early, documented feedback first; a formal Performance Improvement
Plan only if the pattern persists; and, if it still does not resolve, a
humane managing-out process. The post turns three workbook tools — the
Performance Improvement Documentation Templates (four follow-up letters:
initial documentation, progress update, back on track, insufficient
progress), the Performance Improvement Plan Template (role expectations, up
to three improvement areas with standard/gap/examples, goals and milestones
with deadlines, resources and an HR partner, weekly progress checks, the
at-will and no-guarantee caveats, signatures), and the Managing Out Checklist
(eight sequenced steps from feedback through announcement) — into a single
operating principle: a PIP should never be the first time someone hears they
are underperforming, and documentation exists to protect the person as much
as the company.

## Audience

Every manager in my organization (so they document early instead of
skipping straight to a PIP, or skipping documentation altogether); people
on a PIP or heading toward one (so they know the process is bounded,
written, and not a surprise); HR partners co-owning the process. First-person
declarative.

## Success criteria

- [x] **Principle is quotable** — highlight states underperformance is
      handled early, in writing, and humanely, and that a PIP that surprises
      anyone is a manager's failure, not the employee's.
- [x] **The arc is explicit** — 1:1/review feedback → documented follow-up
      letters → (if unresolved) formal PIP → (if still unresolved) managing
      out, in that order, never skipping to the middle.
- [x] **The four letters keep their distinct jobs** — initial documentation
      (expectation, gap, dated examples, actionable next steps, weekly
      check-ins for X weeks), progress update (restate expectation, progress
      examples, what still must change and why it's core to the role), back
      on track (confirm expectations met, expect sustained performance,
      congratulate), insufficient progress (recap unaddressed feedback,
      propose a realistic conversation about whether they can meet
      expectations).
- [x] **The PIP mechanics survive** — typically four to six weeks; 3–5 role
      expectation bullets from JD/level/ladder; up to three improvement areas
      each with expected standard, gap and impact, and dated examples; goals,
      deliverables, and milestones with deadlines; resources and a named HR
      partner; weekly progress checks; the caveats (may end early, no
      employment guarantee, at-will unchanged, sustained performance required
      after passing); signatures.
- [x] **The Managing Out Checklist keeps all eight steps in sequence.**
- [x] **Local law caveat is explicit** — the workbook's own note that the PIP
      template needs adapting to local employment regulations is carried
      into the article.
- [x] **The checklist survives intact** — all three tools reproduced as the
      runnable tool in the Checklist tab (`checklist.md`).
- [x] **Credit is explicit** — References name Claire Hughes Johnson's
      *Scaling People* companion workbooks as the source.

## Non-goals

- Not [[performance-reviews]] — that record covers the regular review cycle;
  this one covers what happens when a review or 1:1 surfaces a gap that does
  not close.
- Not [[compensation-conversations]] — pay is a separate conversation; this
  record does not touch compensation decisions during a PIP.
- Not a jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction employment-law guide — the workbook
  flags that its templates need local legal adaptation, and this record
  repeats that caveat rather than resolving it.
- Not a termination or severance playbook — the Managing Out Checklist ends
  at the announcement step; what follows procedurally (offboarding,
  severance mechanics) is out of scope here.
