*The working tool behind this record. The Article tab carries the rationale and anti-patterns.*

## 1. Initial documentation (after a 1:1 or a "partially meets expectations" review)

- [ ] State the meeting this recaps ("yesterday" / "over the past few weeks" / "1:1" / "performance review")
- [ ] Name the area of development where expectations are not currently being met
- [ ] Summarize performance relative to expectation in one line
- [ ] Give at least two dated examples of not meeting expectations, each with: the situation, the observable behavior, and the impact it had
- [ ] State each performance expectation as an actionable next step (not a vague aspiration)
- [ ] Set a weekly check-in cadence for a stated number of weeks
- [ ] Close supportively: offer help, and state the goal is to see them get back on track and succeed

## 2. Progress update

- [ ] Restate the performance expectation from the initial documentation
- [ ] Describe performance relative to expectation and give a progress assessment
- [ ] Give at least two dated examples that demonstrate progress: situation, observable behavior, impact
- [ ] Name what they should keep doing, plus other things they could also do
- [ ] Restate any expectation that still needs work
- [ ] Give at least one dated example of what still needs to change: situation, observable behavior, impact
- [ ] State explicitly that the remaining area is core to the role and to meeting expectations at their ladder/level
- [ ] Confirm the weekly check-in cadence continues

## 3. Employee getting back on track

- [ ] Name the time period covered and the area of development
- [ ] Summarize performance relative to expectation
- [ ] Give at least two recent dated examples of meeting expectations: situation, observable behavior, impact
- [ ] Confirm they are meeting performance expectations for their role and level
- [ ] State the expectation that this performance is sustained going forward
- [ ] Note that these areas will continue to be monitored in regular 1:1s
- [ ] Close by congratulating them

## 4. Insufficient progress (after a reasonable period)

- [ ] Name the time period covered and the area(s) still not meeting expectations
- [ ] Summarize performance relative to expectation and its impact
- [ ] Give at least two recent dated examples of not meeting expectations: situation, observable behavior, impact
- [ ] Acknowledge any additional effort observed
- [ ] State plainly that these examples show prior feedback has not been addressed
- [ ] Propose a realistic next conversation about whether they can meet expectations for their ladder/level
- [ ] Deliver this directly and acknowledge it is difficult to hear, while making sure they understand the situation

## 5. Before starting a Performance Improvement Plan (PIP)

- [ ] Confirm several documented conversations already exist identifying the development areas
- [ ] Confirm those conversations included examples of the performance gap
- [ ] Confirm support was already offered to help the person meet expectations
- [ ] Confirm a PIP is not the first time this person will hear they are underperforming
- [ ] Check local employment regulations before drafting — the template needs jurisdiction-specific adaptation

## 6. PIP: header and framing

- [ ] Record name, manager, and date
- [ ] State plainly that performance has not met expectations for the role and level, as previously discussed
- [ ] State the goal: identify development areas and provide a real opportunity to improve, with the tools and resources needed

## 7. PIP: role expectations

- [ ] Include 3–5 bullets summarizing key expectations for the role, based on job description, level, and ladder
- [ ] Make each bullet specifically relate to an area needing improvement

## 8. PIP: areas for improvement (up to three)

| Area | Expected standard | Gap / deficiency and impact | Dated examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Standard, tied to operating principles where relevant | Where the person falls short and the impact on the project/team | Specific, demonstrable, dated |
| 2 | Standard | Gap and impact | Dated examples |
| 3 | Standard | Gap and impact | Dated examples |

- [ ] Limit to three improvement areas maximum
- [ ] Ensure every area already appeared in prior documented feedback

## 9. PIP: goals, deliverables, and milestones

- [ ] **Goal and time frame** — for each improvement area, what must be accomplished, by when, what success looks like
- [ ] **Deliverable** — for each improvement area, what demonstrates the goal was met
- [ ] **Milestones** — list concrete milestones (e.g. specific projects) with their own hard deadlines

## 10. PIP: resources and support

- [ ] Confirm commitment to helping the person achieve the goals and pass the PIP
- [ ] Offer to help prioritize and remove or navigate blockers
- [ ] State that the person is expected to take initiative and self-direct within the plan
- [ ] Point to relevant educational resources or training where useful
- [ ] Name the HR partner available for additional support or guidance

## 11. PIP: progress checks and evaluation

- [ ] Set a weekly meeting cadence to discuss progress against the plan
- [ ] Ask the person to keep notes and records of accomplishments and blockers
- [ ] Set the PIP duration — typically four to six weeks — and the evaluation date
- [ ] State plainly that the PIP may be ended early
- [ ] State plainly that entering the PIP does not guarantee employment for its duration
- [ ] State plainly that the at-will nature of employment is unchanged throughout
- [ ] State plainly that, given the duration and seriousness of the deficiencies, failing to meet the outlined expectations may result in termination
- [ ] State plainly that successfully completing the PIP requires sustained performance afterward, and further underperformance may end employment without a further PIP

## 12. PIP: close and signatures

- [ ] Summarize that this is an important opportunity to demonstrate meeting all expectations for the role
- [ ] Reaffirm commitment to clear feedback and guidance throughout
- [ ] Invite questions about the process at any time
- [ ] Confirm the person has read the plan, discussed it with their manager, and understands it
- [ ] Collect employee signature
- [ ] Collect manager signature

## 13. Managing Out Checklist (run in this order)

- [ ] Provide feedback on the performance issues
- [ ] Document the feedback
- [ ] If relevant, run a formal performance improvement process
- [ ] Familiarize yourself with local employment regulations
- [ ] Inform your manager and your HR partner, if you have one
- [ ] Hold the coaching-out conversation with your report
- [ ] Agree on the timeline for leaving and the communication plan
- [ ] Announce the departure to the team or organization, including transition-plan details if the role requires immediate coverage
