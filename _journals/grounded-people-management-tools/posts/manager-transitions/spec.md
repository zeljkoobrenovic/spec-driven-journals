# Spec: Manager Transitions

> Working doc for the post in this folder. The spec drives the post; the post
> is the artifact.

## Intent

State how I run a manager change when a report moves to another team: a
manager transition is a moment where development context is either handed
over deliberately or lost, and I don't let reports restart from zero. The
post turns the *Scaling People* Manager Transitions Guide into an operating
principle: a three-step sequence — the current manager previews the
transition one-on-one, the current manager schedules a three-way transition
meeting using a fixed email agenda, and the new manager owns follow-up on
whatever action items come out of it. The record names what must survive the
handoff: current scope and projects, strengths and development areas,
ambitions, preferred ways of working, and candid feedback about the old
manager that is useful for the new one to hear.

## Audience

Managers handing a report to another team (so they run the preview
conversation and the three-way meeting instead of skipping straight to an
introduction email); managers receiving a report (so they know they own
follow-up from day one, not just the relationship going forward); the
transitioning employee (so they know what to expect and that their voice is
part of the process, not an afterthought). First-person declarative.

## Success criteria

- [ ] **Principle is quotable** — highlight states a manager change is where
      development context is "handed over deliberately or lost" and that I
      "don't let reports restart from zero."
- [ ] **The three steps are explicit and ordered** — preview (current
      manager, solo) → three-way transition meeting (current manager
      schedules, fixed agenda) → follow-up (new manager owns it going
      forward).
- [ ] **The preview conversation's four items survive** — confirm the formal
      move date in the HR system, when the new manager gains system access
      and visibility into reviews, announce the transition meeting, take
      questions.
- [ ] **The email agenda template survives** — all five discussion points
      (current scope/projects/goals; strengths and development areas;
      three-way discussion on supporting ambitions; preferred ways of
      working plus feedback on the old manager; recap of next steps)
      reproduced in the Checklist tab, in order.
- [ ] **Ownership handoff is explicit** — the new manager follows up on
      action items because they own the relationship going forward; this is
      stated as a commitment, not left implicit.
- [ ] **Credit is explicit** — References name Claire Hughes Johnson's
      *Scaling People* companion workbooks and the specific tool (Manager
      Transitions Guide, Chapter 3).

## Non-goals

- Not [[new-leader-onboarding]] — that record covers a leader arriving into
  a new team or scope; this one covers an individual contributor or manager
  moving between existing teams under existing leadership.
- Not [[career-conversations]] — that record covers the recurring
  development dialogue with one's own manager; this one covers the
  discrete handoff moment when the manager relationship itself changes.
- Not [[working-with-me]] — that record covers how I operate day to day
  once someone is on my team; this one covers what gets carried over when
  they arrive from elsewhere.
- No claim that the transition meeting replaces the new manager's own
  onboarding work — it is the handoff of context, not a substitute for the
  new manager building their own relationship with the person.
